## SECTION 1.0 – INTRODUCTION

This manual is based on the site hydrological characteristics, climatic conditions, water balances, and project water balances. It describes all flow management systems for the different conditions present in the valley leach system [TBX:T0005] (VLS) (surface water, infiltration, contacted water, solution, etc.), from operation through closure. This document includes the chapter corresponding to flow management under contingency situations. It details how direct precipitation and runoff are managed in all cases within the valley, with no discharge of flows to the exterior.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Introductory paragraph establishing manual scope and objectives, TBX term for valley leach system applied]

### BARREN AND PLS PIPELINES WITH THEIR PUMPING SYSTEMS

The solution management system is based on pipelines that run from the plant to the irrigation cells, and from the cells to the storage areas. The VLS irrigation flow is transported by a 24" steel pipeline system, designated Barren and PLS.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Standard description of pipeline infrastructure, VLS abbreviation for valley leach system]

The Barren pipeline transports the irrigation flow from the Process Plant to the various sectors under irrigation. Along its route, there is a repumping [TBX:T0016] system designated Booster Barren 1 and 2, which ensures the irrigation elevation. The nominal transport flow rate is 2900 m³/h. The pumps located in Sump [TBX:T0009] 420 (Booster Barren 1) are designated 1A and 1B. These are vertical turbine type pumps with a capacity of 749 kW and a nominal flow rate of 1350 m³/h. From this sump [TBX:T0009] they conduct the solution to Sump [TBX:T0009] 2 (Booster Barren 2) which houses pumps 2A and 2B, which are also vertical turbine type pumps, with a capacity of 1300 kW and a nominal flow rate of 1550 m³/h. From sump [TBX:T0009] 2, the pipelines exit to irrigate phases 5 and 6 to 9.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Technical description of barren solution pumping infrastructure with TBX term for sump, repumping preferred term applied]

The PLS pipeline is responsible for recirculating the solution that does not enter the Process Plant to a set of previously agreed-upon irrigation cells. Flow pumping is performed from pumps 8A and 8B, installed in the pregnant solution storage area [TBX:T0007] (PSSA), whose nominal flow rate is 1200 m³/h. These pumps drive the PLS solution to phases 1 to 5 for irrigation or to PLS2, where pumps 9A and 9B re-pump it to irrigate phases 6 to 9.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: PLS recirculation system description with TBX term for pregnant solution storage area, acronym PSSA introduced]

### LEAK COLLECTION AND RECOVERY SYSTEM

The leak collection and recovery system [TBX:T0020] (LCRS [TBX:T0020] 1 and LCRS [TBX:T0020] 2) is one of the essential components required by the International Cyanide Management Code. Its purpose is to capture seepage [TBX:T0041] that passes through the primary geomembrane [TBX:T0024] of the VLS lining system.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: LCRS introduction with TBX terms for leak collection and recovery system, seepage, and geomembrane]

It is located in the lower sectors of the VLS, where an accumulation of solution could occur. There, a double geomembrane [TBX:T0024] lining is provided. Between both layers there is a permeable medium that allows the flow to circulate by gravity. This flow is conducted to the lowest part, coinciding with the PSSA. From there, a set of automated pumps keeps the system drained, avoiding the presence of underpressures in the upper geomembrane [TBX:T0024]. The seepage [TBX:T0041] extracted from the LCRS [TBX:T0020] is discharged back into PSSA1. The LCRS [TBX:T0020] 1 pumping system is designated 4A and 4B.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: LCRS operation description with TBX terms for geomembrane, seepage, and LCRS]

LCRS [TBX:T0020] 1 associated with phases 1 to 5 covers the bottom of PSSA1 up to elevation 3,943 m.a.s.l.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: LCRS1 extent with TBX LCRS term]

In phase 1, the LCRS [TBX:T0020] configuration is that of a poorly graded gravel channel, whose particle size distribution favors high permeability. Such material was placed in a layer of approximately 0.50 m thickness and variable width along the phase, since it conforms to the channel geometry. Eventual seepage [TBX:T0041] from the primary (upper) geomembrane [TBX:T0024] infiltrates through the granular material and is then conducted over the secondary geomembrane [TBX:T0024] by gravity, favoring flow through the porous medium. This conveyance ends at a point below PSSA1 and near the upstream face of the containment wall, where the pump pit is located.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Phase 1 LCRS configuration with TBX terms for LCRS, seepage, and geomembrane]

Under normal operating conditions, operation is exclusive to pump 4B and 4A remains as a backup. The starting and stopping of the pumps is automatic and is associated with the level measured in the LCRS [TBX:T0020]. The pumping system is installed inside a caisson, formed by two 18" carbon steel pipes, which extend from the bottom of the LCRS [TBX:T0020] sump [TBX:T0009] to the crest of the PSSA embankment.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Pumping system description with TBX terms for LCRS and sump]

This material consists of two layers of non-woven geotextile, between which a synthetic mesh (geonet) is interposed. The complete package has a thickness of 7.5 mm. The operating principle is the same, gravity conveyance of eventual seepage [TBX:T0041] to the successive phase. In the case of phase 2 connection with phase 1, a 100 mm diameter perforated HDPE [TBX:T0025] pipeline was provided.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: LCRS phases 2-5 configuration with TBX terms for LCRS, seepage, and HDPE]

## SECTION 3.0 – GROUNDWATER FLOW MANAGEMENT SYSTEM

This system comprises the management of groundwater and subsurface flows below the heap [TBX:T0013] area, with the purpose of preventing underpressures in the liner.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Section introduction with TBX heap leaching term]

The network of perforated pipelines housed in drainage material trenches has the purpose of capturing all flows under the lining system and transporting them downstream of the valley. This system has an important network of observation and monitoring wells described in this section that allows verification of the quality of the captured flow before discharge to natural channels.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Subdrainage system purpose and monitoring description]

The subdrainage systems for phases 1 to 5 and phases 6 to 9 are independent.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: System independence statement]

### PHASES 1 TO 5 SUBDRAINAGE SYSTEM

The system consists of a network of double-wall perforated pipelines of 300, 200, and 100 mm diameter. ## SECTION 5.0 – CONTINGENCY MANAGEMENT

Contingency management is an aspect of special interest when operating the VLS. The types of contingencies that may occur are associated with the occurrence of meteorological events and fortuitous situations related to operation.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Section introduction defining contingency types]

The main objective during contingency management is to preserve the health and occupational safety of workers, as well as promote environmental preservation practices in the area of influence and in which VLS operations take place. Among the main objectives of contingency management are:

- Prevent and respond in a timely, rapid, and efficient manner to any emergency, with possibility of risk to human life, health, and the environment, managing it with serenity, responsibility, and specific methods.
- Develop procedures in relation to activities, zones, or high-risk events that present consequences of great magnitude in damage to people, equipment, environment, and processes.
- Train, train, and sensitize personnel in each area to act quickly and orderly in case of emergencies.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Contingency management objectives]

According to the Emergency Action Plan (PADE), contingencies can be classified as:

- **Level 1 - Operational anomaly:** This type of anomaly does not cause a system failure in the short term, but could increase if it continues. In this type of anomaly, corrective actions are taken to proceed to cease it.
- **Level 2 - Emergency - Dangerous anomaly:** This does imply that an anomaly or contingency is developing that if it continues could cause total or partial failure. It is considered that corrective actions can be taken to prevent VLS failure.
- **Level 3 - Emergency - Catastrophic anomaly:** A VLS failure has occurred with loss of contained solution or it is imminent. It is not possible to control or delay the failure; the effect is the sudden discharge of contained solution with downstream flooding. Impacts downstream of the embankment cannot be avoided.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Three-level contingency classification system]

The following describes VLS contingency management in relation to:

- Meteorological events
- Pump shutdown
- Valve failure in the north sector of the VLS
- Pipeline leaks [TBX:T0041] in the south sector of the VLS

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Contingency scenario outline with TBX seepage/leak term]
