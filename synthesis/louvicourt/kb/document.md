# Seismotectonic Setting
## Seismicity and Tectonic Regime

The seismicity of the region is controlled by stable continental region tectonics characteristic of the Canadian Shield. Within approximately 1000 km of the Louvicourt site, the tectonic regime controlling seismicity is intraplate deformation within this stable continental region rather than active plate boundary tectonics. The region exhibits widely distributed seismicity that is not associated with mapped surface fault traces, characteristic of stable continental interior zones. This intraplate seismicity is generally characterized by lower earthquake occurrence rates compared to active tectonic margins, diffuse spatial distribution of epicenters, and absence of well-defined surface-rupturing active faults. Historical seismicity within the region includes the 1935 Timiskaming earthquake with moment magnitude 6.1, which occurred approximately 190 km southwest of the Louvicourt Mine.
The PSHA document indicates that no active neotectonic faults have been identified within 100 km of the Louvicourt site. Although major structural features exist in the region, including the Cadillac-Larder Lake fault zone located approximately 10 km from the site, these structures are not considered seismically active.

## Seismic Source Model

WSP adopted the CanadaSHM6 seismic source model developed by the Geological Survey of Canada for southeastern Canada without modification. All seismic sources employed in the CanadaSHM6 model for the Louvicourt site are classified as Stable Continental Region sources. The PSHA uses the sixth-generation seismic hazard model of Canada developed by Natural Resources Canada, which divides Canada into three regional models: Southeastern Canada, Western Canada, and Eastern Arctic Canada. The Louvicourt TSF site falls within the Southeastern Canada model. The CanadaSHM6 southeastern Canada model comprises three sub-models designated as Historical (H2), Regional (R2), and Hybrid (HY), with assigned weights of 0.4, 0.2, and 0.4 respectively. Each sub-model defines areal seismic source zones appropriate for stable continental regions in central and eastern North America. Within the 1,000 km region surrounding the site, no sources are classified as active crustal tectonic types associated with plate boundaries or subduction zones. The major contributing seismic source zones identified through hazard decomposition are the Charlevoix source zone (CHV and CHVHY variants) and the Gatineau source zone, both representing intraplate seismic sources within the stable continental environment of southeastern Canada.


## Active Neotectonic Faults

No active neotectonic faults have been systematically identified within 100 km of the Louvicourt site. The site is located within the wider Cadillac-Larder Lake fault zone, approximately 10 km north of the main sub-Province contact between the Pontiac and Abitibi sub-provinces. While the Cadillac-Larder Lake fault zone represents a major structural complex from Archean times, the available evidence indicates this fault system is not currently seismically or geologically active. Systematic surveys to identify candidate neotectonic faults in this part of Canada have not been conducted, representing a knowledge gap in the regional assessment [@Leblanc2019]. While glacially induced faults and post-glacial reactivation have been documented in other parts of Quebec [@Aylsworth2000], no such features have been identified within 100 km of the Louvicourt Mine site. The seismic source model adopted from CanadaSHM6 does not include the Cadillac-Larder Lake fault zone or other regional faults as discrete seismic sources, instead treating all seismicity in the region as arising from diffuse areal sources. This approach is consistent with the characterization of the region as a stable continental interior where seismicity is not controlled by identifiable active fault structures.

# Seismic Hazard Assessment
## Peak Ground Acceleration for 1/10,000 Year Return Period

The Peak Ground Acceleration (PGA) values obtained for the 1/10,000 year return period (annual exceedance probability of 1/10,000) at the Louvicourt TSF site with a measured $V_{S30}$ of 1,853 m/s exhibit a range characterized by the mean value and associated fractiles representing epistemic uncertainty in the seismic hazard model. The mean PGA for the 1/10,000 year return period is 0.193 g. The uncertainty range is bounded by the 5th fractile at 0.072 g and the 95th fractile at 0.376 g, representing approximately two standard deviations from the mean. The 16th and 84th fractiles, representing approximately one standard deviation, are 0.083 g and 0.235 g respectively, with the median (50th fractile) value at 0.147 g. The ratio of the 95th fractile to the 5th fractile is approximately 5.2, indicating substantial epistemic uncertainty in the hazard estimate primarily attributable to relatively low historical earthquake occurrence rates in the region, lack of recorded earthquakes near the site to constrain recurrence parameters, epistemic uncertainties in the NGA-East ground motion model weighted logic-tree framework, and aleatory uncertainty in the AA13 standard deviation models. These PGA values were calculated using the OpenQuake software version 3.16.3 with a minimum earthquake magnitude of $M_w$ 4.8 and for a maximum of five standard deviations (epsilon $\pm$5) above and below the mean ground motion predictions [@LouvicourtPSHA2024].

## Comparison with National Building Code of Canada

The site-specific PSHA results for the Louvicourt TSF site indicate higher PGA values compared to those reported in the 2020 National Building Code of Canada (NBCC) for equivalent site conditions and $V_{S30}$ of 1,853 m/s. At the 1/2,475 year return period, the mean PGA from this site-specific assessment is 0.0992 g compared to 0.0852 g from the 2020 NBCC, representing an increase of 16.5 percent. The differences are more pronounced at other spectral periods, with increases ranging from 16.5 percent at PGA to 43.1 percent at 0.2 s spectral period. These differences result primarily from the use of the final suite of 17 NGA-East ground motion models in the site-specific assessment, superseding the preliminary suite of 13 NGA-East GMMs weighted at 50 percent and the AA13 GMMs weighted at 50 percent that were implemented in CanadaSHM6 for the 2020 NBCC. Additionally, the site-specific assessment applies equally weighted Stewart (2020) and Hashash (2020) site amplification models to adjust from the NGA-East reference hard-rock condition with $V_{S30}$ of 3,000 m/s to the site-specific $V_{S30}$ of 1,853 m/s, whereas the 2020 NBCC used the site amplification factors from CanadaSHM5 combined with a hybrid site model for the preliminary 13 NGA-East GMMs. While the percentage differences are moderate to large, the absolute differences in earthquake accelerations are relatively small, reflecting the fact that the seismic hazard at the Louvicourt TSF site remains relatively low compared to other parts of southeastern Canada and more active regions in western Canada. The 2020 NBCC does not provide results for the 1/5,000 and 1/10,000 year return periods, precluding direct comparison at those annual exceedance probabilities [@LouvicourtPSHA2024].

## Ground Motion Selection for Rare Events

The ground motion selection for the 1/10,000 year return period rare events at the Louvicourt TSF site employed magnitude and distance ranges derived from the disaggregation analysis presented in Table 6 and Figure 17, but the selection criteria adopted relatively loose bounds to permit consideration of secondary intensity measures as an alternative constraint. The disaggregation results for the 1/10,000 annual exceedance probability indicated modal magnitudes of $M_w$ 7.2 to 7.4 across all spectral periods (PGA, 0.2 s, 0.5 s, and 1.0 s), with mean and median magnitudes consistently in the range of $M_w$ 6.8 to 7.4. Modal source-to-site distances were in the range of 160 km to 180 km, with mean distances between 120 km and 220 km depending on spectral period. The ground motion selection process adopted a representative scenario of $M_w$ 7.3 at 170 km rupture distance based on these modal values. However, the actual selection criteria for the candidate ground motion catalog permitted magnitudes from $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.8 and distances from 0 km to 200 km, explicitly relaxing the magnitude constraints from strict adherence to deaggregation ranges. This relaxation was justified on the basis that explicit consideration of secondary intensity measures, including cumulative absolute velocity, Arias intensity, and significant duration metrics conditioned to the target hazard scenario, provides adequate constraint on the suitability of selected records. The site parameter $V_{S30}$ was not constrained in creating the prospective ground motion catalogs, with selected records having $V_{S30}$ values ranging from 312 m/s to 826 m/s for the short period suite and 315 m/s to 735 m/s for the long period suite, substantially softer than the Louvicourt site condition of 1,853 m/s. This approach prioritized achieving a good match to the target spectrum and secondary intensity measures over strict adherence to causal parameter similarity, following guidance that secondary intensity measures provide a more direct link to engineering response than magnitude-distance constraints alone when records are amplitude-scaled [@LouvicourtPSHA2024].


# Seismic Record Selection

## Selection Criteria
The seismic record selection methodology employed by WSP for the Louvicourt TSF site developed two suites of eight two-horizontal-component earthquake acceleration time histories amplitude-scaled to match the mean uniform hazard spectrum for 1/10,000 AEP. The methodology addressed two distinct fundamental periods relevant to embankments of differing height and stiffness at the site: a shorter fundamental period range from 0.25 s to 0.37 s, and a longer fundamental period of approximately 1.15 s. For the short period range, a conditioning period of 0.31 s was adopted with a period range of interest from 0.062 s to 0.62 s for ground motion scaling. For the long period range, a conditioning period of 1.15 s was used with a period range of interest from 0.23 s to 2.3 s. The causal parameters for record selection were derived from disaggregation analysis results at 1/10,000 AEP, establishing a target scenario with moment magnitude 7.3, rupture distance 170 km, hypocentral depth 20 km, depth to top of rupture 10 km, and rupture plane dip 90 degrees.

The 170 km rupture distance criterion originated directly from the disaggregation analysis results for the 1/10,000 AEP hazard level. The disaggregation results presented for 1/10,000 AEP show characteristic source-to-site distances with mode values consistently near 160-180 km across the examined spectral periods including peak ground acceleration, 0.2 s, 0.5 s, and 1.0 s. The mean distances from disaggregation range from approximately 120-140 km for peak ground acceleration up to 220-240 km for the 1.0 s spectral period. The adopted 170 km rupture distance represents a central value within the range of modal disaggregation distances and serves as the representative distance for the causal earthquake scenario used in ground motion selection. This 170 km criterion is specifically valid for the 1/10,000 AEP return period, as the disaggregation analysis was performed at this hazard level to inform the development of earthquake acceleration time histories scaled to the 1/10,000 AEP uniform hazard spectrum.

## Candidate Record Selection Process

The selection process for the candidate catalogue involved querying the PEER NGA-West2 database [@Ancheta2014] to identify prospective ground motions within defined parameter ranges appropriate for the target earthquake scenario. The criteria used for developing the candidate catalogue included earthquake magnitude ranging from $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.8 and source-to-site rupture distance from 0 to 200 km. These bounds were deliberately set wider than the specific disaggregation scenario values of $M_w$ 7.3 and 170 km to increase the number of prospective candidate records available for consideration. The rationale for using relatively loose bounds was that the ground motion selection process explicitly considered secondary intensity measures, making it less critical to precisely target specific causal parameters such as exact earthquake magnitude. The NGA-East database was also consulted during the candidate identification phase, but this database lacked suitable records with magnitudes greater than $M_w$ 5.85, eliminating it as a viable source for large-magnitude candidate motions required for the $M_w$ 7.3 scenario. No explicit filters were described in the report for site ground conditions, suggesting that $V_{S30}$ was not a limiting parameter in creating the candidate catalogue, again to maximize the pool of available prospective records. From this broader candidate catalogue, the final selection of eight two-horizontal-component records for each fundamental period was made by evaluating amplitude scaling factors, spectral shape fit to the target uniform hazard spectrum, and agreement of secondary intensity measures with conditional target values.

Candidate ground motion records were drawn from the PEER NGA-West2 database spanning magnitudes $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.8 and distances 0 to 200 km, with relatively loose bounds justified by explicit consideration of secondary intensity measures. The NGA-East database was consulted but lacked suitable high-magnitude records above $M_w$ 5.85. Each suite of eight records was amplitude-scaled to achieve geometric mean agreement with the target spectrum within 90 percent to 130 percent across the period range of interest, with scale factors generally maintained between 0.5 and 4.0. Secondary intensity measures including Arias intensity, cumulative absolute velocity, and significant durations were conditioned to the target hazard level at each fundamental period to validate the selected ground motions beyond spectral shape matching alone.

## Amplitude Scaling Factors

The amplitude scaling factors applied to the selected ground motion records to match the target 1/10,000 AEP uniform hazard spectrum exhibit substantial variation across the two suites of earthquake acceleration time histories. For the short period range suite scaled for the fundamental period of 0.31 s, the scaling factors range from a minimum of 0.59 to a maximum of 8.28. The minimum scale factor of 0.59 was applied to record RSN 5618 from the 2008 Iwate, Japan earthquake recorded at station IWT010, while the maximum scale factor of 8.28 was applied to record RSN 1833 from the 1999 Hector Mine earthquake recorded at Snow Creek station. For the long period range suite scaled for the fundamental period of 1.15 s, the scaling factors range from a minimum of 0.64 to a maximum of 2.87. The minimum scale factor of 0.64 was applied to record RSN 68 from the 1971 San Fernando earthquake recorded at LA Hollywood Stor FF station, while the maximum scale factor of 2.87 was applied to record RSN 923 from the 1992 Big Bear earthquake recorded at Phelan Wilson Ranch station. The presence of scale factors substantially exceeding the commonly recommended limit of 2.0, particularly the factor of 8.28 and 6.85 in the short period suite, indicates aggressive scaling was required for some records [@Bahrampouri2024]. The WSP report noted that while scale factors were generally kept within the range 0.5 to 4.0, the scale factor was considered less important than the comparison of secondary intensity measures and the fit of selected ground motions to the target spectrum. Several records in both suites required scale factors in the range of 2.0 to 3.0, while two records in the short period suite exceeded 6.0. These large amplitude scaling factors were required to capture the high-frequency ground motion content indicated by the 1/10,000 AEP uniform hazard spectrum at the Louvicourt site.

## Compliance with Selection Criteria

The seismic record suite shown in Tables 10 and 11 of the WSP report was selected using criteria that focused primarily on spectral shape matching to the target 1/10,000 AEP uniform hazard spectrum and agreement with conditional secondary intensity measures, rather than strict compliance with all criteria established at the beginning of Chapter 8.0. The stated criteria at the outset of Chapter 8.0 included a target earthquake scenario with moment magnitude 7.3, rupture distance 170 km, hypocentral depth 20 km, depth to top of rupture 10 km, and rupture plane dip 90 degrees. Examination of Tables 10 and 11 reveals partial rather than complete compliance with these established causal parameters. The selected records span a magnitude range from $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.62, with many records having magnitudes substantially below the target $M_w$ 7.3, including three records at $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 6.2. The rupture distances of selected records range from 11 km to 159 km, with the majority of records having distances substantially shorter than the specified 170 km criterion, particularly in the short period suite where distances as close as 11 km, 16 km, and 19 km were accepted. Source mechanism types in the selected suites include reverse, reverse oblique, strike slip, and normal faulting, which have characteristic rupture plane dips that typically differ from the specified 90 degrees. The geometric mean of each suite achieves agreement with the target spectrum within the specified 90 to 130 percent tolerance over the period range of interest, satisfying the fundamental spectral matching objective. The secondary intensity measures of the selected scaled records, including Arias intensity, cumulative absolute velocity, and significant durations, show general agreement with estimated conditional target values, though with some scatter and a tendency for scaled motion intensity measures to exceed median target values. The WSP approach explicitly prioritized spectral shape matching and secondary intensity measure agreement over precise matching of individual causal parameters, justified by the argument that explicit consideration of secondary intensity measures reduces the importance of targeting specific causal parameters such as exact earthquake magnitude and distance. This selection strategy represents a tradeoff between the ideal of matching all specified criteria and the practical reality of limited suitable candidate records in the NGA-West2 database for a $M_w$ 7.3 stable continental region scenario.



## Compliance with Tectonic Type

The seismic record selection criteria employed by WSP present limitations with respect to the Continental Stable tectonic type of the region. The fundamental issue is that all candidate ground motion records were selected from the PEER NGA-West2 database, which contains exclusively earthquake recordings from shallow crustal events in active tectonic regimes, not stable continental regions [@Ancheta2014]. The NGA-West2 database explicitly excludes stable continental region recordings by design, focusing instead on active tectonic plate boundary earthquakes. For a site characterized by stable continental tectonic conditions, the use of active tectonic region ground motion records introduces tectonic incompatibility between the source region of the recordings and the target site conditions. WSP acknowledged this limitation, noting that candidate records have shorter source-to-site distances than indicated by disaggregation results and that suitable candidate ground motions from intraplate stable continental conditions would have source-to-site distances more consistent with disaggregation. 

The NGA-East database, developed specifically for stable continental regions in Central and Eastern North America [@Goulet2021], would provide tectonically appropriate candidate records but was noted to lack sufficient high-magnitude recordings above $M_w$ 5.85 for the target scenario of $M_w$ 7.3 events. The rationale provided by WSP for using NGA-West2 records relates to differences in site stiffness and attenuation characteristics between the plate boundary recording sites with weaker ground conditions and the Louvicourt site with high shear wave velocity of 1,853 m/s, arguing that shorter-distance active tectonic records were required to capture high-frequency content. While the selected motions were scaled to match target spectra and conditional intensity measures derived from stable continental region ground motion models, the fundamental waveform characteristics, duration properties, and frequency content of the actual recordings originate from a different tectonic environment than the Continental Stable region where the Louvicourt site is located.


## Short-Period Spectral Matching Challenges

The ground motion selection for the Louvicourt TSF site exhibits good geometric mean agreement with the target 1/10,000 year uniform hazard spectrum within the defined period ranges of interest, with the selected suite falling between 90 percent and 130 percent of the target spectrum as shown in Figure 18 and Figure 19. However, analysis of the individual selected records and their source-to-site characteristics reveals systematic challenges in achieving spectral matching at short periods that relate to the interaction between site condition differences, attenuation characteristics, and the period-dependent nature of ground motion scaling. The selected earthquake acceleration time histories were primarily drawn from the PEER NGA-West2 database representing active crustal regions, with site conditions characterized by $V_{S30}$ values substantially lower than the Louvicourt bedrock condition of 1,853 m/s. Several selected records, notably RSN 1833 and RSN 2927 in the short period suite, required large scale factors of 8.28 and 6.85 respectively to achieve adequate matching. The necessity for these large scale factors and the selection of records with source-to-site distances shorter than indicated by disaggregation analysis (with actual distances as close as 11 km to 73 km compared to modal disaggregation distances of 160 km to 180 km) arises from the faster attenuation of moderate- to high-frequency ground motions in the softer site conditions where the NGA-West2 records were obtained compared to the stiffer bedrock conditions at Louvicourt. High-frequency content at short periods is particularly sensitive to both site amplification effects and distance attenuation, with softer soils attenuating high frequencies more rapidly than stiff bedrock. To capture the high-frequency ground motions indicated by the target spectrum for a very stiff site condition, the selection process required records from closer distances that experienced less attenuation, and these records then required substantial amplitude scaling to match the target levels. The magnitude and distance constraints from deaggregation alone were insufficient to identify suitable records because those constraints were developed for stable crustal conditions while the available ground motion database predominantly contains active crustal recordings from softer site conditions. The lack of ground motion records in the NGA-East database with magnitudes exceeding $M_w$ 5.85 precluded use of stable crustal recordings that would have inherently better site condition compatibility, forcing reliance on active crustal records with different attenuation and site response characteristics [@LouvicourtPSHA2024].
