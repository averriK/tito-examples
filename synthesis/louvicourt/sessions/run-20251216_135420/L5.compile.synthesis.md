# Synthesis (L5)

### SLOT 1: Seismotectonic Setting Summary

The Louvicourt site is located within a stable continental region of the Canadian Shield, characterized by intraplate deformation rather than active plate boundary tectonics. Seismicity within approximately 1000 km of the site exhibits the typical characteristics of stable continental interiors: low earthquake occurrence rates, diffuse spatial distribution of epicenters, and absence of well-defined surface-rupturing active faults. Historical seismicity in the region includes the 1935 Timiskaming earthquake with moment magnitude 6.1, which occurred approximately 190 km southwest of the Louvicourt Mine. The seismic source model employed in the PSHA is the CanadaSHM6 model developed by the Geological Survey of Canada, which classifies all seismic sources for the Louvicourt site as Stable Continental Region sources. This model divides Canada into three regional models, with the Louvicourt site falling within the Southeastern Canada model, which comprises three sub-models (Historical, Regional, and Hybrid) with assigned weights of 0.4, 0.2, and 0.4 respectively.

The maximum expected magnitudes for the region are derived from the CanadaSHM6 seismic source model, which identifies the Charlevoix source zone and the Gatineau source zone as the major contributing seismic sources through hazard decomposition. The disaggregation analysis for the 1/10,000 annual exceedance probability indicated modal magnitudes of $M_w$ 7.2 to 7.4 across all spectral periods, with mean and median magnitudes consistently in the range of $M_w$ 6.8 to 7.4. The ground motion selection process adopted a representative scenario of $M_w$ 7.3 at 170 km rupture distance based on these modal values, though the actual selection criteria for the candidate ground motion catalog permitted magnitudes from $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.8. Historical seismicity within the region is exemplified by the 1935 Timiskaming earthquake with moment magnitude 6.1. The CanadaSHM6 model does not include the Cadillac-Larder Lake fault zone or other regional faults as discrete seismic sources, instead treating all seismicity in the region as arising from diffuse areal sources consistent with stable continental interior characterization.

### SLOT 2: Seismic Hazard Assessment Summary

The PSHA study for the Louvicourt TSF site calculated Peak Ground Acceleration values for the 1/10,000 year return period at a site-specific measured $V_{S30}$ of 1,853 m/s. The mean PGA for this return period is 0.193 g, with the uncertainty range bounded by the 5th fractile at 0.072 g and the 95th fractile at 0.376 g. The median (50th fractile) value is 0.147 g, while the 16th and 84th fractiles are 0.083 g and 0.235 g respectively. The ratio of the 95th fractile to the 5th fractile is approximately 5.2, indicating substantial epistemic uncertainty in the hazard estimate primarily attributable to relatively low historical earthquake occurrence rates in the region, lack of recorded earthquakes near the site to constrain recurrence parameters, epistemic uncertainties in the NGA-East ground motion model weighted logic-tree framework, and aleatory uncertainty in the AA13 standard deviation models. These PGA values were calculated using OpenQuake software version 3.16.3 with a minimum earthquake magnitude of $M_w$ 4.8 and for a maximum of five standard deviations above and below the mean ground motion predictions.

The site-specific PSHA results indicate higher PGA values compared to those reported in the 2020 National Building Code of Canada for equivalent site conditions. At the 1/2,475 year return period (the only return period for which NBCC provides comparable data), the mean PGA from the site-specific assessment is 0.0992 g compared to 0.0852 g from the 2020 NBCC, representing an increase of 16.5 percent. The differences are more pronounced at other spectral periods, with increases ranging from 16.5 percent at PGA to 43.1 percent at 0.2 s spectral period. These differences result primarily from the use of the final suite of 17 NGA-East ground motion models in the site-specific assessment, superseding the preliminary suite of 13 NGA-East GMMs weighted at 50 percent and the AA13 GMMs weighted at 50 percent that were implemented in CanadaSHM6 for the 2020 NBCC. Additionally, the site-specific assessment applies equally weighted Stewart (2020) and Hashash (2020) site amplification models to adjust from the NGA-East reference hard-rock condition with $V_{S30}$ of 3,000 m/s to the site-specific $V_{S30}$ of 1,853 m/s. The 2020 NBCC does not provide results for the 1/5,000 and 1/10,000 year return periods, precluding direct comparison at those annual exceedance probabilities.

### SLOT 3: Seismic Record Selection Summary

The seismic record selection methodology for the Louvicourt TSF site developed two suites of eight two-horizontal-component earthquake acceleration time histories amplitude-scaled to match the mean uniform hazard spectrum for 1/10,000 annual exceedance probability. The methodology addressed two distinct fundamental periods: a shorter period range from 0.25 s to 0.37 s (with conditioning period of 0.31 s and period range of interest from 0.062 s to 0.62 s), and a longer fundamental period of approximately 1.15 s (with period range of interest from 0.23 s to 2.3 s). The causal parameters for record selection were derived from disaggregation analysis results at 1/10,000 AEP, establishing a target scenario with moment magnitude 7.3, rupture distance 170 km, hypocentral depth 20 km, depth to top of rupture 10 km, and rupture plane dip 90 degrees. However, the actual selection criteria for the candidate ground motion catalog explicitly relaxed these constraints, permitting magnitudes from $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.8 and distances from 0 km to 200 km. This relaxation was justified on the basis that explicit consideration of secondary intensity measures (cumulative absolute velocity, Arias intensity, and significant duration metrics conditioned to the target hazard scenario) provides adequate constraint on the suitability of selected records beyond magnitude-distance matching alone.

The selected earthquake acceleration time histories exhibited limitations in their match to the stated causal parameter criteria, particularly regarding source-to-site distances and earthquake magnitudes. The selected records span a magnitude range from $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.62, with many records having magnitudes substantially below the target $M_w$ 7.3, including three records at $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 6.2. The rupture distances of selected records range from 11 km to 159 km, with the majority of records having distances substantially shorter than the specified 170 km criterion, particularly in the short period suite where distances as close as 11 km, 16 km, and 19 km were accepted. All candidate ground motion records were selected from the PEER NGA-West2 database, which contains exclusively earthquake recordings from shallow crustal events in active tectonic regimes, not stable continental regions. The NGA-East database, developed specifically for stable continental regions in Central and Eastern North America, would provide tectonically appropriate candidate records but was noted to lack sufficient high-magnitude recordings above $M_w$ 5.85 for the target scenario of $M_w$ 7.3 events. The amplitude scaling factors applied to achieve spectral matching exhibit substantial variation: for the short period suite, scaling factors range from 0.59 to 8.28, while for the long period suite they range from 0.64 to 2.87, with the presence of scale factors substantially exceeding the commonly recommended limit of 2.0 indicating that aggressive scaling was required for some records.

The limitations of the current ground motion selection approach are attributed to fundamental incompatibilities between the available ground motion databases and the characteristics of the Louvicourt site. The necessity for large scale factors and the selection of records with source-to-site distances shorter than indicated by disaggregation analysis arises from the faster attenuation of moderate- to high-frequency ground motions in the softer site conditions where the NGA-West2 records were obtained (with $V_{S30}$ values ranging from 312 m/s to 826 m/s for the short period suite and 315 m/s to 735 m/s for the long period suite) compared to the stiffer bedrock conditions at Louvicourt ($V_{S30}$ of 1,853 m/s). High-frequency content at short periods is particularly sensitive to both site amplification effects and distance attenuation, with softer soils attenuating high frequencies more rapidly than stiff bedrock. To capture the high-frequency ground motions indicated by the target spectrum for a very stiff site condition, the selection process required records from closer distances that experienced less attenuation, and these records then required substantial amplitude scaling to match the target levels. The lack of ground motion records in the NGA-East database with magnitudes exceeding $M_w$ 5.85 precluded use of stable crustal recordings that would have inherently better site condition compatibility, forcing reliance on active crustal records with different attenuation and site response characteristics.

The recommendation emerging from these limitations is to consider relaxing certain selection parameters to better represent the frequency content characteristic of stable continental regions. The WSP approach explicitly prioritized spectral shape matching and secondary intensity measure agreement over precise matching of individual causal parameters such as exact earthquake magnitude and distance, justified by the argument that explicit consideration of secondary intensity measures reduces the importance of targeting specific causal parameters. This selection strategy represents a tradeoff between the ideal of matching all specified criteria and the practical reality of limited suitable candidate records in the NGA-West2 database for a $M_w$ 7.3 stable continental region scenario. For stable continental zones, achieving adequate representation of seismic records may require not strictly adhering to disaggregation-derived magnitude and distance constraints, instead accepting records with shorter distances and smaller magnitudes that can better reflect the frequency content in seismic records of such regions, particularly when combined with explicit conditioning on secondary intensity measures that provide a more direct link to engineering response than magnitude-distance constraints alone when records are amplitude-scaled.
