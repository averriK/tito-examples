# Seismic Record Selection Methodology for Site Compatibility

## SLOT 1: Executive summary of seismic record selection for site compatibility

Seismic record selection for the project site followed a comprehensive methodology designed to ensure compatibility between the earthquake acceleration time histories and the site-specific seismic hazard characteristics derived from probabilistic seismic hazard analysis. The overall approach involved amplitude-scaling of candidate ground motions to match a target spectrum defined as the mean uniform hazard spectrum for the design annual exceedance probability, with careful consideration of secondary intensity measures to ensure the selected records represent realistic earthquake characteristics. The selection methodology used disaggregation results from the PSHA to identify representative earthquake magnitudes and distances that contribute most significantly to the site hazard, then selected recorded ground motions from established databases with similar causal parameters. Two distinct suites of eight two-horizontal-component earthquake acceleration time histories were developed, each tailored to different fundamental periods relevant to the structures at the site, with one suite addressing shorter fundamental periods in the range 0.25 to 0.37 seconds and another suite addressing a longer fundamental period of approximately 1.15 seconds. The compatibility was ensured through a multi-step process that began with reviewing hazard disaggregation to identify representative earthquake scenarios, followed by selecting candidate records from the PEER NGA-West2 database with generally similar magnitudes and distances, then applying amplitude-scaling to match the target spectrum within specific period ranges of interest defined relative to each fundamental period. The selected records were further validated by comparing their secondary intensity measures including cumulative absolute velocity, Arias intensity, and significant durations against conditional target values estimated using ground motion prediction equations and correlation models. This approach ensured that the final suite of ground motions not only matched the target spectral shape across the periods of engineering significance but also represented realistic ground motion characteristics in terms of duration and energy content, making them suitable for advanced geotechnical stability analyses at the site. ## SLOT 2: Scaling methodology across period ranges

The seismic records were scaled across two distinct period ranges to address different fundamental periods relevant to the site structures. For the shorter fundamental period denoted $T_S$ in the range 0.25 to 0.37 seconds, a conditioning period $T_S^*$ of 0.31 seconds was selected as the center of this range, and a period range of interest spanning 0.062 to 0.62 seconds was defined, corresponding to $0.2T_S^*$ to $2.0T_S^*$, within which candidate ground motions were amplitude-scaled to match the target spectrum. For the longer fundamental period $T_L$ of approximately 1.15 seconds, the conditioning period $T_L^*$ was taken as 1.15 seconds, and a period range of interest from 0.23 to 2.3 seconds, equivalent to $0.2T_L^*$ to $2.0T_L^*$, was adopted for scaling purposes. The target spectrum for both period ranges was the mean uniform hazard spectrum for the 1/10,000 annual exceedance probability derived from the site-specific probabilistic seismic hazard analysis, reflecting the spectral accelerations expected at the measured seismic ground condition of $V_{S30}$ equal to 1,853 meters per second. Candidate ground motions from the PEER NGA-West2 database with magnitudes ranging from $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.8 and source-to-site distances from 0 to 200 kilometers were evaluated, and individual amplitude scale factors were applied to each record to achieve optimal agreement between the horizontal geometric mean response spectrum of the record and the target spectrum across the respective period range of interest. Scale factors were generally maintained within the range 0.5 to 4.0, although in some cases larger scale factors were necessary to capture the high-frequency ground motions indicated by the target spectrum, particularly for records with shorter source-to-site distances selected to compensate for the difference between the stiff site conditions at the project location and the generally softer ground conditions at the original recording stations. The final suite for each period range consisted of eight two-horizontal-component time histories, and the scaling was validated by confirming that the geometric mean spectrum of each suite agreed with the target spectrum to within 90 to 130 percent across the period range of interest. ## SLOT 3: PEER API spectral matching equations and scaling procedures

The PEER Ground Motion Database provides web-based tools for ground motion selection and scaling, although the specific project described in the knowledge base utilized amplitude-scaling rather than true spectral matching through wavelet addition or frequency-domain modification. The PEER database API offers three primary scaling options for users selecting ground motion records. The first option is no scaling, where records are used as-recorded with a scale factor $f = 1.0$. The second option is single-period scaling, where each record is scaled to match the target spectrum at one specific period $T_s$ by applying a constant scale factor to the entire time history. The third option is to minimize the mean square error between the scaled record spectrum and the target spectrum over a specified period range, where the scale factor for each record is determined by minimizing the function $\text{MSE} = \int_{T_{\text{min}}}^{T_{\text{max}}} w(T) \left[ \ln(Sa_{\text{target}}(T)) - \ln(f \cdot Sa_{\text{record}}(T)) \right]^2 dT$, with $w(T)$ representing a user-specified weight function that emphasizes the periods of engineering significance. The PEER API applies these scale factors to the downloaded records, which are initially provided as unscaled and unrotated as-recorded data, and the system supports various spectral components including RotD50, RotD100, geometric mean, and individual horizontal components for matching purposes. True spectral matching techniques, when employed, adjust ground motion records to more closely approach the target response spectrum by either adding wavelet components to the original time series or iteratively modifying the Fourier amplitude spectrum while preserving the phase characteristics of the original record. These spectral matching procedures alter the frequency content of the ground motion to achieve tighter conformance to the target spectrum compared to simple amplitude-scaling, but they may also modify the secondary intensity measure characteristics of the original record. ## SLOT 4: Spectral matching method equations with amplification factors

For spectral matching through amplitude-scaling where $N$ seismic records with $M$ spectral ordinates $Sa_i(T_n)$ for $i = 1, \ldots, N$ and $n = 1, \ldots, M$ are scaled to produce a target average spectrum, the amplification factors $\alpha_i$ for each record can be determined by solving an optimization problem that minimizes the difference between the scaled average spectrum and the target spectrum. The objective is to find amplification factors such that the weighted geometric mean of the scaled spectra matches the target spectrum $Sa_{\text{target}}(T_n)$ across all periods of interest, which can be expressed as minimizing the function $$\sum_{n=1}^{M} w_n \left[ \ln(Sa_{\text{target}}(T_n)) - \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \ln(\alpha_i \cdot Sa_i(T_n)) \right]^2$$ where $w_n$ represents weighting factors that can be assigned to emphasize specific periods within the range of interest. For the case where all records are scaled by the same factor to minimize mean square error over a period range, the optimal scale factor $\alpha$ can be determined analytically as $$\alpha = \exp\left( \frac{\sum_{n=1}^{M} w_n \ln(Sa_{\text{target}}(T_n)) - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{M} w_n \ln(Sa_i(T_n))}{\sum_{n=1}^{M} w_n} \right)$$ which ensures that the geometric mean of the scaled spectra best matches the target spectrum in a least-squares sense. When individual amplification factors are permitted for each record, the problem becomes finding $\alpha_i$ for each record that minimizes the misfit between the average scaled spectrum and the target, subject to practical constraints on the range of scale factors, typically $0.5 \leq \alpha_i \leq 4.0$ for engineering applications to maintain reasonable similarity to the original recorded ground motion characteristics. The weighted sum formulation allows the selection process to emphasize matching at periods most relevant to the structural response, such as the fundamental period and its harmonics, while permitting greater deviation at periods of lesser engineering significance. In practice, the selection and scaling process often proceeds iteratively, where candidate records are first screened based on compatibility with target causal parameters such as magnitude and distance from disaggregation results, then individual scale factors are determined for each candidate, and finally a subset of records is selected whose average best matches the target spectrum while also satisfying constraints on secondary intensity measures. ## SLOT 5: Baker methodology for additional intensity measures in seismic selection

The methodology developed by Baker and collaborators for incorporating additional intensity measures beyond spectral acceleration into seismic record selection centers on the concept of conditional distributions of secondary intensity measures given a target spectral acceleration value at a conditioning period. The approach recognizes that spectral acceleration alone may not fully characterize ground motion severity for all structural response types, and that intensity measures related to ground motion duration and energy content such as cumulative absolute velocity $\text{CAV}$, Arias intensity $\text{AI}$, and significant durations $D_{5-75}$ and $D_{5-95}$ can significantly influence structural and geotechnical behavior. The conditional distribution of a secondary intensity measure $\text{IM}_2$ given a target spectral acceleration $Sa(T^*)$ at conditioning period $T^*$ can be expressed using the correlation between the two intensity measures, following the assumption that the logarithms of ground motion intensity measures are jointly normally distributed. For a secondary intensity measure $\text{IM}_2$ with median prediction $\mu_{\ln(\text{IM}_2)}$ and standard deviation $\sigma_{\ln(\text{IM}_2)}$ from a ground motion prediction equation, and a target spectral acceleration corresponding to epsilon $\epsilon = \frac{\ln(Sa(T^*)) - \mu_{\ln(Sa(T^*))}}{\sigma_{\ln(Sa(T^*))}}$ standard deviations above the median prediction, the conditional median of the secondary intensity measure is given by $$\mu_{\ln(\text{IM}_2) | Sa(T^*)} = \mu_{\ln(\text{IM}_2)} + \rho \cdot \sigma_{\ln(\text{IM}_2)} \cdot \epsilon$$ where $\rho$ represents the correlation coefficient between $\ln(\text{IM}_2)$ and $\ln(Sa(T^*))$ derived empirically from recorded ground motion databases or established through conditional correlation models. The conditional standard deviation of the secondary intensity measure is reduced from its unconditional value according to $$\sigma_{\ln(\text{IM}_2) | Sa(T^*)} = \sigma_{\ln(\text{IM}_2)} \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}$$ reflecting the reduction in uncertainty about the secondary intensity measure once the spectral acceleration at the conditioning period is known. This approach has been formalized in the generalized conditional intensity measure framework developed by Bradley, which extends the concept to condition on multiple spectral acceleration values simultaneously and construct multivariate distributions of any set of ground motion intensity measures. Correlation models have been developed by Bradley and others relating spectral acceleration to cumulative absolute velocity, Arias intensity, and significant durations, enabling practical application of this methodology where correlation coefficients $\rho_{\text{AI}}$ and $\rho_{\text{CAV}}$ between spectral acceleration and these secondary measures can be estimated as functions of earthquake magnitude, distance, and conditioning period. The application of this methodology involves first conducting probabilistic seismic hazard analysis to determine the target spectral acceleration at the conditioning period and its associated epsilon value from hazard disaggregation, then using ground motion prediction equations such as those by Farhadi and Pezeshk for stable continental regions to estimate unconditional median and standard deviation of secondary intensity measures for representative earthquake scenarios, and finally applying the conditional correlation models such as those proposed by Bradley to compute conditional target distributions of the secondary intensity measures. Selected ground motion records are then evaluated to ensure their secondary intensity measures fall within reasonable bounds of these conditional target distributions, typically assessed by comparing the empirical cumulative distribution of the secondary intensity measures from the selected scaled records against the theoretical conditional distribution, with acceptable suites showing general agreement although individual records may deviate from the median.
