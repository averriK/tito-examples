# Seismic Record Selection Methodology for Site-Specific Applications

## SLOT 1: Executive summary of seismic record selection for site compatibility

The seismic record selection methodology employed for site compatibility involves a comprehensive approach that integrates probabilistic seismic hazard analysis results with ground motion database selection procedures. The methodology begins with disaggregation analysis of seismic hazard to identify the earthquake magnitude and source-to-site distance that contribute most significantly to the hazard at the site. For the reference case, the disaggregation results indicated that the dominant contribution to seismic hazard came from earthquakes with magnitudes of approximately $M_w$ 6.8 to $M_w$ 7.5 and source-to-site distances of about 100 to 200 km for both shorter and longer spectral periods. Based on these disaggregation results, a modal scenario of $M_w$ 7.3 and 170 km source-to-site distance was selected as the causal parameter target for ground motion selection. The selection process then queries candidate records from ground motion databases such as the PEER NGA-West2 database, using relatively broad bounds for magnitude (e.g., $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.8) and distance (0 to 200 km) to create a sufficiently large candidate catalog. The selected records are then amplitude-scaled to achieve agreement between the geometric mean of the scaled suite and the target uniform hazard spectrum (UHS) within a defined period range of interest. Site compatibility is further ensured by considering secondary intensity measures including Arias intensity (AI), cumulative absolute velocity (CAV), and significant durations ($D_{5-75}$ and $D_{5-95}$), which are conditioned to the target hazard level using published ground motion models and correlation models. This multi-faceted approach ensures that the selected ground motion suite is representative of the seismic hazard at the site in terms of both spectral shape and engineering intensity measures that influence geotechnical behavior. ## SLOT 2: Scaling methodology across period ranges

The scaling methodology across different period ranges is tailored to the fundamental periods of the structures being analyzed. For structures with varying stiffness and height, multiple fundamental periods are identified, requiring separate ground motion suites scaled to different period ranges of interest. In the reference methodology, two distinct fundamental periods were considered: a shorter period $T_S$ ranging from 0.25 s to 0.37 s, and a longer period $T_L$ of approximately 1.15 s. For each fundamental period, a conditioning period $T^*$ is defined at the center of the range or at the specific period of interest. For the short fundamental period, $T_S^*$ was taken as 0.31 s, while for the long fundamental period, $T_L^*$ was 1.15 s. The period range of interest for scaling candidate ground motions is then defined as a multiple of the conditioning period, typically from $0.2T^*$ to $2.0T^*$. This results in a period range of 0.062 s to 0.62 s for the short fundamental period and 0.23 s to 2.3 s for the long fundamental period. Individual scale factors are applied to each ground motion record in the candidate suite to minimize the deviation between the geometric mean spectrum of the scaled suite and the target UHS within the period range of interest. The scale factors are typically constrained to a range of 0.5 to 4.0, although this constraint may be relaxed when necessary to achieve adequate spectral fit and when secondary intensity measures remain consistent with conditional targets. The final acceptance criterion requires that the geometric mean of the selected scaled ground motions falls between 90% and 130% of the target spectrum within the period range of interest. This period-range-specific scaling approach ensures that the scaled ground motions adequately represent the expected seismic demand for structures with different dynamic characteristics while maintaining physical realism in the modified records. ## SLOT 3: PEER API spectral matching equations and scaling procedures

The PEER Ground Motion Database provides web-based tools for ground motion selection and scaling, though the specific application described in the reference methodology utilized amplitude scaling rather than spectral matching through the PEER API. The PEER database supports multiple scaling approaches, with the most common being single-period scaling and minimum mean square error (MSE) scaling. In single-period scaling, each record is scaled by a factor $f_i$ such that the spectral acceleration of the scaled record matches the target spectrum at a specified period $T_s$. The scale factor for the $i$-th record is computed as $f_i = Sa_{target}(T_s) / Sa_i(T_s)$, where $Sa_{target}(T_s)$ is the target spectral acceleration at period $T_s$ and $Sa_i(T_s)$ is the spectral acceleration of the unscaled $i$-th record at that period. For minimum MSE scaling, scale factors are determined to minimize the mean square error between the mean scaled spectrum and the target spectrum across a range of periods. The MSE is computed as $MSE = \sum_{j=1}^{M} w(T_j) [Sa_{target}(T_j) - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} f_i Sa_i(T_j)]^2$, where $M$ is the number of spectral ordinates, $N$ is the number of records, $w(T_j)$ is a weight function at period $T_j$, and $f_i$ is the scale factor for record $i$. The weight function $w(T)$ is user-defined and typically emphasizes periods within the range of structural interest. For the PEER database API, spectral matching can be performed using algorithms that iteratively adjust the Fourier amplitude spectrum of the records while preserving phase information to achieve closer agreement with the target spectrum. However, the reference methodology opted for amplitude scaling of unmatched records because amplitude scaling preserves the original frequency content and non-stationary characteristics of recorded ground motions, which are important for capturing realistic seismic response, particularly for geotechnical applications where ground motion characteristics beyond spectral shape influence behavior. ## SLOT 4: Spectral matching method equations with amplification factors

The spectral matching method for determining amplification factors can be formulated as a least-squares optimization problem. Given $N$ seismic records, each with spectral acceleration ordinates $Sa_i(T_j)$ at $M$ discrete periods $T_j$ (where $i = 1, 2, \ldots, N$ and $j = 1, 2, \ldots, M$), and a target spectrum with ordinates $Sa_{target}(T_j)$ at the same periods, the objective is to determine amplification factors $f_i$ for each record such that the weighted sum of the scaled spectra approximates the target average spectrum. The problem can be expressed in matrix form. Let $\mathbf{S}$ be an $M \times N$ matrix where element $S_{ji} = Sa_i(T_j)$ represents the spectral acceleration of record $i$ at period $T_j$. Let $\mathbf{f}$ be an $N \times 1$ vector of amplification factors $[f_1, f_2, \ldots, f_N]^T$, and let $\mathbf{t}$ be an $M \times 1$ vector of target spectral ordinates $[Sa_{target}(T_1), Sa_{target}(T_2), \ldots, Sa_{target}(T_M)]^T$. The objective function to minimize is:

$$
\text{minimize} \quad J(\mathbf{f}) = \sum_{j=1}^{M} w(T_j) \left[ Sa_{target}(T_j) - \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} f_i Sa_i(T_j) \right]^2
$$

which can be written in matrix notation as:

$$
J(\mathbf{f}) = \left\| \mathbf{W}^{1/2} \left( \mathbf{t} - \frac{1}{N} \mathbf{S} \mathbf{f} \right) \right\|^2
$$

where $\mathbf{W}$ is an $M \times M$ diagonal weight matrix with diagonal elements $w(T_j)$. To find the optimal amplification factors, we take the derivative of $J(\mathbf{f})$ with respect to $\mathbf{f}$ and set it equal to zero:

$$
\frac{\partial J}{\partial \mathbf{f}} = -\frac{2}{N} \mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{W} \left( \mathbf{t} - \frac{1}{N} \mathbf{S} \mathbf{f} \right) = \mathbf{0}
$$

Solving for $\mathbf{f}$ yields the normal equation:

$$
\mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{W} \mathbf{S} \mathbf{f} = N \mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{W} \mathbf{t}
$$

The solution is:

$$
\mathbf{f} = N \left( \mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{W} \mathbf{S} \right)^{-1} \mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{W} \mathbf{t}
$$

This formulation assumes that the matrix $\mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{W} \mathbf{S}$ is invertible, which is generally satisfied when the number of records $N$ is less than or equal to the number of spectral ordinates $M$ and the records are linearly independent. The weight function $w(T)$ is typically chosen to emphasize periods within the range of structural interest, often taking the form $w(T) = 1$ within the period range of interest and $w(T) = 0$ or a smaller value outside this range. Constraints on the amplification factors, such as $0.5 \leq f_i \leq 4.0$, can be incorporated by formulating the problem as a constrained least-squares problem or by iteratively adjusting the solution. This mathematical framework provides the basis for determining scale factors that produce the best average match to a target spectrum, though practical implementations may use iterative procedures or additional constraints to ensure physically realistic scaling factors. ## SLOT 5: Baker methodology for additional intensity measures in seismic selection

The methodology for incorporating additional intensity measures (IMs) in seismic record selection, as developed by Baker and colleagues, addresses the limitation that traditional selection methods based solely on matching response spectra may not capture the full range of ground motion characteristics that influence structural and geotechnical response. The generalized conditional intensity measure (GCIM) framework extends the conditional spectrum approach by conditioning multiple intensity measures on a target hazard level. The fundamental concept involves the joint distribution of multiple intensity measures conditioned on a primary intensity measure, typically spectral acceleration at a conditioning period $T^*$. For a primary IM denoted as $IM_1 = Sa(T^*)$ at a specified hazard level (corresponding to a specified epsilon value $\epsilon$), the conditional distribution of a secondary IM, $IM_2$, is computed using the correlation structure between $IM_1$ and $IM_2$. The conditional median of the secondary intensity measure is given by:

$$
\mu_{\ln IM_2 | IM_1} = \mu_{\ln IM_2} + \rho_{\ln IM_1, \ln IM_2} \cdot \sigma_{\ln IM_2} \cdot \frac{\ln IM_1 - \mu_{\ln IM_1}}{\sigma_{\ln IM_1}}
$$

where $\mu_{\ln IM_2}$ and $\sigma_{\ln IM_2}$ are the median and logarithmic standard deviation of $IM_2$ from an appropriate ground motion model, $\mu_{\ln IM_1}$ and $\sigma_{\ln IM_1}$ are the median and logarithmic standard deviation of $IM_1$, and $\rho_{\ln IM_1, \ln IM_2}$ is the correlation coefficient between $\ln IM_1$ and $\ln IM_2$. For the conditioning scenario at a target hazard level where $Sa(T^*)$ has an epsilon value $\epsilon$, we have $\ln Sa(T^*) = \mu_{\ln Sa(T^*)} + \epsilon \cdot \sigma_{\ln Sa(T^*)}$, which can be substituted into the conditional median equation:

$$
\mu_{\ln IM_2 | Sa(T^*)} = \mu_{\ln IM_2} + \rho_{\ln Sa(T^*), \ln IM_2} \cdot \sigma_{\ln IM_2} \cdot \epsilon
$$

The conditional standard deviation is:

$$
\sigma_{\ln IM_2 | Sa(T^*)} = \sigma_{\ln IM_2} \sqrt{1 - \rho_{\ln Sa(T^*), \ln IM_2}^2}
$$

This formulation allows for the estimation of target values for secondary intensity measures such as Arias intensity (AI), cumulative absolute velocity (CAV), and significant durations ($D_{5-75}$ and $D_{5-95}$) that are consistent with the seismic hazard at the site. The correlation models between spectral acceleration and these secondary intensity measures have been developed by researchers including Bradley (2012) for the CAV-SA correlation and Bradley (2015) for the AI-SA correlation. In the ground motion selection process, candidate records are evaluated not only for their spectral match but also for their agreement with the conditional target values of these secondary IMs. For period-independent intensity measures such as significant duration, the conditioning is typically not applied, and instead the unconditional median values from ground motion models are used as targets. The selection algorithm then identifies a suite of records that collectively matches both the target spectrum and the distribution of secondary intensity measures, ensuring that the selected ground motions are representative of the full suite of characteristics expected at the target hazard level. This approach explicitly recognizes that spectral shape alone is insufficient to fully characterize ground motion demand and that intensity measures related to duration, cumulative effects, and energy content play important roles in structural and geotechnical response.
