# Louvicourt PSHA Peer Review

## Introduction

The subject site is the Louvicourt Mine Tailings Storage Facility located in southwestern Quebec, Canada, at coordinates 48.135 degrees N latitude and 77.595 degrees W longitude. WSP Canada Inc. conducted a site-specific seismic hazard assessment for the tailings storage facility at Teck Resources Ltd.'s closed Louvicourt Mine site near Val d'Or, Quebec. The assessment focused on Dam 1D of the Louvicourt TSF, understood to be the critical structure for the overall stability of the facility. The objectives of WSP's study were to quantify earthquake ground motions with annual exceedance probabilities of 1/2,475, 1/5,000, and 1/10,000, equivalent to return periods of approximately 2,475, 5,000, and 10,000 years respectively. The study included probabilistic seismic hazard analysis to estimate earthquake ground motions at these annual exceedance probabilities and provided earthquake acceleration time histories amplitude-scaled to the 1/10,000 AEP site-specific mean uniform hazard spectrum. This peer review work by SRK examines WSP's seismic hazard assessment methodology, seismic source characterization, ground motion model selection, and earthquake acceleration time history development for the Louvicourt TSF site.

## Tectonic Setting and Seismic Sources

### Regional Tectonic Framework

The Louvicourt site is located within a stable continental region characterized by the Canadian Shield physiographic province of Canada. The region is part of the North American Craton, containing very old crystalline basement rocks dating back more than 1 billion years that have remained tectonically stable through multiple cycles of continental rifting, basin formation, and contraction. The Canadian Shield formed from collision of Archean-age microcontinents during the Paleoproterozoic era between 2.5 and 1.6 billion years ago, combining to form the ancient continent of Laurentia. Within approximately 1000 km of the Louvicourt site, the tectonic regime controlling seismicity is intraplate deformation within this stable continental region rather than active plate boundary tectonics. The region exhibits widely distributed seismicity that is not associated with mapped surface fault traces, characteristic of stable continental interior zones. Historical seismicity within the region includes the 1935 Timiskaming earthquake with moment magnitude 6.1, which occurred approximately 190 km southwest of the Louvicourt Mine. The seismicity in southeastern Canada results primarily from reactivation of inherited Precambrian structures under the present-day northeast-southwest-striking regional maximum horizontal stress field [@Bent2021]. Stable continental regions such as the area surrounding Louvicourt exhibit lower seismicity rates and larger felt areas for equivalent magnitude earthquakes compared to active tectonic plate boundary regions [@Atkinson2007].

### Seismic Source Model Classification

All seismic sources employed in the WSP model for the Louvicourt site are of the Continental Stable type. WSP adopted the CanadaSHM6 seismic source model developed by the Geological Survey of Canada for southeastern Canada without modification. The CanadaSHM6 southeastern Canada model comprises three sub-models designated as Historical (H2), Regional (R2), and Hybrid (HY), with assigned weights of 0.4, 0.2, and 0.4 respectively. Each sub-model defines areal seismic source zones appropriate for stable continental regions in central and eastern North America. The ground motion models applied by WSP further confirm the stable continental classification, consisting of 17 NGA-East ground motion models developed specifically for the Central and Eastern North America tectonic environment, which is characterized as a stable continental region [@Goulet2021]. These models supersede the preliminary 13 NGA-East models and reflect the most up-to-date published ground motion characterization for stable continental regions. The AA13 standard deviation model implemented alongside the NGA-East median ground motions was also developed for eastern North America stable continental conditions. Within the 1000 km radius surrounding the site, no active plate boundary sources or subduction zone sources contribute to the seismic hazard, consistent with the intraplate stable continental tectonic setting of the region.

### Active Faults

No active neotectonic faults have been systematically identified within 100 km of the Louvicourt site. The site is located within the wider Cadillac-Larder Lake fault zone, approximately 10 km north of the main sub-Province contact between the Pontiac and Abitibi sub-provinces. While the Cadillac-Larder Lake fault zone represents a major structural complex from Archean times, the available evidence indicates this fault system is not currently seismically or geologically active. Historical earthquake epicenters in the region are widely distributed and not aligned with the Cadillac-Larder Lake fault zone or other major mapped faults. Where three earthquake epicenters near the site plot within the wider Cadillac-Larder Lake fault zone, earthquakes are notably absent in other parts of this extensive fault system. The nature of the fault zone rocks, their ancient tectonic setting within the Canadian Shield, the lack of earthquake epicenter alignments along fault traces, and absence of geomorphic evidence for recent surface fault rupture collectively indicate that the Cadillac-Larder Lake fault zone and other major regional faults are presently inactive. Consequently, these structures were not included as discrete seismic sources in the site-specific seismic source model. Systematic surveys to identify candidate neotectonic faults in this part of Canada have not been conducted, representing a knowledge gap in the regional assessment [@Leblanc2019]. While glacially induced faults and post-glacial reactivation have been documented in other parts of Quebec, particularly in the Western Quebec Seismic Zone and near Lake Timiskaming [@Aylsworth2000], no such features have been identified within 100 km of the Louvicourt Mine site.

## Seismic Hazard Characterization

### Controlling Earthquake Scenarios

For frequent events with annual exceedance probabilities between 1/475 and 1/1000 years, the seismic hazard at the Louvicourt site is controlled by moderate to large magnitude earthquakes at intermediate distances. At 1/2,475 AEP, the disaggregation analysis indicates characteristic moment magnitudes ranging from $M_w$ 6.6 to $M_w$ 7.4, with mean magnitudes between $M_w$ 6.6-6.8 and mode magnitudes between $M_w$ 7.2-7.4 depending on spectral period. The characteristic epicentral distances for these frequent events range from approximately 140 km to 280 km. At shorter spectral periods such as peak ground acceleration and 0.2 s, the mean distances cluster around 140-180 km with mode distances near 160-180 km. At longer spectral periods of 0.5 s and 1.0 s, the characteristic distances increase, with mean distances ranging from 220-260 km and mode distances remaining near 160-180 km. The epsilon values for these events, representing the number of standard deviations above the median ground motion, range from approximately 1.0 to 1.5, indicating that ground motions controlling the hazard are moderately elevated above median predictions. The dominant contributing seismic sources for these frequent events are the Charlevoix seismic source zone in both the Historical (CHV) and Hybrid (CHVHY) sub-models of CanadaSHM6, followed by the Gatineau source zone.

For rare events with annual exceedance probabilities of 1/5,000 to 1/10,000 years, the controlling earthquake characteristics shift toward larger magnitudes while distances remain broadly similar to frequent events. At 1/5,000 AEP, the characteristic moment magnitudes range from $M_w$ 6.8 to $M_w$ 7.4, with mean magnitudes between $M_w$ 6.8-7.2 and mode magnitudes consistently near $M_w$ 7.2-7.4. The mean epicentral distances range from 140 km to 260 km depending on spectral period, with shorter periods controlled by closer distances around 140-160 km and longer periods by distances extending to 240-260 km. At 1/10,000 AEP, the characteristic magnitudes increase further, ranging from $M_w$ 6.8 to $M_w$ 7.4, with mean magnitudes between $M_w$ 6.8-7.2 and mode magnitudes of $M_w$ 7.2-7.4. The characteristic distances for this very rare hazard level show mean values from 120 km to 240 km, with modal distances consistently near 160-180 km across all spectral periods examined. The epsilon values for rare events remain elevated at 1.0 to 1.3, indicating that ground motions substantially above median predictions control the hazard at these low annual exceedance probabilities. These events represent the upper range of credible earthquake scenarios in the stable continental region surrounding the site [@Petersen2014].

## Seismic Record Selection Methodology

### Selection Approach and Parameters

The seismic record selection methodology employed by WSP for the Louvicourt TSF site developed two suites of eight two-horizontal-component earthquake acceleration time histories amplitude-scaled to match the mean uniform hazard spectrum for 1/10,000 AEP. The methodology addressed two distinct fundamental periods relevant to embankments of differing height and stiffness at the site: a shorter fundamental period range from 0.25 s to 0.37 s, and a longer fundamental period of approximately 1.15 s. For the short period range, a conditioning period of 0.31 s was adopted with a period range of interest from 0.062 s to 0.62 s for ground motion scaling. For the long period range, a conditioning period of 1.15 s was used with a period range of interest from 0.23 s to 2.3 s. The causal parameters for record selection were derived from disaggregation analysis results at 1/10,000 AEP, establishing a target scenario with moment magnitude 7.3, rupture distance 170 km, hypocentral depth 20 km, depth to top of rupture 10 km, and rupture plane dip 90 degrees.

The 170 km rupture distance criterion originated directly from the disaggregation analysis results for the 1/10,000 AEP hazard level. The disaggregation results presented for 1/10,000 AEP show characteristic source-to-site distances with mode values consistently near 160-180 km across the examined spectral periods including peak ground acceleration, 0.2 s, 0.5 s, and 1.0 s. The mean distances from disaggregation range from approximately 120-140 km for peak ground acceleration up to 220-240 km for the 1.0 s spectral period. The adopted 170 km rupture distance represents a central value within the range of modal disaggregation distances and serves as the representative distance for the causal earthquake scenario used in ground motion selection. This 170 km criterion is specifically valid for the 1/10,000 AEP return period, as the disaggregation analysis was performed at this hazard level to inform the development of earthquake acceleration time histories scaled to the 1/10,000 AEP uniform hazard spectrum.

### Candidate Record Selection Process

The candidate ground motion records were selected by the WSP project team conducting the seismic hazard assessment. The selection process for the candidate catalogue involved querying the PEER NGA-West2 database [@Ancheta2014] to identify prospective ground motions within defined parameter ranges appropriate for the target earthquake scenario. The criteria used for developing the candidate catalogue included earthquake magnitude ranging from $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.8 and source-to-site rupture distance from 0 to 200 km. These bounds were deliberately set wider than the specific disaggregation scenario values of $M_w$ 7.3 and 170 km to increase the number of prospective candidate records available for consideration. The rationale for using relatively loose bounds was that the ground motion selection process explicitly considered secondary intensity measures, making it less critical to precisely target specific causal parameters such as exact earthquake magnitude. The NGA-East database was also consulted during the candidate identification phase, but this database lacked suitable records with magnitudes greater than $M_w$ 5.85, eliminating it as a viable source for large-magnitude candidate motions required for the $M_w$ 7.3 scenario. No explicit filters were described in the report for site ground conditions, suggesting that $V_{S30}$ was not a limiting parameter in creating the candidate catalogue, again to maximize the pool of available prospective records. From this broader candidate catalogue, the final selection of eight two-horizontal-component records for each fundamental period was made by evaluating amplitude scaling factors, spectral shape fit to the target uniform hazard spectrum, and agreement of secondary intensity measures with conditional target values.

Candidate ground motion records were drawn from the PEER NGA-West2 database spanning magnitudes $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.8 and distances 0 to 200 km, with relatively loose bounds justified by explicit consideration of secondary intensity measures. The NGA-East database was consulted but lacked suitable high-magnitude records above $M_w$ 5.85. Each suite of eight records was amplitude-scaled to achieve geometric mean agreement with the target spectrum within 90 percent to 130 percent across the period range of interest, with scale factors generally maintained between 0.5 and 4.0. Secondary intensity measures including Arias intensity, cumulative absolute velocity, and significant durations were conditioned to the target hazard level at each fundamental period to validate the selected ground motions beyond spectral shape matching alone.

### Amplitude Scaling Factors

The amplitude scaling factors applied to the selected ground motion records to match the target 1/10,000 AEP uniform hazard spectrum exhibit substantial variation across the two suites of earthquake acceleration time histories. For the short period range suite scaled for the fundamental period of 0.31 s, the scaling factors range from a minimum of 0.59 to a maximum of 8.28. The minimum scale factor of 0.59 was applied to record RSN 5618 from the 2008 Iwate, Japan earthquake recorded at station IWT010, while the maximum scale factor of 8.28 was applied to record RSN 1833 from the 1999 Hector Mine earthquake recorded at Snow Creek station. For the long period range suite scaled for the fundamental period of 1.15 s, the scaling factors range from a minimum of 0.64 to a maximum of 2.87. The minimum scale factor of 0.64 was applied to record RSN 68 from the 1971 San Fernando earthquake recorded at LA Hollywood Stor FF station, while the maximum scale factor of 2.87 was applied to record RSN 923 from the 1992 Big Bear earthquake recorded at Phelan Wilson Ranch station. The presence of scale factors substantially exceeding the commonly recommended limit of 2.0, particularly the factor of 8.28 and 6.85 in the short period suite, indicates aggressive scaling was required for some records [@Bahrampouri2024]. The WSP report noted that while scale factors were generally kept within the range 0.5 to 4.0, the scale factor was considered less important than the comparison of secondary intensity measures and the fit of selected ground motions to the target spectrum. Several records in both suites required scale factors in the range of 2.0 to 3.0, while two records in the short period suite exceeded 6.0. These large amplitude scaling factors were required to capture the high-frequency ground motion content indicated by the 1/10,000 AEP uniform hazard spectrum at the Louvicourt site, given that the candidate records originated from active tectonic plate boundary regions with different attenuation characteristics than the stable continental region site conditions.

### Compliance with Selection Criteria

The seismic record suite shown in Tables 10 and 11 of the WSP report was selected using criteria that focused primarily on spectral shape matching to the target 1/10,000 AEP uniform hazard spectrum and agreement with conditional secondary intensity measures, rather than strict compliance with all criteria established at the beginning of Chapter 8.0. The stated criteria at the outset of Chapter 8.0 included a target earthquake scenario with moment magnitude 7.3, rupture distance 170 km, hypocentral depth 20 km, depth to top of rupture 10 km, and rupture plane dip 90 degrees. Examination of Tables 10 and 11 reveals partial rather than complete compliance with these established causal parameters. The selected records span a magnitude range from $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 7.62, with many records having magnitudes substantially below the target $M_w$ 7.3, including three records at $M_w$ 6.0 to $M_w$ 6.2. The rupture distances of selected records range from 11 km to 159 km, with the majority of records having distances substantially shorter than the specified 170 km criterion, particularly in the short period suite where distances as close as 11 km, 16 km, and 19 km were accepted. Source mechanism types in the selected suites include reverse, reverse oblique, strike slip, and normal faulting, which have characteristic rupture plane dips that typically differ from the specified 90 degrees. The geometric mean of each suite achieves agreement with the target spectrum within the specified 90 to 130 percent tolerance over the period range of interest, satisfying the fundamental spectral matching objective. The secondary intensity measures of the selected scaled records, including Arias intensity, cumulative absolute velocity, and significant durations, show general agreement with estimated conditional target values, though with some scatter and a tendency for scaled motion intensity measures to exceed median target values. The WSP approach explicitly prioritized spectral shape matching and secondary intensity measure agreement over precise matching of individual causal parameters, justified by the argument that explicit consideration of secondary intensity measures reduces the importance of targeting specific causal parameters such as exact earthquake magnitude and distance. This selection strategy represents a tradeoff between the ideal of matching all specified criteria and the practical reality of limited suitable candidate records in the NGA-West2 database for a $M_w$ 7.3 stable continental region scenario.

## Tectonic Compatibility Issues

### Inadequacy of Record Selection for Continental Stable Conditions

The seismic record selection criteria employed by WSP present limitations with respect to the Continental Stable tectonic type of the region. The fundamental issue is that all candidate ground motion records were selected from the PEER NGA-West2 database, which contains exclusively earthquake recordings from shallow crustal events in active tectonic regimes, not stable continental regions [@Ancheta2014]. The NGA-West2 database explicitly excludes stable continental region recordings by design, focusing instead on active tectonic plate boundary earthquakes. For a site characterized by stable continental tectonic conditions, the use of active tectonic region ground motion records introduces tectonic incompatibility between the source region of the recordings and the target site conditions. WSP acknowledged this limitation, noting that candidate records have shorter source-to-site distances than indicated by disaggregation results and that suitable candidate ground motions from intraplate stable continental conditions would have source-to-site distances more consistent with disaggregation. The NGA-East database, developed specifically for stable continental regions in Central and Eastern North America [@Goulet2021], would provide tectonically appropriate candidate records but was noted to lack sufficient high-magnitude recordings above $M_w$ 5.85 for the target scenario of $M_w$ 7.3 events. The rationale provided by WSP for using NGA-West2 records relates to differences in site stiffness and attenuation characteristics between the plate boundary recording sites with weaker ground conditions and the Louvicourt site with high shear wave velocity of 1,853 m/s, arguing that shorter-distance active tectonic records were required to capture high-frequency content. While the selected motions were scaled to match target spectra and conditional intensity measures derived from stable continental region ground motion models, the fundamental waveform characteristics, duration properties, and frequency content of the actual recordings originate from a different tectonic environment than the Continental Stable region where the Louvicourt site is located.

### Tectonic Classification of Selected Records

None of the earthquake records listed in Tables 10 and 11 effectively belong to Stable Continental Regions. All selected records originate from earthquakes that occurred in active tectonic plate boundary regions, specifically from shallow crustal earthquakes in tectonically active areas. The earthquakes represented in the short period suite (Table 10) include the North Palm Springs earthquake in California, the Hector Mine earthquake in California, multiple Chi-Chi earthquake aftershocks in Taiwan, the Umbria Marche earthquake in Italy, and the Iwate earthquake in Japan. The earthquakes in the long period suite (Table 11) include the San Fernando earthquake in California, the Loma Prieta earthquake in California, the Landers earthquake in California, the Big Bear earthquake in California, and additional Chi-Chi earthquake events in Taiwan. All of these seismic events occurred in regions characterized by active plate boundary tectonics, including the San Andreas fault system and related structures in California, the subduction-related tectonic setting of Taiwan, and the extensional tectonic regime of the Apennines in Italy. California earthquakes recorded in the NGA-West2 database are explicitly from active tectonic crustal settings associated with the Pacific-North America plate boundary. Taiwan seismicity is dominated by the complex convergent tectonics resulting from interaction between the Philippine Sea Plate and the Eurasian Plate. The Japanese earthquake (Iwate) occurred in the active island arc setting associated with Pacific Plate subduction beneath Japan. None of these tectonic environments represent stable continental region conditions analogous to the Canadian Shield setting of the Louvicourt site. The use of active tectonic region records for a stable continental region application was acknowledged by WSP as a consequence of the lack of suitable large-magnitude ground motion recordings in the NGA-East stable continental region database, which contained insufficient records above $M_w$ 5.85. The fundamental tectonic mismatch between the source regions of all selected records and the target stable continental region site represents a significant limitation in the ground motion selection for the Louvicourt TSF seismic assessment.
