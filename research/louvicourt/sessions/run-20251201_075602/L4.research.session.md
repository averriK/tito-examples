## SLOT 2: Tectonic regimes controlling seismicity within 1000 km

The Louvicourt site is located within a stable continental region characterized by the Canadian Shield physiographic province of Canada. The region is part of the North American Craton, containing very old crystalline basement rocks dating back more than 1 billion years that have remained tectonically stable through multiple cycles of continental rifting, basin formation, and contraction. The Canadian Shield formed from collision of Archean-age microcontinents during the Paleoproterozoic era between 2.5 and 1.6 billion years ago, combining to form the ancient continent of Laurentia. Within approximately 1000 km of the Louvicourt site, the tectonic regime controlling seismicity is intraplate deformation within this stable continental region rather than active plate boundary tectonics. The region exhibits widely distributed seismicity that is not associated with mapped surface fault traces, characteristic of stable continental interior zones. Historical seismicity within the region includes the 1935 Timiskaming earthquake with moment magnitude 6.1, which occurred approximately 190 km southwest of the Louvicourt Mine. The seismicity in southeastern Canada results primarily from reactivation of inherited Precambrian structures under the present-day northeast-southwest-striking regional maximum horizontal stress field. ## SLOT 7: Maximum Credible Earthquake (MCE) calculation consideration

The WSP assessment did not explicitly calculate a Maximum Credible Earthquake for any particular source as part of the probabilistic seismic hazard analysis. The study implemented maximum magnitudes through the CanadaSHM6 seismic source model framework, which incorporates three alternative maximum magnitude branches in its logic tree structure for epistemic uncertainty treatment. These maximum magnitude alternatives were collapsed into a single magnitude-frequency distribution branch for computational efficiency, as is standard practice in CanadaSHM6. The Maximum Credible Earthquake concept represents a deterministic approach where the greatest earthquake reasonably expected from a specific source is estimated based on seismological and geological evidence. In contrast, the WSP study employed a fully probabilistic approach using the truncated exponential recurrence model with weighted maximum magnitude estimates embedded in the CanadaSHM6 source model. For stable continental regions such as southeastern Canada, probabilistic seismic hazard assessment with appropriate treatment of maximum magnitude uncertainty is generally preferred over deterministic MCE calculations because earthquake sources are diffuse areal zones rather than well-defined active fault structures. The maximum magnitudes incorporated in CanadaSHM6 for southeastern Canada stable continental region sources are based on worldwide stable continental region earthquake databases, with typical weighted average maximum magnitudes near $M_w$ 7.0 for cratonic sources and $M_w$ 7.4 for passive margin and rift sources. ## SLOT 11: Verification of Depth-to-Top Rupture and Rupture plane dip criteria application

The WSP report specified that depth-to-top rupture of 10 km and rupture plane dip of 90 degrees were adopted as causal parameters for the earthquake scenario used in ground motion selection and secondary intensity measure estimation. These parameters are listed explicitly in the ground motion selection criteria section as inputs for calculating target secondary intensity measures consistent with the 1/10,000 AEP hazard. However, verification of whether these criteria were actually applied as selection filters for the candidate ground motion records listed in Tables 10 and 11 cannot be conclusively determined from the information provided in the report. The NGA-West2 database from which candidate records were selected does contain rupture geometry parameters including depth to top of rupture and rupture plane dip in its metadata for individual earthquake recordings, as these parameters are standard components of the NGA-West2 flat file. Examination of the selected records in Tables 10 and 11 reveals that the actual source mechanisms include reverse, reverse oblique, strike slip, and normal faulting, which would have varying rupture plane dips not uniformly equal to 90 degrees. The source-to-site distances listed in Tables 10 and 11 are reported as rupture distances, indicating that rupture geometry was considered in the distance calculations. The report states that relatively loose bounds were used for earthquake magnitude and distance in creating the prospective ground motion catalogues, and that site parameters were not limited to increase the number of prospective motions. Modern practice recognizes that excessive amplitude scaling can distort the relationship between spectral ordinates and other ground motion characteristics including duration, energy content, and peak velocities.
