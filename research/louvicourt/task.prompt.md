# STRUCTURED PROMPT — Louvicourt PSHA Peer Review

## CONTEXT

The document in kb/ is a Probabilistic Seismic Hazard Assessment (PSHA) conducted by WSP, consisting of two parts. The first seven chapters relate to the PSHA and hazard results, providing context. SRK has been commissioned to perform an expert peer review of this document to identify gaps in the study.

## SLOTS

### SLOT 1: Introduction section

Prepare an introduction section that indicates: (1) what the project is, (2) who conducted the PSHA study, (3) what the study objectives were, and (4) what the purpose of this review work is.

### SLOT 2: Tectonic regimes controlling seismicity

Identify the tectonic regimes that control the seismicity of the zone within a radius of 1000 km.

### SLOT 3: Source types in the model

Determine whether all sources employed in the model are of the Stable Continental type or if there are sources within 1000 km that are of other types.

### SLOT 4: Characteristic magnitudes and distances for frequent events

Identify the "characteristic" magnitudes and epicentral distances of events that control the hazard for "frequent" events with AEP of 1/475 to 1/1000 years.

### SLOT 5: Characteristic magnitudes and distances for rare events

Identify the "characteristic" magnitudes and epicentral distances of events that control the hazard for "rare" events with AEP of 1/5000 to 1/10,000 years.

### SLOT 6: Active neotectonic faults identification

Determine whether active neotectonic faults have been identified in the region within less than 100 km from the site.

### SLOT 7: Maximum Credible Earthquake calculation

Determine whether the calculation of a Maximum Credible Earthquake (MCE) has been considered in the analysis for any particular source.

### SLOT 8: Executive summary of seismic record selection

Provide an executive summary of how seismic records (accelerograms) were selected to be compatible with the project site.

### SLOT 9: Adequacy of criteria for tectonic type

Evaluate whether the seismic record selection criteria are adequate for the tectonic type (Stable Continental) of the region.

### SLOT 10: Origin of 170 km rupture distance criterion

Determine the origin of the 170 km Rupture Distance criterion used for selection: whether it comes from disaggregation and for which return periods it is valid.

### SLOT 11: Verification of rupture parameter criteria application

Confirm whether the "Depth-to-Top Rupture" and "Rupture plane dip 90" criteria were effectively applied in the construction of Tables 10 and 11, given that these parameters should be available in public catalogs.

### SLOT 12: Candidate catalog selection criteria

Identify who selected the candidate ground motion records mentioned in point 3 of Chapter 8.0 and with what criteria.

### SLOT 13: Amplitude scaling factors

Determine the minimum and maximum amplification factors obtained in the amplitude scaling described in point 4 of Chapter 8.0, where candidate ground motions were scaled to agree with the target spectrum.

### SLOT 14: State of the art for amplitude scaling

Review what the literature and state of the art indicate regarding amplitude scaling of real records.

### SLOT 15: Reasonableness of large scaling factors

Evaluate whether it is reasonable to accept amplification factors much greater than 2 or much less than 0.5. Cite modern literature that discusses limits on scaling factors.

### SLOT 16: Table 10 and 11 selection criteria verification

Determine the criteria used to select the seismic record suite shown in Tables 10 and 11 of the PSHA report and verify whether they comply with the criteria established at the beginning of Chapter 8.0.

### SLOT 17: SCR records confirmation from tables

Confirm which records from Tables 10 and 11 are effectively from Stable Continental Regions (SCR).
