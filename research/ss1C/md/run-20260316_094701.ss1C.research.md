# Seismotectonic Setting: Rampura Agucha Mine

The Rampura Agucha Mine (RAM) tailings storage facility, owned and operated by Hindustan Zinc Limited (HZL), is located approximately 220 km south of Jaipur in Bhilwara district, Rajasthan, India, at approximately 25.37$^\circ$N, 74.67$^\circ$E. The mine occupies a position within the central Aravalli fold belt, an ancient Proterozoic terrane that constitutes part of the stable interior of the Indian craton. This document characterizes the seismotectonic environment of the site and surrounding region, addressing regional seismic generation mechanisms, the seismogenic domain applicable to the site, historical seismicity, active and neotectonic faults, and quantitative fault parameters relevant to seismic hazard assessment of the tailings storage facility.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Introductory location information (Bhilwara district, approximately 220 km from Jaipur, Aravalli fold belt) is consistent with publicly available geographic data and the context established in the KB source document covering the Indian subcontinent PSHA model; no quantitative claims requiring external verification are made at this stage.]

## SLOT 1: Regional Seismic Generation Mechanisms

The seismic hazard of the region surrounding the Rampura Agucha Mine is governed by four classes of seismic generation mechanisms reflecting the multi-domain tectonic character of the Indian subcontinent. The Indian Plate moves northward relative to Eurasia at 35-45 mm/yr, driving deformation and seismicity across several tectonically distinct domains. The Global Earthquake Model (GEM) hazard model for India (Nath and Thingbaijam, 2012), implemented in the OpenQuake (OQ) engine, recognizes Active Shallow Crust (ASC), Stable Continental Crust (SCC), Subduction Interface (SIF), and Subduction Intra-slab (SIS) seismic generation mechanisms, expressed in the model through tectonic region type labels including "active shallow crust," "stable shallow crust," "subduction interface," and "subduction intraslab," as well as intraplate margin zone variants [KB:ind-report.pdf.md] [DOI:10.1785/0120110113].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Indian Plate convergence rate of 35-45 mm/yr and the list of tectonic region types in the GEM India model are stated explicitly in the KB document, which describes the GEM PSHA input model for India and surroundings based on Nath and Thingbaijam (2012).]

Stable Continental Crust (SCC) is the dominant seismic generation mechanism in the region immediately surrounding the RAM site. Peninsular India, including the entirety of Rajasthan, constitutes a stable continental region (SCR) in which the Aravalli-Bundelkhand craton has remained largely intact since the Proterozoic. Seismicity in the SCR is intraplate in origin, produced by reactivation of ancient basement faults under far-field compressive stresses transmitted from the India-Eurasia collision zone. The GEM India model classifies central and western Rajasthan within the "stable shallow crust" tectonic region type and assigns GMPEs calibrated for stable continental regions, including Raghu Kanth and Iyengar (2007) and Atkinson and Boore (2006). Despite the absence of contemporaneous plate-boundary processes at the site, the strong Indian crust is capable of transmitting compressive stresses over great distances, and large-magnitude intraplate earthquakes such as the 2001 Bhuj event ($M_W$ 7.6) demonstrate that damaging seismicity can occur well within the stable interior of the plate [KB:ind-report.pdf.md] [WEB:https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9487858/].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The SCC/SCR classification for Peninsular India and the specific GMPE assignments for the "stable shallow crust" tectonic region type are explicitly stated in the KB document; the 2001 Bhuj Mw 7.6 intraplate earthquake is referenced in the KB tectonic overview section and is a well-established historical fact.]

Active Shallow Crust (ASC) seismicity characterizes the orogenic belts flanking peninsular India, particularly the Himalayan collision zone and adjacent fold-thrust systems. The Himalayan arc, including the Main Boundary Thrust, Main Central Thrust, and associated fold-thrust belt systems in northwest and northeast India, hosts the highest seismic hazard in South Asia; faults in the Himalayan belt accommodate at least half of the total India-Eurasia plate convergence and represent the greatest source of seismic hazard to northern India. The Himalayan belt lies approximately 600-800 km north of the RAM site; however, the large magnitudes of potential Himalayan thrust earthquakes and the low attenuation properties of the Peninsular Indian Shield at large source-to-site distances mean that ASC events contribute to the regional seismic hazard envelope. The GEM India model includes "active shallow crust normal" and "active shallow crust strike-slip reverse" tectonic region types for these zones [KB:ind-report.pdf.md] [WEB:https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9487858/].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The description of ASC seismicity in the Himalayan belt and the GEM model's tectonic region types for active shallow crust are directly supported by the KB document, including its tectonic overview and GMPE table; the approximate distance to the Himalayan belt is consistent with geographic data.]

Subduction Interface (SIF) and Subduction Intra-slab (SIS) mechanisms are present within the broader Indian Plate system at considerable distances from the RAM site. The Makran subduction zone, located along the coast of Pakistan and Iran approximately 1,100-1,200 km west-northwest of RAM, is the principal active subduction interface in the northwestern Indian Ocean domain; the 1945 Makran earthquake ($M_W$ 8.1, approximately 1,115 km from RAM) is the largest historical event on this structure. In the east, the Andaman-Nicobar subduction system and the Indo-Burmese arc generate additional subduction interface and intraslab seismicity. Subduction intra-slab (SIS) earthquakes also occur beneath the Indo-Gangetic Plain and Himalayan arc where the Indian slab descends under Eurasia, producing deep seismic events. The GEM India model includes "subduction interface," "subduction interface megathrust," "subduction intraslab," and "subduction intraslab Himalayas" tectonic region types to capture these source zones [KB:ind-report.pdf.md] [KB:epicenters.csv].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The SIF/SIS tectonic region types and their associated GMPEs are explicitly listed in the KB document; the 1945 Makran event Mw 8.1 and its approximate distance from RAM are verifiable from the epicenters catalog in the KB.]

## SLOT 2: Site-Specific Seismogenic Setting

The Rampura Agucha Mine site is situated within the Stable Continental Crust (SCC) seismogenic domain. The site occupies the central Aravalli fold belt, a Proterozoic terrane that was fully cratonized by the end of the Precambrian and exhibits no evidence of active plate-boundary deformation in its interior. GPS geodetic measurements at the Udaipur station, approximately 70 km south of the RAM site, record velocities of approximately 49 mm/yr toward the northeast, consistent with rigid Indian Plate motion and indicating negligible internal strain accumulation within the Aravalli block. Under the Bureau of Indian Standards seismic zoning classification (IS 1893:2016), Bhilwara district falls within Seismic Zone II, the lowest hazard category in India, corresponding to a zone factor $Z = 0.10$ and expected maximum Modified Mercalli Intensity (MMI) of VI or below [KB:ind-report.pdf.md] [DOI:10.1785/0120110113] [WEB:https://asc-india.org/seismi/seis-rajasthan.htm].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The SCC classification of the Aravalli fold belt is supported by the KB document and the Nath and Thingbaijam (2012) PSHA model; the GPS velocity (~49 mm/yr NE) and BIS Zone II classification for Bhilwara district are consistent across the referenced external sources and are coherent with the stable continental characterization established in the GEM model.]

Although the Indian subcontinent hosts multiple coexisting seismogenic mechanisms (SCC, ASC, SIF, SIS), the RAM site falls within the SCC seismogenic domain for site-specific hazard assessment. The seismic sources nearest to the site are intraplate faults within and adjacent to the Aravalli fold belt, reactivated under the far-field stress regime imposed by the India-Eurasia collision. The GEM source zone containing the RAM site area has a Gutenberg-Richter b-value of approximately 0.93 and a maximum magnitude of $M_W$ 6.5, consistent with the historical seismicity record of central Rajasthan. Intraplate seismicity in the Aravalli-Delhi fold belt (ADFB) is characterized by diffuse seismicity extending to approximately 40 km depth on NE-SW trending basement fault systems, with the principal stress axis ($\sigma_1$) oriented NNE-SSW to NE-SW in accord with the far-field plate convergence direction [DOI:10.1785/0120110113] [WEB:https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9487858/] [WEB:https://github.com/nackerley/indian-subcontinent-psha].^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The GEM source zone b-value (~0.93) and Mmax (~6.5) for the zone near the RAM site are from secondary web sources describing the Nath and Thingbaijam 2012 model parameters; these could not be directly verified from the primary KB document, which does not include the full source zone parameter tables. The ADFB seismicity depth and stress orientation are from the referenced PMC paper and are consistent with published seismological literature.]

## SLOT 4: Active Faults with Neotectonic Activity

Under ANCOLD (Australian National Committee on Large Dams) guidelines, a fault is classified as active if it has produced historical earthquakes or has experienced displacement in the Holocene (last approximately 11,000 years), or for large faults in the latest Pleistocene (11,000-35,000 years before present). Faults satisfying either of these conditions are considered capable of future movement and are directly relevant to seismic hazard assessment for dam and tailings storage facilities under the ANCOLD neotectonic classification [WEB:https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3263/14/3/68] [WEB:https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11805392/].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The ANCOLD active fault classification criteria (Holocene up to 11,000 years; latest Pleistocene extension to 35,000 years for large faults) are confirmed by the referenced MDPI Geosciences paper on active fault classification schemes and the PMC paper on engineering fault classification, and are consistent with the 10,000-35,000 year window specified in the task.]

The Great Boundary Fault (GBF) is the most significant neotectonic structure in the immediate region surrounding the RAM site. The GBF is a NE-SW trending reverse fault zone approximately 400 km in length that separates the Aravalli fold belt from the Vindhyan sedimentary platform, passing through Chittorgarh and Bundi districts in Rajasthan. Evidence of Holocene neotectonic activity includes soft-sediment deformation structures (SSDS) and seismites proximal to the fault trace, attributed to the Usawan paleoearthquake, with estimated magnitude $M_W$ 6.5-7.2 based on the scale of observed liquefaction and deformation features. Tectonic geomorphic indicators, including laterally displaced drainage networks and fault scarps consistent with late Quaternary to Holocene reactivation, further support the GBF's classification as an ANCOLD-capable fault [WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169555X23003574] [WEB:https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19475705.2013.868371].^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The GBF neotectonic evidence (SSDS/seismites, Usawan paleoearthquake Mw 6.5-7.2, geomorphic indicators) is supported by the referenced ScienceDirect geomorphological study and the Tandfonline active fault research review for India; the Mw 6.5-7.2 range is from paleoseismic interpretation based on indirect evidence (SSDS scale) rather than a primary paleoseismic trench study, introducing moderate uncertainty in the magnitude estimate.]

The Narmada-Son Lineament (NSL) is a major E-W Precambrian rift structure approximately 1,200 km in length, extending across central India at approximately 22-23$^\circ$N latitude, approximately 200-250 km south of the RAM site. The NSL exhibits confirmed Holocene neotectonic activity based on morphotectonic evidence including differential uplift, basin inversion, and incision patterns of rivers crossing the lineament. The 1997 Jabalpur earthquake ($M_W$ 5.8) on the eastern NSL segment confirms present-day seismogenic activity, and GPS-derived convergence rates of approximately 2 $\pm$ 1 mm/yr across the structure indicate ongoing far-field strain loading [WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/article/pii/S2666033421000149] [WEB:https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013Geomo.180..292J/abstract].^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The NSL characterization, Holocene activity evidence, 1997 Jabalpur event, and GPS convergence rate (~2 +/- 1 mm/yr) are derived from the referenced PSInSAR/GNSS study and the morphotectonic segmentation paper; these secondary web sources summarize peer-reviewed research that is internally consistent. The specific convergence rate value has not been directly verified against the primary geodetic publication.]

The Kachchh Mainland Fault (KMF) and associated Kachchh fault system in Gujarat constitute one of the best-documented neotectonic fault systems in India, located approximately 600-650 km WSW of the RAM site. Paleoseismic investigations of the Jhura segment of the KMF have identified multiple Holocene surface-rupturing earthquakes, with five events dated between approximately 10,000 and 890 years before present (BP) and estimated magnitudes ranging from $M_W$ 5.8 to 6.5 per event. Fault-normal slip rates of 0.04-0.36 mm/yr have been determined from geomorphic and geodetic methods, with lower rates on the western KMF and higher rates on the eastern segment. The 1819 Allah Bund earthquake and the 2001 Bhuj earthquake ($M_W$ 7.6) confirm that the broader Kachchh seismogenic zone is capable of generating large to great intraplate earthquakes [WEB:https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11109187/] [WEB:https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-62086-z].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Kachchh KMF paleoseismic data (Holocene events, Mw 5.84-6.47, slip rates 0.04-0.36 mm/yr, event ages) are well documented in two peer-reviewed Scientific Reports papers cited; the 2001 Bhuj Mw 7.6 event is a well-established historical fact consistent with the KB epicenters catalog.]

Additional faults exhibiting current or recent seismogenic activity in the broader region include the Cambay Basin fault system, approximately 200-250 km SW of RAM, which represents a Neogene-Quaternary failed rift with ongoing seismicity documented in recent decades, and the Delhi-Haridwar Ridge, approximately 350-400 km NNE of RAM, a NNE-SSW subsurface basement ridge associated with the northern Aravalli-Delhi fold belt that has generated moderate historical earthquakes. No published paleoseismic trench studies documenting Holocene fault displacement are available for either of these structures in the directly accessed scientific literature, leaving their precise neotectonic status indeterminate under strict ANCOLD criteria [WEB:https://pubs.geoscienceworld.org/ssa/bssa/article-abstract/109/6/2240/574248/New-Insight-into-the-Recent-Earthquake-Activity-in] [WEB:https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1092927/full].^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The Cambay Basin fault characterization (failed rift, ongoing seismicity) and Delhi-Haridwar Ridge description are from the referenced BSSA paper and Frontiers Earth Science paper; distance estimates are approximate.
