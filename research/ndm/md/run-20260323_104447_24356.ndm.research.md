# Performance-Based Horizontal Seismic Coefficient for Pseudo-Static Slope Stability Analysis

---

## 1. Problem Introduction

Pseudo-static slope stability analysis employs a horizontal seismic coefficient $k$ that represents the equivalent static inertial force per unit weight applied to the potential sliding mass, replacing the transient dynamic loading with a steady-state horizontal body force. The rational selection of $k$ is not reducible to a single universal value; it requires calibration against two independent engineering dimensions that together define a performance-based design objective consistent with site-specific seismic hazard and facility-specific consequence requirements. The performance-based horizontal seismic coefficient $k_{\max}$ is defined as the minimum yield acceleration ratio for which the probability that permanent slope displacement exceeds an allowable threshold is controlled at a specified target level. [KB:pbkmax.qmd][KB:newmark.qmd]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The two-dimension calibration framework and the definition of kmax are directly consistent with the treatment in KB:pbkmax.qmd. The characterization of pseudo-static analysis as a limit-equilibrium approach is standard geotechnical practice confirmed across multiple KB files.]

The first calibration dimension is the target seismic intensity level, linked directly to the consequence class of the structure. Facilities whose failure entails severe consequences - such as tailings storage facilities (TSF) and waste rock dumps (WRD) - require evaluation against infrequent ground-motion scenarios with annual exceedance probabilities (AEP) spanning approximately $1/2{,}475$ to $1/10{,}000$. Structures with lower failure consequences may be assessed against less extreme seismic return periods. The consequence class maps onto the hazard return period which, through probabilistic seismic hazard analysis (PSHA), specifies the site-specific intensity measures - including peak ground acceleration ($\mathrm{PGA}$), spectral acceleration $S_a(T)$, Arias intensity ($\mathrm{AI}$), and peak ground velocity ($\mathrm{PGV}$) - against which slope performance is evaluated. Seismic performance is characterized across a suite of service levels spanning AEP $1/100$ to AEP $1/10{,}000$, providing a comprehensive view of the sensitivity of slope behavior to seismic demand intensity. [KB:pbkmax.qmd][KB:newmark.qmd][WEB:https://www.ancold.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Tailings-Guideline-Addendum-July-2019_v2.pdf]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The service-level range (AEP 1/100 to 1/10,000) and application to TSF and WRD are explicitly stated in KB:pbkmax.qmd. ANCOLD AEP thresholds for high-consequence tailings dams are corroborated by the ANCOLD web source.]

The second calibration dimension encompasses the physical and dynamic characteristics of the slope. The yield acceleration $k_y = a_y/g$ - the horizontal acceleration ratio at which the limit-equilibrium factor of safety against sliding on the governing failure surface equals unity - quantifies the resistance of the slope to seismic loading and is determined from geotechnical analysis. It reflects the combined influence of slope geometry, material shear strength, and the kinematic mechanism of the potential failure surface. The fundamental period $T_n$ of the potential sliding mass characterizes the dynamic amplification behavior of the slope: stiff, shallow masses with short $T_n$ respond primarily to the peak ground acceleration, while softer, deeper masses with longer $T_n$ are governed by spectral accelerations at longer periods, subject to site-specific amplification effects. The shear modulus $G$ as a function of confining stress and void ratio controls the stiffness profile of the slope and thereby determines $T_n$ for deformable earth structures. [KB:newmark.qmd][KB:newmark_model.qmd][KB:site_model.qmd]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The definitions of k_y, T_n, and their roles in slope displacement analysis are directly supported by KB:newmark.qmd and KB:newmark_model.qmd. Shear modulus parameterization as a function of void ratio and confining stress is addressed in KB:newmark_model.qmd. These concepts are standard in seismic geotechnical engineering.]

The performance-based seismic coefficient $k_{\max}$ integrates both calibration dimensions by identifying the minimum yield acceleration at which the probability that permanent displacement $D_n$ exceeds an allowable threshold $D_n^{\star}$ is controlled at or below a target exceedance probability $p$. The defining relationship is: [KB:pbkmax.qmd]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This lead-in directly summarizes the kmax formulation in KB:pbkmax.qmd, which presents the infimum definition explicitly. All content accurately reflects the KB source.]

$$k_{\max}(p\,|\,D_n^{\star}) = \inf \left\{ k_y : \mathbb{P}[D_n(k_y) > D_n^{\star}] \leq p \right\}$$

This expression links the seismic coefficient to the site-specific hazard, the displacement-based performance objective, and the full statistical characterization of ground-motion variability and displacement model uncertainty. The distribution $D_n(k_y)$ reflects both the variability in intensity measures derived from PSHA and the model-specific dispersion in displacement prediction. Displacement values below a numerical floor are treated as negligibly small to maintain well-defined logarithmic operations. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The interpretation of the kmax formula and the sources of variability in D_n are directly supported by KB:pbkmax.qmd, including the explicit mention of the numerical floor for logarithmic stability.]

## 2. Current State of Practice

The determination of pseudo-static seismic coefficients for slope stability analysis has evolved from purely prescriptive values adopted by engineering convention toward mechanically grounded, performance-based procedures calibrated against site-specific hazard and allowable displacement criteria. Early practice in the United States and Japan employed coefficients of $k = 0.1$ to $0.2\,g$ selected by reference to regional seismicity levels without explicit connection to a displacement performance objective. The limitations of this approach were demonstrated by the behavior of earth dams during the 1971 San Fernando earthquake, which motivated a rational, displacement-based basis for coefficient selection grounded in the sliding-block model introduced by Newmark (1965). [WEB:https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%29GT.1943-5606.0000012]
^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The historical narrative of pseudo-static coefficient evolution from prescriptive to performance-based is broadly consistent with the geotechnical literature and the ASCE source cited. The KB does not provide this historical background directly; claims are derived from web sources and established field knowledge. Confidence is MEDIUM because specific historical details are not verified against KB content.]

Hynes-Griffin and Franklin (1984) used the Newmark sliding-block framework to establish the conservative criterion $k = 0.5\,\mathrm{PGA}/g$ as a lower bound sufficient to limit displacements below 1 m for most ground-motion records available at the time. Bray and Travasarou (2007, 2009) [DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)1090-0241(2007)133:4(381)] advanced the field by presenting a semiempirical procedure that derives the seismic coefficient directly from site-specific hazard parameters and slope dynamic properties, using 688 recorded ground motions and a nonlinear fully coupled stick-slip displacement model. Their central finding - that the coefficient cannot be determined rationally without specifying the allowable displacement and the seismic exposure of the site - has become the organizing principle of current practice. Macedo, Bray, Abrahamson, and Travasarou (2018) subsequently formalized a probabilistic framework integrating the full distribution of intensity measure values at the target hazard level, enabling direct computation of displacement exceedance probabilities without reference to a single representative ground-motion parameter. [DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)1090-0241(2007)133:4(381)][WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1193/122516EQS251M]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Bray and Travasarou (2007) details (688 records, nonlinear coupled model, DOI confirmed) are consistent with KB:newmark.qmd citations and web search results. Macedo et al. (2018) probabilistic framework is corroborated by web search results and referenced within KB:newmark.qmd. The Hynes-Griffin and Franklin (1984) criterion is standard established knowledge in geotechnical engineering.]

Contemporary normative guidance reflects the performance-based consensus. The Federal Emergency Management Agency guidelines (FEMA P-65) establish probabilistic seismic hazard analysis as the standard basis for selecting design earthquake parameters and endorse Newmark-type displacement analysis as the primary method for evaluating seismic adequacy of earth dams where liquefaction is not the governing failure mode. [WEB:https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-08/fema_dam-safety_earthquake-analysis_P-65.pdf] The Australian National Committee on Large Dams (ANCOLD) guidelines for tailings storage facilities specify minimum design earthquake return periods of AEP $1/2{,}475$ for the operational phase and maximum design earthquakes approaching AEP $1/10{,}000$ for post-closure passive care phases. The International Commission on Large Dams (ICOLD) similarly emphasizes probabilistic methods for defining seismic demand and accepts Newmark-based displacement calculations as a recognized approach for embankment dam performance assessment. Technical practice in major mining jurisdictions - including Chile, Australia, and Canada - has adopted the performance-based approach as standard, requiring site-specific PSHA, displacement-based seismic assessment using recognized regression models, and explicit performance objectives defined by allowable displacement thresholds at specified hazard return periods. The development of tectonic-source-specific displacement models represents a significant methodological advance. Bray, Macedo, and Travasarou (2017) [DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)GT.1943-5606.0001833] developed a flexible-block displacement model for subduction zone interface earthquakes, calibrated against 810 two-component ground-motion recordings, recognizing that the long-duration, broad-band spectral characteristics of subduction events produce systematically higher displacements than shallow crustal events at equivalent peak acceleration levels. Bray and Macedo (2019) [DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)GT.1943-5606.0002143] subsequently updated the shallow crustal earthquake model using 6,711 recordings from the NGA-West2 database, introducing $\mathrm{PGV}$ as an additional predictor for near-fault, high-amplitude ground motions. The concurrent application of event-specific models within a single probabilistic framework - with logic-tree weights assigned to reflect the relative contribution of each tectonic source to the site hazard - is the appropriate treatment of source-type uncertainty in multi-tectonic settings. The convergence of normative guidance, regulatory frameworks, and technical literature toward a performance-based displacement approach reflects broad recognition that the seismic coefficient cannot be specified rationally without reference to three site-specific inputs: the seismic hazard expressed through PSHA-derived intensity measures at the applicable return period, the dynamic properties of the slope, and an explicit allowable displacement threshold appropriate to the facility's consequence class and performance objectives. The framework described in this document operationalizes this three-part calibration through a probabilistic Monte Carlo simulation that propagates ground-motion uncertainty, site-amplification uncertainty, and displacement model uncertainty jointly. ## 3. Newmark Displacement Analysis Methods

The Newmark sliding-block method conceptualizes the seismically loaded slope as a rigid or flexible block resting on an inclined frictional surface. When the driving acceleration component along the failure plane exceeds the yield acceleration $k_y g$, the block undergoes incremental downslope movement; when the driving acceleration falls below this threshold, relative motion ceases. The total permanent displacement $D$ accumulates over all exceedance episodes throughout the duration of the ground-motion record. In probabilistic applications, the expected mean log-displacement $\mu_{\ln D}$ is expressed as a regression function of intensity measures and slope properties, and the full stochastic displacement for a given realization is obtained by adding a scaled residual: [KB:newmark.qmd][KB:newmark_model.qmd]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This description of the Newmark framework is consistent with KB:newmark.qmd and KB:newmark_model.qmd, which present the general probabilistic formulation explicitly. The conceptual description is standard in seismic geotechnical engineering.]

$$\ln D = \mu_{\ln D}(\mathrm{IM},\,k_y,\,T_n,\,M_w) + \epsilon\,\sigma_{\ln D}$$

In this expression, $\mathrm{IM}$ is the set of ground-motion intensity measures required by the specific model (e.g., $\mathrm{PGA}$, $S_a$ at a reference period, $\mathrm{AI}$, $\mathrm{PGV}$), $\mu_{\ln D}$ is the model-predicted mean in natural-log space, $\sigma_{\ln D}$ is the model standard deviation in natural-log space, and $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ is a standard normal variate capturing record-to-record variability not explained by the regression predictors. All models are formulated in lognormal space for consistency with probabilistic simulation. [KB:newmark.qmd]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The parameter definitions for the general Newmark model equation are taken directly from KB:newmark.qmd, which presents the identical formulation and notation. No external verification is required beyond KB content.]

The rigid-block idealization treats the sliding mass as an undistorted body that moves as a whole relative to the underlying ground. Under this assumption, the base of the sliding block experiences the free-field ground-motion acceleration without dynamic amplification within the mass, and the governing intensity measure is $\mathrm{PGA}$, supplemented in multi-parameter models by $\mathrm{AI}$ or $\mathrm{PGV}$ to capture duration and velocity effects. This idealization is appropriate for shallow translational failure surfaces in relatively stiff materials where the fundamental period of the sliding mass is short relative to the dominant periods of the input motion. Four rigid-block models are retained in the analysis ensemble: Ambraseys and Menu (1988), Yegian et al. (1991), Jibson (2007) [DOI:10.1016/j.enggeo.2007.01.013], and Saygili and Rathje (2008) [DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)1090-0241(2008)134:6(790)]. The Jibson (2007) model represents a significant updating of earlier rigid-block relationships, calibrated against 2,270 strong-motion records from 30 worldwide earthquakes, with regression forms expressed in terms of Arias intensity $\mathrm{AI}$ and the critical acceleration ratio $r = k_y/\mathrm{PGA}$. The Saygili and Rathje (2008) model extends the rigid-block approach to a multi-parameter framework using a database exceeding 2,000 acceleration time-histories, identifying $\mathrm{PGA}$ and $\mathrm{AI}$ as the most efficient predictor combination; its standard deviation is displacement-dependent, increasing as $r$ decreases to reflect greater prediction uncertainty for weaker slopes. [KB:newmark_model.qmd][DOI:10.1016/j.enggeo.2007.01.013][DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)1090-0241(2008)134:6(790)]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The rigid-block model suite, their input parameters, and database sizes are consistent with KB:newmark_model.qmd and confirmed by web search results with verified DOIs. The displacement-dependent sigma for Saygili-Rathje is taken directly from KB:newmark_model.qmd. The record counts for Jibson (2007) and Saygili-Rathje (2008) are confirmed by web search.]

The flexible-block (compliant-block) idealization accounts for the dynamic response of the sliding mass as a deformable body. The governing intensity measure is the spectral acceleration $S_a$ evaluated at a period proportional to the elongated fundamental period of the partially yielding slope - at $1.3\,T_n$ for the Bray and Macedo (2019) model and at $1.5\,T_n$ for the Bray and Travasarou (2007) and Bray, Macedo, and Travasarou (2017) models - rather than the peak ground acceleration. This treatment captures the amplification or de-amplification of motion within the earth structure and introduces $T_n$ and moment magnitude $M_w$ as explicit predictors. Flexible-block models are appropriate for deeper-seated failure surfaces in earth dams, natural slopes, compacted fills, and waste embankments where the sliding mass has significant deformability. Three flexible-block models are employed: Bray and Travasarou (2007) [DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)1090-0241(2007)133:4(381)], Bray, Macedo, and Travasarou (2017) [DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)GT.1943-5606.0001833], and Bray and Macedo (2019) [DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)GT.1943-5606.0002143]. The 2007 model applies across a range of tectonic environments and is calibrated against 688 ground-motion recordings. The 2017 model is calibrated specifically for subduction zone interface earthquakes using 810 two-component recordings, with $M_w$ included to account for the long duration of subduction events. The 2019 model targets shallow crustal earthquakes and employs 6,711 recordings from the NGA-West2 database, with a $\mathrm{PGV}$-based correction for high-amplitude ground motions ($\mathrm{PGV} > 115$ cm s$^{-1}$). [KB:newmark_model.qmd][DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)1090-0241(2007)133:4(381)][DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)GT.1943-5606.0001833][DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)GT.1943-5606.0002143]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The flexible-block model characteristics including Sa evaluation periods, database sizes, and tectonic specialization are directly supported by KB:newmark_model.qmd and confirmed by web search results with verified DOIs. The PGV correction threshold of 115 cm/s for BM2019 is explicitly stated in KB:newmark_model.qmd.]

The following presents the complete regression equations for each individual Newmark displacement model employed in the analysis framework. General notation applies throughout: $D$ denotes permanent displacement in cm; $k_y = a_y/g$ is the dimensionless yield-acceleration ratio; $\mathrm{PGA}$ is peak ground acceleration in units of $g$; $\mathrm{PGV}$ is peak ground velocity in cm s$^{-1}$; $\mathrm{AI}$ is Arias intensity in m s$^{-1}$; $T_n$ is the fundamental period of the sliding mass in s; $S_a$ is spectral acceleration in $g$ evaluated at the model-specified period; $M_w$ is moment magnitude; and $r = k_y/\mathrm{PGA}$. ### A.1 Bray and Macedo (2017) - Flexible-Block, Subduction Zone Earthquakes

The Bray and Macedo (2017) model [DOI:10.1061/(ASCE)GT.1943-5606.0001833] is a flexible-block (compliant) formulation calibrated for subduction zone interface earthquakes against 810 two-component ground-motion recordings. The model is applicable to earth dams, natural slopes, and engineered embankments, and employs the spectral acceleration $S_a$ evaluated at the undegraded fundamental period $T_n$ of the sliding mass. The mean log-displacement and coefficient definitions are: [KB:newmark_model.qmd]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The BM2017 model type, tectonic application, and database size are consistent with KB:newmark_model.qmd and confirmed by web search. The spectral acceleration evaluation period is specified in KB:newmark_model.qmd.]

$$\begin{aligned}
\mu_{\ln D} &= a_0 + a_1\ln S_a + a_2(\ln S_a)^2 \\[6pt]
a_0 &= \begin{cases}

\end{aligned}$$

## 5. Ensemble Numerical Model

The ensemble displacement model adopted in this framework consists of a weighted combination of the seven individual Newmark predictive models documented in the appendix. Rather than selecting a single preferred regression, all models are retained and their results aggregated using a logic-tree weighting scheme, thereby representing epistemic uncertainty in model choice as a formal component of the probabilistic analysis. The weighting strategy assigns greater influence to more recent models on the basis that they were calibrated against substantially larger and more comprehensive ground-motion databases, providing improved regression accuracy and reduced systematic bias relative to earlier, smaller-sample formulations. [KB:newmark.qmd][KB:uncertainty_model.qmd]

Ground-motion intensity measures required by each model are drawn from the empirical quantile distributions produced by the probabilistic seismic hazard analysis. For each oscillator period, the PSHA hazard fractiles - typically the 2nd, 5th, 16th, 50th, 84th, 95th, and 98th percentiles and the mean - form the empirical distribution of $\mathrm{IM}$ at that period. For each Monte Carlo realization, a uniform random variable $p \in (0,1)$ is mapped through a piecewise quantile function $Q_{\mathrm{IM}}(p)$ to obtain the corresponding intensity measure value. Spectral ordinates not explicitly tabulated in the hazard output are obtained by logarithmic interpolation in period. When the site hazard is provided at reference-rock conditions ($v_{\mathrm{ref}} = 760$ m/s), period-dependent site amplification factors are applied with amplification model uncertainty propagated as a lognormal variable. Inter-period correlation of spectral ordinates across the simultaneously sampled period vector is preserved using the Baker and Jayaram (2009) [@BakerJayaram2009] exponential decay model. [KB:newmark.qmd][KB:uncertainty_model.qmd]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The IM sampling procedure, quantile function construction, logarithmic interpolation, site amplification, and Baker-Jayaram inter-period correlation model are all directly supported by KB:uncertainty_model.qmd and KB:newmark.qmd. The Baker-Jayaram 2009 reference and the 760 m/s reference condition are mentioned explicitly in the KB sources.]

For each Monte Carlo realization and for each model in the ensemble, the log-displacement is computed as: [KB:newmark.qmd]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This lead-in for the displacement computation equation directly reflects the simulation procedure described in KB:newmark.qmd and KB:uncertainty_model.qmd.]

$$\ln D = \mu_{\ln D}(\mathrm{IM},\,k_y,\,T_n,\,M_w) + \epsilon\,\sigma_{\ln D}$$

Model-specific aleatory uncertainty is represented by the regression standard deviation $\sigma_{\ln D}$, which is treated as a constant for all models except Saygili and Rathje (2008), whose standard deviation varies with $r$. For each realization, a standard normal deviate $\epsilon$ is drawn independently for each model, preserving the independence of aleatory variability across models and preventing artificial reduction of total uncertainty through averaging. The ensemble displacement for a given scenario is obtained by aggregating model-specific results in accordance with the assigned logic-tree weights. Quantiles of the assembled empirical distribution - including the median, 84th percentile, and 95th percentile - are computed directly from the full set of realizations across models and intensity-measure scenarios. Displacement values below a numerical threshold are floored at $10^{-16}$ m to maintain well-defined logarithmic operations. [KB:newmark.qmd][KB:uncertainty_model.qmd][KB:pbkmax.qmd]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The treatment of sigma as constant except for the SR2008 variable-sigma model, independent epsilon draws, quantile reporting at median/84th/95th, logic-tree aggregation, and the numerical floor are all directly supported by KB:newmark.qmd, KB:uncertainty_model.qmd, and KB:pbkmax.qmd.]

The performance-based seismic coefficient $k_{\max}$ is derived from the assembled ensemble by identifying, for a given target exceedance probability $p$ and allowable displacement $D_n^{\star}$, the minimum yield acceleration at which the estimated exceedance probability $\hat{p}(k_y) = \hat{\mathbb{P}}[D_n(k_y) > D_n^{\star}]$ satisfies $\hat{p}(k_y) \leq p$. This calculation is performed for each combination of material scenario, slope geometry, and service level, producing $k_{\max}$ values tabulated across the range of return periods from AEP $1/100$ to AEP $1/10{,}000$. These results directly inform the selection of the pseudo-static horizontal seismic coefficient for use in limit-equilibrium stability calculations under performance-based seismic design criteria applicable to tailings storage facilities and waste rock dumps.
