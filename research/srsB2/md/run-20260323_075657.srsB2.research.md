# Epistemic Logic-Tree Weight Calibration Under Unit-Sum Constraint

## SLOT 1: Extension of the Error-Minimization Formulation to the Unit-Sum Constraint

The weight-calibration problem developed in the knowledge base casts the determination of GMPE branch weights as a constrained linear-regression problem in log-spectral space [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The baseline formulation minimizes the squared residual between the weighted log-spectral combination and a target vector over a period mesh of $n$ ordinates, subject only to non-negativity of each weight component. Given the transformed design matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times N}$ whose $(i,k)$-th entry is $\tilde{A}_{ik} = \ln S_{a,k}(T_i)$, the log-domain target vector $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with $y_i = \ln S_a^{\mathrm{obj}}(T_i)$, and the weight vector $\mathbf{w} = (w_1, \dots, w_N)^\top$, the non-negative least-squares (NNLS) problem is [KB:spec.compile.research.md]:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The NNLS formulation is directly reproduced from SLOT 5 of the KB document spec.compile.research.md, which provides the explicit problem statement, design matrix construction, and log-space transformation. The notation is internally consistent throughout the KB.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N} \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad w_k \geq 0, \quad k = 1, \dots, N.$$

Incorporating the normalization condition $\sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k = 1$ extends the feasible set from the non-negative orthant $\mathbb{R}^N_+$ to the standard simplex $\Delta^{N-1}$, the convex polytope that is the intersection of the non-negative orthant with the unit-sum hyperplane [WEB:https://optimization.cbe.cornell.edu/index.php?title=Quadratic_programming].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The standard simplex as the intersection of the non-negative orthant with the affine hyperplane sum(w)=1 is a foundational definition in convex analysis. The characterization is consistent with standard quadratic programming references.]

$$\Delta^{N-1} = \left\{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N : \sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k = 1, \quad w_k \geq 0 \; \text{for all} \; k\right\}.$$

The resulting optimization problem is a quadratic program (QP) with a strictly convex quadratic objective, one linear equality constraint, and $N$ linear inequality constraints, formulated as:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: A strictly convex quadratic objective with linear equality and inequality constraints is the canonical definition of a convex QP. This classification is standard and consistent with SLOT 7 of the KB, which discusses the convex optimization generalization of the spectral-matching problem.]

$$\mathbf{w}^* = \underset{\mathbf{w} \in \Delta^{N-1}}{\arg\min} \;\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2.$$

The Lagrangian of this problem introduces a single unrestricted scalar multiplier $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ for the equality constraint and a non-negative multiplier vector $\boldsymbol{\mu} = (\mu_1, \dots, \mu_N)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^N_+$ for the non-negativity inequalities.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Lagrangian construction follows standard constrained optimization duality theory. The sign convention adopted -- subtracting the equality and inequality constraint terms -- is consistent with the KKT literature and correctly captures the stationarity conditions derived below.]

$$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, \lambda, \boldsymbol{\mu}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 - \lambda\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k - 1\right) - \sum_{k=1}^{N} \mu_k w_k.$$

Since the objective is strictly convex and $\Delta^{N-1}$ is a compact convex polytope with non-empty interior for $N \geq 2$, Slater's constraint qualification is satisfied and the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions are both necessary and sufficient for global optimality [WEB:https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~ryantibs/convexopt-F16/scribes/kkt-scribed.pdf]. Setting $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial w_k = 0$ yields the stationarity condition:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: For a strictly convex QP over a non-empty compact feasible set, KKT conditions are necessary and sufficient under Slater's constraint qualification, which holds for the interior of the simplex when N >= 2. This result is established in standard convex optimization references, including the CMU lecture notes cited.]

$$2\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y}\right)\right]_k = \lambda + \mu_k, \quad k = 1, \dots, N.$$

Complementary slackness requires $\mu_k w_k^* = 0$ for all $k$, dual feasibility requires $\mu_k \geq 0$, and primal feasibility requires $\mathbf{w}^* \in \Delta^{N-1}$. Partitioning the index set into the passive set $\mathcal{P}^* = \{k : w_k^* > 0\}$ and the active set $\mathcal{Z}^* = \{k : w_k^* = 0\}$, complementary slackness forces $\mu_k = 0$ for every $k \in \mathcal{P}^*$, so the stationarity condition specializes to two distinct sub-conditions.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The partition into passive and active sets and the application of complementary slackness to force mu_k = 0 on the passive set is a direct algebraic consequence of the KKT conditions. The derivation is self-contained and standard.]

$$2\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y}\right)\right]_k = \lambda \quad \forall k \in \mathcal{P}^*, \qquad 2\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y}\right)\right]_k \geq \lambda \quad \forall k \in \mathcal{Z}^*.$$

This optimality structure differs fundamentally from the NNLS KKT conditions, which require gradient components to vanish for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$ and to be non-negative for $k \in \mathcal{Z}$ [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The presence of the equality constraint introduces a scalar offset $\lambda/2$ such that all passive-set gradient components are equal to a common, generally non-zero constant, rather than individually zero. This offset arises because the equality constraint removes one degree of freedom and confines the feasible set to a compact polytope; the resulting stationarity condition reflects the fact that no further descent is possible along the simplex tangent space at $\mathbf{w}^*$, a condition weaker than the absolute vanishing of each gradient component required by NNLS.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The comparison between NNLS and simplex QP KKT conditions is derived analytically from the two formulations as presented. The geometric interpretation in terms of the simplex tangent space is standard convex optimization theory and is consistent with the KB's description of the NNLS active-set mechanism in SLOT 5.]

The simplex-constrained QP admits several algorithmic treatments that extend the solvers already applicable to the NNLS and bounded-weight sub-problems. The `quadprog` solver in R accommodates the equality constraint by augmenting the constraint matrix with the unit-sum row, specified as `meq = 1` in the `solve.QP` interface alongside the $N$ non-negativity inequality rows; a small positive-definite perturbation to $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ is recommended when candidate GMPE log-spectra are nearly collinear [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The function `pnnls` in the R package `lsei` handles the unit-sum equality constraint by transforming the simplex-constrained problem into a sequence of NNLS sub-problems through Householder orthogonalization, returning exact zero values for eliminated branches and avoiding numerical precision artifacts present in general QP solvers [WEB:https://rdrr.io/cran/lsei/man/nnls.html]. The `CVXR` disciplined convex programming framework accommodates the simplex constraint by specifying `sum(w) == 1` and `w >= 0` as separate constraint objects, with DCP composition rules verifying convexity automatically and dispatching to solvers such as CLARABEL or OSQP [KB:spec.compile.research.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The descriptions of quadprog, lsei/pnnls, and CVXR are consistent with SLOT 6 and SLOT 7 of the KB and with the lsei package documentation fetched from rdrr.io. The recommendation for a ridge perturbation on near-collinear Gram matrices is explicitly stated in the KB SLOT 6. No unverified quantitative claims are made.]

## SLOT 2: Validity of Post-Hoc Normalization Versus Direct Constrained Optimization

The knowledge-base formulation yields an NNLS solution $\hat{\mathbf{w}} \in \mathbb{R}^N_+$ that satisfies non-negativity but does not enforce the unit-sum condition [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. A natural procedural question is whether the normalized vector $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}} / s$, where $s = \sum_{k=1}^N \hat{w}_k$, is identical to the solution $\mathbf{w}^*$ of the simplex-constrained QP analyzed in SLOT 1. A rigorous analysis of the optimality conditions of both problems establishes that this equivalence does not hold in general.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The claim that NNLS does not enforce the unit-sum condition is explicit in the KB (SLOT 5). The claim that the equivalence to the simplex QP solution does not hold in general is the principal analytical result of this section, derived from first principles. The high confidence reflects that the argument rests on standard optimality theory, not on approximation-dependent or empirical reasoning.]

The fundamental mechanism of non-equivalence is the scale dependence of the quadratic objective. The function $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ is a non-homogeneous quadratic form; evaluating it at the rescaled point $s\mathbf{w}$ for $s > 0$ gives:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The scale-dependence of the squared-residual objective is established by direct algebraic expansion and is a basic property of the L2 norm. No citation is required for this standard result; the derivation is self-contained.]

$$f(s\mathbf{w}) = \|s\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 = s^2\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 - 2s\,\mathbf{y}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} + \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2.$$

This expression reduces to $f(\mathbf{w})$ only when $s = 1$, confirming that the objective is not scale-invariant -- equivalently, it is not homogeneous of degree zero in $\mathbf{w}$ [WEB:https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02388v1]. Because $f$ changes value when $\mathbf{w}$ is rescaled by any factor $s \neq 1$, the minimizer $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ over $\mathbb{R}^N_+$ and the minimizer $\mathbf{w}^*$ over $\Delta^{N-1}$ are governed by qualitatively different trade-offs: the NNLS problem imposes no constraint on the total weight magnitude, allowing the optimizer to reach any scale that minimizes prediction error, while the simplex QP fixes the scale and forces the optimizer to find the best directional allocation within that scale.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The algebraic conclusion that f(sw) = f(w) only at s = 1 follows directly from the expansion above. The cited ArXiv preprint (arXiv:2501.02388) on optimization under parameter sum constraints addresses this class of scale-dependent objectives. The interpretation of scale-versus-direction trade-offs is consistent with standard constrained optimization theory.]

The incompatibility of the NNLS optimality conditions with the simplex QP conditions at the normalized point $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ can be established by direct computation. For any index $k$ in the NNLS passive set $\mathcal{P}$ (where $\hat{w}_k > 0$), the NNLS KKT stationarity condition requires [KB:spec.compile.research.md]:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The NNLS KKT stationarity condition for passive-set indices is explicitly derived in SLOT 5 of the KB and is a classical result from the Lawson-Hanson active-set theory. The source is authoritative and directly supports this claim.]

$$\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}} - \mathbf{y}\right)\right]_k = 0 \quad \Leftrightarrow \quad \left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}}\right]_k = \left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}\right]_k \quad \forall k \in \mathcal{P}.$$

Substituting $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}/s$ into the gradient of $f$ and applying the NNLS condition yields, for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The gradient computation is a direct algebraic substitution of the NNLS stationarity condition into the gradient expression evaluated at the normalized point. The derivation is self-contained.]

$$\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\tilde{\mathbf{w}} - \mathbf{y}\right)\right]_k = \frac{1}{s}\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}}\right]_k - \left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}\right]_k = \left(\frac{1}{s} - 1\right)\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}\right]_k.$$

For the simplex QP KKT conditions to hold at $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$, all passive-set gradient components must equal a common scalar $\lambda/2$ (as established in SLOT 1). This requires $\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}\right]_k$ to take an identical value for every $k \in \mathcal{P}$, meaning every positive-weight GMPE must have the same inner product with the target log-spectrum $\mathbf{y}$. Under the typical operating conditions of the GMM logic tree -- candidate GMPEs that differ in source scaling, geometric spreading, anelastic attenuation, and site-amplification parameterizations -- the cross-correlation vector $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$ has distinct components across the passive set. The normalized NNLS solution therefore violates the simplex QP optimality conditions in all but degenerate circumstances [WEB:https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02388v1][KB:spec.compile.research.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The derivation is rigorous and based on the KKT conditions established in both the KB (NNLS) and SLOT 1 of this document (simplex QP). The assertion that cross-correlation components are distinct for genuinely different GMPEs reflects the physical differences among GMPEs described in the task context. The cited ArXiv preprint supports the general principle that scale-dependent objectives require direct constraint embedding rather than post-hoc normalization.]

A geometric interpretation reinforces the analytical conclusion. The NNLS solution $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ identifies the closest point in the convex cone $\mathcal{C} = \{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{v} : \mathbf{v} \geq 0\}$ to the target vector $\mathbf{y}$ under the Euclidean metric of $\mathbb{R}^n$. The simplex QP solution $\mathbf{w}^*$ identifies the closest point in the convex hull $\mathrm{conv}\{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{\cdot 1}, \dots, \tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{\cdot N}\}$ to $\mathbf{y}$, where $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{\cdot k}$ denotes the $k$-th column of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$. Post-hoc normalization maps $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ to the simplex by radial projection from the origin in weight space; this operation does not correspond to orthogonal projection of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ onto the convex hull, and the two operations coincide only when $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ already lies in the convex hull -- which is precisely the condition $\hat{\mathbf{w}} \in \Delta^{N-1}$, i.e., $s = 1$.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The geometric interpretation in terms of projection onto a cone versus projection onto the convex hull of column vectors is standard convex geometry. The equivalence condition s = 1 follows from the definition of the simplex. No quantitative empirical claim is made; the argument is purely geometric.]

Two limiting cases admit closed-form analysis. First, when $s = \sum_k \hat{w}_k = 1$ the normalization is trivial ($\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}$), and the NNLS conditions are compatible with the simplex QP stationarity condition if and only if $\lambda = 0$; substituting $\lambda = 0$ into the simplex stationarity equation recovers the NNLS condition exactly, so the two solutions coincide. This case is of no practical significance because the value of $s$ is determined solely by the structure of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ and $\mathbf{y}$ and cannot be guaranteed to equal unity for arbitrary candidate GMPE sets. Second, when all columns of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ are collinear -- meaning all candidate GMPEs share identical log-spectral profiles up to a common positive scale factor -- the Gram matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ is rank-deficient and the NNLS solution is non-unique; every element of the solution manifold, when normalized, maps to the same point on the simplex, and that point coincides with the simplex QP solution along the degenerate direction. Neither limiting case applies to a GMM logic tree assembled from genuinely distinct candidate models.^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The two limiting cases are derived analytically and are internally consistent with the optimality conditions established above. The claim that s = 1 cannot be guaranteed for arbitrary inputs is a direct consequence of the analysis; however, no quantitative estimate of how far s typically deviates from unity in practice is provided, as no empirical data on GMPE weight sums is cited. This omission limits confidence to MEDIUM for the practical significance portion of this paragraph.]

The literature on optimization under parameter-sum constraints confirms that embedding the constraint directly into the problem formulation is the preferred approach when the objective is scale-dependent, as post-hoc renormalization of solutions obtained without the constraint does not in general preserve optimality [WEB:https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02388v1]. The correct treatment of the unit-sum condition in GMM logic-tree weight calibration therefore demands solving the simplex-constrained QP directly. The `pnnls` function in the R `lsei` package, the `solve.QP` interface with `meq = 1`, and the `CVXR` framework all provide this capability while maintaining the non-negativity requirement [WEB:https://rdrr.io/cran/lsei/man/nnls.html][KB:spec.compile.research.md]. Post-hoc normalization of the NNLS solution constitutes an approximation whose accuracy is controlled by the proximity of $s$ to unity and the degree of uniformity in $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$ across the passive set; it does not carry the formal guarantee of optimality that the directly constrained approach provides.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The conclusion is supported by both the analytical derivation in this section and the external literature cited. The recommendation to use direct constrained solvers is consistent with the KB (SLOT 7) and the lsei package documentation. The characterization of post-hoc normalization as an approximation follows rigorously from the established non-equivalence result. No quantitative claim about approximation quality is asserted without citation.]
