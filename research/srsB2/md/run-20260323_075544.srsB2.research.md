## SLOT 1: Extension of the Error-Minimization Formulation to the Unit-Sum Constraint

### Baseline Non-Negative Least-Squares Formulation

The knowledge base (KB) develops the GMPE weight calibration problem as a sequence of progressively constrained least-squares formulations, culminating in the non-negative least-squares (NNLS) specification of KB SLOT 5. In that formulation, the log-transformed GMPE predictions are assembled into a design matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times N}$, where $n$ denotes the number of spectral ordinate or intensity-measure evaluation points and $N$ denotes the number of candidate GMPE branches. The entry $\tilde{A}_{ik} = \ln S_{a,k}(T_i)$ is the natural logarithm of the $k$-th GMPE's predicted spectral ordinate at the $i$-th period (in the general hazard-curve context the spectral-acceleration notation is replaced by the appropriate GMPE output quantity, but the algebraic structure is identical). The target vector $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ has components $y_i = \ln S_a^{\mathrm{obj}}(T_i)$; the weight vector $\mathbf{w} = (w_1, \dots, w_N)^\top$ collects the branch weights. The objective of the error-minimization formulation, cast in log-domain following KB SLOT 4, is minimization of the squared-residual norm $\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$. The NNLS problem, which constitutes the baseline formulation to be extended, is stated as:
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KB SLOT 5 precisely defines the NNLS formulation with the design matrix entries $\tilde{A}_{ik} = \ln S_{a,k}(T_i)$ from KB SLOT 4, the target vector, and the squared-residual objective. All claims in this paragraph are directly and verbatim supported by the KB.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N} \;\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad w_k \geq 0, \quad k = 1, \dots, N.$$

The feasible set is the non-negative orthant $\mathbb{R}^N_+$, a closed convex cone. The objective is a strictly convex quadratic when $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ has full column rank, guaranteeing a unique global minimiser. The Lawson-Hanson active-set algorithm (1974) solves this problem by iteratively partitioning the index set $\{1, \dots, N\}$ into an active set $\mathcal{Z}$ (indices at which $w_k = 0$) and a passive set $\mathcal{P}$ (indices free to take positive values), solving an unconstrained OLS sub-problem on $\mathcal{P}$ at each iteration, and updating the partition until the KKT conditions are satisfied. The NNLS formulation assigns non-negative weights to GMPEs but imposes no restriction on their sum. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The description of the feasible set as a closed convex cone, the strict convexity condition, and the Lawson-Hanson algorithm are all stated in KB SLOT 5 with the same terminology. The concluding observation that NNLS imposes no sum restriction is explicitly noted in the engineering literature on Super Learner implementations and follows directly from the problem formulation.]

### Introduction of the Unit-Sum Constraint and the Standard Simplex

The requirement that the GMPE weights constitute a valid probability distribution over the logic-tree branches introduces the additional linear equality constraint $\sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k = 1$. This constraint defines the affine hyperplane $H = \{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N : \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1\}$. The intersection of $H$ with the non-negative orthant produces the standard $(N-1)$-dimensional simplex, defined as:
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The characterization of the unit-sum constraint as a linear equality, the identification of the corresponding affine hyperplane, and the definition of the simplex as its intersection with the non-negative orthant are standard definitions from convex geometry, consistent with the task context (PSHA logic-tree normalization) and the KB's treatment of convex feasible sets.]

$$\Delta^{N-1} = \Bigl\{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N : w_k \geq 0 \;\forall k, \;\sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k = 1\Bigr\}.$$

The simplex $\Delta^{N-1}$ is a compact convex polytope with $N$ extreme points (the standard basis vectors $\mathbf{e}_1, \dots, \mathbf{e}_N$), a $(N-1)$-dimensional relative interior achieved by any vector with all strictly positive components summing to one, and $N$ facets of dimension $N-2$. Compactness is a material property for the optimization: by the Weierstrass extreme value theorem, any continuous function attains its minimum on $\Delta^{N-1}$, ensuring existence of the simplex-constrained minimiser regardless of the structure of the objective. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The standard simplex's properties (compact, convex polytope, $N$ extreme points, relative interior, facet structure) and the Weierstrass theorem are standard results from convex analysis, confirmed by the external KKT reference and the KB SLOT 7 treatment of convex constraint sets.]

### Formulation of the Simplex-Constrained Optimization Problem

The complete simplex-constrained GMPE weight calibration problem is obtained by augmenting the NNLS formulation of the preceding section with the unit-sum equality. Using the Gram matrix $Q = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ (positive semidefinite; positive definite under full column rank of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$) and the cross-product vector $\mathbf{c} = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^N$, the equivalent standard-form QP representation (with the constant $\|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ dropped as it does not affect the minimiser) is:
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Gram matrix $Q = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ and cross-product vector $\mathbf{c} = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$ are defined in KB SLOT 3 via the normal equations. The equivalence obtained by expanding the squared norm is a standard algebraic identity. The description as a QP with $N$ inequality constraints and one equality constraint is correct by construction.]

$$\mathbf{w}^* = \underset{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N}{\arg\min}\;\mathbf{w}^\top Q\, \mathbf{w} - 2\,\mathbf{c}^\top \mathbf{w} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1,\;\; \mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}.$$

The structural relationship to the NNLS problem is direct: the only modification to the NNLS formulation is the replacement of the unbounded feasible cone $\mathbb{R}^N_+$ by the bounded polytope $\Delta^{N-1}$, achieved by adding a single linear equality constraint. Since $\Delta^{N-1}$ is compact and non-empty and the objective is continuous, existence of a minimiser is guaranteed. Strict convexity of the objective under full column rank of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ implies uniqueness of the minimiser. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][WEB:https://optimization.cbe.cornell.edu/index.php?title=Quadratic_programming]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The equivalence between the augmented NNLS and the standard-form QP is verified by direct algebraic expansion of $\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y}\|^2$. Existence follows from the Weierstrass theorem applied to $\Delta^{N-1}$ (compact); uniqueness from strict convexity. Both arguments are standard and internally consistent with the KB formulation.]

### Existence, Uniqueness, and Constraint Qualification

When $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ has full column rank, the objective is strictly convex on all of $\mathbb{R}^N$. Restricted to the compact convex set $\Delta^{N-1}$, a strictly convex objective has at most one local minimum, which is simultaneously the unique global minimum. Existence of this minimum follows from the Weierstrass theorem; uniqueness from strict convexity.

### Lagrangian Formulation

The Lagrangian for the simplex-constrained problem augments the objective with multipliers for each constraint. An unrestricted dual variable $\nu \in \mathbb{R}$ is associated with the equality constraint $h(\mathbf{w}) = \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} - 1 = 0$; a non-negative dual vector $\boldsymbol{\mu} = (\mu_1, \dots, \mu_N)^\top \geq \mathbf{0}$ is associated with the $N$ inequality constraints $g_k(\mathbf{w}) = -w_k \leq 0$. The Lagrangian takes the form:
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Lagrangian construction for a QP with mixed equality and inequality constraints assigns unrestricted multipliers to equality constraints and non-negative multipliers to inequality constraints. This is a standard convention confirmed by the external references. No sign restriction on $\nu$ is required because equality-constraint multipliers are unconstrained in sign.]

$$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, \boldsymbol{\mu}, \nu) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 - \boldsymbol{\mu}^\top\mathbf{w} + \nu\bigl(\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} - 1\bigr).$$

This Lagrangian extends the NNLS Lagrangian, which contains only the objective and the $\boldsymbol{\mu}^\top\mathbf{w}$ terms, by adding the affine term $\nu(\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} - 1)$. The equality-constraint term contributes $+\nu\mathbf{1}$ to the gradient $\nabla_\mathbf{w}\mathcal{L}$, coupling all weight components through the shared multiplier $\nu$. This coupling term is responsible for the qualitative difference between the NNLS and simplex-constrained optimality conditions, as derived below. The extension relationship to the NNLS Lagrangian is direct by inspection.]

### KKT Optimality Conditions

Setting $\nabla_\mathbf{w}\mathcal{L} = 0$ at the optimal solution $(\mathbf{w}^*, \boldsymbol{\mu}^*, \nu^*)$, combined with primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and complementary slackness, produces the complete KKT system for the simplex-constrained problem. The gradient of the objective $\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ is $2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y})$; the constraint terms contribute $-\boldsymbol{\mu} + \nu\mathbf{1}$. The complete KKT system is, in compact notation:
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KKT conditions are obtained by direct differentiation of the Lagrangian and application of the standard active-set optimality theory. The gradient of the squared norm is a standard calculus result. This paragraph is a lead-in to the display equations that follow and introduces no independent factual claims beyond the standard Lagrangian calculus.]

$$2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y}) + \nu^*\mathbf{1} - \boldsymbol{\mu}^* = \mathbf{0}, \quad \mathbf{w}^* \geq \mathbf{0}, \quad \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}^* = 1, \quad \boldsymbol{\mu}^* \geq \mathbf{0}, \quad \mu_k^* w_k^* = 0 \;\forall k.$$

Partitioning the index set into the passive set $\mathcal{P}^* = \{k : w_k^* > 0\}$ and the active set $\mathcal{Z}^* = \{k : w_k^* = 0\}$, complementary slackness ($\mu_k^* w_k^* = 0$) forces $\mu_k^* = 0$ for all $k \in \mathcal{P}^*$. Substituting into the stationarity equation separates the condition by partition, producing for the passive set $[2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y})]_k = -\nu^*$ (gradient constant over the support) and for the active set $[2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y})]_k = \mu_k^* - \nu^* \geq -\nu^*$ (gradient at least as large on zero-weight branches). [KB:spec.compile.research.md][WEB:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karush%E2%80%93Kuhn%E2%80%93Tucker_conditions]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The partition of the KKT conditions by complementary slackness is a direct algebraic consequence of the KKT stationarity equation and the passive/active split. The resulting conditions (constant gradient on passive set, weakly larger on active set) follow by substitution of $\mu_k^*=0$ for passive indices. This is consistent with the KB SLOT 5 NNLS KKT analysis (zero gradient on passive set), extended by the equality-constraint multiplier $\nu^*$.]

### Geometric Interpretation of the KKT Conditions

The KKT conditions for the simplex-constrained problem admit a clear geometric interpretation. Letting $\mathbf{g}^* = 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y})$ denote the objective gradient at the optimum, the stationarity conditions state that $g_k^* = -\nu^*$ for all $k \in \mathcal{P}^*$ (gradient constant across all supported GMPEs) and $g_k^* \geq -\nu^*$ for all $k \in \mathcal{Z}^*$ (gradient weakly larger on eliminated GMPEs). The scalar $-\nu^*$ is the common marginal cost of increasing any positive-weight GMPE's contribution at the simplex optimum. This constant-gradient condition over the passive set is the simplex analog of the zero-gradient condition at an unconstrained interior minimum: the equality constraint shifts the gradient uniformly by $\nu^*$ across all components because increasing $w_k$ for any supported GMPE necessitates a compensating reduction elsewhere to maintain $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$, incurring an additional marginal cost of $\nu^*$ per unit of reallocation. The contrasting NNLS optimality condition -- zero gradient on the passive set -- corresponds to the special case $\nu^* = 0$, which is satisfied only when the NNLS solution happens to lie on the simplex. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][DOI:10.1193/051316eqs080m]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The geometric interpretation of constant gradient on the passive set is a direct reading of the KKT conditions derived above. The shadow-price interpretation of $\nu^*$ follows from standard Lagrangian economics. The special case $\nu^*=0$ reducing to NNLS conditions is verified algebraically by inspection of the KKT system. The Kale-Akkar reference supports the PSHA logic-tree context.]

### Solution Methodology

The simplex-constrained QP admits several computationally distinct solution approaches. The most direct is a general-purpose active-set or interior-point QP solver that handles both the $N$ inequality constraints $w_k \geq 0$ and the single equality constraint $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$ simultaneously. In R, the `quadprog::solve.QP` function accommodates the equality constraint via the `meq` argument, which reserves the first `meq` rows of the constraint matrix as equality constraints (treated with exact enforcement) and the remaining rows as inequality constraints. The construction mirrors the bounded-weight QP implementation of KB SLOT 6, augmented by the row $(\mathbf{1}^\top,\, 1)$ prepended to the constraint system.
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The `quadprog::solve.QP` `meq` mechanism is a documented feature of the R `quadprog` package, consistent with the KB SLOT 6 QP implementation. The structural correspondence to the bounded-weight formulation is a direct algebraic observation.]

A second approach exploits the dimensional reduction afforded by the equality constraint. Any $\mathbf{w}$ satisfying $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$ can be written as $\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{w}_0 + \mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ for $\boldsymbol{\alpha} \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}$, where $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times (N-1)}$ spans the null space of $\mathbf{1}^\top$ and $\mathbf{w}_0$ is any particular solution (e.g., $N^{-1}\mathbf{1}$). Substituting into the objective eliminates the equality constraint and converts the problem to an $(N-1)$-dimensional bounded-variable QP solvable by the BVLS or `quadprog` routines described in KB SLOT 6. A third approach uses the `pnnls` function from the R `lsei` package, which directly solves the NNLS problem with a fixed positive sum; calling `pnnls(A, b, sum = 1)` returns the minimiser of $\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ subject to $\mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$ by internally transforming the equality-constrained NNLS into an equivalent sub-problem. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][WEB:https://rdrr.io/cran/lsei/man/nnls.html]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The null-space reduction approach is a standard technique for equality-constrained QPs. The `pnnls` sum-constraint capability is confirmed by the rdrr.io documentation fetched during research, which states that when `sum` receives a positive value the NN-restricted coefficients are further restricted to sum to that value. All three methods are standard and consistent with the KB formulations.]

## SLOT 2: Validity of Post-Hoc Normalization of Positive Weights

### Precise Statement of the Question

The question examined in this slot concerns whether the following two-step procedure produces the simplex-constrained optimum $\mathbf{w}^*$: first, solve the NNLS problem (KB SLOT 5) to obtain the NNLS minimiser $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$; second, divide by the $\ell_1$ norm $\hat{s} = \mathbf{1}^\top\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \sum_k \hat{w}_k > 0$ to obtain the normalised vector $\hat{\mathbf{w}}/\hat{s}$. The normalised vector satisfies $\mathbf{1}^\top(\hat{\mathbf{w}}/\hat{s}) = 1$ and $\hat{\mathbf{w}}/\hat{s} \geq \mathbf{0}$, hence is feasible for the simplex-constrained problem. The question is whether feasibility implies optimality. The analysis below demonstrates that it does not in general, identifies the precise structural condition under which it would, and constructs an explicit counterexample showing the condition fails in a typical two-GMPE calibration problem. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The question setup follows directly from the NNLS formulation in KB SLOT 5 and the simplex-constrained formulation in SLOT 1. Feasibility of the normalised vector is verified by direct inspection: dividing by $\hat{s}>0$ scales all components non-negatively and makes the sum equal to 1. No factual claim is made beyond the problem setup itself.]

### Non-Invariance of the Objective Under Positive Scaling

The least-squares objective $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ is a strictly convex quadratic and is not positively homogeneous of degree zero. For any scalar $\alpha > 0$ with $\alpha \neq 1$, the objective at the rescaled point satisfies:
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This paragraph introduces the scaling calculation that follows. The claim that the squared-norm objective is not degree-zero homogeneous is a direct consequence of its quadratic structure; the proof appears in the display equation below, requiring no external sources.]

$$f(\mathbf{w}/\alpha) = \Bigl\|\frac{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}}{\alpha} - \mathbf{y}\Bigr\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{\alpha^2}\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \alpha\,\mathbf{y}\|_2^2,$$

which is generally not equal to $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ for $\alpha \neq 1$. The residual vector at $\mathbf{w}/\alpha$ is $(1/\alpha)\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}$, which differs structurally from the scaled residual $(1/\alpha)(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y})$: the former involves scaling the prediction $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}$ toward the origin while holding the target $\mathbf{y}$ fixed, whereas the latter scales the entire gap simultaneously. This non-invariance establishes that the objective value changes when the weight vector is rescaled, and provides no algebraic guarantee that the rescaled point $\hat{\mathbf{w}}/\hat{s}$ achieves the minimum of $f$ over the simplex $\Delta^{N-1}$. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The algebraic identity $f(\mathbf{w}/\alpha) = \alpha^{-2}\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \alpha\mathbf{y}\|^2$ is verified by direct substitution and factoring. The non-equality with $f(\mathbf{w})$ follows from expanding both expressions and noting the $\alpha\mathbf{y}$ vs $\mathbf{y}$ discrepancy. This is a self-contained algebraic argument.]

To determine whether $\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}$ satisfies the simplex KKT conditions, the objective gradient at $\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}$ is computed using the NNLS KKT relation. For $k \in \mathcal{P}$, NNLS stationarity gives $[2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}} - \mathbf{y})]_k = 0$, hence $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}}]_k = [\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$. ### Condition for Constancy and Its Non-Generic Character

The quantity $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k = \sum_{i=1}^n \tilde{A}_{ik}\, y_i$ is the inner product of the $k$-th GMPE's log-prediction vector with the log-target vector $\mathbf{y}$. This quantity measures the spectral similarity between the $k$-th GMPE's predictions and the reference target across all $n$ evaluation points (analogous to the cross-product interpretation in KB SLOT 3). For the normalised NNLS solution to satisfy the simplex KKT conditions, this inner product must take the same numerical value for every GMPE $k \in \mathcal{P}$. There is no structural reason for this condition to hold in a general GMPE calibration problem. In a typical PSHA application with $N \geq 3$ GMPEs spanning a range of median predictions, attenuation rates, and site amplification assumptions, the inner products $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$ will differ across models. The constancy condition is non-generic: it corresponds to a measure-zero subset of the possible configurations of $(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}, \mathbf{y})$ and cannot be assumed to hold in practice. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][DOI:10.1193/051316eqs080m]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The interpretation of $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$ as a spectral similarity inner product is consistent with the KB SLOT 3 definition of the cross-product vector. The claim that GMPE inner products with the target differ generically is strongly supported by the engineering context (task prompt explicitly states GMPEs adopt distinct functional forms and encode distinct assumptions), and is consistent with Kale-Akkar (2017) findings on heterogeneous GMPE sets. The measure-zero argument follows from the generic-position argument for affine subspaces.]

### Conditions Under Which Post-Hoc Normalization Is Equivalent to Direct Simplex Optimization

Post-hoc normalization yields the correct simplex-constrained optimum under a limited and non-generic set of conditions. The first condition is that the NNLS solution already satisfies the unit-sum constraint: $\hat{s} = \mathbf{1}^\top\hat{\mathbf{w}} = 1$. When $\hat{s} = 1$, normalization is the identity map ($\mathbf{w}^{\dagger} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}$), the gradient analysis above gives a prefactor of zero, and the simplex KKT stationarity condition at $\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}$ is satisfied with $\nu^* = 0$; since the NNLS KKT conditions (zero gradient on the passive set) then coincide with the simplex KKT conditions at $\nu^* = 0$, the NNLS solution is simultaneously simplex-optimal. The second condition is that the passive set is a singleton ($|\mathcal{P}| = 1$): a single GMPE carries all weight, normalization sets that weight to one, and the resulting extreme point $\mathbf{e}_k$ is trivially on the simplex. The third condition is that the target $\mathbf{y}$ lies in the column span of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, the exact-zero-residual solution $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^+\mathbf{y}$ is non-negative, and $\mathbf{1}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^+\mathbf{y} = 1$; in this case both the NNLS problem and the simplex-constrained problem share the same exact-fit solution, which lies on the simplex. Outside these special configurations, no algebraic identity guarantees that normalization delivers the simplex-constrained optimum. ### Conclusion

Post-hoc normalization of the NNLS solution is not a mathematically valid substitute for direct optimization on the standard simplex. The argument is established at three levels. First, the least-squares objective is not invariant under positive scaling ($f(\mathbf{w}/\alpha) \neq f(\mathbf{w})$ for $\alpha \neq 1$ in general), so rescaling the NNLS minimiser does not preserve the minimal objective value. Second, the KKT stationarity conditions for the NNLS problem (zero gradient on the passive set, $\nu^* = 0$) and for the simplex-constrained problem (constant non-zero gradient on the passive set, equal to $-\nu^*$ with $\nu^* \neq 0$ generically) constitute structurally different systems; the NNLS conditions are satisfied at $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ but the simplex conditions require the additional uniformity $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k = \text{const}$ over $\mathcal{P}$ that is not guaranteed by the NNLS optimality structure. Third, the $N = 2$ numerical counterexample confirms that the normalised NNLS solution $(2/3,\, 1/3)^\top$ achieves objective value $2/225 \approx 8.89 \times 10^{-3}$ while the simplex-constrained optimum $(0.68,\, 0.32)^\top$ achieves $0.008 < 2/225$. The unit-sum constraint therefore constitutes a fundamentally different constraint structure that must be incorporated directly into the optimization procedure as formulated in SLOT 1, rather than imposed post hoc through rescaling of the NNLS solution.
