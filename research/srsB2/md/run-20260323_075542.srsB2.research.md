# Epistemic Logic-Tree Weight Calibration with Unit-Sum Constraint

## SLOT 1: Extension of the Error-Minimization Formulation to the Unit-Sum Constraint

The error-minimization formulation developed in the knowledge base casts GMPE weight
calibration as a non-negative least-squares (NNLS) problem in log-spectral space.
The log-space design matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times N}$ has
entries $\tilde{A}_{ik} = \ln\hat{\mu}_k(s_i)$, where $\hat{\mu}_k(s_i)$ is the
median prediction of the $k$-th GMPE at calibration scenario $s_i$
($i = 1, \ldots, n$; $k = 1, \ldots, N$). The target vector
$\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ collects the corresponding log-observed ground-motion
values. In the spectral-matching context of the KB the same matrix arises with rows
indexed by period ordinates and columns by candidate records; the algebraic structure
is identical and carries over without modification to the PSHA GMPE-weighting context.
The KB NNLS problem is [KB:spec.compile.research.md]:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The notation and design-matrix construction are reproduced directly from SLOT 5 of spec.compile.research.md, where the identical formulation is presented for the spectral-matching context. The adaptation from K candidate records to N GMPEs does not alter the algebraic structure. The log-space target vector convention is consistent with SLOT 4 of spec.compile.research.md.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N} \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2
\quad \text{subject to} \quad w_k \geq 0, \quad k = 1, \ldots, N.$$

The unit-sum constraint $\sum_{k=1}^N w_k = 1$, equivalently
$\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$ where $\mathbf{1} = (1, \ldots, 1)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^N$,
restricts the feasible region from the non-negative orthant $\mathbb{R}^N_+$ to the
standard $(N-1)$-dimensional simplex, defined as the compact convex set:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The identification of the unit-sum constraint as restricting the non-negative orthant to the standard simplex is a classical result in convex analysis. Compactness follows from the simplex being closed and bounded in finite dimensions. No external citation is required for this standard set-theoretic characterization.]

$$\Delta^{N-1} = \{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N : \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1,\;
w_k \geq 0,\; k = 1, \ldots, N\}.$$

The simplex is the intersection of the affine hyperplane
$\{\mathbf{w} : \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1\}$ with the non-negative orthant; it is
therefore a proper closed convex subset of $\mathbb{R}^N_+$, and in particular a
compact convex polytope with $N$ vertices $\mathbf{e}_k$ (the standard basis vectors).
Appending the equality constraint to the KB NNLS formulation yields the extended
calibration problem:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The geometric description of the simplex as the intersection of the unit-sum hyperplane with the non-negative orthant is standard convex geometry. Listing the N vertices as standard basis vectors is a well-known property of the standard simplex. The step of appending the equality constraint to the KB formulation follows directly from the problem statement.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N} \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2
\quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1,\quad
w_k \geq 0, \quad k = 1, \ldots, N. \tag{P}$$

Dropping the equality constraint from (P) recovers the KB NNLS problem exactly, so (P)
constitutes a proper restriction of the NNLS problem to the compact subset
$\Delta^{N-1}$ of $\mathbb{R}^N_+$ [KB:spec.compile.research.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The characterization of (P) as the KB NNLS problem restricted to the simplex follows immediately by comparing the feasible sets. Because $\Delta^{N-1} \subset \mathbb{R}^N_+$, any feasible point for (P) is also feasible for the NNLS problem, making (P) a restriction. This is stated consistently with the KB formulation in SLOT 5 of spec.compile.research.md.]

Expanding the squared-norm objective of (P) and dropping the additive constant
$\|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ (which is independent of $\mathbf{w}$ and does not affect the
minimizer) yields the equivalent quadratic form
$\mathbf{w}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\mathbf{w} - 2(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y})^\top\mathbf{w}$.
Defining the Gram matrix $\mathbf{G} = 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$
(positive semidefinite; positive definite when $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ has full column rank)
and the cross-correlation vector $\mathbf{g} = 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^N$,
problem (P) is equivalent to the linearly constrained convex quadratic program (QP)
with one equality constraint and $N$ inequality constraints. The $(\ell, k)$ entry of
$\mathbf{G}$ is $2\sum_{i=1}^n \tilde{A}_{i\ell}\tilde{A}_{ik}$, measuring twice
the inner product of the log-prediction vectors of GMPEs $\ell$ and $k$ over all
calibration scenarios -- the same Gram-matrix structure described in SLOT 3 of the KB
for the spectral-matching context [KB:spec.compile.research.md]:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The algebraic expansion of the squared norm and the definition of G and g are standard results in quadratic programming. The structure of the Gram matrix entries is explicitly stated in SLOT 3 of spec.compile.research.md for the identical algebraic construction. Equivalence between (P) and (QP) follows from dropping the additive constant.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w}} \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{G}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{g}^\top\mathbf{w}
\quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1,
\quad \mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}. \tag{QP}$$

Since (QP) is a convex program with linear equality and inequality constraints and a
differentiable objective, the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions are both necessary
and sufficient for global optimality. Slater's condition is satisfied because the
interior of $\Delta^{N-1}$ is nonempty for $N \geq 2$: any strictly positive weight
vector summing to one constitutes a Slater point. The Lagrangian of (QP) is
$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, \lambda, \boldsymbol{\mu}) = \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{G}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{g}^\top\mathbf{w} + \lambda(\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} - 1) - \boldsymbol{\mu}^\top\mathbf{w}$,
where $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ is the Lagrange multiplier for the equality constraint
and $\boldsymbol{\mu} = (\mu_1, \ldots, \mu_N)^\top \geq \mathbf{0}$ is the vector
of KKT multipliers for the non-negativity inequalities. Setting the gradient of
$\mathcal{L}$ with respect to $\mathbf{w}$ to zero and substituting the definitions
of $\mathbf{G}$ and $\mathbf{g}$ gives the component-wise stationarity condition for
the optimal triple $(\mathbf{w}^*, \lambda^*, \boldsymbol{\mu}^*)$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The sufficiency of KKT conditions for convex programs satisfying Slater's condition is a standard result in convex optimization (Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004). The Slater point argument is correct: any w >> 0 with sum 1 lies in the interior of the simplex. The inline Lagrangian formula is derived by direct construction from (QP). The component-wise form is obtained by substituting G=2A^T A and g=2A^T y into the vector stationarity condition.]

$$2[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y})]_k
+ \lambda^* = \mu_k^*, \quad k = 1, \ldots, N.$$

Together with the primal feasibility conditions $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}^* = 1$,
$\mathbf{w}^* \geq \mathbf{0}$, dual feasibility $\boldsymbol{\mu}^* \geq \mathbf{0}$,
and complementary slackness $\mu_k^* w_k^* = 0$ for all $k$, the stationarity
condition characterizes the unique global minimizer. Partitioning the index set
into the passive set $\mathcal{P} = \{k : w_k^* > 0\}$ (where complementary
slackness forces $\mu_k^* = 0$) and the active set $\mathcal{Z} = \{k : w_k^* = 0\}$,
the stationarity condition specializes to:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The four KKT conditions and the complementary slackness argument are standard results. The partitioning into passive and active sets and the consequent simplification of stationarity are consistent with the active-set interpretation described for the NNLS case in SLOT 5 of spec.compile.research.md [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The derivation is algebraically straightforward.]

$$2[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y})]_k
= -\lambda^* \quad \text{for all } k \in \mathcal{P},$$

$$2[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y})]_k
\geq -\lambda^* \quad \text{for all } k \in \mathcal{Z}.$$

The first condition states that the component of the residual gradient corresponding
to every positively weighted GMPE takes a common value $-\lambda^*$; the equality
multiplier $\lambda^*$ is the shadow price of the unit-sum constraint, quantifying the
marginal rate at which the minimum RSS would decrease if the right-hand side of the
equality constraint were relaxed by $\epsilon > 0$. The second condition is the
simplex-analogue of the NNLS optimality condition for excluded models: the gradient
component for every zero-weight GMPE must be no smaller than $-\lambda^*$, ensuring
that no marginal reallocation of weight toward any excluded model can reduce the
objective. Comparing with the NNLS KKT conditions of the KB -- where the
passive-set gradient is required to vanish exactly (threshold zero) and no equality
multiplier exists -- the unit-sum constraint introduces precisely one structural
modification: it replaces the zero threshold with the non-zero shadow price $-\lambda^*$,
shifting the stationarity level uniformly across all positively weighted GMPEs
[KB:spec.compile.research.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The shadow-price interpretation of the Lagrange multiplier is a standard result in constrained optimization. The contrast with the NNLS KKT conditions (zero threshold for the passive set, no equality multiplier) accurately reflects SLOT 5 of spec.compile.research.md. Both sets of conditions are derived from the same objective function with differing feasible sets, and the structural comparison is algebraically correct.]

Existence of a global minimizer of (QP) is guaranteed by the Weierstrass
extreme-value theorem applied to the continuous objective over the compact simplex
$\Delta^{N-1}$. Uniqueness holds whenever $\mathbf{G}$ is positive definite, which
requires $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ to have full column rank
($\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}) = N$, necessitating $n \geq N$ and the $N$ GMPE
log-prediction vectors to be linearly independent over the calibration scenarios);
under positive definiteness the objective is strictly convex on the convex simplex,
so any local minimizer is the unique global minimizer. Three primary algorithms are
available for (QP). The active-set method of Lawson and Hanson (1974), which forms
the basis of the NNLS solver described in the KB, is extended by augmenting each
inner-loop subproblem with the equality constraint $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$;
the passive-set unconstrained subproblem becomes an equality-constrained least-squares
problem solvable via a rank-one QR update. Interior-point methods introduce a
logarithmic barrier $-\rho\sum_k \ln(w_k)$ for the non-negativity constraints and
treat the equality constraint directly in the Newton system, reducing (QP) to a
sequence of Newton subproblems at decreasing barrier parameter $\rho > 0$. Projected-
gradient and Frank-Wolfe methods iterate gradient steps followed by projection onto
$\Delta^{N-1}$; the simplex projection is computed in $O(N \log N)$ time via a
sorting-based algorithm. All three families converge to the same unique global
solution under the full-column-rank condition. In the CVXR framework described in
SLOT 7 of the KB, problem (QP) is implemented by adding the equality constraint
`sum(w) == 1` alongside `w >= 0` in the constraint list, with the solver dispatching
to an interior-point or active-set QP solver automatically
[KB:spec.compile.research.md][WEB:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative_least_squares].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Existence via the Weierstrass theorem on a compact set is classical. The uniqueness argument under positive definiteness of G is standard for strictly convex QP. The Lawson-Hanson active-set method and its generalizability are confirmed in SLOT 5 of spec.compile.research.md and consistent with the web sources on NNLS [WEB:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative_least_squares]. Interior-point and projected-gradient methods for simplex-constrained QP are well-established algorithmic results. The CVXR reference is grounded in SLOT 7 of spec.compile.research.md.]

## SLOT 2: Validity of Post-Hoc Normalization of Positive Weights

A natural computational shortcut is to solve the KB NNLS formulation (without the
unit-sum constraint) to obtain $\mathbf{w}^*_+ \in \mathbb{R}^N_+$, and then to
normalize: $\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \mathbf{w}^*_+ / (\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}^*_+)$,
provided $s \equiv \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}^*_+ > 0$. The resulting vector
$\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ is primal feasible for (QP) by construction -- it satisfies
$\hat{w}_k \geq 0$ and $\mathbf{1}^\top\hat{\mathbf{w}} = 1$ -- and it preserves
the sparsity pattern of $\mathbf{w}^*_+$, since both vectors share the same passive
set $\mathcal{P}$. The critical question is whether $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ is also the
minimizer $\mathbf{w}^*_\Delta$ of the simplex-constrained problem (QP), i.e., whether
post-hoc normalization preserves RSS optimality over $\Delta^{N-1}$
[KB:spec.compile.research.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The description of the normalization procedure is elementary and algebraically correct. The claim that the normalized vector satisfies non-negativity and the unit-sum constraint follows immediately from dividing a non-negative vector by its positive element sum. The preservation of the sparsity pattern (same zero-weight indices) is equally immediate. The question of dual feasibility (whether the normalized point is the actual minimizer) is correctly identified as the substantive issue.]

The fundamental obstruction to the validity of post-hoc normalization is the
non-homogeneity of the RSS objective $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$.
For any scalar $s > 0$, expanding the squared norm gives:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The claim of non-homogeneity of f is the key analytical observation for this slot. It is a direct consequence of the affine structure of the residual Aw - y: because y is a non-zero constant, scaling w by s scales Aw but not y, making f(sw) differ from f(w) for s not equal to 1. The derivation is purely algebraic and requires no external citation.]

$$f(s\mathbf{w}) = s^2\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}\|_2^2
- 2s(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w})^\top\mathbf{y} + \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2,$$

which equals $f(\mathbf{w})$ only when $s = 1$ or in the degenerate zero-prediction case
$\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{0}$ (which yields $f = \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2$, the
worst achievable fit). Because $f$ is not invariant under positive rescaling, there is
no algebraic mechanism that forces $\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \mathbf{w}^*_+/s$ to minimize
$f$ over the simplex; the minimizers of $f$ on $\mathbb{R}^N_+$ and on $\Delta^{N-1}$
are generically distinct points.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The expansion f(sw) = s^2||Aw||^2 - 2s(Aw)^T y + ||y||^2 is an exact algebraic identity obtained by direct substitution. The conclusion that f(sw) = f(w) requires s=1 (given y non-zero and Aw non-zero, the generic case in PSHA calibration) follows by comparing coefficients. The inference that distinct minimizers exist on the orthant and the simplex is a direct consequence.]

A rigorous demonstration proceeds through the KKT analysis. The NNLS optimality
conditions from the KB (SLOT 5 of spec.compile.research.md) require, for every index
$k$ in the passive set $\mathcal{P}$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Appealing to the NNLS KKT conditions from spec.compile.research.md SLOT 5 as the starting point for the derivation is correct and appropriate. These conditions are stated explicitly in the KB for the identical NNLS problem structure.]

$$[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^*_+ - \mathbf{y})]_k = 0,
\quad k \in \mathcal{P},$$

equivalently $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^*_+]_k = [\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$
for $k \in \mathcal{P}$. Using this substitution, the gradient of $f$ evaluated at
the normalized point $\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \mathbf{w}^*_+/s$ is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The algebraic substitution from the NNLS KKT condition ([A^T A w*_+]_k = [A^T y]_k for k in the passive set) into the gradient expression at w*/s is elementary. The derivation is internally consistent and grounded in the KB's NNLS conditions [KB:spec.compile.research.md].]

$$[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}} - \mathbf{y})]_k
= \frac{1}{s}[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^*_+]_k
- [\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k
= \frac{1}{s}[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k
- [\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k
= \left(\frac{1}{s} - 1\right)[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k, \quad k \in \mathcal{P}.$$

For $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ to satisfy the QP stationarity condition (which requires this
gradient to take a common value $-\lambda^*/2$ for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$), it is
necessary that $(1/s - 1)[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$ be constant over
$\mathcal{P}$. When $s \neq 1$, the factor $(1/s-1)$ is nonzero, and the requirement
reduces to the condition that $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$ takes a common
value $c$ for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$. This is the equicorrelation condition: every
positively weighted GMPE's log-prediction vector must have the same inner product with
the calibration target $\mathbf{y}$. The equicorrelation condition is not implied by
the NNLS optimality conditions (which only require $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^*_+ - \mathbf{y})]_k = 0$,
leaving $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$ unconstrained within the passive set)
and is not generically satisfied by arbitrary problem data $(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}, \mathbf{y})$
[KB:spec.compile.research.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The gradient at w*/s is computed correctly by substituting the NNLS KKT condition into the gradient expression; each algebraic step is elementary. The equicorrelation condition is correctly identified as the necessary condition for QP stationarity at the normalized point. The statement that NNLS optimality conditions leave [A^T y]_k unconstrained within the passive set is confirmed by inspection of the NNLS KKT system in SLOT 5 of spec.compile.research.md, which imposes [A^T A w*]_k = [A^T y]_k but places no requirement on the value of [A^T y]_k itself.]

Post-hoc normalization yields the simplex-constrained optimizer if and only if one of
two conditions holds. The first is the trivial case $s = 1$: when the NNLS solution
already satisfies the unit-sum constraint, $\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \mathbf{w}^*_+$ and no
adjustment is made. The second is the equicorrelation condition:
$[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k = c$ for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$, for some
constant $c$; in this case the required equality multiplier is
$\lambda^* = 2(1 - 1/s)c$ and the dual feasibility condition for the active set,
$[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}} - \mathbf{y})]_k \geq -\lambda^*/2$
for $k \in \mathcal{Z}$, must additionally be checked. The equicorrelation condition
imposes $|\mathcal{P}| - 1$ independent algebraic equations on the pair
$(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}, \mathbf{y})$ that are not enforced by the NNLS problem
formulation; it therefore defines a set of measure zero in the space of problem
instances and fails for generic calibration data. Outside these two cases, $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$
is a primal-feasible but suboptimal point on the simplex and achieves a strictly
higher RSS value than the true minimizer $\mathbf{w}^*_\Delta$
[WEB:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative_least_squares].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The two equivalence conditions (s=1 and equicorrelation) are derived correctly and exhaustively from the KKT analysis. The measure-zero characterization follows from the counting argument: the equicorrelation condition imposes |P|-1 independent polynomial equations on a matrix with nN + n real entries, constraining a set of zero Lebesgue measure. The claim that the normalized solution achieves strictly higher RSS than the true simplex optimizer follows from the definition of the latter as the unique minimizer of f on the compact simplex, combined with the demonstrated failure of the KKT conditions at the normalized point in the generic case.]

In the PSHA logic-tree calibration context, the equicorrelation condition is unlikely
to hold and the sum $s = \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}^*_+$ is not constrained to equal
one. When calibration data are sparse, GMPE predictions are near-collinear (a
frequent occurrence when multiple contemporary models share similar functional forms
and database coverage), or the evidence strongly favors a small subset of models, the
Lawson-Hanson active-set method concentrates positive weight on few GMPEs; under
these conditions $s$ can deviate substantially from one, and normalization introduces
a systematic distortion of the RSS geometry. Scherbaum and Kuehn (2011) argue that
logic-tree branch weights must be treated as probabilities in the axiomatic sense --
satisfying non-negativity and summing to one -- from the outset of the calibration
process rather than as the result of post-processing; their argument reinforces the
conclusion that optimization must be performed directly on $\Delta^{N-1}$
[WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1193/1.3652744]. The general convex
framework of SLOT 7 of the KB implements this requirement by appending the constraint
`sum(w) == 1` as an equality to the CVXR problem formulation, and the R `quadprog`
solver provides an equivalent route by including $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$ as a
direct equality constraint; both solve problem (QP) exactly without any post-hoc
adjustment [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The mathematical conclusion is unambiguous:
post-hoc normalization of the NNLS solution is valid only in the degenerate cases
identified above, and the unit-sum constraint requires a fundamentally different
optimization treatment -- direct minimization of the RSS objective over the standard
simplex $\Delta^{N-1}$ via the quadratic program (QP).^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The practical observation that s can deviate from one under sparsity and collinearity is consistent with the NNLS active-set behavior described in SLOT 5 of spec.compile.research.md. The Scherbaum-Kuehn (2011) reference is confirmed by the web search results identifying the paper in Earthquake Spectra 27:1237-1251 [WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1193/1.3652744]. The CVXR and quadprog references are grounded in SLOT 7 of spec.compile.research.md [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The concluding statement summarizes the mathematical analysis consistently with all preceding derivations.]
