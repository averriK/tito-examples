# Epistemic Logic-Tree Weight Calibration Under Unit-Sum Constraint

## SLOT 1: Extension of the Error-Minimization Formulation to the Unit-Sum Constraint

The weight-calibration problem documented in the knowledge base is formulated as a non-negative least-squares (NNLS) regression, in which the weight vector $\mathbf{w} = (w_1, \dots, w_K)^\top$ minimises the squared spectral residual $\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ over the non-negative orthant $\mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}$ [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The normalization condition $\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k = 1$ converts this into a constrained quadratic program on the unit simplex $\Delta^{K-1} = \{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K : \mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0},\, \mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{w} = 1\}$, which is a closed, bounded, convex subset of $\mathbb{R}^K$.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The feasibility set characterization follows directly from the definitions; KB document confirms the NNLS base formulation and the unit-sum constraint is introduced in the task file context. Both sources are consistent.]

The extended problem takes the form shown below.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This is a transitional statement introducing the formal problem definition; it contains no independent factual claim beyond what is established in the preceding paragraph and confirmed by the KB formulation.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K} \;\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{w} = 1, \quad w_k \geq 0, \quad k = 1, \dots, K,$$

where $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times K}$ is the log-spectral design matrix with entries $\tilde{A}_{ik} = \ln S_{a,k}(T_i)$, $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the log-domain target vector with $y_i = \ln S_a^{\mathrm{obj}}(T_i)$, and $n$ is the number of spectral period ordinates [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The equality constraint reduces the effective dimension of the feasible region from $K$ to $K-1$: the simplex $\Delta^{K-1}$ is a convex polytope whose vertices are the standard basis vectors $\mathbf{e}_k$ in $\mathbb{R}^K$, with each vertex corresponding to a degenerate solution that assigns all weight to a single candidate GMPE.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The geometric characterization of the unit simplex is standard in convex analysis; the connection to GMPE weighting follows from the KB's definition of the design matrix and the task's CONTEXT section.]

The objective $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ is a strictly convex quadratic with Hessian $H = 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, which is positive definite when $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ has full column rank -- a condition that holds when the $K$ log-spectral vectors are linearly independent over the period mesh [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. Because the objective is strictly convex and the feasible set $\Delta^{K-1}$ is non-empty, compact, and convex, the extended problem admits a unique global minimiser $\mathbf{w}^{**}$ [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/equality.pdf]. No regularisation is needed to guarantee uniqueness, in contrast to the underdetermined regime of the unconstrained problem.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Existence and uniqueness of the minimiser on a compact convex set for a strictly convex objective follows from standard results in convex analysis (Weierstrass theorem plus strict convexity). The KB confirms positive definiteness of the Gram matrix under the full-rank assumption.]

The first-order optimality conditions (Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions) for the extended problem are as follows. Let $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$ be the Lagrange multiplier associated with the equality constraint $\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{w} = 1$, and let $\lambda_k \geq 0$ be the dual variable associated with the inequality constraint $-w_k \leq 0$ for each $k$. At the optimum $\mathbf{w}^{**}$, the following conditions hold simultaneously: stationarity, $2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^{**} - \mathbf{y}) = \mu \mathbf{1} + \boldsymbol{\lambda}$; primal feasibility, $\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{w}^{**} = 1$ and $w_k^{**} \geq 0$; dual feasibility, $\lambda_k \geq 0$; and complementary slackness, $\lambda_k w_k^{**} = 0$ for all $k$ [WEB:https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~ryantibs/convexopt-F16/scribes/kkt-scribed.pdf]. Because the feasible set satisfies a linear constraint qualification (the equality and inequality constraints are all linear, so Slater's condition holds at any interior point such as $w_k = 1/K$ for all $k$), the KKT conditions are both necessary and sufficient for global optimality [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/equality.pdf].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KKT conditions for a strictly convex QP with linear equality and inequality constraints are standard results; the sufficiency under Slater's condition is confirmed by Boyd-Vandenberghe and the CMU convex optimization lecture notes cited.]

The stationarity condition takes the following component-wise form.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This is a transitional phrase introducing the derived component-wise KKT stationarity equation; the equation itself follows deterministically from the gradient of the Lagrangian and is verified by the KKT conditions stated in the preceding paragraph.]

$$2\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\right)_{k\cdot}\mathbf{w}^{**} - 2\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}\right)_k = \mu + \lambda_k, \quad k = 1, \dots, K,$$

with $\lambda_k = 0$ when $w_k^{**} > 0$ (the passive set $\mathcal{P}$) and $\lambda_k \geq 0$ when $w_k^{**} = 0$ (the active set $\mathcal{Z}$). The scalar $\mu$ enforces the equality constraint and plays the role of a shared threshold across all components: a candidate GMPE $k$ receives positive weight if and only if the gradient component $2[(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\mathbf{w}^{**}]_k - 2(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y})_k$ equals $\mu$; otherwise the component is driven to zero by the active-set complementary slackness condition [WEB:https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~ryantibs/convexopt-F16/scribes/kkt-scribed.pdf].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The component-wise KKT interpretation for the simplex-constrained QP is a standard derivation; the active/passive set classification follows directly from complementary slackness. Consistent with the KB's description of the Lawson-Hanson active-set framework for the NNLS sub-problem.]

The unit-sum constraint fundamentally changes the structure of the optimisation problem relative to the pure NNLS formulation. In the unconstrained NNLS problem, the Lawson-Hanson active-set algorithm partitions the index set into $\mathcal{Z}$ (where $w_k = 0$) and $\mathcal{P}$ (where $w_k > 0$), solves the unconstrained least-squares sub-problem on $\mathcal{P}$, and projects negative intermediate solutions back to the boundary [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The addition of the equality constraint requires that the sub-problem solved on $\mathcal{P}$ at each iteration also satisfy $\sum_{k \in \mathcal{P}} w_k = 1$: this reduces the unconstrained sub-problem on $\mathcal{P}$ to an equality-constrained least-squares problem that must be solved via a range-space or null-space method rather than plain QR or SVD [DOI:10.1007/BF01584232]. Specifically, if $|\mathcal{P}| = p$, the sub-problem on $\mathcal{P}$ is to minimise $\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathcal{P}}\mathbf{w}_\mathcal{P} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ subject to $\mathbf{1}_p^\top\mathbf{w}_\mathcal{P} = 1$, which can be solved by eliminating one variable via $w_{k^*} = 1 - \sum_{k \in \mathcal{P} \setminus \{k^*\}} w_k$ or by applying an equality-constrained QR factorisation.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The algorithmic extension follows directly from the mathematical structure; the Springer DOI reference (Dax 1980) treats this exact class of problems (LS with equality and nonnegativity). The KB confirms the Lawson-Hanson base framework to which the equality constraint is appended.]

The bounded-variable least-squares (BVLS) and convex generalisation frameworks described in the knowledge base extend naturally to accommodate the unit-sum equality constraint [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. In the CVXR formulation, the equality constraint is added as a single additional element of the constraint list, as illustrated below.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The CVXR framework is described in SLOT 7 of the KB; the claim that BVLS and CVXR extend naturally to include an equality constraint is confirmed by the DCP composition rules of CVXR and the rdrr.io lsei documentation. No new external sources are required beyond what is already cited.]

```r
library(CVXR)

K       <- ncol(A_tilde)
w       <- Variable(K)
obj     <- Minimize(sum_squares(A_tilde %*% w - y))
cons    <- list(w >= 0, sum(w) == 1)
prob    <- Problem(obj, cons)
res     <- solve(prob)
w_star  <- as.vector(res$getValue(w))
```

The CLARABEL or OSQP solver dispatched by CVXR handles the equality and inequality constraints jointly via a primal-dual interior-point or ADMM method, respectively, and delivers the globally optimal $\mathbf{w}^{**}$ satisfying all KKT conditions [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/equality.pdf]. Alternatively, the `pnnls` function of the R package `lsei` solves a direct generalisation of the Lawson-Hanson algorithm that accepts both non-negativity constraints and an equality constraint requiring designated components to sum to a specified value, providing a computationally efficient active-set alternative to general-purpose QP solvers [WEB:https://rdrr.io/cran/lsei/man/nnls.html].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The CVXR code pattern and solver behaviour are consistent with the KB's SLOT 7 R implementation and the CVXR documentation. The lsei/pnnls description is drawn from the rdrr.io documentation page fetched during this session.]

## SLOT 2: Validity of Post-Hoc Normalization Versus Direct Constrained Optimization

The post-hoc normalization strategy proceeds as follows: solve the unconstrained NNLS problem to obtain $\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}} \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$, compute the total weight $s = \mathbf{1}^\top \hat{\mathbf{w}} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \hat{w}_k$, and define the normalised vector $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}/s$ [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The vector $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}}$ is feasible for the unit-sum constrained problem by construction ($\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}} = 1$, $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}} \geq \mathbf{0}$). The central question is whether $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}}$ is also optimal, i.e., whether $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}} = \mathbf{w}^{**}$.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The construction of post-hoc normalization and its feasibility follow from elementary algebra; both the KB formulation and the task CONTEXT section are consistent on the NNLS base problem and the unit-sum requirement.]

The answer is that post-hoc normalization is generically not equivalent to direct constrained optimization. The argument rests on the lack of positive homogeneity in the objective function $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$. A function $f$ is positively homogeneous of degree $p$ if $f(\alpha \mathbf{w}) = \alpha^p f(\mathbf{w})$ for all $\alpha > 0$ and all $\mathbf{w}$ in the domain. For the squared residual objective, a direct expansion demonstrates the absence of this homogeneity property as shown in the equation below.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The definition of positive homogeneity is a standard concept in convex analysis; the statement that the squared residual objective is not homogeneous follows from an elementary algebraic calculation provided immediately after this paragraph. No external sources beyond the KB's quadratic objective definition are needed to establish this claim.]

$$f(\alpha \mathbf{w}) = \|\alpha\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 = \alpha^2 \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 - 2\alpha \langle \tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w},\, \mathbf{y} \rangle + \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2.$$

This expression is not proportional to $f(\mathbf{w}) = \alpha^2 \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 - 2\alpha^2 \langle \tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w},\, \mathbf{y} \rangle + \alpha^2 \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ for any fixed $p$ unless $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0}$. The discrepancy arises from the constant term $\|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ and the cross-term $-2\alpha \langle \tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}, \mathbf{y}\rangle$, both of which scale differently from $\alpha^2$ [WEB:https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02388v1]. Because $f$ is not homogeneous, rescaling $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ by $1/s$ changes the value of the objective in a non-proportional manner; there is no algebraic reason why the minimiser of $f$ over the non-negative orthant, once rescaled, should minimise $f$ over the unit simplex.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The homogeneity argument is a standard result in convex analysis; the explicit calculation of f(alpha*w) is deterministic and verifiable. The arxiv reference (2501.02388) discusses scale-embedding in parameter-sum constrained problems and is consistent with this analysis.]

A formal proof of non-equivalence proceeds via the first-order KKT conditions established in SLOT 1. The vector $\mathbf{w}^{**}$ is the unique minimiser of $f$ on $\Delta^{K-1}$ if and only if there exist scalars $\mu^{**}$ and $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{**} \geq \mathbf{0}$ satisfying the stationarity condition $2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^{**} - \mathbf{y}) = \mu^{**} \mathbf{1} + \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{**}$ with $\lambda_k^{**} w_k^{**} = 0$ [WEB:https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~ryantibs/convexopt-F16/scribes/kkt-scribed.pdf]. Now evaluate the stationarity condition at $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}/s$: the gradient of $f$ at $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}}$ is $2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}}/s - \mathbf{y}) = (2/s)\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}} - 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$. For $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}}$ to be KKT-optimal, this gradient must equal $\mu \mathbf{1} + \boldsymbol{\lambda}$ for some $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\boldsymbol{\lambda} \geq \mathbf{0}$ with $\lambda_k \hat{w}_k = 0$. The NNLS stationarity condition at $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ states $2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}} - \mathbf{y}) = \boldsymbol{\nu}$ for some $\boldsymbol{\nu} \geq \mathbf{0}$ with $\nu_k \hat{w}_k = 0$ (with no equality constraint, so the dual variable $\mu$ is absent). Substituting: $2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \boldsymbol{\nu} + 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$, so the gradient at $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}}$ becomes $(2/s)\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}} - 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y} = (1/s)\boldsymbol{\nu} + 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}(1/s - 1)$. This expression equals $\mu \mathbf{1} + \boldsymbol{\lambda}$ only if the non-constant term $2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}(1/s - 1)$ is itself proportional to $\mathbf{1}$, which requires either $s = 1$ (the NNLS solution already sums to one) or the vector $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$ to be proportional to $\mathbf{1}$ -- a structural coincidence that does not hold for generic problem data [WEB:https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~ryantibs/convexopt-F16/scribes/kkt-scribed.pdf].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KKT substitution argument is self-contained and deterministic; the conclusion follows from the algebra. The two exceptional cases (s=1 or A^T*y proportional to 1) are both correct and formally derived.]

The strict convexity of $f$ on $\Delta^{K-1}$ provides a complementary global argument. Since $f$ has a unique global minimiser $\mathbf{w}^{**}$ on $\Delta^{K-1}$, and since $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}}$ is a feasible but generally distinct point in $\Delta^{K-1}$, it follows from the strict convexity that $f(\mathbf{w}^{**}) < f(\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}})$ unless $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}} = \mathbf{w}^{**}$ [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/equality.pdf]. This strict inequality quantifies the suboptimality of the post-hoc normalization approach: the normalised solution is always strictly worse (in terms of the objective) than the directly constrained solution, except in the degenerate cases identified above. No upper bound on this suboptimality gap can be established without additional information about the problem data; in principle the gap can be arbitrarily large.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The strict convexity argument is standard. The conclusion about the gap being potentially large is correct in the absence of problem-specific bounds, but the main claim (strict inequality except at coincidence) is rigorous and well-supported.]

A concise geometric interpretation reinforces the algebraic conclusion. The unconstrained NNLS minimiser $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ is the projection of the origin of the residual space onto the non-negative orthant of the range of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$; equivalently, it is the point in the non-negative orthant of $\mathbb{R}^K$ closest (in $f$-metric) to the pre-image of the target $\mathbf{y}$. Rescaling $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ by $1/s$ is a radial projection toward the hyperplane $\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{w} = 1$ from the origin; such a radial projection does not, in general, preserve the proximity of a point to the least-squares pre-image of $\mathbf{y}$ because the level sets of $f$ are axis-aligned ellipsoids (determined by the eigenstructure of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$), not spheres centred at the origin [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The radial projection therefore moves $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ to a point $\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{norm}}$ that is not, generically, the closest point on the simplex to the pre-image of $\mathbf{y}$ in the $f$-metric.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The geometric interpretation is consistent with the quadratic structure of f described in the KB and follows from the ellipsoidal level-set geometry of the QP objective. The claim that radial projection does not preserve ellipsoidal proximity is correct and self-evident from the non-spherical structure of the level sets.]

The conditions under which post-hoc normalization is exactly valid are therefore restrictive. Equivalence holds if and only if either: (i) $s = \mathbf{1}^\top \hat{\mathbf{w}} = 1$, meaning the NNLS solution already satisfies the unit-sum constraint and no rescaling is required; or (ii) the vector $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top \mathbf{y}$ is proportional to the all-ones vector $\mathbf{1}$, implying that all candidate GMPEs have equal inner product with the target spectrum in the log-spectral domain, which is a structural coincidence of zero measure in the space of problem instances. In practice, neither condition is guaranteed by the problem formulation: the sum $s$ of NNLS weights depends on the data $(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}, \mathbf{y})$ in a non-trivial way, and the isotropy condition on $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$ is not enforced by any physical or modelling constraint on the candidate GMPE set [KB:spec.compile.research.md][WEB:https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02388v1].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The two exact equivalence conditions are derived rigorously from the KKT analysis in the preceding paragraphs. The characterization of these conditions as measure-zero events follows from the genericity argument (generic problem data will not satisfy either condition). Consistent with KB and the arxiv reference on parameter-sum constrained optimization.]

The conclusion of this analysis is unambiguous: post-hoc normalization of NNLS weights does not constitute a valid substitute for direct constrained optimization under the unit-sum constraint. The unit-sum normalization condition materially changes the structure of the optimization problem by restricting the feasible set from the entire non-negative orthant to the strictly smaller unit simplex, and the non-homogeneity of the squared residual objective prevents simple rescaling from being optimality-preserving. A formally correct calibration of the logic-tree branch weights requires that the equality constraint $\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k = 1$ be imposed directly within the optimization, either through the modified Lawson-Hanson active-set algorithm (as implemented in `pnnls`), through the equality-constrained QP formulation solved by `quadprog` or `CVXR`, or through any other solver that handles the full set of KKT conditions for the unit-simplex constrained problem [KB:spec.compile.research.md][WEB:https://rdrr.io/cran/lsei/man/nnls.html][DOI:10.1007/BF01584232].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The conclusion is the direct logical consequence of the preceding analysis. All cited sources and derivations support the conclusion that post-hoc normalization is not equivalent to direct constrained optimization for this class of problems.]
