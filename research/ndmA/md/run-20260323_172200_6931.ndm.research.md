# Performance-Based Seismic Coefficient Estimation for Slopes: A Displacement-Based Framework

## SLOT 1: Problem Introduction - Performance-Based Seismic Coefficient Selection

The selection of horizontal seismic coefficients for use in pseudo-static slope stability analysis represents a fundamental challenge in geotechnical earthquake engineering. The traditional approach, whereby a single seismic coefficient is selected based on precedent or simplified relationships with seismic hazard, lacks explicit connection to the consequences of failure and the physical response of the slope system itself. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Core framework for problem statement is clearly articulated in the performance-based coefficient definition provided in the KB source, which frames the coefficient selection as a probabilistic problem linked to displacement thresholds.]

The performance-based approach reconceptualizes the problem by establishing the horizontal seismic coefficient as the minimum acceleration required to satisfy a prescribed displacement performance objective. This objective is specified as an allowable permanent displacement threshold $D_n^{\star}$, which directly reflects the consequences of slope failure and the tolerance for ground deformation under seismic loading. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The performance-based framework explicitly links the coefficient to an allowable displacement criterion, establishing the rational basis for design. This is directly supported by the KB source defining the relationship between exceedance probability and displacement threshold.]

The horizontal seismic coefficient is calibrated to two groups of parameters: (a) the target ground-motion intensity level, which is determined from probabilistic seismic hazard analysis and reflects the specified return period or annual exceedance probability corresponding to the design earthquake, and (b) the physical and dynamic characteristics of the slope itself, including shear stiffness, shear strength parameters, and fundamental period of vibration. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This dual calibration framework is explicitly stated in the task specification and is consistent with the performance-based methodology described in the KB.]

The yield acceleration $k_y$, defined as the ratio $a_y/g$ where $a_y$ is the critical acceleration and $g$ is gravitational acceleration, serves as the threshold above which the slope mass experiences incremental seismic-induced displacement. [KB:newmark.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The yield acceleration definition and notation are standard and clearly defined in the KB source on Newmark sliding-block methodology.]

The framework thus establishes a rational, performance-based procedure for seismic design: given a target exceedance probability for permanent displacement and an allowable displacement threshold, the design seismic coefficient is derived by solving the inverse problem within the full uncertainty space of ground motion, site response, and slope displacement models. This approach replaces prescriptive guidance with an explicit, probabilistically grounded methodology that permits designers to balance the competing demands of seismic safety, economic feasibility, and functional serviceability based on project-specific criteria.

## SLOT 3: Newmark Displacement Analysis Methodologies - Rigid and Flexible Block Models

The Newmark sliding-block approach conceptualizes the slope as a discrete mass element that responds to seismic shaking through incremental downslope displacement when ground acceleration exceeds a critical threshold. This idealization yields a probabilistic characterization of permanent displacement that forms the foundation for the performance-based seismic coefficient framework. [KB:newmark.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Newmark methodology is clearly described in the KB source, which provides both the conceptual foundation and mathematical formulation underlying all displacement models employed in the framework.]

The response of a sliding block is characterized by two distinct physical model idealizations: the rigid-block model and the flexible-block model. The rigid-block idealization assumes that the mass yields instantaneously when ground acceleration exceeds the yield acceleration $k_y$ and then moves as a rigid body under the net driving acceleration. This model is appropriate for relatively stiff slopes with high shear strength, where the failure surface is sharp and well-defined, and where block rotation or internal deformation is negligible. [KB:newmark.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The rigid-block model definition and assumptions are standard in the Newmark framework and are consistent with the methodological literature cited in the KB.]

The flexible-block idealization, conversely, assumes that the sliding mass possesses finite stiffness and therefore exhibits frequency-dependent dynamic response characterized by a fundamental period $T_n$. Under seismic excitation, the flexible block responds through a combination of sliding and elastic deformation, and the dynamic amplification of ground motion is frequency-dependent. This model is appropriate for compliant slopes with lower stiffness, deeper failure surfaces, or systems where dynamic amplification effects are significant. [KB:newmark.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The flexible-block model characterization is directly supported by the KB source and is consistent with modern Newmark displacement methodology incorporating dynamic properties.]

For both block types, the critical yield acceleration $k_y$ is established through geotechnical analysis—typically using limit-equilibrium methods—and is expressed as a dimensionless ratio to gravitational acceleration. The fundamental period $T_n$, required for flexible-block models, is estimated from the stiffness and mass of the sliding system. Ground-motion intensity measures are obtained from probabilistic seismic hazard analysis or site-specific response calculations and include spectral accelerations $S_a(T_n)$ at the period(s) required by the specific displacement prediction model. [KB:newmark.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The definitions of yield acceleration, fundamental period, and intensity measures are standard in the Newmark framework and are clearly articulated in the KB source.]

The general form of all Newmark displacement prediction models employed in this framework is the lognormal regression equation:

$$\ln D = \mu_{\ln D}(\mathrm{IM}, k_y, T_n, M_w) + \epsilon\,\sigma_{\ln D}$$

where $D$ is the permanent displacement (in centimeters or meters), $\mathrm{IM}$ denotes the set of ground-motion intensity measures specific to each model (such as peak ground acceleration $\mathrm{PGA}$, spectral acceleration $S_a$, Arias intensity $\mathrm{AI}$, or peak ground velocity $\mathrm{PGV}$), $T_n$ is the fundamental period of the sliding mass (for flexible-block models), $M_w$ is the earthquake moment magnitude when included, $\mu_{\ln D}$ is the deterministic mean displacement predicted by the regression, $\sigma_{\ln D}$ is the standard deviation in natural-log space, and $\epsilon$ is a standard normal random variable representing record-to-record variability. [KB:newmark.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This general formulation is the foundation for all models described in the KB and is explicitly stated in the source document. The notation and parameter definitions are standard.]

Different displacement prediction models utilize different subsets of these parameters based on the data used to calibrate the regression. Flexible-block models, particularly those developed for subduction and shallow-crustal earthquakes, tend to include explicit dependencies on spectral acceleration (at either the fundamental period $T_n$ or at periods scaled by factors such as $1.3\,T_n$ or $1.5\,T_n$), magnitude $M_w$, and the fundamental period. Rigid-block models more commonly employ peak ground acceleration, Arias intensity, and peak ground velocity as primary intensity measures. [KB:newmark.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The distinction in parameter dependencies between flexible and rigid models is clearly documented in the KB source and reflects the different physical assumptions underlying each approach.]

The selection of the displacement prediction model appropriate to a given slope assessment depends on the block type (rigid or flexible), the seismic scenario (subduction versus shallow-crustal earthquakes), and the data availability for ground-motion characterization at the site. For flexible-block models, the fundamental period $T_n$ must be estimated from the stiffness and mass of the sliding system. For all models, when required intensity measures are not directly available from the hazard output, logarithmic interpolation is used to obtain values at the required periods or for non-tabulated parameters.

## SLOT 4: Appendix - Newmark Displacement Equations, Calibration, and Error Terms

A suite of displacement prediction models, developed and validated through empirical and semi-empirical approaches, form the ensemble used in this framework. Each model is presented with its complete regression equations, input parameter specifications, standard error terms, and applicable failure surface geometries. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KB source provides the complete technical specifications for all ensemble models, including regression coefficients, error terms, and calibration notes.]

**Bray & Macedo (2017) - Flexible-Block Model for Subduction Earthquakes**

This model was calibrated using data from subduction zone earthquakes and is applicable to flexible (compliant) blocks. The mean log-displacement is given by:

$$\mu_{\ln D}=a_0+a_1\ln S_a+a_2(\ln S_a)^2$$

where $S_a$ is the spectral acceleration at the fundamental period $T_n$ (in units of $g$). The coefficient $a_0$ is piecewise-defined according to the fundamental period:

$$a_0 = \begin{cases}-5.864+0.550\,M_w-9.421\,T_n-3.353\ln k_y-0.390(\ln k_y)^2, & T_n<0.1\\-6.896+0.550\,M_w+3.081\,T_n-0.803\,T_n^{2}-3.353\ln k_y-0.390(\ln k_y)^2, & T_n\ge 0.1\end{cases}$$

with $a_1 = 3.060+0.538\ln k_y$ and $a_2 = -0.225$. The standard deviation in log-space is $\sigma_{\ln D}=0.73$. This model is particularly applicable to deep rotational or translational failures in regions subject to subduction seismicity. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: All regression coefficients, piecewise definitions, and error terms are directly transcribed from the KB source with verified accuracy.]

**Bray & Macedo (2019) - Flexible-Block Model for Shallow-Crustal Earthquakes**

This model extends the Bray & Macedo (2017) approach to shallow-crustal (intraplate) seismicity and incorporates a duration-sensitivity term through peak ground velocity $\mathrm{PGV}$. The functional form is identical to the 2017 model but with updated coefficients:

$$a_0 = \begin{cases}-4.551+0.607\,M_w-9.690\,T_n-2.491\ln k_y-0.245(\ln k_y)^2,&T_n<0.1\\-5.894+0.607\,M_w+3.152\,T_n-0.910\,T_n^{2}-2.491\ln k_y-0.245(\ln k_y)^2,&T_n\ge0.1\end{cases}$$

plus an additional duration term:

$$\begin{cases}0,&\mathrm{PGV}\le115\;\text{cm/s}\\ \ln \mathrm{PGV}-4.75,&\mathrm{PGV}>115\;\text{cm/s}\end{cases}$$

with $a_1 = 2.703+0.344\ln k_y$, $a_2=-0.089$, and standard deviation $\sigma_{\ln D}=0.74$. The spectral acceleration is evaluated at $S_a(1.3\,T_n)$. This model accounts for the observation that shallow-crustal earthquakes produce longer durations of strong motion than comparable-magnitude subduction events, influencing cumulative displacement accumulation. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: All coefficients, duration-sensitivity formulation, and error terms are directly extracted from the KB source.]

**Bray & Travasarou (2007) - Flexible-Block Model**

This model applies to flexible-block sliding in seismically active regions and uses the following regression equation:

$$\mu_{\ln D} = -1.10 - 2.83\ln k_y - 0.333(\ln k_y)^2 + 0.566\ln k_y\ln S_a + 3.04\ln S_a - 0.244(\ln S_a)^2 + 0.278(M_w-7)$$

where $S_a$ is evaluated at $1.5\,T_n$, and the standard deviation is $\sigma_{\ln D}=0.66$. This model was among the first to explicitly incorporate the fundamental period and magnitude effects in a unified regression framework. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The regression equation and parameter values are directly supported by the KB source.]

**Jibson (2007) - Rigid-Block Model**

This model applies to rigid-block sliding and uses Arias intensity $\mathrm{AI}$ and the yield acceleration ratio $r = k_y/\mathrm{PGA}$ as primary predictors:

$$\mu_{\ln D}= \left[\,0.561\log_{10}\mathrm{AI} - 3.833\log_{10}(k_y/\mathrm{PGA})-1.474\right]\;\ln10$$

with the standard deviation in log-base-10 space given as $\sigma_{\log_{10}D}=0.616$, which converts to $\sigma_{\ln D}=0.616\ln10 \approx 1.418$ in natural-log space. Arias intensity captures cumulative energy effects and correlates with the total duration of strong shaking, making this model particularly useful when records lack explicit velocity information. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The complete formulation, parameter definitions, and error term conversion are correctly specified from the KB source.]

**Saygili & Rathje (2008) - Rigid-Block Model**

This multi-parameter rigid-block model incorporates both Arias intensity and peak ground acceleration:

$$\mu_{\ln D}=2.39-5.24r-18.78r^{2}+42.01r^{3}-29.15r^{4}-1.56\ln \mathrm{PGA} +1.38\ln \mathrm{AI}$$

where $r=k_y/\mathrm{PGA}$. The standard deviation is dependent on the yield strength ratio:

$$\sigma_{\ln D}=0.46+0.56\,r$$

This variable error term reflects the observation that slopes with lower relative strength (high $r$) exhibit greater dispersion in predicted displacement. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The regression equation, parameter dependencies, and variable error term formulation are directly supported by the KB source.]

**Ambraseys & Menu (1988) - Rigid-Block Model**

This classical rigid-block model is based on early empirical observations and uses the yield strength ratio $r = k_y/\mathrm{PGA}$ as the sole independent variable:

$$\mu_{\log_{10}D}=0.90+\log_{10} \left[(1-r)^{2.53}\,r^{-1.09}\right]$$

with standard deviation in log-base-10 space $\sigma_{\log_{10}D}=0.30$ (equivalent to $\sigma_{\ln D}=0.30\ln10 \approx 0.691$ in natural-log space). Despite its simplicity, this model remains widely used as a reference method in practice. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The model formulation, parameter definition, and error term are directly from the KB source.]

**Yegian et al. (1991) - Rigid-Block Model**

This early rigid-block model employs a polynomial regression in the yield strength ratio:

$$\mu_{\log_{10}D}=0.22-10.12\,r+16.38\,r^{2}-11.48\,r^{3}$$

with standard deviation $\sigma_{\log_{10}D}=0.45$ (equivalent to $\sigma_{\ln D}=0.45\ln10 \approx 1.036$ in natural-log space). [KB:newmark_model.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The regression equation and standard error are directly provided in the KB source.]

All models are formulated in lognormal space to accommodate the skewed, positive-definite nature of displacement data and to permit probabilistic simulation through standard normal random variates. The standard deviation terms $\sigma_{\ln D}$ capture both the regression error from the original model calibration and the inherent variability in earthquake response not explained by the selected intensity measures. When selected intensity measures are not available from the hazard output, logarithmic interpolation in period space is applied to obtain the required spectral ordinates.

## SLOT 5: Ensemble Numerical Model - Weighted Sum of Displacement Predictions

The displacement prediction methodology employed in this framework combines multiple Newmark models into a weighted ensemble, recognizing that no single regression model universally outperforms others across all seismic scenarios and slope types. The ensemble approach preserves model-to-model epistemic uncertainty by assigning logic-tree weights to each model based on its relevance, calibration basis, and applicability to the specific project context. [KB:newmark.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The ensemble methodology and its epistemicuncertainty rationale are clearly explained in the KB source, which describes the logic-tree weighting scheme.]

Within the ensemble framework, more recent models—particularly those calibrated against larger, more comprehensive ground-motion datasets with explicit treatment of magnitude and duration effects—receive proportionally greater weight in the aggregation. Models developed from limited datasets or using simplified functional forms are assigned correspondingly lower weights. This weighting scheme reflects the consensus that newer calibrations benefit from more extensive empirical data and refined understanding of physical mechanisms governing seismic slope displacement. [KB:newmark.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KB explicitly states that models calibrated on larger datasets and incorporating duration-sensitive formulations receive greater weights, reflecting modern best practices in ensemble modeling.]

The computational procedure operates as follows: For each scenario (defined by a specific yield acceleration, slope period, material properties, and seismic hazard realization), the required ground-motion intensity measures are sampled from the empirical quantile distributions obtained from the probabilistic seismic hazard analysis. These samples preserve both epistemic uncertainty (from source and ground-motion model logic trees) and aleatory variability (from earthquake occurrence and ground-motion-to-ground-motion variability). [KB:newmark.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The sampling procedure is described in detail in the KB source, including the use of empirical quantile functions and preservation of correlation structure.]

For each sampled scenario and each displacement model in the ensemble, the deterministic prediction $\mu_{\ln D}$ is computed from the model's regression equation using the sampled intensity measures. The stochastic term $\epsilon\,\sigma_{\ln D}$ is then added, where $\epsilon$ is a standard normal random variate drawn for each realization. This procedure is repeated across all models in the ensemble, yielding a set of displacement predictions that collectively represent the joint effect of ground-motion variability, displacement model epistemic uncertainty, and record-to-record variability. [KB:uncertainty_model.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Monte Carlo simulation procedure is explicitly described in the KB uncertainty model, including the role of standard normal deviates and stochastic terms.]

The ensemble displacement distribution is obtained by aggregating predictions across all models, weighted according to the assigned logic-tree weights. Quantiles of this distribution (such as the median, 84th percentile, and 95th percentile displacements) are computed directly from the accumulated ensemble predictions. These quantile values characterize the full range of uncertainty in seismic-induced permanent displacement, accounting for both the scatter within individual models and the systematic differences between models. [KB:uncertainty_model.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The aggregation procedure and quantile extraction from the ensemble are correctly described in relation to the KB source.]

The weighted ensemble approach yields several practical advantages: (1) it avoids arbitrary selection of a single "best" model, which may be inappropriate for the particular slope geometry or seismic scenario encountered; (2) it explicitly represents epistemic uncertainty through model diversity, allowing downstream decision-making to account for this uncertainty; and (3) it provides a transparent, auditable framework for incorporating new models or updating model weights as research evolves and additional calibration data becomes available.

## SLOT 6: Seismic Coefficient Derivation from Target Residual Displacement

The performance-based seismic coefficient is derived through an inverse procedure that links a target allowable displacement threshold directly to the yield acceleration required to satisfy a specified exceedance probability. This procedure synthesizes the ensemble displacement predictions with the seismic hazard characterization to yield the design seismic coefficient $k_{\max}$. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The framework for deriving the seismic coefficient from target displacement is fully described in the KB source, which provides both conceptual motivation and mathematical formulation.]

Let $D_n$ denote the random variable representing the Newmark-type permanent displacement, incorporating all sources of aleatory and epistemic uncertainty as characterized through the ensemble displacement framework. The allowable displacement threshold $D_n^{\star}$ is specified as a design criterion reflecting the acceptable level of ground deformation under seismic loading for the particular project, given its functional requirements, consequence of failure, and post-earthquake serviceability objectives. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The definition of the allowable displacement threshold as a design criterion is central to the performance-based framework and is clearly stated in the KB source.]

For a given yield acceleration $k_y$, the probability that the predicted displacement exceeds the allowable threshold is defined as:

$$p(k_y) = \mathbb{P}[D_n(k_y) > D_n^{\star}]$$

This exceedance probability depends on the distribution of predicted displacements conditional on $k_y$, which in turn reflects both the variability in ground-motion parameters (such as $\mathrm{PGA}$, $S_a(T_n)$, and other intensity measures) and the model-specific uncertainty in displacement prediction quantified by the standard deviation terms $\sigma_{\ln D}$ of each ensemble member. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The relationship between yield acceleration, displacement distribution, and exceedance probability is mathematically and conceptually well-established in the KB source.]

The performance-based seismic coefficient $k_{\max}$ is defined implicitly as the yield acceleration for which the probability of exceeding the allowable displacement is controlled at a specified target level $p^{\star}$. Mathematically, this is expressed as:

$$k_{\max}(p^{\star}\,|\,D_n^{\star}) = \inf \left\{ k_y : \mathbb{P}[D_n(k_y) > D_n^{\star}] \leq p^{\star} \right\}$$

In words, $k_{\max}$ is the infimum (greatest lower bound) of all yield accelerations for which the probability of exceeding the allowable displacement does not exceed the target exceedance probability. Equivalently, $k_{\max}$ is the minimum horizontal seismic coefficient that, when applied as the yield acceleration in the pseudo-static stability analysis, ensures that the probability of permanent displacement exceeding $D_n^{\star}$ is maintained at or below the specified performance level. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This fundamental equation is the core of the performance-based framework and is explicitly provided in the KB source with clear mathematical and linguistic definitions.]

The practical computation of $k_{\max}$ proceeds as follows: For a range of candidate yield accelerations $k_y$, the ensemble displacement model is evaluated to produce the full distribution of predicted displacements $D_n(k_y)$ across all realizations, models, and scenarios. The proportion of realizations for which $D_n(k_y)$ exceeds $D_n^{\star}$ is counted to estimate $p(k_y)$. The value of $k_y$ for which $p(k_y) \approx p^{\star}$ is then identified through interpolation, yielding the design coefficient $k_{\max}$. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The computational procedure is consistent with the mathematical definition and reflects standard Monte Carlo inversion practices described in the KB.]

For small displacements near the resolution limit of floating-point arithmetic, displacement values are floored at $10^{-16}$ meters to maintain well-defined logarithmic operations within the displacement models. This numerical floor prevents computational artifacts while remaining negligible relative to engineering-scale displacements. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The numerical floor is explicitly mentioned in the KB source as a practical measure to ensure numerical stability without affecting engineering accuracy.]

The relationship expressed in the definition of $k_{\max}$ encapsulates the full uncertainty characterization: it accounts for epistemic uncertainty in seismic hazard (reflected in the logic-tree branches and quantile distributions of intensity measures), epistemic uncertainty in displacement modeling (reflected in the ensemble of models), aleatory variability in earthquake ground motion (reflected in the standard deviation terms of the hazard analysis and the standard errors of the displacement models), and the uncertainty contributions from site response amplification and material property variability. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The comprehensive uncertainty integration is described in both the pbkmax.qmd source and the referenced uncertainty model documentation, establishing that $k_{\max}$ is derived from the full uncertainty space.]

Once the ensemble-estimated mean or quantile-level Newmark displacement has been obtained for a given yield acceleration, the inversion procedure determines the minimum yield acceleration (and therefore the minimum pseudo-static horizontal seismic coefficient applicable in limit-equilibrium analysis) required to maintain the probability of exceeding the allowable displacement at the target level. This coefficient thus provides a rational, performance-grounded basis for pseudo-static slope stability design that explicitly incorporates both seismic hazard and displacement tolerance as integrated constraints.
