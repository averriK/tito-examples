# Horizontal Seismic Coefficient Estimation for Slopes: A Performance-Based Approach

## SLOT 1: Problem Introduction - Performance-Based Seismic Coefficient Selection

The fundamental objective of performance-based seismic slope design is to select a horizontal seismic coefficient that ensures slope stability under a prescribed level of ground-motion intensity while limiting permanent displacement to an acceptable threshold. [KB:pbkmax.qmd] This coefficient, denoted $k_{\max}$, represents the minimum horizontal acceleration required to satisfy a displacement performance objective under combined effects of ground motion variability, model dispersion, and site-specific uncertainty. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Problem statement directly extracted and synthesized from pbkmax.qmd which presents the defining equations and framework for k_max derivation. The framing captures the core engineering objective.]

The coefficient calibration depends on two groups of parameters: first, a target ground-motion intensity level linked to the consequences of slope failure, typically expressed through annual exceedance probabilities ranging from 1/100 to 1/10,000 years; and second, the physical and dynamic characteristics of the slope, including shear strength (expressed as yield acceleration $k_y$), stiffness properties (captured through fundamental period $T_n$), and material response characteristics. [KB:pbkmax.qmd] The yield acceleration $k_y$ is established through geotechnical analysis and expressed as a fraction of gravitational acceleration $g$, serving as the threshold beyond which incremental downslope displacement occurs. [KB:newmark.qmd]

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The two parameter groups and their physical meanings are clearly documented in the KB sources. The definition of k_y aligns with Newmark sliding-block theory presented in newmark.qmd.]

The design framework integrates seismic hazard characterization, site response analysis, and Newmark-based displacement prediction within a probabilistic framework that quantifies both aleatory and epistemic uncertainty. Ground-motion intensity measures are obtained from site-specific probabilistic seismic hazard analysis or reference-rock motions, with amplification applied through site-response models to account for local soil conditions. [KB:newmark.qmd] For each scenario, permanent displacements are computed across an ensemble of predictive models, with results aggregated according to logic-tree weighting to produce displacement distributions that reflect the full range of model and parameter uncertainty.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The integration of hazard, site response, and displacement prediction is documented in newmark.qmd, with clear descriptions of the uncertainty framework.]

Contemporary seismic slope stability design standards recognize performance-based approaches as a fundamental component of modern geotechnical practice. The American Society of Civil Engineers (ASCE) standard ASCE/SEI 7-22 provides prescriptive seismic design loads for building and non-building structures, incorporating probabilistic seismic hazard assessment and establishing explicit performance objectives rather than relying solely on simplified pseudo-static coefficients. [WEB:https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/codes-and-standards/asce-sei-7-22] The U.S. Geological Survey has published comprehensive guidance on seismic landslide analysis and Newmark displacement procedures, including open-file reports documenting computational methods and applications for slope performance assessment during earthquakes. [WEB:https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20035] These regulatory and professional resources emphasize the integration of seismic hazard characterization with displacement prediction to achieve performance-based design objectives.

^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: ASCE/SEI 7-22 is the current published standard. USGS publications provide established guidance on Newmark methods. However, slope-specific guidance within these standards may vary in comprehensiveness.]

The Newmark sliding-block method, originally developed for rigid-body analysis, has become the cornerstone of displacement-based seismic slope design. [KB:newmark_model.qmd] Over the past two decades, refinements to the method have introduced flexible-block formulations that capture dynamic compliance of the sliding mass, and duration-sensitive models that incorporate peak ground velocity and Arias intensity to account for frequency content and seismic energy. [KB:newmark_model.qmd] These developments represent a shift toward physically-motivated predictive models calibrated against both observed earthquake records and simulated ground motions from comprehensive scenario sets. Contemporary research demonstrates that reliability-based allowable-displacement design represents a more robust approach than traditional pseudo-static factor-of-safety procedures for assessing seismic slope stability. Professional practice in mine waste and tailings slope design increasingly adopts performance-based frameworks that explicitly define allowable permanent displacements as a function of consequence severity. [WEB:https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-2184-3_108] For tailings storage facilities and waste rock dumps, acceptable displacement thresholds vary based on facility classification and risk tolerance, with performance criteria established through consequence analysis and regulatory requirements. [KB:pbkmax.qmd] This approach is particularly suited to facilities where the cost and environmental consequences of failure vary significantly with the magnitude of displacement, justifying the investment in refined seismic hazard and displacement analysis. Contemporary tailings management standards and international guidance emphasize integration of performance-based seismic design throughout the facility lifecycle. ## SLOT 3: Newmark Displacement Analysis Methodologies - Rigid and Flexible Block Models

The Newmark sliding-block framework conceptualizes a potential failure mass as either a rigid block or a flexible (compliant) block, depending on whether the sliding surface and surrounding material can accommodate inelastic deformation. [KB:newmark.qmd] In the rigid-block idealization, the entire mass moves as a unit without internal distortion once the yield acceleration is exceeded, and displacement accumulates through repeated incremental movements triggered when ground acceleration surpasses the critical threshold. [KB:newmark.qmd] This assumption is appropriate for failures that occur along well-defined weak layers or through-going rupture surfaces, with negligible compliance.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The rigid block concept and its applicability are clearly defined in newmark.qmd.]

The flexible-block formulation relaxes the assumption of perfect rigidity, allowing the sliding mass to deform elastically during shaking and to accumulate inelastic displacement through progressive degradation or strain softening. [KB:newmark.qmd] The fundamental period $T_n$ of the sliding mass becomes an explicit parameter in flexible-block models, introducing frequency-dependent response characteristics. Flexible-block models are more appropriate for large soil or rock-fill masses where internal stiffness controls dynamic amplification and where the compliance of the failure surface becomes significant relative to the inertial response of the mass.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Flexible block definition and the role of fundamental period T_n are documented in newmark.qmd and newmark_model.qmd.]

All displacement prediction methods operate within a probabilistic framework that is formulated in lognormal space. [KB:newmark.qmd]

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The lognormal framework is documented in newmark.qmd as the standard formulation for all models.]

$$\ln D = \mu_{\ln D}(\mathrm{IM}, k_y, T_n, M_w) + \epsilon\,\sigma_{\ln D}$$

where $\mu_{\ln D}$ is the model-predicted mean displacement, $\sigma_{\ln D}$ is the associated standard deviation in natural-log space, and $\epsilon$ is a standard normal variate accounting for record-to-record variability. [KB:newmark.qmd] The intensity measures $\mathrm{IM}$ are obtained from site hazard results and include parameters such as peak ground acceleration ($\mathrm{PGA}$), spectral accelerations at model-specific periods ($S_a$), Arias intensity ($\mathrm{AI}$), and peak ground velocity ($\mathrm{PGV}$). [KB:newmark.qmd]

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The general form of the displacement model and the role of intensity measures are clearly documented in newmark.qmd.]

Spectral accelerations are typically evaluated at periods related to the fundamental period of the sliding mass, with period scaling factors ranging from 1.3 to 1.5 times $T_n$ depending on the specific model. [KB:newmark.qmd] Where spectral ordinates are not explicitly available in tabular form, logarithmic interpolation in period provides intermediate values. The yield acceleration $k_y$ is established by slope-specific geotechnical analysis, considering friction angle, cohesion, pore-pressure conditions, and geometry.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The period scaling and interpolation methodology are documented in newmark.qmd.]

## SLOT 4: Appendix - Newmark Displacement Equations, Calibration, and Error Terms

The Newmark sliding-block framework encompasses seven principal predictive models, each calibrated to specific failure-surface types and seismic scenarios. The complete set of models is described below, with explicit functional forms, coefficients, and dispersion parameters required for engineering application.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The models presented derive directly from newmark_model.qmd which provides comprehensive documentation of each method.]

### Bray & Macedo (2017) – Flexible-Block Model for Subduction Earthquakes

The Bray & Macedo (2017) flexible-block model is calibrated for subduction-zone earthquakes and incorporates magnitude dependence and period-dependent behavior. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The calibration basis and period dependence are documented in newmark_model.qmd.]

$$\mu_{\ln D}=a_0+a_1\ln S_a+a_2(\ln S_a)^2$$

The coefficient $a_0$ depends on fundamental period, magnitude, yield acceleration ratio, and the period dependence is captured through $a_1$ and $a_2$:

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The functional form and parameter dependencies are from newmark_model.qmd.]

$$\begin{aligned}
a_0 &=
\begin{cases}
-5.864+0.550\,M_w-9.421\,T_n-3.353\ln k_y-0.390(\ln k_y)^2, & T_n<0.1\\
-6.896+0.550\,M_w+3.081\,T_n-0.803\,T_n^{2}-3.353\ln k_y-0.390(\ln k_y)^2, & T_n\ge 0.1
\end{cases}\\
a_1 &= 3.060+0.538\ln k_y,\qquad
a_2 = -0.225
\end{aligned}$$

The standard deviation is $\sigma_{\ln D}=0.73$. [KB:newmark_model.qmd] Model inputs include spectral acceleration at the fundamental period $T_n$ (in units of $g$), fundamental period in seconds, and moment magnitude $M_w$. This model exhibits distinct behavior for short-period ($T_n<0.1$ s) and longer-period ($T_n\ge 0.1$ s) sliding masses, reflecting the transition from quasi-static to dynamic response.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Equations, coefficients, standard deviation, and interpretation taken directly from newmark_model.qmd with faithful transcription to LaTeX format.]

### Bray & Macedo (2019) – Flexible-Block Model for Shallow-Crustal Earthquakes

The Bray & Macedo (2019) formulation extends the 2017 approach to shallow-crustal earthquakes and introduces peak ground velocity ($\mathrm{PGV}$) as an explicit duration modifier. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The extension to shallow-crustal earthquakes and PGV incorporation are documented in newmark_model.qmd.]

$$\begin{aligned}
a_0 &=
\begin{cases}
-4.551+0.607\,M_w-9.690\,T_n-2.491\ln k_y-0.245(\ln k_y)^2,&T_n<0.1\\
-5.894+0.607\,M_w+3.152\,T_n-0.910\,T_n^{2}-2.491\ln k_y-0.245(\ln k_y)^2,&T_n\ge0.1
\end{cases}\\
&\quad+\;
\begin{cases}
0,&\mathrm{PGV}\le115\;\text{cm/s}\\
\ln \mathrm{PGV}-4.75,&\mathrm{PGV}>115\;\text{cm/s}
\end{cases}\\
a_1 &= 2.703+0.344\ln k_y,\qquad a_2=-0.089
\end{aligned}$$

with $\sigma_{\ln D}=0.74$. [KB:newmark_model.qmd] The spectral acceleration is evaluated at $1.3\,T_n$. The duration modifier activates when $\mathrm{PGV}$ exceeds 115 cm/s, capturing increased displacement under high-velocity seismic shaking.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Equations and coefficients transcribed directly from newmark_model.qmd. The duration modifier mechanism is clearly documented.]

### Bray & Travasarou (2007) – Flexible-Block Model

The Bray & Travasarou (2007) flexible-block model employs explicit interaction terms between yield acceleration and spectral acceleration. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The model formulation and interaction terms are documented in newmark_model.qmd.]

$$\mu_{\ln D} = -1.10 - 2.83\ln k_y - 0.333(\ln k_y)^2 + 0.566\ln k_y\ln S_a + 3.04\ln S_a - 0.244(\ln S_a)^2 + 0.278(M_w-7)$$

with $\sigma_{\ln D}=0.66$. [KB:newmark_model.qmd] The spectral acceleration is evaluated at $1.5\,T_n$. This model exhibits nonlinear dependence on both yield acceleration and spectral acceleration through the interaction term $\ln k_y\ln S_a$, and includes magnitude scaling calibrated to reference magnitude $M_w=7$.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Equation and dispersion parameter from newmark_model.qmd.]

### Jibson (2007) – Rigid-Block Model Using Arias Intensity

The Jibson (2007) rigid-block model employs Arias intensity as the primary ground-motion descriptor to capture duration effects in seismic displacement prediction. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The use of Arias intensity in the Jibson model is documented in newmark_model.qmd.]

$$\mu_{\ln D}= \left[\,0.561\log_{10}\mathrm{AI} - 3.833\log_{10}(k_y/\mathrm{PGA})-1.474\right]\;\ln 10$$

with $\sigma_{\log_{10}D}=0.616$ (equivalent to $\sigma_{\ln D}=0.616\ln 10$). [KB:newmark_model.qmd] The model is expressed in base-10 logarithmic space and defines the strength ratio $r=k_y/\mathrm{PGA}$. Arias intensity $\mathrm{AI}$ is estimated from $\mathrm{PGA}$ when direct time-domain integration is not available.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Equation and uncertainty parameter transcribed from newmark_model.qmd.]

### Saygili & Rathje (2008) – Rigid-Block Model with Multiple Parameters

The Saygili & Rathje (2008) model is a multi-parameter rigid-block formulation that incorporates both peak ground acceleration and Arias intensity. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]

where $r=k_y/\mathrm{PGA}$, with dispersion $\sigma_{\ln D}=0.46+0.56\,r$. [KB:newmark_model.qmd] The dispersion increases with increasing strength ratio $r$, reflecting greater prediction uncertainty for "weaker" slopes where the displacement resistance is low relative to the imposed seismic demand.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Equation and the relationship between dispersion and strength ratio documented in newmark_model.qmd.]

### Ambraseys & Menu (1988) – Rigid-Block Model

The Ambraseys & Menu (1988) rigid-block formulation is expressed in base-10 logarithmic space with a functional form emphasizing nonlinear dependence on strength ratio. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]

### Material Shear Stiffness Parameters

The shear modulus at depth for soil materials depends on void ratio and effective stress state and is required for computing fundamental period in sliding-block analysis. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]

[KB:newmark_model.qmd] Reference pressure is $p_{ref}=1$ kPa. Material-specific parameters for common soil types are provided in the referenced literature. [KB:newmark_model.qmd]

## SLOT 5: Ensemble Numerical Model - Weighted Sum of Displacement Predictions

The methodology employs a weighted ensemble of displacement predictions, where each of the seven Newmark models documented in the appendix contributes to the final displacement estimate according to a logic-tree weighting scheme. [KB:newmark.qmd] The ensemble approach acknowledges model-to-model epistemic uncertainty and captures the range of prediction uncertainty inherent in the various published formulations. More recent models, calibrated against larger and more comprehensive ground-motion datasets, receive proportionally greater weight in the ensemble.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The ensemble approach and emphasis on weighting recent models based on data comprehensiveness is documented in newmark.qmd.]

For each seismic scenario and material-geometry configuration, displacement predictions are computed using all seven models with sampled input parameters drawn from the empirical distribution of ground-motion intensity measures. [KB:newmark.qmd] The random sampling of intensity measures accounts for both aleatory uncertainty from the probabilistic hazard analysis and epistemic uncertainty captured through the hazard logic trees. [KB:uncertainty_model.qmd]

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The sampling procedure and uncertainty propagation are documented in newmark.qmd and uncertainty_model.qmd.]

For each realization and each model, a standard normal deviate $\epsilon$ is drawn and the predicted displacement is computed according to the following relationship:

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The realization procedure with standard normal deviates is standard practice in probabilistic frameworks documented in uncertainty_model.qmd.]

$$\ln D = \mu_{\ln D} + \epsilon\,\sigma_{\ln D}$$

Results are aggregated across all models by application of the logic-tree weights, and quantile estimates (median, 84th percentile, 95th percentile) are extracted directly from the ensemble distribution. [KB:newmark.qmd] The displacement distributions thus produced reflect the full uncertainty budget: aleatory variability in earthquake occurrence and ground-motion generation, epistemic uncertainty in ground-motion and site-response models, and model-to-model dispersion in displacement prediction. No predictive models are excluded from the ensemble; all models contribute weighted predictions to the final result.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The aggregation procedure and exclusion policy are documented in newmark.qmd.]

## SLOT 6: Seismic Coefficient Derivation from Target Residual Displacement

The performance-based seismic coefficient $k_{\max}$ is established as the minimum horizontal acceleration required to limit permanent displacement below a prescribed allowable threshold under combined effects of ground motion variability, model dispersion, and site-specific uncertainty. [KB:pbkmax.qmd] Let $D_n$ denote the random variable representing the Newmark-type displacement, incorporating all aleatory and epistemic uncertainties. The allowable displacement $D_n^{\star}$ is specified as a design criterion for permanent ground deformations.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The definition of k_max and the role of D_n and D_n* are from pbkmax.qmd.]

For a given yield acceleration $k_y$, the probability of exceeding the displacement threshold is expressed as:

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The exceedance probability framework is foundational to the performance-based approach documented in pbkmax.qmd.]

$$p(k_y) = \mathbb{P}[D_n(k_y) > D_n^{\star}]$$

The distribution $D_n(k_y)$ reflects variability in ground-motion parameters and model-specific uncertainty in displacement prediction. [KB:pbkmax.qmd]

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The composition of D_n(k_y) from ground-motion and model uncertainty is documented in pbkmax.qmd.]

The performance-based seismic coefficient $k_{\max}$ for a target exceedance probability $p$ is defined by the implicit relationship:

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This defining equation for k_max is the central relationship in the performance-based framework presented in pbkmax.qmd.]

$$k_{\max}(p\,|\,D_n^{\star}) = \inf \left\{ k_y : \mathbb{P}[D_n(k_y) > D_n^{\star}] \leq p \right\}$$

This equation establishes $k_{\max}$ as the lowest value of $k_y$ for which the probability of exceeding the allowable displacement is controlled at the specified level. [KB:pbkmax.qmd] The coefficient inherently couples the seismic hazard (through the distribution of ground-motion intensity measures), the displacement-based design objective (through $D_n^{\star}$), and the full statistical characterization of both ground-motion and material uncertainty.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The interpretation of the equation and its physical meaning are from pbkmax.qmd.]

The inversion procedure that yields $k_{\max}$ from the ensemble displacement framework proceeds as follows: for each candidate value of $k_y$, the ensemble of displacement predictions is computed as described in SLOT 5, with all models evaluated at that particular yield acceleration. The combined distribution of displacement predictions across all models and all realizations is then examined to determine the exceedance probability for the target threshold $D_n^{\star}$. The value of $k_y$ that satisfies the performance criterion is the seismic coefficient $k_{\max}$.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The inversion procedure is consistent with the ensemble methodology documented in newmark.qmd and the probabilistic framework in pbkmax.qmd.]

Performance-based seismic coefficients are reported across service levels corresponding to annual exceedance probabilities ranging from 1/100 to 1/10,000 for different geometry and material scenarios representative of tailings storage facilities and waste rock dumps. [KB:pbkmax.qmd] The coefficients are expressed in units of gravitational acceleration ($g$) and serve as direct inputs to pseudo-static slope stability analysis.

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The service level framework and application context documented in pbkmax.qmd.]
