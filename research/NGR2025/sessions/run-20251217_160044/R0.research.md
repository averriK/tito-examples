## Evidence of Reitfontein Fault activity in the last 35,000 years

The Reitfontein Fault does not meet the conventional criteria for classification as an active fault based on the available evidence. Active faults are conventionally defined as structures known to have produced historical earthquakes or shown evidence of movement in the last 35,000 years [KB:Mmax.research.compile.1.md][KB:Mmax.research.compile.2.md]. The current crustal stress regime in South Africa was established sometime in the last 40 to 11 million years [KB:Mmax.research.compile.1.md]. The Reitfontein Fault is at least 2.0 Ga old and has accommodated both normal and oblique reverse movement in the geological past, but there is no known geological or geomorphological evidence to suggest neotectonic activity on this structure [KB:Mmax.research.compile.2.md]. ## Gutenberg-Richter parameters and b-value logic tree

The Gutenberg-Richter seismicity recurrence equation states that $\log_{10}(n) = a_0 - b \times M$, where $b$ is the Richter b-value representing the relative proportion of small to large events, $M$ is earthquake magnitude, and $n$ is the number of earthquakes in magnitude range $M \pm \Delta M$ [KB:Mmax.research.compile.1.md]. For the Reitfontein Fault, the PSHA study employs three different b-values: 1.35 (weighted 0.30), 1.10 (weighted 0.60), and 1.99 (weighted 0.10) [KB:Mmax.research.compile.1.md]. The b-value affects the slope of the magnitude-frequency distribution, with higher b-values indicating a greater proportion of smaller earthquakes relative to larger ones [KB:Mmax.research.compile.1.md]. Global and regional surveys show that empirically determined b-values are remarkably stable, generally limited to values of $1 \pm 0.2$, being in the range $0.8 \leq b \leq 1.2$ [WEB:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutenberg-Richter_law]. Research on cratonic regions specifically indicates that stable continental blocks tend to have higher b-values; for example, the area with underlying Tanzanian Craton shows a b-value of 1.29, and the stable block in southern Mozambique has a b-value of 1.37 [WEB:https://geoenvironmental-disasters.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40677-023-00249-2]. The regional context shows that the EZ-FRISK Africa model assigns b-values of 1.04 for the Drakensberg zone and 1.06 for the Kaapvaal zone [KB:Mmax.research.compile.1.md][KB:Mmax.research.compile.2.md]. ## Fault activity classification

Based on all findings from previous sections, the Reitfontein Fault is best classified as a paleoseismic structure with uncertain activity status rather than as a definitively active neotectonic fault. A neotectonic fault is defined as one that has hosted displacement under conditions imposed by the current crustal stress regime and therefore may move again in the future [KB:Mmax.research.compile.2.md]. The current crustal stress regime in South Africa was established sometime in the last 40 to 11 million years [KB:Mmax.research.compile.1.md][KB:Mmax.research.compile.2.md]. The Reitfontein Fault is at least 2.0 Ga old and there is no known geological or geomorphological evidence to suggest neotectonic activity on this structure [KB:Mmax.research.compile.2.md]. The Singh et al. (2009) reference cited in the South Deep report specifically identified the Reitfontein Fault as one of several faults requiring reassessment to determine if still active, indicating the fault's activity status was uncertain and merited further investigation rather than constituting evidence of active classification [KB:Mmax.research.compile.1.md]. The absence of quantified slip-rate data, lack of geomorphological evidence for recent movement, and only minor present-day seismic activity all point to a fault that may have been active in the geological past but lacks definitive evidence of capability for future large-magnitude rupture under the current stress regime. The conservative engineering approach of treating the fault as potentially active with $M_{\max}=7.2$ and 1 m/Myr slip rate is appropriate for safety-critical infrastructure such as tailings storage facilities [KB:Mmax.research.compile.1.md][KB:Mmax.research.compile.2.md], but should be recognized as a precautionary assumption pending detailed paleoseismic investigations.
