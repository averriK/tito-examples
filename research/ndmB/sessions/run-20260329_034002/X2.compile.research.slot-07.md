## SLOT 7: Flexible Block Assembly Model and Applicability

The flexible-block assembly model is an ensemble of three independently calibrated displacement regression models - Bray and Travasarou (2007) [@BrayTravasarou2007], Bray, Macedo, and Travasarou (2017) [@BrayEtAl2017], and Bray and Macedo (2019) [@BrayMacedo2019] - combined within a logic tree whose branch weights reflect the tectonic and geotechnical conditions at the site. Each model provides a complete lognormal predictive distribution for displacement conditional on the same flexible-block predictor set: yield acceleration $k_y$, fundamental period $T_n$, spectral acceleration $S_a$ evaluated at a period proportional to $T_n$, and moment magnitude $M_w$. The assembly produces a composite predictive distribution that retains both within-model aleatory uncertainty (through $\sigma_{\ln D}$) and between-model epistemic uncertainty (through logic-tree weight variation), yielding a displacement estimate that reflects the full uncertainty budget applicable to compliant slope systems.

The flexible-block assembly model is appropriate for slopes and earth structures in which the sliding mass has significant deformability relative to the dominant periods of the input ground motion. This condition is typically satisfied by deep-seated failure surfaces in earth dams, compacted fill embankments, mine waste rock dumps, tailings storage facilities, and natural slopes composed of relatively soft or thick materials. The practical criterion distinguishing flexible from rigid mass behavior is a comparison of the fundamental period $T_n$ of the sliding mass to the characteristic periods of the seismic demand: when $T_n$ is not negligible relative to those periods, the internal dynamic response of the mass amplifies or de-amplifies the base excitation in a manner that rigid-block models cannot capture. Flexible-block models explicitly account for this amplification through the spectral acceleration evaluated at $\alpha T_n$, and they incorporate earthquake magnitude $M_w$ to quantify the additional displacement accumulated during the extended strong-shaking phase of large-magnitude events, a consideration of particular importance for subduction zone earthquakes.

In multi-tectonic settings, the flexible-block assembly assigns event-type-specific model weights that reflect the relative contribution of shallow crustal versus subduction zone sources to the site hazard at each return period. Bray and Travasarou (2007) provides a baseline flexible-block estimate applicable to general tectonic environments, calibrated against a diverse record set. Bray, Macedo, and Travasarou (2017) receives elevated weight when the seismic hazard is dominated by subduction zone interface earthquakes, reflecting its calibration against 810 two-component subduction ground-motion recordings. Bray and Macedo (2019) receives elevated weight for sites where shallow crustal sources dominate the hazard and near-fault effects are significant, given its calibration against 6,711 NGA-West2 recordings and its explicit near-fault velocity correction for $\mathrm{PGV} > 115$ cm s$^{-1}$. The disaggregation of the site seismic hazard by tectonic source type informs the logic-tree weight assignment and ensures that the composite displacement distribution is dominated by the models most relevant to the actual seismic threat at the site.
