## SLOT 6: Maximum Base Stiffness Estimation (Shear Modulus and Shear Wave Velocity)

The estimation of maximum base stiffness constitutes a foundational step in flexible-block Newmark analysis, because the shear-wave velocity at the base of the embankment - and thereby the fundamental period $T_n$ - depends on the small-strain shear modulus $G_o$. The shear modulus at arbitrary depth $z$ is estimated as a function of void ratio $e_o$ and octahedral effective stress $\sigma'_o(z)$ using the Ishihara (1997) formulation [@Ishihara1997]:

$$G(z,\,e_o) \approx A\,\frac{(C_e - 1)^2}{1 + e_o}\,\left(\frac{\sigma'_o(z)}{p_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)^n$$

In this expression, $A$ is a dimensionless material-dependent amplitude constant; $C_e$ is a void-ratio shape parameter controlling the sensitivity of $G$ to void ratio; $e_o$ is the in-situ void ratio of the material; $p_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a reference pressure (typically atmospheric, 101.3 kPa); $n$ is the stress exponent governing the power-law dependence of stiffness on confining pressure; and $\sigma'_o(z)$ is the octahedral effective stress at depth $z$. The model parameters $A$, $C_e$, and $n$ are determined from laboratory tests on undisturbed or remolded samples of the fill or native material - typically resonant column or bender element tests - and Ishihara (1997) provides tabulated values for a range of soil types classified by USCS designation together with the corresponding void ratio ranges for which the correlations were calibrated.

The maximum (small-strain) shear modulus $G_o$ corresponds to the evaluation at the base of the embankment, at depth $z = H_{\max}$, where the octahedral stress reaches its maximum value under the self-weight of the fill. Once $G_o$ is established, the base shear-wave velocity follows directly:

$$v_S^{o} = \sqrt{\frac{G_o}{\rho}}$$

where $\rho$ is the bulk mass density of the material at the base. The complete stiffness profile $G(z)$ over the full height of the embankment follows from the power-law expression with the estimated parameters, and the homogeneity ratio $m_o$ is determined by fitting the predicted profile $G(z)/G_o = (z/H_{\max})^{m_o}$ to the measured or inferred stiffness data. In the context of tailings storage facility (TSF) and waste rock dump (WRD) design, the relevant material types span the full range of the Ishihara parameter table - from coarse-grained rockfill and sandy tailings to fine-grained slime tailings - and the estimation is performed separately for each material zone contributing to the critical failure surface. The chain of calculations linking laboratory-calibrated Ishihara parameters to $G_o$, then to $v_S^{o}$, and finally to the fundamental period $T_s^{(1)}$ via the eigenvalue expression, provides the $T_n$ input required by all flexible-block displacement models.
