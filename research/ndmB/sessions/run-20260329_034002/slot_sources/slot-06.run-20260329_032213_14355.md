### Post-2019 Newmark Displacement Methods

Following the establishment of the Bray and Macedo (2019) shallow crustal model as the current state of practice for flexible-block displacement estimation, subsequent research has extended the probabilistic framework to additional tectonic regimes. The most significant recent development is the seismic slope displacement procedure of Macedo, Bray, and Liu (2023), which provides models for both interface and intraslab subduction zone earthquakes using a large contemporary ground-motion database [DOI:10.1061/JGGEFK.GTENG-11445]. Published in the Journal of Geotechnical and Geoenvironmental Engineering (Volume 149, Issue 11), this procedure recognizes the systematic differences in duration and spectral content between interface and intraslab subduction events - factors not fully captured by the earlier Bray, Macedo, and Travasarou (2017) interface-only model - and provides separate predictive equations for each subduction event type. Chilean seismic engineering practice has contributed a complementary parameterization of seismic demand relevant to Newmark displacement analysis. G. Rodolfo Saragoni developed the earthquake destructiveness potential factor $P_d$, defined as the ratio of the Arias intensity to the square of the zero-crossing frequency $f_z$ of the accelerogram. This dimensionless-like parameter encodes both the cumulative energy content (through $I_a$) and the frequency content (through $f_z$) of the ground motion in a single scalar, providing a more discriminating characterization of subduction-zone accelerograms than either $I_a$ or $\mathrm{PGA}$ alone. $$P_d = \frac{I_a}{f_z^2}$$

Saragoni demonstrated that empirical regression equations for $P_d$ in terms of Newmark rigid-block displacement can be used to derive seismic coefficients $k_c$ for sliding surfaces, providing a link between the spectral and cumulative-energy characteristics of subduction-zone motions and the pseudo-static coefficient. This approach is particularly relevant in the Chilean context, where subduction earthquakes exhibit strong high-frequency content and large cumulative energy that influence rigid-block displacement predictions in ways not fully captured by PGA-only parameterizations. ### Fundamental Period Estimation

The fundamental period of an embankment or slope $T_n$ is a key parameter for all flexible-block Newmark displacement models, because these models evaluate spectral acceleration at a period proportional to $T_n$. An approximate expression for the $j$-th modal period $T_s^{(j)}$ follows from the analytical solution for the dynamic response of a trapezoidal embankment with a shear-wave velocity that varies as a power law with depth. In this formulation, $a^{(j)}$ denotes the $j$-th root of the characteristic eigenvalue equation, which depends on the homogeneity ratio $m_o$ and the truncation ratio $\lambda_o$, and $v_S^o$ is the shear-wave velocity at the base established from the Ishihara model described in SLOT 6. [KB:periods.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The modal period formulation, the roles of $m_o$, $\lambda_o$, and $v_S^o$, and the eigenvalue dependence are directly stated in the KB (periods.md). The derivation is consistent with the classical analytical approach for vibration of inhomogeneous earth structures.]

$$T_s^{(j)} \approx \frac{4\,\pi\,H_{max}}{a^{(j)}\,(2-m_o)\,v_S^{o}}$$

The truncation ratio $\lambda_o$ captures the geometric influence of the berm width $B_o$ on the dynamic response of the embankment. It is defined in terms of the maximum height $H_{max}$, the berm width $B_o$, and the average slope $\tan\beta = 1/s$ as follows, where $B_{max} = 2\,H_{max}\,s + B_o$ is the base width. [KB:periods.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The truncation ratio definition and its dependence on embankment geometry are directly stated in the KB (periods.md). The expression is reproduced faithfully from the source, and the geometric interpretation is consistent with standard trapezoid embankment geometry.]

$$\lambda_o = \frac{B_o}{B_{max}} = \frac{B_o}{2\,H_{max}\,s + B_o}$$

For a given height $H_{max}$, the fundamental period of the embankment increases with the truncation ratio $\lambda_o$. The homogeneity ratio $m_o$ reflects the power-law variation of the shear modulus with depth, $G(z) \approx G_o(z/H_{max})^{m_o}$, where $G_o$ is the maximum shear modulus at the base. In practice, $m_o$ is calibrated by fitting the power-law profile to the shear-wave velocity profile derived from the Ishihara (1997) stiffness model, establishing a direct link between the site characterization developed in SLOT 6 and the dynamic response parameter required by the flexible-block displacement models. The fundamental period $T_s = T_s^{(1)}$ corresponding to the first eigenvalue ($j = 1$) is the quantity used in flexible-block displacement models, as higher modes contribute negligibly to accumulated displacement under the typical frequency content of earthquake records. The practical estimation procedure involves: (1) characterizing the shear modulus profile using the Ishihara model with laboratory-derived parameters; (2) fitting a power-law depth profile to obtain $m_o$ and the base velocity $v_S^o$; (3) computing the geometric truncation ratio $\lambda_o$ from the embankment dimensions; (4) evaluating the eigenvalue $a^{(1)}$ for the resulting $(m_o,\,\lambda_o)$ pair; and (5) applying the period formula to obtain $T_s$. This procedure yields a site-specific and geometry-specific period estimate that correctly accounts for the combined influence of material stiffness, stress level, void ratio, and embankment geometry on the dynamic response of the slope.
