## SLOT 5: Modern Methods Post-Bray (2019) and Fundamental Period Estimation

The methodological development of Newmark displacement estimation continued after the Bray and Macedo (2019) calibration for shallow crustal earthquakes. A primary advance was the recognition that the 2017 subduction zone model - calibrated specifically for interface events - did not adequately distinguish between interface and intraslab mechanisms, despite evidence that these two subduction source types produce systematically different displacement demands. Macedo, Bray, and Liu (2023) addressed this gap by formulating separate seismic slope displacement procedures for interface and intraslab subduction zone earthquakes using the comprehensive NGA-Sub ground motion database. Their work demonstrated that displacement scaling differs fundamentally between interface and intraslab events - a distinction that prior models had not incorporated explicitly - and provided updated regression models that account for this source-type separation as a distinct structural predictor. The Macedo and Candia (2020) framework represents a parallel post-2019 development that restructured the coefficient estimation procedure around seismically induced displacement hazard curves rather than a single design scenario [@MacedoCandia2020]. In this approach, the pseudo-static coefficient is derived by first computing the full displacement hazard curve $\lambda(D_n > d)$ for a range of yield accelerations $k_y$ and then inverting it at the specified allowable displacement $D_n^{\star}$ and target annual exceedance rate $\lambda_0$. The resulting $k_{\max}$ is consistent with the allowable displacement level, slope dynamic properties, seismic demand, and hazard design level simultaneously, and the method was implemented in a computational platform providing seismic hazard models for Peru, Chile, Ecuador, Mexico, and the United States. [DOI:10.1016/j.soildyn.2020.106109]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Macedo and Candia (2020) methodology is confirmed by web search (DOI: 10.1016/j.soildyn.2020.106109, Soil Dynamics and Earthquake Engineering Vol 133, 2020). In the South American seismic context, the contributions of G.R. Saragoni are relevant through the Araya-Saragoni intensity measure, which combines the energy content of the Arias intensity with the number of zero crossings of the accelerogram to characterize both the energy and the frequency content of strong ground motion. This composite measure has been employed in regional assessments of earthquake-induced slope displacement in subduction zone environments, where the broad-band energy distribution and extended duration of interface records render single-parameter characterizations less efficient. While the Araya-Saragoni measure is not itself a Newmark displacement regression model, it represents an alternative intensity parameterization that may be incorporated into empirical displacement frameworks applied in the Chilean and broader South American tectonic setting. Fundamental period estimation for a flexible sliding mass is a prerequisite for applying the flexible-block displacement models. For an embankment or earth fill whose shear stiffness increases with depth according to a power-law profile $G(z) \approx G_o(z/H_{\max})^{m_o}$, the fundamental period $T_s^{(j)}$ of the $j$th vibration mode is given by: [KB:periods.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The fundamental period formula and the power-law stiffness profile are directly stated in periods.md. The connection to V_s^o and the characteristic equation eigenvalues is explicitly provided in the KB.]

$$T_s^{(j)} \simeq \frac{4\,\pi\,H_{\max}}{a^{(j)}\,(2 - m_o)\,v_S^{o}}$$

In this expression, $H_{\max}$ is the maximum height of the embankment; $a^{(j)}$ is the $j$th root of the characteristic equation - the $j$th eigenvalue - which depends on the homogeneity ratio $m_o$ and the truncation ratio $\lambda_o$; and $v_S^{o} = \sqrt{G_o/\rho}$ is the shear-wave velocity at the base of the embankment, where $G_o$ is the maximum shear modulus at depth $z = H_{\max}$ and $\rho$ is the mass density of the material. For practical evaluation of slope performance, the fundamental mode ($j = 1$) dominates and the expression reduces to a function of the base shear-wave velocity, the maximum height, and two dimensionless geometric ratios. [KB:periods.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The interpretation of the formula, the fundamental mode dominance, and the roles of the dimensionless ratios are directly supported by periods.md. The expression V_s^o = sqrt(G_o/rho) is explicitly stated in the KB.]

The truncation ratio $\lambda_o$ quantifies the influence of the berm width $B_o$ on the dynamic response of the embankment. It is defined as: [KB:periods.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The truncation ratio definition and its role are directly stated in periods.md. The formula below is reproduced from the KB.]

$$\lambda_o = \frac{B_o}{B_{\max}} = \frac{B_o}{2\,H_{\max}\,s + B_o}$$

where $B_{\max}$ is the base width of the embankment and $s$ is the reciprocal of the average slope gradient ($\tan\beta = 1/s$). For a given height $H_{\max}$, the fundamental period increases with the truncation ratio $\lambda_o$, reflecting the increasing influence of the berm width on the overall stiffness. The homogeneity ratio $m_o$ reflects the depth-dependence of the shear modulus: $G(z) \approx G_o(z/H_{\max})^{m_o}$. A larger $m_o$ implies a steeper stiffness gradient from surface to base, which lengthens the fundamental period relative to the uniform-stiffness case because a greater fraction of the mass is supported by softer material near the surface. The eigenvalue $a^{(1)}$ is determined by solving the characteristic equation associated with the coupled differential equations of the laterally constrained embankment under harmonic base excitation, and its value depends jointly on $m_o$ and $\lambda_o$.
