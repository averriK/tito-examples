## SLOT 7: Flexible Block Assembly Model and Applicability

The probabilistic ensemble for flexible-block Newmark displacement estimation assembles three models - Bray and Travasarou (2007), Bray, Macedo, and Travasarou (2017), and Bray and Macedo (2019) - under a logic-tree weighting scheme in which each model's contribution is proportional to its relevance to the tectonic source contributing to the site hazard. The aggregate displacement distribution is obtained by combining the individual model predictions using their assigned weights, propagating each model's lognormal standard deviation $\sigma_{\ln D}$ and the between-model epistemic uncertainty jointly through Monte Carlo simulation. This ensemble structure retains full model-to-model epistemic uncertainty rather than collapsing to a single best-estimate equation, which would suppress the contribution of model selection to the total uncertainty budget. The flexible-block assembly model is appropriate for slope configurations in which the sliding mass cannot be treated as an undistorted rigid body. The primary applicability criteria are: (1) the potential failure surface is deep-seated, involving substantial deformable mass; (2) the earth structure is an embankment, earth dam, compacted fill, or natural slope with a fundamental period $T_n$ appreciably longer than approximately 0.05 s; (3) the material consists of soft to medium-stiff soils, tailings, mine waste, or municipal solid waste with significant deformability under cyclic loading; and (4) the predicted spectral acceleration $S_a(1.3\,T_n)$ or $S_a(1.5\,T_n)$ is meaningfully different from the peak ground acceleration, indicating that spectral amplification at the structure's natural frequency plays a role in the seismic response. The inclusion of $M_w$ as a predictor in all three flexible-block models reflects the sensitivity of accumulated displacement to earthquake duration, which is a key factor for deep-seated failure surfaces subjected to the long-duration records characteristic of subduction zone events. The Bray and Travasarou (2007) model serves as the broadest-applicability flexible-block component, calibrated under a general tectonic-environment assumption, and provides a reference displacement estimate across both crustal and subduction settings. The Bray, Macedo, and Travasarou (2017) model receives increased weight for sites at which subduction interface earthquakes dominate the hazard, given its calibration against 810 recordings from subduction events and its piecewise treatment of very short-period sliding masses. The Bray and Macedo (2019) model receives increased weight for sites governed by shallow crustal seismicity and for near-fault scenarios in which $\mathrm{PGV}$ exceeds 115 cm s$^{-1}$. At sites in multi-tectonic environments - such as convergent margins where both crustal and subduction sources contribute to hazard - the three models are employed concurrently, with logic-tree weights reflecting the fractional hazard contribution of each source type at the design return period.
