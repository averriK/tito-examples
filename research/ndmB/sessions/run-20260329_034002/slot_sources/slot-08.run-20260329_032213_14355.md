## SLOT 8: Rigid Block Assembly Methods and Applicability

The rigid-block assembly retains four models - Ambraseys and Menu (1988), Yegian, Marciano, and Varzaghian (1991), Jibson (2007), and Saygili and Rathje (2008) - aggregated under a logic-tree scheme analogous to the flexible-block ensemble. Each model provides an estimate of the mean log-displacement and the associated dispersion, and the aggregate is formed by weighting the individual lognormal distributions and propagating the resulting uncertainty through Monte Carlo simulation. The four-model ensemble covers a range of formulation strategies: single-predictor regression in $\log_{10}$ space (Ambraseys-Menu, Yegian), multi-parameter Arias-intensity-based formulation (Jibson), and simultaneous PGA-AI multi-predictor regression with amplitude-dependent dispersion (Saygili-Rathje), thereby reducing the sensitivity of the aggregate prediction to the choice of any single parameterization. The rigid-block idealization is appropriate under a specific set of geotechnical and geometric conditions that ensure the sliding mass can be modeled as an undistorted body responding to base excitation. The primary applicability criteria are: (1) the failure surface is shallow and translational, with the center of mass of the sliding body lying close to the base of the failure surface; (2) the material is relatively stiff, with a small-strain shear modulus $G_0$ sufficiently large that the fundamental period $T_n$ of the sliding mass is short compared to the dominant periods of the input ground motion - typically $T_n \leq 0.1$ s; (3) no significant cyclic stiffness degradation is expected under the design ground motion; and (4) the failure mechanism is kinematically constrained to translational sliding without rotational components that would invalidate the rigid-body assumption. Under these conditions, the dynamic amplification within the mass is negligible and the base PGA governs the sliding response. Rigid-block methods are widely employed in preliminary design stages and in regional seismic landslide hazard mapping, where the simplified parameterization - requiring only $k_y$, $\mathrm{PGA}$, and optionally $\mathrm{AI}$ or $\mathrm{PGV}$ - reduces the data requirements compared to flexible-block procedures. This simplicity renders rigid-block analysis an effective screening tool; slopes identified as potentially hazardous at the rigid-block level are typically subjected to more detailed flexible-block or fully dynamic analyses. The Jibson (2007) and Saygili-Rathje (2008) models, by incorporating Arias intensity as a predictor, extend the predictive capability of the rigid-block framework to duration-sensitive scenarios and are preferred over the simpler single-predictor models when $\mathrm{AI}$ estimates are available. The substantial dispersion values associated with rigid-block models - particularly Jibson (2007) with $\sigma_{\ln D} \approx 1.42$ and Yegian et al. (1991) with $\sigma_{\ln D} \approx 1.04$ - reflect the inherent variability in the single-record sliding response and underscore the importance of probabilistic treatment in assessing displacement exceedance probabilities rather than relying on point estimates.
