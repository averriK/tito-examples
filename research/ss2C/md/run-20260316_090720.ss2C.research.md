# Seismotectonic Environment and Active Faults - Longonjo Project

## SLOT 1: Earthquake Generation Mechanisms Controlling Regional Seismic Hazard

The seismic source model applicable to the Longonjo project site is built on a classification of seismogenic sources into two tectonic region types: Active Shallow Crust (ASC) and Stable Continental Crust (SCC). The XAF seismic source model - based on the GEM North Africa Probabilistic Seismic Hazard Model (Poggi et al., 2020) - contains 255 sources in total, of which 150 are classified as ASC and 105 as SCC. Neither Subduction Interface (SIF) nor Subduction Intra-slab (SIS) source types are present in the model, as Angola lies on the passive margin of the African Plate, far removed from any active convergent plate boundary; subduction-related seismogenic mechanisms are therefore not applicable to this region. [KB:longonjo.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KB compile.research.md explicitly lists 150 ASC and 105 SCC sources and states the model domain. The absence of SIF and SIS mechanisms is consistent with the well-established passive margin setting of western Africa; no contradictions were detected in either the KB or external sources.]

Within the regional framework, SCC sources dominate all seismogenic zones within approximately 1,400 km of the Longonjo project site. This regime reflects the stable intraplate character of the Congo craton, where seismicity is driven by the reactivation of pre-existing crustal discontinuities under ambient lithospheric stress, rather than by active plate boundary processes. The ASC mechanism - associated with the extensional tectonics of the East African Rift System - becomes relevant only beyond approximately 1,670 km from the site, where the first ASC-regime source (MPS-800, Western Rift-Tanganyika, $M_{\max} = 7.90$ Mw) is encountered. Despite the higher magnitude potential of East African Rift sources, the large source-to-site distance renders them unlikely to contribute significantly to ground-motion hazard at the Longonjo location. [KB:longonjo.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KB explicitly states that all sources within approximately 1,400 km are SCC and that the nearest ASC source (MPS-800) is at 1,673 km, with Mmax 7.90 Mw. The conclusion about negligible ASC hazard contribution at large distance is stated directly in the KB and is physically well-founded.]

The contemporary stress field in the Angola craton region is characterized by east-west to northeast-trending compressive horizontal stresses, as inferred from focal mechanism inversions (Delvaux and Barth, 2010, cited in Schirbel et al., 2024). Seismicity is dominated by strike-slip and reverse faulting mechanisms, consistent with an intraplate compressional environment. Proposed mechanisms for seismogenic stress loading in this setting include far-field strain transfer from the active transforms of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, post-rift crustal relaxation along the rifted margin, and gravitational potential energy contrasts associated with the Angolan coastal escarpment. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md]^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The stress field description is well-documented in the PSHA KB via multiple cited studies. ## SLOT 2: Seismogenic Source Mechanisms Nearest to the Site

The Longonjo project site (latitude $-12.939727$ S, longitude $+15.240812$ E) is located entirely within the Stable Continental Crust (SCC) tectonic regime. The site lies within the Angola craton - an Archaean and Palaeoproterozoic crystalline basement province forming part of the larger Congo craton, which is recognized as a stable continental region. Within the XAF seismic source model, every seismogenic source within approximately 1,400 km of the site is classified as SCC; no ASC fault or area sources contribute to the hazard at shorter distances. [KB:longonjo.compile.research.md][KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The SCC classification of the site is stated explicitly in both KB documents. No contradictions detected.]

The nearest seismogenic source in the probabilistic model is MPS-2, a West Africa (WAF) smoothed-seismicity multi-point source of SCC type, with its nearest grid node located at 15.226 E, 12.912 S - a distance of 2.5 km from the project site. MPS-2 is present in both source-model branches with a combined weight of 1.0 and is expected to govern the seismic hazard across all return periods at the Longonjo site. Its magnitude-frequency distribution follows a truncated Gutenberg-Richter model with $a = 3.850$, $b = 1.048$ (range 0.998-1.098 under logic-tree $b$-value perturbation), $M_{\min} = 4.5$ Mw, and $M_{\max} = 6.76$ Mw (range 6.56-6.96 Mw under $\pm 0.20$ Mw perturbation). The seismogenic depth range spans 0.0-45.0 km, with a hypocentral depth distribution at 5 km (probability 0.267), 15 km (probability 0.267), 25 km (probability 0.333), and 35 km (probability 0.133), yielding a mean hypocentral depth of 18.3 km. The broader SCC regime at the site is reinforced by independent seismological studies. Neto et al. (2018) characterize Angola as one of the least seismically active regions globally even by stable continental region standards, reporting a regional Gutenberg-Richter $b$-value of $0.66 \pm 0.06$ and a completeness magnitude of 3.0 for the Angolan earthquake catalog. The second-nearest seismogenic source, MPS-1 (at 271.5 km), is also SCC-classified with $M_{\max} = 5.76$ Mw (range 5.56-5.96 Mw) and is not expected to contribute significantly to site hazard. Beyond 500 km, sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) collapsed-grid sources become relevant, with $M_{\max}$ values of 6.90 Mw; the nearest of these is MPS-1200 (Mweru-Katanga-Upemba, SCC) at 593 km. ## SLOT 3: Relevant Historical Seismic Events

The historical seismic record for the region surrounding the Longonjo site is compiled primarily from the ISC Bulletin for the period 1900 to 21 October 2025, covering latitudes 5 degrees S to 18.5 degrees S and longitudes 10 degrees E to 24.5 degrees E. The PSHA seismotectonic review sourced 249 events from this window from multiple contributing agencies including the NEIC, GCMT, ISC, and GFZ. Within a 500 km radius of the project site, the seismic catalog is sparse, with events predominantly below $M = 5$. The USGS ISC-GEM Global Instrumental Earthquake Catalogue confirms a single event with $M \geq 5.5$ within the 500 km radius - the 1914 Angola earthquake ($M_s = 6.0$) - and no event exceeding $M_w = 6.5$ has been documented within this radius. The ISC-GEM catalog applies magnitude-dependent completeness thresholds, with coverage of continental events at $M \geq 6.5$ back to 1900; the absence of $M > 6.5$ events within 500 km is therefore not an artifact of catalog incompleteness but reflects the genuinely low seismicity of the Angola craton. ## SLOT 4: Known Active Faults with Neotectonic Activity

No formally characterized Holocene- or late Quaternary-active faults meeting the ANCOLD neotectonic criterion - defined as demonstrable fault activity within the last 10,000 to 35,000 years - have been identified within a 500 km radius of the Longonjo project site. The Congo craton is a tectonically stable intraplate region, and the available geologic and seismological evidence does not support the identification of confirmed active fault structures with documented surface rupture or paleoseismic evidence of this age within the immediate region. The GEM Global Active Faults Database, while the most comprehensive global compilation of active fault traces, acknowledges substantial data gaps throughout central Africa; no active fault traces are registered for the Angola craton or the broader Congo craton in the current version of the database. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md][WEB:https://www.globalquakemodel.org/product/active-faults-database][DOI:10.1177/8755293020944182]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The PSHA KB explicitly concludes that the activity status of local faults is unclear and that evidence for coseismic surface rupture or active fault scarps is insufficient. The GEM GAF-DB coverage gap for central Africa is confirmed by web research (Styron and Pagani, 2020, DOI:10.1177/8755293020944182). No published source was found identifying a confirmed ANCOLD-criterion active fault within 500 km of the site.]

The most significant structural feature in the vicinity of the project site is the Lucapa basement fault zone (Lucapa corridor), a NE-SW-trending (050-060 degrees) crustal lineament extending over 1,600 km across the Congo craton. The corridor is 50-90 km wide and hosts Early to Late Cretaceous kimberlite and carbonatite pipes and dykes emplaced at the intersection of NE-SW and NW-SE structural trends within the Angolan shield. The Lucapa corridor can be traced southwestward to the transform zones of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. While recent instrumental seismicity - specifically the Cassongue ($M_b$ 4.2) and Balombo ($M_b$ 4.1) events - clusters within the Lucapa Graben, no paleoseismic evidence, surface fault scarp, or trenching study documenting activity within the 10,000-35,000 year ANCOLD window has been published for this structure. The PSHA seismotectonic review concludes that "it is not clear if these faults or shear zones are active," while noting the potential for brittle reactivation based on the spatial clustering of intraplate earthquakes along the corridor. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md]^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The Lucapa corridor characteristics (length, trend, Cretaceous age) are well-documented in the PSHA KB via White et al. (1995) and Guiraud et al. (2010). The absence of paleoseismic evidence is stated in the PSHA conclusions. Confidence is MEDIUM because the corridor hosts recent seismicity that is consistent with reactivation, and the absence of evidence is partly a function of the very limited paleoseismic investigations in this region rather than confirmed inactivity.]

Within the 500 km radius, the offshore Angolan passive margin contains salt-tectonic fault systems of the Outer Kwanza Basin that exhibit documented Cenozoic to Recent deformation. These systems consist of four NE-SW-striking fault arrays, each approximately 13 km long, with normal and reverse throws reaching up to 617 m and 830 m, respectively, formed above the Aptian salt decollement through post-Albian gravity-driven deformation. Erdi and Jackson (2022), cited in the PSHA document, demonstrate that two subsets of these arrays remain geologically active; however, their offshore character and the absence of paleoseismic investigations preclude formal classification under the ANCOLD neotectonic criterion. These offshore fault systems are located south-southwest of the project site, at distances of approximately 250-400 km. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md]^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The salt-tectonic fault description is based on Erdi and Jackson (2022) as cited in the PSHA KB. At the regional scale, stress analysis by Nkodia et al. (2022) indicates that pre-existing NNE-, NNW-, and N-S trending strike-slip faults and normal faults along the western Central African continental margin exhibit high slip tendency under the contemporary stress field and are therefore considered susceptible to reactivation. However, the same study notes that "there are insufficient details of the ambient stress field and the evidence for coseismic surface fault rupture or the presence of active fault scarps" to establish formal neotectonic status. Whether these structures would reactivate by seismic rupture or stable aseismic creep remains unresolved, as it depends on the frictional stability of fault rocks at the relevant crustal temperature and pressure conditions. Confidence is MEDIUM because the stress analysis indicates potential for reactivation, but the formal ANCOLD classification cannot be made from available data.]

## SLOT 5: Distance from the Project Site to the Nearest Active Fault

The Longonjo project site appears to lie within the Lucapa basement fault zone itself. The PSHA seismotectonic review explicitly states that "the project site appears to lie within the Lucapa basement fault zone," which trends at 050-060 degrees (NE-SW) and forms a 50-90 km wide tectonic corridor. If the Lucapa corridor is treated as a potentially seismogenic structure in the broadest engineering sense, the effective distance from the project site to this fault zone is approximately zero, as the site is hosted within the corridor. However, this structural proximity must be interpreted in the context of the unresolved activity status of the Lucapa structures: no confirmed active fault trace with ANCOLD-compliant neotectonic evidence has been identified within the corridor at or near the project site. ## SLOT 6: Slip Rates, Maximum Magnitude, and Distances for Major Active Faults

The slip-rate data are not encoded in the OpenQuake source-model XML files (zero sources with slip rate parameterized in the XML); occurrence rates for fault sources are instead specified directly through incremental magnitude-frequency distributions. Maximum magnitudes ($M_{\max}$) for the 115 fault sources range from 6.05 to 7.75 Mw, with the highest values concentrated among ASC fault sources in the North African orogenic belt. All of these fault sources are located several thousand kilometers from the Longonjo project site and do not represent meaningful hazard contributors at this location.
