### SLOT 1: Earthquake Generation Mechanisms Controlling Regional Seismic Hazard

The Longonjo project site is situated in central-western Angola within the Congo craton, a geologically ancient and stable continental region. The seismic hazard of the surrounding region is controlled by two of the four standard earthquake generation mechanism types recognised in probabilistic seismic hazard analysis: Stable Continental Crust (SCC) and Active Shallow Crust (ASC). Neither Subduction Interface (SIF) nor Subduction Intra-slab (SIS) mechanisms are present within or adjacent to the region, as Angola lies entirely within the interior of the African Plate at distances of several thousand kilometres from any active subduction boundary. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md][KB:longonjo.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The classification of the region as stable continental crust, and the absence of subduction sources, is directly supported by both the PSHA seismotectonic review (SRK, 2025) and the XAF seismic source model documentation in the compiled research file, both of which identify only ASC and SCC tectonic region types for Central and Southern Africa. No contradictions detected.]

The XAF seismic source model, which forms the primary PSHA input for the site, comprises 255 seismic sources classified into two tectonic region types: 150 ASC sources and 105 SCC sources. [@Ref001] The model domain spans approximately 19.5 W to 52.9 E and 28.7 S to 38.5 N, covering North Africa, West Africa, and parts of sub-Saharan Africa. Fault source geometries and kinematics are derived from the GEM North Africa Active Fault Database (NAAFD) compiled by Styron and Poggi, and the model was developed under the GEM Foundation's Global Seismic Hazard Map programme. [KB:longonjo.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Parameters of the XAF model (source count, tectonic region type classification, domain extent, and data provenance) are stated precisely in the compiled research KB document with explicit references to Poggi et al. (2020) and Styron and Poggi (2018). Internal consistency is good and no contradictions are identified across the two KB sources.]

SCC sources dominate the tectonic environment immediately surrounding the site. All seismic sources within approximately 1,400 km of the Longonjo site are classified as SCC, reflecting the stable intraplate character of the Congo craton and the broader Central and Southern African plate interior. The nearest ASC sources are associated with the East African Rift System (EARS), located at distances exceeding 1,670 km from the site. [@Ref005][@Ref002] [KB:longonjo.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The distance thresholds and tectonic region type assignments for all sources within and beyond 1,400 km are explicitly tabulated in the compiled research file, which identifies MPS-800 (Western Rift-Tanganyika) as the first ASC source at 1,673 km. This is internally consistent with the broader characterisation of the Congo craton as a stable continental region.]

Within the SCC tectonic regime, seismicity is infrequent and of low-to-moderate magnitude, primarily driven by the reactivation of pre-existing Precambrian and Mesozoic fault and shear zones under the contemporary stress field. The regional Gutenberg-Richter $b$-value for Angola has been estimated at approximately 0.66, which is characteristic of stable continental regions and reflects a relatively flat magnitude-frequency relationship compared to tectonically active regions. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The $b$-value of 0.66 $\pm$ 0.06 is cited directly from Neto et al. (2018), as reported in the PSHA seismotectonic review, which also explicitly characterises it as consistent with stable continental regions following Talwani et al. (2017). The completeness magnitude of 3.0 for 177 events provides adequate statistical basis.]

The ASC regime, represented by the EARS beyond 1,670 km, does not contribute significantly to ground-motion hazard at the Longonjo site despite its higher seismogenic potential (maximum magnitude up to $M_w$ 7.90 for the Western Rift-Tanganyika segment). The attenuation of ground motion over such distances, combined with the SCC-consistent ground-motion models that govern the hazard calculation, renders the ASC contribution negligible for all practical return periods. [KB:longonjo.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The claim regarding the negligible contribution of distant ASC sources is directly supported by the compiled research file, which states that "the nearest Active Shallow Crust sources associated with the East African Rift system lie beyond 1,670 km and ... are unlikely to contribute significantly to the ground-motion hazard at the site due to the large source-to-site distances." This is physically well-founded given standard ground-motion attenuation rates.]

### SLOT 2: Seismogenic Source Mechanisms Nearest to the Site

The Longonjo project site is located within the Stable Continental Crust (SCC) tectonic regime, which characterises the entire Angola craton and the broader Congo craton. The nearest seismogenic source in the XAF model is MPS-2, a smoothed-seismicity multi-point source from the West Africa (WAF) collapsed grid, whose nearest grid node lies at 15.226 E, $-12.912$ S, at a distance of 2.5 km from the project site. MPS-2 is classified as SCC and carries a base maximum magnitude $M_{\max} = 6.76$ Mw (epistemic uncertainty range 6.56-6.96 Mw under logic-tree perturbation), a truncated Gutenberg-Richter magnitude-frequency distribution with $a = 3.850$ and $b = 1.048$, and a seismogenic depth range of 0.0-45.0 km with a mean hypocentral depth of 18.3 km. [@Ref005][@Ref002] [KB:longonjo.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: All numerical parameters for MPS-2 are taken directly from the compiled research file, which extracts them from the XAF OpenQuake source-model XML. The source distance, tectonic region type, and MFD parameters are explicitly stated. No contradictions with other KB sources are detected.]

The two seismogenic sources within 300 km of the site -- MPS-2 at 2.5 km and MPS-1 at 271.5 km -- both belong to the WAF smoothed-seismicity group and are both classified as SCC. MPS-1 has a substantially lower $M_{\max} = 5.76$ Mw (range 5.56-5.96 Mw) and a combined weight of 1.0 across logic-tree branches, but is unlikely to contribute significantly to the hazard given both its lower maximum magnitude and its distance from the site. [@Ref005][@Ref002] The project site is therefore situated within an SCC-dominated seismogenic environment at all practically relevant distances. [KB:longonjo.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Parameters for MPS-1 and the characterisation of the SCC-dominated environment within 300 km are directly stated in the compiled research KB file. The claim that all sources within approximately 1,400 km are SCC is confirmed by the source inventory discussed therein.]

The specific seismogenic zone enclosing the site corresponds to zone 1 of the Neto et al. (2018) seismotectonic zonation of Angola -- the Angola craton -- within which the largest known event is a $M$ 6.0 earthquake in 1914. Within this zone, the majority of epicentres are located along or within grabens that cross-cut the craton and are associated with Meso-Cenozoic tectonic-magmatic reactivation. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The seismogenic zone classification and the characteristics of zone 1 (Angola craton) are described explicitly in the PSHA seismotectonic review, citing Neto et al. The site additionally lies within the Lucapa basement fault zone, a broad NE-SW-trending corridor extending over 1,600 km across the Congo craton and hosting Early to Late Cretaceous-age kimberlite and carbonatite pipes. While this structural corridor provides the most immediate seismotectonic context for the site, it is classified as an SCC structural feature subject to intraplate seismicity rather than an ASC source system. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Lucapa basement fault zone description -- including its trend, extent, and kimberlite content -- is sourced from the PSHA seismotectonic review, which draws on White et al. ### SLOT 4: Known Active Faults with Neotectonic Activity

Under the ANCOLD guideline definition, an active fault is one known to have produced historical earthquakes or exhibiting evidence of movement in Holocene time (last approximately 11,000 years), with larger faults that have moved in the Latest Pleistocene (11,000-35,000 years before present) also qualifying under a broader active criterion consistent with the 10,000-35,000-year window cited by ANCOLD. [WEB:https://ancold.org.au/product/2018-evaluating-potentially-active-tectonic-faults-for-probabilistic-seismic-hazard-assessments-in-australia/]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The ANCOLD definition of active fault is taken directly from the 2019 ANCOLD guidelines as confirmed through web research. The definition is consistent with the framing in the task specification (10,000-35,000 years). No contradictions detected.]

Within Angola and the immediate surroundings of the Longonjo site, no faults with confirmed neotectonic activity meeting the ANCOLD criterion have been identified in the published literature. The most significant local structural feature is the Lucapa basement fault zone, a 50-90 km wide NE-SW-trending corridor that hosts Cretaceous-age kimberlite and carbonatite pipes and has been subject to Meso-Cenozoic tectonic-magmatic reactivation. Although the Lucapa corridor is associated with the contemporary clustering of intraplate seismic activity and is considered susceptible to brittle reactivation of pre-existing basement-rooted faults under the current stress field, no evidence of surface rupture, fault scarps, or offset of Quaternary sediments indicative of Holocene or Latest Pleistocene activity has been documented for this zone. The PSHA seismotectonic review concludes that it is "not clear if these faults or shear zones are active" under modern neotectonic criteria. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The characterisation of the Lucapa corridor as a Precambrian to Mesozoic structural feature with Cretaceous magmatic reactivation is well-supported by the PSHA seismotectonic review and multiple peer-reviewed sources cited therein (White et al. 1995, Guiraud et al. 2010, Nkodia et al. 2022). The conclusion that active status per ANCOLD is unconfirmed is directly quoted from the SRK seismotectonic review conclusions section.]

In the broader region, the faults with the best-documented neotectonic activity within a 1,000 km radius of the site are the faults of the Okavango Rift Zone (ORZ) in northwestern Botswana. The ORZ comprises a system of NE-trending normal and transtensional faults, including the Thamalakane, Kunyere, Lecha, Tsau, Phuti, and Nare faults, formed at the southwest terminus of the East African Rift System. Neotectonic activity in the ORZ is documented by uplift along the Zimbabwe-Kalahari axis and displacement along NE-SW-trending faults between approximately 120 ka and 40 ka, within the Quaternary interval. The Thamalakane and Lecha faults are characterised by fault-zone conductors attributable to present-day channelised groundwater migration, indicating active contemporary displacement. Seismicity in the ORZ is predominantly micro-seismic, though historical events with magnitudes up to $M$ 6.7 were recorded during the period May 1952 to May 1953. The ORZ is located at approximately 900-960 km from the Longonjo site. [WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0040195117302433][WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1464343X12000167]^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The neotectonic activity of the ORZ (Quaternary displacement at 40-120 ka) and the identification of the Thamalakane and Lecha faults as actively displacing are well-supported by peer-reviewed literature cited in the web search results. The distance estimate of 900-960 km is consistent with the XAF model's MPS-1300 (Kariba-Okavango) nearest-node distance of 922 km from the compiled research file. However, the precise timing of the neotectonic activity (40-120 ka) predates the strictest ANCOLD 10,000-35,000-year window, introducing some uncertainty as to whether ORZ faults fully satisfy the ANCOLD criterion. The MEDIUM confidence reflects this ambiguity.]

Beyond 1,000 km from the site, the Luangwa Rift in eastern Zambia hosts 18 active faults with documented evidence of Quaternary activity, including fault scarps up to approximately 30 m high and offset of Quaternary sediments. The Chipola Fault (western border, approximately 207 km long) and the Molaza Fault (eastern border, approximately 142 km long) are the principal structures in this rift segment. The Luangwa Rift represents the southwestern branch of the East African Rift System and is located at approximately 1,500-1,600 km from the Longonjo site. [WEB:https://se.copernicus.org/articles/13/1731/2022/][DOI:10.5194/se-13-1731-2022]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Luangwa Rift Active Fault Database is a peer-reviewed, open-access dataset (Wedmore et al. 2022, Solid Earth) that documents 18 faults with Quaternary activity indicators (fault scarps, offset sediments). The 207 km and 142 km lengths for the Chipola and Molaza faults, respectively, are directly stated in the database documentation. The distance estimate from Longonjo is derived from geographic coordinates and is consistent with the broader regional source distances documented in the compiled research file.]

The East African Rift System in its full extent -- encompassing the Western Rift (Albertine, Tanganyika, Malawi rifts) and the Eastern Rift (Kenya Rift, Ethiopian Rift) -- constitutes the primary system of Active Shallow Crust (ASC) faults in the region. These faults have confirmed Holocene activity and in several segments are currently seismically active. The nearest segment of the EARS contributing as an ASC source in the XAF model is the Western Rift-Tanganyika segment (MPS-800), at a distance of 1,673 km from the site.
