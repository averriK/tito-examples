# Seismotectonic Setting and Seismic Hazard Characterisation: Longonjo Project Site

## SLOT 1: Earthquake generation mechanisms controlling regional seismic hazard

The seismic hazard of the region surrounding the Longonjo project site (latitude
$-12.94^\circ$ S, longitude $+15.24^\circ$ E) is controlled by the Stable Continental Crust
(SCC) seismogenic mechanism. The site is located within the Congo craton, a geologically
ancient and tectonically stable intraplate region characterised by low seismic activity relative
to active plate-boundary settings. Seismicity in this regime is infrequent and of
low-to-moderate magnitude, driven primarily by the reactivation of pre-existing Precambrian and
Mesozoic fault and shear zones under the contemporary lithospheric stress field. The regional
Gutenberg-Richter $b$-value for Angola has been estimated at $0.66 \pm 0.06$ (completeness
magnitude $M_c = 3.0$, based on 177 events from 1914 to 2014), a value characteristic of
stable continental regions [@Neto2018][@SRK2025].

The seismic source model used in the PSHA applicable to the site is the XAF model (GEM North
Africa probabilistic seismic hazard model), which covers a domain from approximately
$19.5^\circ$ W to $52.9^\circ$ E and $28.7^\circ$ S to $38.5^\circ$ N. The model comprises
255 seismic sources classified into two tectonic region types (TRTs): 150 Active Shallow Crust
(ASC) sources and 105 Stable Continental Crust (SCC) sources [@LongonjoResearch2025][@Poggi2020].
Subduction Interface (SIF) and Subduction Intra-slab (SIS) source types are absent from the
model domain; Angola lies on the passive margin of the African Plate at distances of several
thousand kilometres from any active convergent plate boundary, and no subduction-related
seismogenic mechanism is applicable to this region [@SRK2025][@LongonjoResearch2025].

Within the regional model, all seismic sources within approximately 1,400 km of the Longonjo
site are classified as SCC, reflecting the stable intraplate character of the Congo craton and
the broader Central and Southern African plate interior. The nearest ASC source in the model is
MPS-800 (Western Rift-Tanganyika segment), located at approximately 1,673 km from the site and
associated with the extensional tectonics of the East African Rift System (EARS). Despite its
higher seismogenic potential ($M_{\max} = 7.90$ Mw), the large source-to-site distance renders
the ASC contribution negligible for all practical return periods, given standard ground-motion
attenuation rates [@LongonjoResearch2025].

Intraplate seismicity within the SCC regime is generated by the reactivation of pre-existing
fault systems inherited from past tectonic events. Regional seismicity clustering along
pre-existing tectonic lineaments indicates that seismogenic rupture of basement-rooted faults
constitutes the principal earthquake generation mechanism for the site region. Proposed
mechanisms for stress loading in this intraplate setting include gravitational potential energy
variations associated with the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, post-rift crustal relaxation along the
rifted Angolan margin, far-field strain transfer from active oceanic fracture zones, and
hydroseismicity related to reservoir impoundment [@SRK2025][@Nkodia2022].

## SLOT 2: Seismogenic source mechanisms nearest to the site

The Longonjo project site is situated within Seismogenic Zone 1 of the five-zone seismotectonic
classification of Angola developed by Neto et al. (2018), designated the Angola craton (also
referred to as the Bie platform). This zone encompasses Archaean and Eburnian granitoid rocks,
gneisses, and metavolcano-sedimentary sequences forming part of the broader Congo craton. The
dominant seismogenic mechanism is SCC, with earthquake generation occurring through reactivation
of inherited basement structures. Within this zone, the largest known instrumentally recorded
event is a $M$ 6.0 earthquake in 1914, and the majority of epicentres are associated with
grabens that cross-cut the craton along Meso-Cenozoic tectonic-magmatic reactivation corridors
[@SRK2025][@Neto2018].

The site additionally lies within the Lucapa basement fault zone, a broad NE-SW-trending
corridor extending over 1,600 km across the Congo craton and hosting Early to Late Cretaceous
kimberlite and carbonatite pipes. This structural feature provides the most immediate
seismotectonic context for the project area and is classified as an SCC structural feature
subject to intraplate seismicity [@SRK2025].

Within the XAF seismic source model, the nearest seismogenic source to the Longonjo site is
MPS-2, a West Africa (WAF) smoothed-seismicity multi-point source of SCC type. Its nearest
grid node is located at $15.226^\circ$ E, $-12.912^\circ$ S, at a distance of approximately
2.5 km from the project site. MPS-2 is present in both source-model logic-tree branches with a
combined weight of 1.0 and is expected to govern seismic hazard at all return periods applicable
to the site. Its magnitude-frequency distribution follows a truncated Gutenberg-Richter model
with activity parameter $a = 3.850$, $b$-value $= 1.048$ (epistemic range $0.998$-$1.098$),
minimum magnitude $M_{\min} = 4.5$ Mw, and base maximum magnitude $M_{\max} = 6.76$ Mw (range
6.56-6.96 Mw under $\pm 0.20$ Mw logic-tree perturbation). The seismogenic depth range spans
0.0-45.0 km, with a hypocentral depth distribution at 5 km (probability 0.267), 15 km
(probability 0.267), 25 km (probability 0.333), and 35 km (probability 0.133), yielding a mean
hypocentral depth of 18.3 km [@LongonjoResearch2025].

The second-nearest seismogenic source, MPS-1 (also WAF, SCC), is located at 271.5 km from the
site with a substantially lower base $M_{\max} = 5.76$ Mw (range 5.56-5.96 Mw) and is not
expected to contribute significantly to the site hazard. Beyond 500 km, sub-Saharan Africa
(SSA) smoothed-seismicity sources become relevant; the nearest is MPS-1200
(Mweru-Katanga-Upemba, SCC) at 593 km with $M_{\max} = 6.90$ Mw. The first ASC-classified
source in the model appears at approximately 1,673 km, confirming that the SCC tectonic regime
exclusively characterises the seismogenic environment at all practically relevant distances from
the project site [@LongonjoResearch2025].

## SLOT 3: Relevant historical seismic events

The historical seismic record for Angola is sparse, reflecting both the low intrinsic seismicity
of the stable Congo craton and the disruption of monitoring activities during the civil conflict
period (approximately 1965-2002). Neto et al. (2018) reported 116 events with magnitudes between
2.6 and 6.0 recorded in the ISC Bulletin from 1914 to March 2014 for the Angolan territory.
The largest known onshore Angolan event is a $M$ 6.0 earthquake in 1914; no event exceeding
$M$ 6.5 has been documented within a 500 km radius of the Longonjo site. The absence of such
events within this radius reflects the genuinely low seismicity of the Angola craton rather than
an artifact of catalogue incompleteness [@SRK2025][@Neto2018].

The most relevant historical seismic events identified for the region are listed below.

**Events within or proximal to Angola:**

- **1914, $M_s$ 6.0, Angola craton** (Kwanza River / Capanda area, approximately 35 km from the
  Lauca dam area): The largest known historical earthquake in Angola; no macroseismic reports
  were associated with this event. This event represents the empirical upper bound on natural
  earthquake magnitude within the Angola craton [@SRK2025].
- **2023-11-19, $M_b$ 4.2, Cassongue, Bie province** (Lucapa Graben): One of the most recent
  moderate onshore Angolan events; located within the Lucapa Graben tectonic-magmatic
  reactivation zone [@SRK2025].
- **2014-11-10, $M_b$ 4.1, Balombo, Bie province** (Lucapa Graben): Located within the Lucapa
  Graben; consistent with intraplate seismicity along the Lucapa corridor [@SRK2025].
- **2013-11-19, $M$ 3.9, near Capanda, Angola craton**: Recorded on the Capanda Seismograph
  Network (SNC) operated by GAMEK [@SRK2025].
- **2013-07-23, $M$ 2.2, near Capanda, Angola craton**: Recorded on the SNC; epicentre
  approximately 17 km from the Capanda reservoir; potentially associated with hydroseismicity
  triggered by reservoir impoundment [@SRK2025].
- **2018-2024, up to $M$ 3.0, Lauca reservoir area**: A sequence of 287 induced-seismicity
  events recorded following impoundment of the Lauca reservoir (commencing late 2017); the
  largest event ($M$ 3.0) was felt in nearby villages and towns [@SRK2025][@Schirbel2024].

**Significant regional events with $M \geq 6.5$** (all at epicentral distances exceeding
900 km from the project site; sourced from the ISC Bulletin as documented in the PSHA
seismotectonic review):

- **1963-09-24, $M$ 6.5, southern Zambia** ($28.5^\circ$ E, $-16.5^\circ$ S; epicentral
  distance $\approx 1{,}479$ km): The closest $M \geq 6.5$ event in the regional catalogue
  [@SRK2025].
- **1952-1953 (May-May), $M$ 5.0-6.7, Okavango Rift Zone, NW Botswana** (epicentral distance
  $\approx 900$-$960$ km): A series of events associated with the Okavango Rift Zone fault
  system [@OkavangoJAES2012].
- **1919-10-31, $M_s$ 6.5, southern Zambia-Zimbabwe border** ($30.0^\circ$ E, $-25.0^\circ$
  S; $\approx 2{,}048$ km) [@SRK2025].
- **1942-10-09, $M_s$ 6.8, Tanzania-Malawi border region** ($35.0^\circ$ E, $-11.0^\circ$
  S; $\approx 2{,}160$ km) [@SRK2025].
- **1956-04-04, $M$ 6.5, East Africa** ($35.25^\circ$ E, $-5.0^\circ$ S;
  $\approx 2{,}366$ km) [@SRK2025].
- **2006-02-22, $M_{wb}$ 7.0, SE of Chipinge, Zimbabwe** ($33.6^\circ$ E, $-21.3^\circ$
  S; $\approx 2{,}158$ km) [@SRK2025].

## SLOT 4: Known active faults with neotectonic activity

> structured details preserved to avoid dropping essential structure during compilation.

Under the ANCOLD criterion, an active fault is defined as one that has experienced movement
within the last 10,000 to 35,000 years [@ANCOLD2019]. Within a 500 km radius of the Longonjo
project site, no faults with confirmed neotectonic activity meeting this criterion have been
identified in the published literature. The GEM Global Active Faults Database (GAF-DB), the
most comprehensive global compilation of active fault traces, acknowledges substantial data gaps
throughout central Africa; no active fault traces are registered for the Angola craton or the
broader Congo craton in the current database version
[@StyronPagani2020][@GEMGAF2020].

The most significant structural feature in the vicinity of the project site is the Lucapa
corridor introduced in the preceding section: a NE-SW-trending (approximately 050-060 degrees)
crustal lineament with a width of 50-90 km traceable southwestward to the transform zones of
the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. While recent instrumental seismicity -- specifically the Cassongue
($M_b$ 4.2, November 2023) and Balombo ($M_b$ 4.1, November 2014) events -- clusters within
the Lucapa Graben, no paleoseismic evidence, surface fault scarp, or trenching study
documenting activity within the ANCOLD 10,000-35,000-year window has been published for this
structure. The PSHA seismotectonic review concludes that "it is not clear if these faults or
shear zones are active" under modern neotectonic criteria [@SRK2025].

Within approximately 350-500 km west of the project site, the salt-tectonic fault arrays of the
Outer Kwanza Basin represent the only structures with geological evidence for geologically recent
activity in the broader onshore-to-offshore region. These comprise four NE-SW-striking fault
arrays approximately 13 km in length, with normal and reverse throws of up to 617 m and 830 m
respectively. Thickness variations demonstrate that at least two subsets remain active in the
post-Aptian geological record. Their offshore character and the absence of paleoseismic
investigations preclude formal ANCOLD classification, and their distance from the project site
precludes any direct surface-rupture hazard [@SRK2025].

At the regional scale, the faults with the best-documented neotectonic activity within a
1,000 km radius of the site are those of the Okavango Rift Zone (ORZ) in northwestern Botswana,
at approximately 900-960 km from the Longonjo site. The ORZ comprises NE-trending normal and
transtensional faults -- including the Thamalakane, Kunyere, Lecha, Tsau, Phuti, and Nare
faults -- formed at the southwestern terminus of the EARS. Neotectonic activity is documented
by displacement along NE-SW-trending fault blocks at approximately 40-120 ka and by fault-zone
electrical conductors associated with the Thamalakane and Lecha faults indicative of present-day
channelised groundwater migration. Historical seismicity in the ORZ included events reaching
$M$ 6.7 during the 1952-1953 sequence [@OkavangoTecto2017][@OkavangoJAES2012].

Beyond 1,000 km, the Luangwa Rift in eastern Zambia hosts 18 active faults with documented
Quaternary activity, including fault scarps and offset of Quaternary sediments. The Chipola
Fault (western border, approximately 207 km long) and the Molaza Fault (eastern border,
approximately 142 km long) are the principal structures, located at approximately 1,500-1,600 km
from the Longonjo site [@Wedmore2022]. The EARS faults as a whole have confirmed Holocene
activity and fully satisfy the ANCOLD criterion; the nearest ASC contribution to the XAF model
is the Western Rift-Tanganyika segment (MPS-800) at 1,673 km [@LongonjoResearch2025].

## SLOT 5: Distance from the project site to the nearest active fault

> Confidence: MIXED -- This SLOT includes HIGH-confidence content plus selected MEDIUM-confidence
> structured details preserved to avoid dropping essential structure during compilation.

The Longonjo project site appears to lie within the Lucapa basement fault zone itself, as
explicitly stated in the PSHA seismotectonic review [@SRK2025]. If this corridor is treated as
a potentially seismogenic fault zone in engineering terms, the effective distance from the
project site to the zone boundary is approximately zero. This structural proximity requires
qualification: no confirmed active fault trace meeting the ANCOLD neotectonic criterion
(activity within 10,000-35,000 years) has been identified within the corridor at or near the
project site, and the PSHA review explicitly notes that the activity status of the Lucapa fault
structures remains unresolved [@SRK2025].

Within the XAF seismic source model, no formally parameterised fault sources
(simpleFaultSource entries) are present within 500 km of the Longonjo site. All 115 fault
sources in the model are derived from the GEM North Africa Active Faults Database and are
confined to the North African orogenic belt. The nearest seismogenic point source, MPS-2,
encapsulates the distributed SCC seismicity of the West Africa region -- including the Lucapa
corridor -- within a smoothed-seismicity formulation at 2.5 km from the site
[@LongonjoResearch2025].

The nearest seismically active structure in the instrumental earthquake catalogue is the Lucapa
Graben, within which the 2023 Cassongue ($M_b$ 4.2) and 2014 Balombo ($M_b$ 4.1) events
occurred. Both epicentres lie within Bie province, the same administrative region as the
Longonjo site, and the closest documented epicentre is estimated to be less than approximately
100 km from the project site [@SRK2025].

For formally characterised active faults consistent with the ANCOLD criterion, the nearest
known example is the ORZ fault system in northwestern Botswana, at an estimated distance of
approximately 900-960 km from the Longonjo site. This estimate is consistent with the distance
to the nearest node of the XAF model source MPS-1300 (Kariba-Okavango) at 922 km
[@LongonjoResearch2025][@OkavangoTecto2017]. The nearest ASC-classified fault source in the
XAF model is MPS-800 (Western Rift-Tanganyika segment, EARS) at 1,673 km; EARS faults satisfy
the ANCOLD active-fault criterion but their distance from the site renders them irrelevant to
site-specific fault-rupture hazard assessment [@LongonjoResearch2025].

## SLOT 6: Slip rates, maximum magnitude, and distances for major active faults

> structured details preserved to avoid dropping essential structure during compilation.

The seismogenic sources and active fault systems relevant to the Longonjo PSHA are summarised
below in order of increasing distance from the project site. For smoothed-seismicity sources
(MPS-type), no explicit fault slip rate is assigned; earthquake occurrence rates are specified
through magnitude-frequency distributions derived from the regional seismicity catalogue.

**MPS-2 (WAF smoothed-seismicity, SCC -- nearest source)**
Distance from site: 2.5 km. Base $M_{\max} = 6.76$ Mw (epistemic range 6.56-6.96 Mw under
$\pm 0.20$ Mw logic-tree perturbation). Truncated Gutenberg-Richter MFD with $a = 3.850$ and
$b = 1.048$ (range 0.998-1.098). Seismogenic depth range 0.0-45.0 km; mean hypocentral depth
18.3 km. No fault slip rate assigned [@LongonjoResearch2025].

**Lucapa corridor fault structures (SCC, within Lucapa basement fault zone)**
Distance from site: site is located within the corridor; nearest Lucapa Graben seismicity at
$\leq$ approximately 100 km. No formal slip rate has been parameterised; the Lucapa faults are
subsumed within the MPS-2 smoothed-seismicity formulation rather than modelled as discrete fault
sources. The maximum observed instrumental event within the Lucapa Graben is $M_b$ 4.2
(Cassongue, November 2023); the maximum known historical event in the broader Angola craton is
$M_s$ 6.0 (1914) [@SRK2025][@LongonjoResearch2025].

**MPS-1 (WAF smoothed-seismicity, SCC)**
Distance from site: 271.5 km. Base $M_{\max} = 5.76$ Mw (range 5.56-5.96 Mw); $a = 4.047$,
$b = 1.048$. No fault slip rate assigned. Minor contribution to regional hazard
[@LongonjoResearch2025].

**MPS-1200 (Mweru-Katanga-Upemba, SSA smoothed-seismicity, SCC)**
Distance from site: 593 km. $M_{\max} = 6.90$ Mw; $a = 4.045$, $b = 0.991$. No fault slip
rate assigned [@LongonjoResearch2025].

**Okavango Rift Zone faults (Thamalakane, Kunyere, Lecha faults; NW Botswana)**
Distance from site: approximately 900-960 km (consistent with XAF model MPS-1300 nearest-node
distance of 922 km). NE-trending normal to transtensional fault system at the southwestern
terminus of the EARS. Slip rate: approximately 1 mm/yr (combined components, inferred from
published geological estimates). Maximum observed historical magnitude: $M$ 6.7 (May 1952-May
1953 sequence) [@OkavangoTecto2017][@OkavangoJAES2012][@LongonjoResearch2025].

**Luangwa Rift -- Chipola Fault (western border fault, eastern Zambia)**
Distance from site: approximately 1,500-1,600 km. Normal fault, SE-dipping; length approximately
207 km; offsets Quaternary sediments. Slip rate: 0.4-1.7 mm/yr. Estimated $M_{\max}$ up to
$M_w$ 8.1 based on empirical length-magnitude scaling. Estimated mean rupture recurrence
interval: approximately 5,000 years [@Wedmore2022].

**Luangwa Rift -- Molaza Fault (eastern border fault, eastern Zambia)**
Distance from site: approximately 1,500-1,600 km. Normal fault, NW-dipping; length approximately
142 km; offsets Quaternary sediments. Slip rate: 0.4-1.6 mm/yr. Estimated $M_{\max}$ up to
$M_w$ 7.8. Associated with historical events of approximately $M_w$ 6.7 (1919) and
approximately $M_w$ 6.5 (1940). Estimated mean rupture recurrence interval: approximately
4,000 years [@Wedmore2022].

**MPS-800 (EARS Western Rift-Tanganyika segment, ASC -- nearest ASC source)**
Distance from site: 1,673 km. Assigned $M_{\max} = 7.90$ Mw in the XAF model. Slip rates for
Western Rift border faults in this segment are generally in the range of 0.5 to several mm/yr
based on geodetic and geological estimates. This source is the nearest ASC contribution to the
XAF hazard model for the Longonjo site [@LongonjoResearch2025].

For context, the 115 XAF fault sources located in North Africa have $M_{\max}$ values ranging
from 6.05 to 7.75 Mw, with slip rates of 0.01-2.5 mm/yr documented for 15 of those sources.
These North African sources do not contribute materially to the ground-motion hazard at the
Longonjo site given their distances exceeding 4,000 km [@LongonjoResearch2025].
