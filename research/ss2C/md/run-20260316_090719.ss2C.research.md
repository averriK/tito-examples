# Seismotectonic Environment and Active Faults - Longonjo Project Site, Angola

## SLOT 1: Earthquake Generation Mechanisms Controlling Regional Seismic Hazard

The seismic hazard of the region surrounding the Longonjo project site (latitude $-12.939727$ S, longitude $+15.240812$ E) is predominantly controlled by Stable Continental Crust (SCC) seismogenic mechanisms. The project site lies within the Congo craton, which constitutes a stable continental region characterised by low levels of seismic activity relative to active plate-boundary settings. Within the North Africa/sub-Saharan Africa probabilistic seismic hazard model (XAF) that encompasses the site, the 255 seismic sources are classified into two tectonic region types: Active Shallow Crust (ASC) and Stable Continental Crust (SCC). All sources within approximately 1,400 km of the Longonjo site are assigned to the SCC tectonic region type. The nearest ASC source (MPS-800, Western Rift-Tanganyika) is located at approximately 1,673 km from the site and is associated with the East African Rift system; despite its higher maximum magnitude potential ($M_{\max}$ up to 7.90 Mw), the large source-to-site distance renders it an insignificant contributor to ground-motion hazard at Longonjo. [KB:longonjo.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The compile research KB document explicitly quantifies distances to all sources in the XAF model and confirms all sources within ~1,400 km are SCC-classified, with the first ASC source at 1,673 km. These are quantitative model parameters directly read from the source model.]

Active Shallow Crust mechanisms, while present in the broader XAF model domain (150 of the 255 sources), are confined to the East African Rift system and North African regions and do not control seismic hazard at the Longonjo site. Subduction Interface (SIF) and Subduction Intra-slab (SIS) mechanisms are entirely absent from the region: the African continent hosts no active subduction zones in proximity to the Congo craton, and the passive continental margin of western Angola is not associated with subduction-related seismicity. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The absence of SIF and SIS sources in the region is well-established; the western Angolan margin is a passive continental margin, and no subduction zone exists within thousands of kilometres. The KB PSHA document and the XAF model both confirm that only ASC and SCC tectonic region types are represented in the model, with no SIF or SIS sources.]

Intraplate seismicity within the SCC regime is generated primarily by the reactivation of preexisting fault systems inherited from past tectonic events. Regional clustering of earthquakes along pre-existing tectonic lineaments indicates that seismogenic rupture of basement-rooted faults constitutes the principal earthquake generation mechanism for the site region. Potential drivers for stress loading include gravitational potential energy variations associated with the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, post-rift crustal relaxation along the rifted Angolan margin, far-field strain transfer from active oceanic fracture zones, and hydroseismicity related to reservoir impoundment. ## SLOT 2: Seismogenic Source Mechanisms Nearest to the Site

The Longonjo project site is situated within Seismogenic Zone 1 - the Angola craton - as defined by the five-zone seismotectonic classification of Angola developed by Neto et al. (2018). This zone encompasses the Angola craton (also referred to as the Bie platform), characterised by Precambrian basement consisting of Archaean and Eburnian granitoid rocks, gneisses, and metavolcano-sedimentary sequences forming part of the broader Congo craton. The dominant seismogenic mechanism for the site is Stable Continental Crust (SCC), reflecting the intraplate tectonic setting with earthquake generation occurring through reactivation of inherited basement structures. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KB PSHA report explicitly places the project site within Seismogenic Zone 1 (Angola craton) as defined by Neto et al. Within the XAF seismic source model, the nearest seismogenic source to the Longonjo site is the WAF (West Africa) collapsed multi-point source MPS-2, whose nearest grid node is at $15.226^\circ$ E, $12.912^\circ$ S, at a distance of approximately 2.5 km from the project site. MPS-2 is classified as SCC and is present in both source-model logic-tree branches with a combined weight of 1.0. Its magnitude-frequency distribution follows a truncated Gutenberg-Richter model with activity parameter $a = 3.850$, $b$-value $= 1.048$ (epistemic range $0.998$-$1.098$), and a base maximum magnitude $M_{\max} = 6.76$ Mw (range 6.56-6.96 Mw under $\pm 0.20$ Mw logic-tree perturbation). The seismogenic depth range is 0.0-45.0 km with a mean hypocentral depth of 18.3 km. This source is expected to control ground-motion hazard across all return periods at the site within the SCC tectonic regime. ## SLOT 4: Known Active Faults with Neotectonic Activity

The ANCOLD criterion defines a fault as active if it has experienced movement within the last 10,000 to 35,000 years. Within the 500 km radius of the Longonjo site, the characterisation of faults meeting this criterion is constrained by the sparse coverage of paleoseismic investigations in central-western Africa and the absence of documented coseismic surface rupture or Holocene fault scarps. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KB PSHA report explicitly notes that "there are insufficient details of the ambient stress field and the evidence for coseismic surface fault rupture or the presence of active fault scarps" in western Africa (citing Nkodia et al., 2022). The ANCOLD criterion is invoked in the task context; the lack of paleoseismic evidence is confirmed by the source documents.]

The most significant fault structure in proximity to the project site is the Lucapa basement fault zone (also referred to as the Lucapa corridor), a NE-SW-trending structural zone extending over 1,600 km across the Congo craton with a width of 50-90 km. The corridor hosts Early to Late Cretaceous kimberlite and carbonatite pipes at the intersection of NE-SW and NW-SE structural trends. Within the Lucapa zone, fault and fracture trends are east-northeast, consistent with internal R-shear geometry for a dextral sense of displacement. The Lucapa fault zone can be traced southwestward to the transforms of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. The occurrence of recent seismicity, including the Cassongue ($m_b 4.2$, November 2023) and Balombo ($m_b 4.1$, November 2014) earthquakes within the Lucapa Graben, provides evidence of ongoing seismogenic reactivation potential. However, documented Holocene coseismic surface rupture or fault scarps confirming activity within the ANCOLD criterion window have not been reported for these structures in the available literature. [KB:605368_Longonjo TSF_PSHA_Seismotectonic Review_Draft Letter Report_20251031.docx.md][DOI:10.1029/2022GC010377]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The geometry, extent, and seismicity of the Lucapa fault zone are well-documented in the KB PSHA report. The absence of Holocene surface rupture evidence is acknowledged in both the PSHA report and in Nkodia et al. (2022). The characterisation is internally consistent across sources.]

Regional stress analyses indicate a high slip tendency for NNE-, NNW-, and N-S-trending strike-slip faults and normal faults within the contemporary stress field along the western African continental margin. Within the cratonic interior, NW- and N-S-trending thrust faults represent the most probable structures for reactivation, while strike-slip faults are comparatively less likely. The frictional stability of these fault systems, which determines whether reactivation occurs as stable creep or seismic rupture, remains poorly constrained in this region due to the absence of detailed subsurface characterisation. [DOI:10.1029/2022GC010377]^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The stress analysis conclusions are from Nkodia et al. (2022) as referenced via DOI citation confirmed from web search. The KB PSHA report also cites these findings. The assessment of frictional stability uncertainty is consistent with the general state of knowledge for intraplate African faults, though specific slip tendency values are not quantified in the available KB extracts.]

Within approximately 350-500 km west of the project site, the salt tectonic fault arrays of the Outer Kwanza Basin represent the only demonstrably active fault systems with geological evidence for recent activity. These comprise four NE-SW-striking fault arrays approximately 13 km in length, with normal and reverse throws of up to 617 m and 830 m respectively. Thickness variations associated with these fault arrays demonstrate that at least two subsets were active at different times and remain active in the post-Aptian geological record. These offshore and near-coastal structures are too distant to pose a direct surface-rupture hazard at the project site, but their activity reflects the ongoing tectonic regime of the Angolan passive margin. The Precambrian shear zones and fault zones within the Angola craton exhibit structural geometries that are favourably oriented for reactivation in the contemporary stress field; however, the available evidence for present-day activity meeting the ANCOLD neotectonic criterion (10,000-35,000 years) is insufficient to formally classify them as active faults under that definition.
