# Introduction and Scope of Report

A Multi-channel Analysis of Surface Waves (MASW) geophysical survey was conducted on the proposed Longonjo TSF Site. The survey was designed by SRK before the execution in the field by a geophysical services field contractor, Applied Scientific Services & Technologies Pty Ltd (ASST). The purpose of this report is to document the process, results, and provide an interpretation of the data recorded during this geophysical survey.

# Geophysical Survey Design, Instrumentation and Quality Control

This section summarises the geophysical survey design (Figure 2-1) and data quality control for the MASW surveys.

The MASW survey was conducted for single test positions at an interval spacing of 10 m along the planned survey lines using a single 24-channel seismic recording system, 24-channel spread and a sledgehammer. Quality control was done on the field data by ASST by monitoring the signal-to-noise levels in the data during the collection of the data. If erroneous, the data point was repeated until the signal-to-noise ratio was acceptable. Note that the MASW data was processed by ASST and reviewed by SRK.

# Results

## MASW

Note that all measurements concerning depth are relative to ground surface level.

The MASW model along line 1 shows that there are three discontinuous layers of differing shear wave velocities above the faster shear-wave velocity basement rock (Figure 4-1). A slow velocity zone (L1) is mapped near the surface and has shear wave velocities ranging from ~150 m/s to ~190 m/s. The depth of L1 ranges from ~2 m to ~10 m. Below the L1 shear wave velocity zone is a faster shear wave velocity zone (L2) with a shear wave velocity of ~200 m/s to ~300 m/s that extends to a depth of ~10-15 m from the surface below L1. The third shear wave velocity zone (L3) is situated below L2 and above the faster shear wave velocity basement. L3 has shear wave velocities ranging from ~150 m/s to ~190 m/s. The three near-surface shear wave velocity zones are not continuous but are interrupted by various lithological features along the profile. A low small-strain shear stiffness zone (LS) is mapped in the near-surface at a depth of <10 m. Figure 4-2 isolates the low small-strain shear stiffness in two categories 0-10 MPa (red) and 10-20 MPa (blue). There are only a few isolated areas between 10-20 MPa mapped near the surface of the survey for this model. Figure 4-3 shows an approximate depth to the basement rock for survey line L1. The depth to basement varies between 10-20 m, the shallowest basement occurs on the western side of the TSF site.

# Discussion

Figure 5-1 shows historically mapped faults and lithological contacts. The small-strain shear stiffness calculated from the MASW data is plotted in 3D in Figure 5-2. The black dashed lines show a region of low small-strain shear stiffness values that occur at greater depths when compared to the remainder of the MASW model. These relatively deep low small-strain shear stiffness values could indicate a possible extension of the fault to the north extending through the TSF area or a deeply weathered zone.

The low stiffness zones mapped on the western side of the TSF site appear to correspond to the historical lithological contact mapped in the area. If it is necessary to confirm the location of these contacts, other geophysical methods will have to be applied, preferably electrical resistivity imaging.

# Conclusions

Through the use of the MASW geophysical datasets, lithological layering, fracture zones, and stiffness were mapped in the TSF site area of interest. The main findings are:

The MASW data show three discontinuous layers of alternating low and high shear-wave velocities that may correspond to the subsurface layers above the faster shear-wave velocity basement.

Fractures or deeply weathered zones were mapped throughout the TSF site.

A region of low small-strain shear stiffness values that occur at greater depths relative to the remainder of the survey is mapped on the eastern side of the TSF and is possibly an extension of a fault zone mapped to the north of the site.

On the western side of the TSF, lower small-strain shear stiffness values are mapped near a historically mapped lithological contact.

In general, there are only isolated areas of small-strain shear stiffness values 10-20 MPa, while there are no clear areas mapped with lower stiffness than 10-20 MPa.

The basement rock is mapped to a depth of ~10-14 m on the western side of the TSF.

The basement rock is mapped to a depth of ~20-27 m on the eastern side of the TSF.
