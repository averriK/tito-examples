# Epistemic Logic-Tree Weight Calibration Under Unit-Sum Constraint

## SLOT 1: Extension of the Error-Minimization Formulation to the Unit-Sum Weight Constraint

The non-negative least-squares (NNLS) formulation established in the knowledge base constitutes the direct mathematical precursor to the unit-sum-constrained problem. In log-spectral coordinates, the design matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times K}$ has entries $\tilde{A}_{ik} = \ln S_{a,k}(T_i)$, where $S_{a,k}(T_i)$ is the pseudo-spectral acceleration of the $k$-th candidate GMPE branch at period $T_i$; the log-target vector $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ has components $y_i = \ln S_a^{\mathrm{obj}}(T_i)$; and the weight vector $\mathbf{w} = (w_1, \dots, w_K)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^K$ collects the branch weights to be determined. The NNLS problem of KB SLOT 5 imposes only non-negativity on the weights, leaving the sum $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}$ unconstrained [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@WikipediaNNLS]. The extension to the unit-sum case appends the single linear equality $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$, which converts the feasible set from the non-negative orthant $\mathbb{R}^K_+$ into the standard probability simplex.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The design matrix construction, log-spectral target, and NNLS starting formulation are taken directly from KB SLOT 4 and SLOT 5 of spec.compile.research.md, which are cited. The identification of the unit-sum constraint as an equality appended to NNLS is a direct mathematical generalization consistent with that KB content.]

The simplex-constrained (SC) problem is stated as follows. The objective function retains the mean-squared log-spectral error of the KB formulation, and the feasible region $\Delta^K$ is the probability simplex:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This lead-in paragraph introduces the SC problem by retaining the KB SLOT 5 log-space objective and appending the unit-sum constraint [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The problem structure is definitional and requires no factual claims beyond the KB formulation.]

$$(\mathrm{SC}) \quad \min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K} \; f(\mathbf{w}) := \bigl\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\bigr\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad w_k \geq 0, \; k = 1, \dots, K, \quad \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1.$$

The feasible set is the probability simplex $\Delta^K = \{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K : w_k \geq 0 \; \forall k,\; \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1\}$. The set $\Delta^K$ is a compact convex polytope: it is the convex hull of the $K$ standard basis vectors $\mathbf{e}_1, \dots, \mathbf{e}_K$ in $\mathbb{R}^K$, bounded between the hyperplane $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$ and the $K$ non-negativity half-spaces. This set is non-empty for any $K \geq 1$.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The SC problem formulation follows directly from the NNLS problem in KB SLOT 5 by appending the equality constraint. The characterization of the probability simplex as a compact convex polytope is standard convex analysis [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf]. No external assumption beyond what is established in the KB and standard optimization theory is required.]

The objective function $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ expands to the strictly quadratic form:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This lead-in states that the squared-norm objective admits a standard quadratic expansion; the expansion is algebraically immediate from the definition of the Euclidean norm and is consistent with the KB SLOT 3 and SLOT 5 regression formulations [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. No factual claim is made that requires external verification.]

$$f(\mathbf{w}) = \mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{Q}\, \mathbf{w} - 2\,\mathbf{c}^\top \mathbf{w} + \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2,$$

where $\mathbf{Q} = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$ is the Gram matrix of the log-spectral design matrix and $\mathbf{c} = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^K$ is the vector of cross-correlations between each candidate log-spectrum and the log-target. The Hessian of $f$ is $\nabla^2 f = 2\mathbf{Q}$. When $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ has full column rank -- which holds whenever no candidate GMPE has a log-spectrum expressible as a linear combination of the others over the period mesh -- $\mathbf{Q}$ is positive definite and $f$ is strictly convex over $\mathbb{R}^K$ [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. A strictly convex objective minimized over the compact non-empty convex set $\Delta^K$ attains its minimum at exactly one point; this guarantees both existence and uniqueness of the solution $\mathbf{w}^* \in \Delta^K$ [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Gram matrix structure, the positive-definiteness condition under full column rank, and the strict-convexity uniqueness argument are all standard results in convex optimization theory [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf]. The full-column-rank condition is discussed in KB SLOT 3 of spec.compile.research.md. The Weierstrass extreme value theorem guaranteeing existence over a compact set is a classical result.]

The Lagrangian associated with problem (SC) introduces a dual variable $\mu_k \geq 0$ for each inequality constraint $w_k \geq 0$ and an unrestricted dual variable $\nu \in \mathbb{R}$ for the equality constraint $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This lead-in describes the multiplier structure for the mixed-constraint Lagrangian. The assignment of non-negative multipliers to inequality constraints and an unrestricted multiplier to the equality constraint is a standard construction in Lagrangian duality theory [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf]. No empirical claims are made.]

$$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, \boldsymbol{\mu}, \nu) = f(\mathbf{w}) - \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mu_k\, w_k + \nu\!\left(\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} - 1\right).$$

The sign convention follows the standard form for inequality constraints $w_k \geq 0$ written as $-w_k \leq 0$, so the Lagrangian subtracts the inequality-multiplier term and adds the equality-multiplier term. Because $\nu$ enters through an equality constraint, it is not required to be non-negative; it is free to take any real value and will be determined by the stationarity and primal-feasibility conditions at the optimum [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Lagrangian construction for a quadratic program with mixed equality and inequality constraints is standard; the sign convention and the fact that the equality multiplier is unrestricted in sign are textbook results in convex optimization [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf]. The formulation here is a direct extension of the NNLS Lagrangian implicit in KB SLOT 5 [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@Mullen2012].]

Because the objective is convex and the constraints are linear, the problem (SC) is a convex quadratic program (QP), and the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions are both necessary and sufficient for global optimality (no constraint qualification is needed beyond linear independence of the active constraints, which is satisfied here since all constraints are linear). The KKT conditions comprise five components. First, the stationarity condition $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}} \mathcal{L} = \mathbf{0}$ gives:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This paragraph states that KKT conditions are necessary and sufficient for convex QPs with linear constraints. This is a standard theorem of convex optimization [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf]. The LICQ argument is standard and holds whenever all constraints are linear. The stationarity equation displayed below is obtained by differentiating the Lagrangian with respect to w.]

$$2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y}\right) - \boldsymbol{\mu} + \nu\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}.$$

Denoting the $k$-th component of the objective gradient at $\mathbf{w}^*$ as $g_k^* := [\nabla f(\mathbf{w}^*)]_k = 2[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y})]_k$, the stationarity condition becomes $g_k^* + \nu = \mu_k$ for each $k = 1, \dots, K$. Second, primal feasibility of the equality constraint requires $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}^* = 1$. Third, primal feasibility of the inequality constraints requires $w_k^* \geq 0$ for all $k$. Fourth, dual feasibility of the inequality multipliers requires $\mu_k \geq 0$ for all $k$, which through the stationarity condition is equivalent to $g_k^* + \nu \geq 0$ for all $k$. Fifth, complementary slackness requires $\mu_k w_k^* = 0$ for all $k$, i.e., $(g_k^* + \nu)\, w_k^* = 0$ for all $k$ [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf][WEB:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karush%E2%80%93Kuhn%E2%80%93Tucker_conditions].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KKT necessary and sufficient conditions for convex QPs with linear constraints are a fundamental theorem of constrained optimization [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf]. The equivalence of the sufficiency argument with the linear constraint qualification (LICQ) holding everywhere on the simplex boundary is standard. The component-wise gradient notation is introduced consistently with the KB formulation [KB:spec.compile.research.md].]

Combining the dual-feasibility and complementary-slackness conditions yields the following compact characterization of the KKT system. Define the passive set $\mathcal{P}^* = \{k : w_k^* > 0\}$ (indices of GMPE branches assigned positive weight) and the active set (zero-weight set) $\mathcal{Z}^* = \{k : w_k^* = 0\}$. At the unique global minimizer $\mathbf{w}^*$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This paragraph introduces the passive/active-set partition, which is standard in active-set methods for constrained QPs [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@Mullen2012], and leads into the compact KKT display. The definitions of $\mathcal{P}^*$ and $\mathcal{Z}^*$ are consistent with the Lawson-Hanson terminology from KB SLOT 5, extended by the presence of the equality multiplier $\nu$.]

$$g_k^* = -\nu \quad \forall\, k \in \mathcal{P}^*, \qquad g_k^* \geq -\nu \quad \forall\, k \in \mathcal{Z}^*, \qquad \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}^* = 1.$$

This characterization reveals the structural role of the equality multiplier $\nu$: at the optimum, every branch with strictly positive weight shares the same value of the projected gradient $g_k^* = -\nu$, while every branch assigned zero weight has a projected gradient that is no less than this common level. The scalar $\nu$ acts as a uniform shift on the gradient components -- a Lagrange price for the unit-sum restriction -- that modulates which branches receive positive weight. This should be compared with the NNLS KKT conditions from KB SLOT 5, where the absence of the equality multiplier ($\nu = 0$) requires $g_k = 0$ for positive-weight branches and $g_k \geq 0$ for zero-weight branches [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@Mullen2012]: the unit-sum constraint replaces the zero threshold with the (potentially non-zero) common level $-\nu$, thereby permitting a different balance among the $K$ branches.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The compact KKT characterization is derived algebraically from the stationarity, dual-feasibility, and complementary-slackness conditions stated in the previous paragraph, which are well-established results. The comparison with the NNLS KKT conditions follows directly from KB SLOT 5 [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@Mullen2012], where the no-equality-constraint case has $\nu = 0$ by construction.]

The problem (SC) belongs to the class of simplex-constrained quadratic programs, a well-studied subfamily of convex QP. Algorithmically, (SC) is amenable to active-set generalization of the Lawson-Hanson algorithm -- extending the partition strategy of KB SLOT 5 to account for the equality constraint [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@Mullen2012] -- or to interior-point methods, or to the simplex-projection operator described in the literature [ARXIV:1309.1541]. In the R programming environment, (SC) is solvable via the `quadprog` solver (with `meq = 1` to designate the unit-sum row of `Amat` as an equality constraint) or via the `CVXR` disciplined convex programming interface with the constraint `sum(w) == 1` appended to the non-negativity constraint [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The reduction from $K$ free variables to $K - 1$ free variables by substituting $w_K = 1 - \sum_{k=1}^{K-1} w_k$ and imposing $w_k \geq 0$ for $k = 1, \dots, K-1$ and $\sum_{k=1}^{K-1} w_k \leq 1$ also transforms (SC) into a pure inequality-constrained QP in $K - 1$ dimensions, which can then be passed to any standard bounded-variable least-squares solver.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The connection to the Lawson-Hanson active-set strategy and the CVXR/quadprog implementation references are grounded in KB SLOT 5 and SLOT 7 [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@Mullen2012]. The simplex-projection approach is documented in [ARXIV:1309.1541]. The variable-elimination reduction to a pure inequality-constrained QP is a standard algebraic manipulation.]

## SLOT 2: Validity of Post-Hoc Normalization versus Direct Imposition of the Unit-Sum Constraint

Let $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{nnls}} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w} \geq 0} f(\mathbf{w})$ denote the NNLS solution produced by the formulation of KB SLOT 5, and let $s = \mathbf{1}^\top\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{nnls}} = \sum_{k=1}^K \hat{w}_k > 0$ be its $\ell_1$-norm (assuming at least one branch receives positive weight, which holds whenever the target $\mathbf{y}$ is not in the null space of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$). Post-hoc normalization produces the vector $\bar{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{nnls}} / s$, which satisfies $\bar{w}_k \geq 0$ for all $k$ and $\mathbf{1}^\top\bar{\mathbf{w}} = 1$ by construction, and therefore lies in $\Delta^K$. The central question is whether $\bar{\mathbf{w}}$ coincides with the solution $\mathbf{w}^*$ of problem (SC) from SLOT 1 -- that is, whether the two-step procedure of (i) solving the NNLS problem and (ii) normalizing the result yields the same outcome as solving the unit-sum constrained problem directly [KB:spec.compile.research.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The problem statement is a direct consequence of the KB SLOT 5 NNLS formulation and the unit-sum extension derived in SLOT 1. The notation is consistent with both the KB and the SLOT 1 analysis. The feasibility of the normalized vector is immediate from its construction.]

To determine when $\bar{\mathbf{w}} = \mathbf{w}^*$, the gradient of $f$ at the normalized point is examined. Using the decomposition $\bar{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{nnls}} / s$, the gradient is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This lead-in announces the analytical strategy of evaluating the gradient at the normalized point. The decomposition identity in the following display follows by linearity of the gradient of a quadratic form and is algebraically exact. No external claims are made.]

$$\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{w}}) = 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{w}} - \mathbf{y}\right) = \frac{1}{s}\,\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{nnls}}) - \frac{2(s-1)}{s}\,\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}.$$

For each branch $k$ in the passive set $\mathcal{P} = \{k : \hat{w}_k > 0\}$, the NNLS KKT conditions from KB SLOT 5 require $[\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{nnls}})]_k = 0$ [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@Mullen2012]. Substituting this into the expression above gives, for $k \in \mathcal{P}$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This paragraph applies the vanishing-gradient KKT condition for positive-weight branches from KB SLOT 5 [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@Mullen2012] to the gradient decomposition derived above. The substitution is algebraically direct; the result in the following display is obtained by setting $[\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{w}})]_k = 0$.]

$$[\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{w}})]_k = -\frac{2(s-1)}{s}\,[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k.$$

For $\bar{\mathbf{w}}$ to satisfy the unit-sum KKT conditions derived in SLOT 1 -- which require $[\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{w}})]_k = -\nu$ (a single constant) for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$ -- it is necessary that $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$ be the same constant for every $k \in \mathcal{P}$. The vector $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^K$ has components $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln S_{a,k}(T_i) \cdot \ln S_a^{\mathrm{obj}}(T_i)$, the inner product of the log-spectrum of GMPE $k$ with the log-target spectrum over the period mesh. Because distinct GMPEs possess distinct spectral shapes -- differing in their scaling, attenuation, and site-amplification parameterizations -- these inner products are generically different for $k \in \mathcal{P}$, and the necessary condition fails generically [KB:spec.compile.research.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The gradient decomposition is an algebraic identity derived from the linearity of the gradient operator of a quadratic form. The NNLS KKT condition $[\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{w}})]_k = 0$ for positive-weight branches is stated explicitly in KB SLOT 5 [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@Mullen2012]. The interpretation of $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$ as a spectral inner product follows from the design matrix definition in KB SLOT 4. The claim that these inner products are generically distinct for different GMPEs is well-supported by the KB discussion of spectral shape diversity.]

The necessary condition for normalization validity can be stated precisely. Let $s \neq 1$ (the non-trivial case). Post-hoc normalization produces the exact unit-sum minimizer $\bar{\mathbf{w}} = \mathbf{w}^*$ if and only if the cross-correlations $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$ are identical for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$, or equivalently, if the vector $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$ restricted to the passive-set indices is a constant vector. The corresponding Lagrange multiplier is then $\nu = 2(s-1)/s \cdot [\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$ (common value). When $s = 1$ the normalization is an identity transformation and is trivially valid.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This is a direct consequence of the gradient analysis in the preceding paragraph. The condition that the passive-set components of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$ be constant is derived algebraically from requiring the unit-sum KKT conditions to hold at $\bar{\mathbf{w}}$. The identified Lagrange multiplier value follows by substituting the condition back into the stationarity equation.]

A concrete counterexample demonstrates the failure of post-hoc normalization under generic conditions. Let $n = 2$ period ordinates and $K = 2$ candidate GMPE branches, with log-spectral design matrix and log-target vector:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This lead-in introduces a minimal $(n = 2, K = 2)$ counterexample. The specific matrix values are chosen to yield a tractable closed-form NNLS solution with both positive components, making the example transparent and arithmetically verifiable. No KB or external source is required to validate the problem setup, which is self-contained.]

$$\tilde{\mathbf{A}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{y} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}.$$

The Gram matrix and cross-correlation vector are:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This is a purely computational lead-in presenting the Gram matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ and the cross-correlation vector $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$ for the example matrices. Both quantities are obtained by direct matrix multiplication and are arithmetically exact.]

$$\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 5 \\ 5 & 10 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y} = \begin{bmatrix} 9 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix}.$$

With $\det(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}) = 25$, the unconstrained OLS solution is $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{OLS}} = (1/25)\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}10&-5\\-5&5\end{smallmatrix}\bigr](9,12)^\top = (6/5,\, 3/5)^\top$. Both components are positive, so the NNLS solution coincides with the OLS solution: $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{nnls}} = (6/5,\, 3/5)^\top$, with $s = 9/5$. Post-hoc normalization gives $\bar{\mathbf{w}} = (2/3,\, 1/3)^\top$. The cross-correlations are $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_1 = 9$ and $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_2 = 12$, which are unequal, confirming that the necessary condition fails. The direct unit-sum solution is found by parametrizing the simplex as $w_1 = t$, $w_2 = 1 - t$ for $t \in [0, 1]$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The example is self-contained and arithmetically verifiable. The OLS solution is computed from the closed-form normal equations of KB SLOT 3 [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The non-negativity of both OLS components makes the NNLS solution equal to the OLS solution, which is established in KB SLOT 5. All numerical values are directly checkable.]

$$f(t,\, 1 - t) = \bigl(t + 3(1-t) - 3\bigr)^2 + \bigl(2t + (1-t) - 3\bigr)^2 = 4t^2 + (t - 2)^2 = 5t^2 - 4t + 4.$$

Setting $df/dt = 10t - 4 = 0$ yields the direct unit-sum minimizer $t^* = 2/5$, giving $\mathbf{w}^* = (2/5,\, 3/5)^\top$ with $f(\mathbf{w}^*) = 5(4/25) - 4(2/5) + 4 = 16/5 = 3.20$. The post-hoc normalized solution attains $f(\bar{\mathbf{w}}) = 5(4/9) - 4(2/3) + 4 = 32/9 \approx 3.56$. Since $16/5 < 32/9$, the normalized solution is strictly suboptimal: the direct unit-sum formulation reduces the objective by approximately $0.35$ log-spectral units squared relative to the normalized approximation. The magnitude of this suboptimality depends on the specific spectral shapes of the candidate GMPEs and the target and is not bounded a priori.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The simplex parametrization and the resulting univariate quadratic in $t$ follow directly from the substitution $w_2 = 1 - w_1$. The minimum at $t^* = 2/5$ is verified by setting the derivative to zero. The objective values $f(\mathbf{w}^*) = 16/5 = 3.20$ and $f(\bar{\mathbf{w}}) = 32/9 \approx 3.56$ are computed by direct substitution and are arithmetically exact. The strict inequality confirms suboptimality of the normalized solution.]

The geometric origin of the failure is illuminating. The level sets of $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ are ellipsoids in $\mathbb{R}^K$ centered at the unconstrained minimizer $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{OLS}}$, with principal-axis directions and semi-axis lengths determined by the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of $\mathbf{Q} = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$. The NNLS solution is the point in the non-negative orthant $\mathbb{R}^K_+$ that is closest to $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{OLS}}$ in the metric induced by $\mathbf{Q}$, while the unit-sum solution $\mathbf{w}^*$ is the point on the simplex $\Delta^K$ that is $\mathbf{Q}$-closest to $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{OLS}}$. Post-hoc normalization performs a radial (Euclidean) rescaling of the NNLS solution from the non-negative orthant onto the simplex hyperplane $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$. This radial projection preserves the $\mathbf{Q}$-distance ranking -- and hence the optimality ordering among simplex points -- if and only if all rays through the origin intersect the simplex in the same order as their $\mathbf{Q}$-distance to $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{OLS}}$. For this to hold for arbitrary targets $\mathbf{y}$ and arbitrary feasible sets, the ellipsoidal level sets of $f$ must be spherical, i.e., $\mathbf{Q} = c\mathbf{I}$ for some positive scalar $c$. The condition $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \propto \mathbf{I}$ requires the columns of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ to be mutually orthogonal and of equal Euclidean norm; in spectral terms, this requires the candidate GMPEs to have uncorrelated log-spectral shapes and equal integrated log-spectral energy over the period mesh -- a condition that does not hold for any realistic ensemble of physically based ground-motion models [KB:spec.compile.research.md][WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf].^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The geometric interpretation via ellipsoidal level sets and Q-metric projection is a standard result in convex optimization [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf]. The claim that Q proportional to I is equivalent to column orthogonality and equal norms is a direct algebraic result. The statement that real GMPE ensembles do not satisfy this condition is well-supported qualitatively by the KB discussion of spectral shape diversity and GMPE construction [KB:spec.compile.research.md], but the KB does not provide a quantitative demonstration of the non-sphericity of Q for any specific GMPE set, hence the MEDIUM rating.]

An additional perspective on the failure is provided by analyzing $f$ along the ray $\{c\bar{\mathbf{w}} : c > 0\}$. Along this ray, $f(c\bar{\mathbf{w}}) = c^2\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\|_2^2 - 2c\,\mathbf{y}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{w}} + \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2$, a univariate quadratic in $c$ minimized at $c^* = (\mathbf{y}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}) / \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\|_2^2$. The NNLS solution satisfies $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{nnls}} = s\bar{\mathbf{w}}$, meaning the optimal scaling along direction $\bar{\mathbf{w}}$ within the non-negative orthant is $c^* = s$. The unit-sum constraint forces $c = 1$. The two coincide only when $s = 1$, i.e., when the NNLS solution already satisfies the unit-sum constraint, which is a non-generic event determined entirely by the spectral properties of the candidate GMPEs and the specific target hazard spectrum under consideration. Outside this degenerate case, the unit-sum constraint imposes a scaling that conflicts with the minimizing scaling along the NNLS direction, and the objective at $\bar{\mathbf{w}}$ exceeds that at $\mathbf{w}^*$.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The ray analysis is an algebraic derivation: expanding $f(c\bar{\mathbf{w}})$ and minimizing over $c$ yields the expression for $c^*$ by standard calculus. The identification of $s$ as the NNLS optimal scaling along the direction $\bar{\mathbf{w}}$ follows from $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{nnls}} = s\bar{\mathbf{w}}$ and the NNLS optimality. The conclusion that $s = 1$ is a non-generic event is a consequence of the fact that $\sum_k \hat{w}_k^{\mathrm{nnls}}$ depends continuously and non-trivially on $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ and $\mathbf{y}$, with no reason for it to equal unity except by coincidence.]

In summary, post-hoc normalization of the NNLS solution is not a mathematically valid substitute for direct imposition of the unit-sum constraint. The NNLS objective $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ is not homogeneous of degree zero in $\mathbf{w}$; its value changes under rescaling of $\mathbf{w}$ in a manner that depends on the cross-correlations $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$ between the candidate GMPE spectra and the target. Normalization is at best a heuristic approximation whose quality degrades as the ratio $s = \mathbf{1}^\top\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\mathrm{nnls}}$ departs from unity, which in turn depends on the spectral diversity and hazard-level characteristics of the specific GMPE ensemble and target spectrum. The exact solution over the probability simplex requires solving problem (SC) directly, using a QP solver capable of handling the equality constraint $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$ simultaneously with the non-negativity constraints, as established in SLOT 1 above [KB:spec.compile.research.md][WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The conclusion is fully supported by the preceding analysis: the gradient decomposition demonstrates the generic failure of normalization, the explicit counterexample verifies strict suboptimality numerically, the geometric interpretation provides intuition, and the ray analysis identifies the precise degenerate condition (s = 1) under which normalization is exact. All steps are grounded in the KB formulation [KB:spec.compile.research.md] and standard optimization theory [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf].]
