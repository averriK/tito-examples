# Epistemic Logic-Tree Weight Calibration with Unit-Sum Constraint

## SLOT 1: Extension of Error Minimization Formulation to the Unit-Sum Weight Constraint

### Baseline Formulation from the Knowledge Base

The error minimization formulation in the knowledge base addresses the spectral-matching weight calibration problem by casting it as a non-negative least-squares (NNLS) optimization in the log-spectral domain. [KB:spec.compile.research.md] The transformed design matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times K}$ has entries $\tilde{A}_{ik} = \ln S_{a,k}(T_i)$, where $S_{a,k}(T_i)$ denotes the pseudo-spectral acceleration predicted by the $k$-th candidate ground-motion model (GMM) at spectral period $T_i$, for $i = 1, \dots, n$ period ordinates and $k = 1, \dots, K$ candidate models. The target vector $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ has components $y_i = \ln S_a^{\mathrm{obj}}(T_i)$, the PSHA-derived log-spectral target at period $T_i$. The weight vector $\mathbf{w} = (w_1, \dots, w_K)^\top$ is the vector of unknowns, and the baseline NNLS problem is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The log-spectral design matrix definition, the target vector notation, and the overall NNLS framing are stated verbatim in KB SLOT 4 and SLOT 5 [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. No quantitative claims require independent verification beyond the KB, which is treated as authoritative. No contradictions detected.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K} \;\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad w_k \geq 0, \quad k = 1, \dots, K.$$

The feasible region is the non-negative orthant $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^K$, an unbounded closed convex cone. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@WikipediaNNLS] The objective is a strictly convex quadratic in $\mathbf{w}$ when $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ has full column rank, and the unique global minimizer is recovered by the Lawson-Hanson active-set algorithm, which iteratively maintains a partition of the index set into an active set $\mathcal{Z}$ (indices constrained to zero) and a passive set $\mathcal{P}$ (indices free to be positive) until the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions are satisfied. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@Mullen2012][DOI:10.1137/1.9781611971217]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The characterization of the non-negative orthant as the feasible region, the strict convexity under full column rank, and the Lawson-Hanson algorithm description are all consistent with KB SLOT 5 [KB:spec.compile.research.md] and confirmed by Lawson and Hanson (DOI:10.1137/1.9781611971217). The [@WikipediaNNLS] and [@Mullen2012] citekeys are preserved from the KB. No contradictions detected.]

### Full Constrained Optimization Problem

The normalization condition $\sum_{k=1}^K w_k = 1$ imposed by the PSHA logic tree framework introduces an equality constraint absent from the baseline NNLS formulation. In vector notation this condition is $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$, where $\mathbf{1} = (1, \dots, 1)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^K$. Adding this constraint restricts the feasible region from the non-negative orthant $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^K$ to its intersection with the hyperplane $\{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K : \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1\}$. This intersection is the standard probability simplex:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The derivation that adding the equality constraint 1'w=1 to the non-negative orthant produces the probability simplex is a standard geometric fact confirmed by Boyd and Vandenberghe (WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/). The vector notation 1'w=1 is consistent with the KB matrix conventions. No contradictions detected.]

$$\Delta^{K-1} = \left\{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K \;:\; \sum_{k=1}^K w_k = 1,\; w_k \geq 0 \;\text{for all}\; k = 1, \dots, K\right\}.$$

The simplex $\Delta^{K-1}$ is a compact convex polytope: it is bounded (all components lie in $[0,1]$) and closed. Because $\Delta^{K-1}$ is compact and the objective $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ is continuous, the Weierstrass extreme value theorem guarantees the existence of a global minimizer; when $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ has full column rank, $f$ is strictly convex and the minimizer is unique. The fully constrained optimization problem extending the KB formulation is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The compactness of the simplex, the Weierstrass guarantee, and the uniqueness under strict convexity are standard results confirmed by Boyd and Vandenberghe (WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/) and Nocedal and Wright (DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5). The condition that full column rank implies positive definiteness of the Gram matrix is consistent with KB SLOT 3 and SLOT 4 [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. No contradictions detected.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K} \;\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1, \quad w_k \geq 0, \quad k = 1, \dots, K.$$

This is the complete statement of the unit-sum constrained problem. The only modification relative to the NNLS baseline is the addition of the equality constraint $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$; the objective function and the non-negativity constraints are carried over unchanged from the KB formulation. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The claim that the constrained problem differs from the NNLS baseline only by the addition of the equality constraint is a direct reading of the two problem statements. Both the objective and the non-negativity constraints are identical to KB SLOT 5 [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. No other modifications have been introduced. No contradictions detected.]

### Objective Function and Standard Quadratic Program Form

The objective $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ expands via matrix algebra into a quadratic form. Noting that the constant $\mathbf{y}^\top\mathbf{y}$ does not affect the minimizer and may be omitted, the problem is equivalent to the standard quadratic program (QP) with Hessian matrix $\mathbf{Q} = 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$ and linear objective coefficient $\mathbf{c} = -2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^K$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The expansion of a squared Euclidean norm into quadratic form, the identification of Q = 2A'A and c = -2A'y, and the irrelevance of the constant term are standard algebraic facts fully consistent with the KB Gram matrix notation in SLOT 3 [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. Nocedal and Wright (DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5) confirm the standard QP form. No errors detected.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K} \;\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{Q}\,\mathbf{w} \;+\; \mathbf{c}^\top\mathbf{w} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1, \quad \mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}.$$

The matrix $\mathbf{Q}$ is positive semidefinite for any $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, and positive definite when $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ has full column rank. The constraint set consists of one linear equality (the unit-sum condition) and $K$ linear inequalities (the non-negativity bounds), yielding $K + 1$ constraints in total. This is a convex QP in standard form, and the problem class is precisely that handled by the Goldfarb-Idnani dual active-set algorithm implemented in R's `quadprog` package. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5][@GoldfarbIdnani1983]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The positive-(semi)definiteness of Q = 2A'A is confirmed by the KB SLOT 3 argument on the Gram matrix [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. The constraint count (K+1) is a trivial enumeration. The Goldfarb-Idnani algorithm handles general linear equality and inequality constraints for strictly convex QPs, as confirmed by DOI:10.1007/BF02591962 ([@GoldfarbIdnani1983]) and DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5. No contradictions detected.]

### Lagrangian Formulation

The Lagrangian for the constrained problem associates a free multiplier $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$ with the equality constraint $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} - 1 = 0$ and non-negative multipliers $\boldsymbol{\lambda} = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_K)^\top$, with $\lambda_k \geq 0$, with the inequality constraints $-w_k \leq 0$. The resulting Lagrangian is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Lagrangian construction for a QP with one linear equality and K linear inequality constraints, assigning a free multiplier to the equality and a non-negative multiplier to each inequality, is the canonical form stated in Boyd and Vandenberghe Chapter 5 (WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/) and Nocedal and Wright Chapter 12 (DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5). No assumptions beyond standard convex optimization theory are invoked. No contradictions detected.]

$$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, \mu, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) \;=\; \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 \;+\; \mu\!\left(\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} - 1\right) \;-\; \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top\mathbf{w}.$$

Expanding in $\mathbf{w}$, the Lagrangian becomes $\mathbf{w}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - 2\mathbf{y}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} + \mathbf{y}^\top\mathbf{y} + \mu(\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} - 1) - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top\mathbf{w}$. The gradient of $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to $\mathbf{w}$ is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The expansion of the Lagrangian is a direct algebraic substitution. The form of the resulting expression is straightforward and consistent with the objective quadratic form identified in the preceding section. No non-trivial claims are made here beyond routine algebra.]

$$\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, \mu, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) \;=\; 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\right) \;+\; \mu\mathbf{1} \;-\; \boldsymbol{\lambda}.$$

The Hessian $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}^2\mathcal{L} = 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ is independent of $(\mu, \boldsymbol{\lambda})$, confirming joint convexity of $\mathcal{L}$ in $\mathbf{w}$ for any fixed multiplier values. Strong duality holds by Slater's constraint qualification: the interior of $\Delta^{K-1}$ is non-empty for $K \geq 2$ (any strictly positive weight vector summing to unity constitutes a strictly feasible point), so the zero-duality-gap property applies and the KKT conditions are both necessary and sufficient for global optimality. [WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/][DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Hessian of the Lagrangian equaling 2A'A is immediate from the form of the gradient. Slater's condition requires the existence of a strictly feasible point, satisfied here by any strictly positive weight vector summing to 1 (such vectors exist for K>=2). Boyd and Vandenberghe (WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/) Theorem 5.2 and Nocedal and Wright (DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5) Chapter 12 confirm that Slater's condition implies strong duality and KKT necessity and sufficiency for convex programs. No contradictions detected.]

### KKT Optimality Conditions

Since the problem is a convex QP with linear constraints and satisfies Slater's condition, the KKT conditions are both necessary and sufficient for global optimality. At the optimal weight vector $\mathbf{w}^*$ with multipliers $(\mu^*, \boldsymbol{\lambda}^*)$, the four KKT conditions are as follows. Stationarity requires $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{L} = \mathbf{0}$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The claim that KKT conditions are necessary and sufficient for convex QPs satisfying Slater's condition is established in the preceding section and confirmed by Boyd and Vandenberghe (WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/) and Nocedal and Wright (DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5). The stationarity condition follows from setting the Lagrangian gradient to zero, derived in the Lagrangian section above. No new assumptions are introduced here.]

$$2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y}\right) \;+\; \mu^*\mathbf{1} \;-\; \boldsymbol{\lambda}^* \;=\; \mathbf{0}.$$

Primal feasibility requires $\mathbf{w}^*$ to lie in the feasible set: $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}^* = 1$ and $w_k^* \geq 0$ for all $k = 1, \dots, K$. Dual feasibility requires $\lambda_k^* \geq 0$ for all $k$. Complementary slackness requires $\lambda_k^*\, w_k^* = 0$ for all $k$. Together, these four conditions fully characterize the unique global minimizer when $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ has full column rank. [WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/][DOI:10.1007/BF02591962][@GoldfarbIdnani1983] The stationarity condition is a direct extension of the NNLS stationarity conditions (for which $\mu = 0$ by the absence of the equality constraint): the equality-constraint multiplier $\mu^*$ enters as an additional degree of freedom that shifts all partial derivatives uniformly, enforcing the unit-sum requirement. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The four KKT conditions for a convex QP with linear constraints are foundational, confirmed by Boyd and Vandenberghe (WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/) Chapter 5, Nocedal and Wright (DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5) Chapters 12 and 16, and Goldfarb and Idnani (DOI:10.1007/BF02591962, [@GoldfarbIdnani1983]). The connection to the NNLS KKT (mu=0 case) is a direct comparison with KB SLOT 5 [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. No contradictions detected.]

### Characterization of the Optimal Solution

The index set $\{1, \dots, K\}$ is partitioned at the optimum into the passive set $\mathcal{P} = \{k : w_k^* > 0\}$ and the active set $\mathcal{Z} = \{k : w_k^* = 0\}$. By complementary slackness, $\lambda_k^* = 0$ for $k \in \mathcal{P}$ and $w_k^* = 0$ for $k \in \mathcal{Z}$. Applying these to the stationarity condition yields component-wise optimality conditions: for each passive index $k \in \mathcal{P}$,^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The passive/active set decomposition and the application of complementary slackness to eliminate multipliers are standard steps in active-set QP analysis, as in Nocedal and Wright (DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5) Chapter 16 and Boyd and Vandenberghe (WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/) Section 5.5. The derivation is exact and relies only on the KKT structure established above. No contradictions detected.]

$$2\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y}\right)\right]_k \;=\; -\mu^*,$$

and for each active index $k \in \mathcal{Z}$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The active-set condition is a direct consequence of complementary slackness (lambda_k* > 0 only when w_k* = 0) combined with the stationarity equation, as derived from the KKT system in the Lagrangian section above. This is an exact algebraic result with no new assumptions. Nocedal and Wright (DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5) confirm the standard form of active-set conditions for inequality-constrained QPs.]

$$\lambda_k^* \;=\; 2\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y}\right)\right]_k \;+\; \mu^* \;\geq\; 0.$$

The passive-set condition states that the partial derivative of the objective with respect to every strictly positive weight equals the same constant $-\mu^*$ at the optimum: the unit-sum constraint forces all active partial derivatives to be equal (rather than zero as in the unconstrained NNLS case). The active-set condition states that the partial derivative for every excluded model is at least as large as $-\mu^*$, so no excluded model could reduce the objective if it were admitted to the solution. These conditions generalize the NNLS optimality conditions (where $\mu^* = 0$) by introducing the Lagrange multiplier as a uniform gradient shift that compensates for the sum constraint. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The component-wise interpretation of the KKT conditions for passive and active indices, and the comparison with the NNLS case (mu*=0), follow directly from the stationarity equation and the active-set decomposition. The claim that the unit-sum constraint forces equal partial derivatives on the passive set (rather than zero as in NNLS) is a correct reading of the two sets of stationarity conditions. KB SLOT 5 [KB:spec.compile.research.md] confirms the NNLS active-set structure. No contradictions detected.]

### Bordered KKT Linear System for the Passive Set

Restricting the stationarity condition to the passive set and appending the primal equality constraint $\mathbf{1}_\mathcal{P}^\top\mathbf{w}^*_\mathcal{P} = 1$ (noting that the active set contributes zero) yields a $(|\mathcal{P}|+1) \times (|\mathcal{P}|+1)$ bordered linear system in the unknowns $(\mathbf{w}^*_\mathcal{P}, \mu^*)$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The reduction to the bordered system follows by substituting lambda_k* = 0 for passive indices into the stationarity equation and appending the unit-sum constraint. The dimensions of the resulting system are (|P| equations from stationarity) + (1 equation from the equality constraint), producing a (|P|+1)-dimensional square system. This is a standard algebraic manipulation for equality-constrained QPs as described in Nocedal and Wright (DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5). No contradictions detected.]

$$\begin{pmatrix} 2\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\right)_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{P}} & \mathbf{1}_{|\mathcal{P}|} \\ \mathbf{1}_{|\mathcal{P}|}^\top & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{w}^*_\mathcal{P} \\ \mu^* \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2\left(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}\right)_\mathcal{P} \\ 1 \end{pmatrix},$$

where $(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}})_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{P}}$ is the principal submatrix of the Gram matrix restricted to the passive set and $(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y})_\mathcal{P}$ is the cross-correlation subvector. The bordered matrix is invertible when $(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}})_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{P}}$ is positive definite -- satisfied when the passive-set columns of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ are linearly independent, a generic condition for distinct GMMs over a rich period mesh -- yielding $\mathbf{w}^*_\mathcal{P}$ and $\mu^*$ simultaneously. The full solution is completed by $w_k^* = 0$ for all $k \in \mathcal{Z}$. [DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5][DOI:10.1007/BF02591962][KB:spec.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The bordered KKT system structure, its invertibility condition (positive definiteness of the passive-set Gram block plus constraint non-degeneracy), and the dimension of the resulting system are confirmed by Nocedal and Wright (DOI:10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5) Chapter 16 and Goldfarb and Idnani (DOI:10.1007/BF02591962). The claim that distinct GMMs over a rich period mesh generically produce linearly independent log-spectral column vectors is consistent with the KB discussion of the design matrix rank condition [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. No contradictions detected.]

### Algorithmic Solution and Relation to the NNLS Baseline

Since the passive set $\mathcal{P}$ is not known a priori, the bordered system is not directly solvable without an iterative procedure. The problem is addressed by a general-purpose convex QP solver. In R, `quadprog::solve.QP` -- which implements the Goldfarb-Idnani dual active-set algorithm -- accepts the equality constraint $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$ by augmenting the constraint matrix with the equality row and setting `meq = 1`. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@GoldfarbIdnani1983] Alternatively, the disciplined convex programming framework `CVXR` with the added constraint `sum(w) == 1` dispatches to interior-point solvers such as CLARABEL or OSQP. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@FuEtAl2020] The resulting solution $\mathbf{w}^*$ is the unique minimizer of the log-spectral squared error over $\Delta^{K-1}$ and satisfies the logic tree normalization requirement by construction. Compared to the NNLS baseline, the only structural addition is the equality row in the constraint matrix and the free Lagrange multiplier $\mu^*$; the Gram matrix $\mathbf{Q} = 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, the cross-correlation vector $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$, and the non-negativity constraints are all carried over unchanged.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Goldfarb-Idnani algorithm and the `quadprog::solve.QP` implementation with the `meq` argument for equality constraints are confirmed by [@GoldfarbIdnani1983] (KB) and DOI:10.1007/BF02591962. The CVXR implementation is confirmed by [@FuEtAl2020] (KB). The claim that only the equality constraint is new relative to the NNLS baseline is verified by direct comparison of the two problem statements. No contradictions detected.]

## SLOT 2: Validity of Post-Hoc Normalization Versus Constrained Optimization

### Definition and Feasibility of the Normalized Solution

Given the NNLS solution $\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}$ obtained by solving the KB baseline formulation with only $w_k \geq 0$ enforced, let $s = \mathbf{1}^\top\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}} = \sum_{k=1}^K \hat{w}_k^{\mathrm{NNLS}}$ denote the total weight. When $s > 0$ -- the generic case in PSHA applications, since at least one GMM will have a positive log-spectral inner product with the target -- post-hoc normalization defines the rescaled vector:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The definition of the total weight s and the condition s > 0 are straightforward. The generic positivity of s follows because the NNLS problem minimizes a residual norm; if s=0 then w=0, which requires all partial gradients at the origin to be non-negative, i.e., all A'y components <= 0 -- an extreme condition inconsistent with positive log-spectral targets typical in PSHA. Lawson and Hanson (DOI:10.1137/1.9781611971217) and KB [KB:spec.compile.research.md] support the NNLS framework. No contradictions detected.]

$$\tilde{\mathbf{w}} \;=\; \frac{1}{s}\,\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}.$$

By construction $\mathbf{1}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = 1$ and $\tilde{w}_k = \hat{w}_k^{\mathrm{NNLS}}/s \geq 0$ for all $k$, so $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} \in \Delta^{K-1}$ is a feasible point for the simplex-constrained problem formulated in SLOT 1. The central question is whether $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ is also the optimal point, i.e., whether $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \mathbf{w}^*$, or whether the simplex-constrained problem requires a distinct optimal weight vector. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][DOI:10.1137/1.9781611971217]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The feasibility of the normalized solution for the simplex problem is a direct algebraic verification (sum-to-one and non-negativity). The NNLS and simplex problem statements from KB [KB:spec.compile.research.md] and SLOT 1 above support the framing. No contradictions detected.]

### Gradient Analysis at the Normalized Point

The gradient of the objective $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ is $\nabla f(\mathbf{w}) = 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y})$. Evaluating at $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}/s$ by the linearity of matrix multiplication gives:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The gradient formula nabla f(w) = 2A'(Aw - y) is the standard gradient of a squared norm, consistent with the KB normal equations in SLOT 3 [KB:spec.compile.research.md]. $$\nabla f(\tilde{\mathbf{w}}) \;=\; \frac{2}{s}\,\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}} \;-\; 2\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}.$$

The NNLS KKT stationarity condition for the passive set $\mathcal{P} = \{k : \hat{w}_k^{\mathrm{NNLS}} > 0\}$ states that the gradient of $f$ vanishes on $\mathcal{P}$: $2[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}} - \mathbf{y})]_k = 0$, equivalently $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}]_k = [\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$, for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@Mullen2012] Substituting this relation into the gradient at $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ yields, for each $k \in \mathcal{P}$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The NNLS KKT stationarity condition for the passive set (gradient vanishes) is stated in KB SLOT 5 [KB:spec.compile.research.md] and confirmed by Lawson and Hanson (DOI:10.1137/1.9781611971217). $$\left[\nabla f(\tilde{\mathbf{w}})\right]_k \;=\; \frac{2}{s}\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}\right]_k \;-\; 2\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}\right]_k \;=\; 2\!\left(\frac{1}{s} - 1\right)\!\left[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}\right]_k.$$

This result shows that the gradient of the objective at the normalized point, restricted to passive indices, is proportional to the cross-correlation vector $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$. When $s \neq 1$, the factor $2(1/s - 1) \neq 0$, so the gradient component at each passive index is directly proportional to the inner product of the $k$-th GMM's log-spectral predictions with the target log-spectrum. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The simplification [(1/s)[A'y]_k - [A'y]_k = (1/s - 1)[A'y]_k] for passive k is an exact algebraic identity that follows directly from the NNLS stationarity substitution. ### Non-Equivalence in the General Case

Post-hoc normalization is not mathematically equivalent to the simplex-constrained optimization in the general case. Distinct GMMs constructed from different empirical datasets, functional forms, attenuation assumptions, and site amplification models will generically produce different values of $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$ for any fixed PSHA target $\mathbf{y}$: inter-model variability in log-spectral predictions is the defining characteristic of epistemic uncertainty in GMM selection. [DOI:10.1785/0120080347][DOI:10.1007/s10950-012-9281-z][KB:spec.compile.research.md] The constant cross-correlation condition $[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k = c$ for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$ is therefore not satisfied in any practical application with $s \neq 1$, and the normalized solution $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}/s$ is a feasible but strictly suboptimal point for the simplex-constrained problem. By strict convexity of $f$, there exists a unique minimizer $\mathbf{w}^* \in \Delta^{K-1}$ with $f(\mathbf{w}^*) < f(\tilde{\mathbf{w}})$ whenever $s \neq 1$ and the cross-correlations are not all equal. The unit-sum constraint must be incorporated directly into the optimization problem and solved with a constrained QP algorithm; post-hoc rescaling of the NNLS output cannot substitute for this treatment.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The non-equivalence conclusion is a direct consequence of the KKT analysis: the constant cross-correlation condition is not met generically. Scherbaum et al. 2009 (DOI:10.1785/0120080347) demonstrates inter-model variability in log-likelihood scores across GMMs, confirming that cross-correlations [A'y]_k differ across models. Delavaud et al. 2012 (DOI:10.1007/s10950-012-9281-z) further shows significant variation in GMPE performance metrics across tectonic regimes. The strict suboptimality of a feasible non-optimal point follows from the strict convexity of f when A has full column rank (confirmed by KB [KB:spec.compile.research.md]). No contradictions detected.]

### Geometric Interpretation

Post-hoc normalization is a radial rescaling: it moves $\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}$ along the ray from the origin through $\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}$ until the unit-sum hyperplane is intersected, preserving the direction in weight space while adjusting the overall magnitude by $1/s$. The true simplex optimum $\mathbf{w}^*$ is characterized geometrically as the minimizer of the quadratic form $\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w}/2 + \mathbf{c}^\top\mathbf{w}$ over $\Delta^{K-1}$, which corresponds to the projection of the unconstrained minimizer $\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{OLS}} = (\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}$ onto the simplex in the metric induced by the Gram matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$. This is a Mahalanobis-type projection, a fundamentally different geometric operation from radial rescaling. The two operations coincide only in the degenerate cases identified by the KKT analysis: when $s = 1$ (trivial case, addressed below) or when all retained GMMs have identical log-spectral cross-correlations with the target. [WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/][ARXIV:1309.1541]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The characterization of post-hoc normalization as radial rescaling is a direct description of the mapping w -> w/s. The characterization of the true optimum as a Mahalanobis-type projection onto the simplex follows from the quadratic form structure of f and is consistent with the theory in Boyd and Vandenberghe (WEB:https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/). Wang and Carreira-Perpinan (ARXIV:1309.1541) provide explicit KKT-based treatment of the projection onto the probability simplex, confirming that it is an orthogonal projection in the Euclidean metric, which differs from radial rescaling. ### Special Case: The NNLS Solution Already on the Simplex

The one exception to the non-equivalence result is the degenerate case $s = 1$, in which the NNLS solution already lies on the simplex. In this case $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}$ and normalization is an identity operation. The NNLS KKT stationarity conditions for the passive set yield $[\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}})]_k = 0$ for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$; the simplex KKT stationarity with $\mu^* = 0$ reduces to the same condition $[\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}})]_k = 0$, which is identically satisfied. The active-set condition of the simplex problem -- $[\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}})]_k + \mu^* \geq 0$ for all $k \in \mathcal{Z}$, i.e., $[\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}})]_k \geq 0$ -- is exactly the NNLS active-set condition, which holds by construction. Therefore, when $s = 1$, the NNLS solution satisfies the KKT conditions of both the NNLS problem and the simplex-constrained problem simultaneously, with $\mu^* = 0$, and the two solutions coincide. The condition $s = 1$ is not imposed by the NNLS problem and is not generically satisfied: it occurs only when the log-spectral configuration of the candidate GMMs and the target jointly produce a NNLS minimizer whose weight components happen to sum to unity without explicit enforcement of the constraint. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][DOI:10.1137/1.9781611971217]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The special case analysis for s=1 is an exact algebraic verification: substituting mu*=0 into the simplex KKT conditions reduces them to the NNLS KKT conditions, which hold at the NNLS minimizer by construction. The claim that s=1 is non-generic follows from the fact that the NNLS objective places no constraint on the sum of weights; the event {sum(w_NNLS) = 1} is a co-dimension-1 constraint in the space of (A,y) pairs and thus has measure zero. Lawson and Hanson (DOI:10.1137/1.9781611971217) and KB [KB:spec.compile.research.md] support the NNLS framework. No contradictions detected.]

### Practical Consequence for PSHA Logic-Tree Weight Calibration

The mathematical non-equivalence established above has direct practical consequences for GMPE logic tree weight calibration in PSHA. Applying the NNLS algorithm to the log-spectral calibration problem and subsequently normalizing the output introduces a calibration error: the normalized weights satisfy the unit-sum requirement of the logic tree but are suboptimal in the least-squares sense, producing a larger mean-squared log-spectral residual than the solution of the directly constrained QP over $\Delta^{K-1}$. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][DOI:10.1785/0120080347] The magnitude of the calibration error is governed by two factors: the departure $|1 - s|$ of the NNLS total weight from unity, and the heterogeneity of the cross-correlations $\{[\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k\}_{k \in \mathcal{P}}$ across retained models. For $|1-s|$ small and cross-correlations approximately equal, the normalization error may be negligible in engineering terms; for solutions where weight is concentrated on one or two models with large absolute values, the error can be substantial. In all cases, the rigorous approach is to enforce the unit-sum constraint directly within the optimization and solve the QP over $\Delta^{K-1}$ as formulated in SLOT 1 -- an operation that requires only the addition of the equality row $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$ to the solver's constraint matrix, with no change to the objective or the Gram matrix.
