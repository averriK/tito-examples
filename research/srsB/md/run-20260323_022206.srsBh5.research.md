# Weight Calibration with Sum-to-One Constraint: Formulation and Validation

## SLOT 1: Extended Formulation for Sum-to-One Constraint

The weight calibration problem for epistemic logic trees in probabilistic seismic hazard analysis requires that weights form a discrete probability distribution over candidate Ground Motion Models. This constraint mandates that the weights sum to unity, formalizing the requirement that the logic tree branches collectively represent certainty in model selection. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

The foundational spectral-matching problem is formulated as a constrained least-squares regression. The design matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times K}$ contains the logarithmic spectral ordinates of $K$ candidate records across $n$ period points, the target vector $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ contains the log-domain target spectral ordinates, and the weight vector $\mathbf{w} = (w_1, \dots, w_K)^\top$ must satisfy all constraints. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

The extended formulation incorporates the sum-to-one normalization constraint alongside non-negativity and (optionally) upper-bound constraints on individual weights. The complete mathematical formulation is:

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K} \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$$

subject to the constraints:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k = 1,$$
$$w_k \geq 0 \quad \text{for all} \quad k = 1, \dots, K,$$
$$w_k \leq w_{\max} \quad \text{for all} \quad k = 1, \dots, K \quad \text{(optional)}.$$

This formulation is a linearly constrained quadratic program (QP). The objective function remains the squared $\ell_2$ norm of residuals, which measures prediction error in log-spectral units and aligns with the probabilistic framework of modern GMPEs. [KB:spec.compile.research.md] The constraint set is a polytope defined by one equality constraint (the sum-to-one requirement), $K$ non-negativity constraints, and $K$ optional upper-bound constraints. The feasible region is a simplex when only non-negativity and sum-to-one constraints are present (i.e., when $w_{\max} = +\infty$), or a subset of the simplex when upper bounds are imposed. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

The key differences between this constrained formulation and the previously documented approaches are as follows. The unconstrained OLS solution (SLOT 3 of the KB) admits negative weights and is unbounded above; it is unsuitable for probability distributions. The non-negative least-squares (NNLS) formulation (SLOT 5 of the KB) enforces non-negativity but does not constrain the sum of weights, allowing some weights to exceed unity while others approach zero. The bounded-variable least-squares (BVLS) formulation (SLOT 6 of the KB) introduces two-sided bounds on individual weights but does not enforce the sum-to-one requirement. The extended formulation presented here combines non-negativity (or non-negativity with upper bounds) with the fundamental probabilistic requirement that weights form a normalized distribution. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

The optimization problem can be reformulated as a standard QP by introducing the Lagrangian. Define the equality constraint matrix $\mathbf{C}_{\text{eq}} = \mathbf{1}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times K}$ (a row vector of ones) and the equality constraint right-hand side $\mathbf{d}_{\text{eq}} = 1 \in \mathbb{R}$. The inequality constraints for non-negativity are $-\mathbf{w} \leq \mathbf{0}$, and the upper-bound constraints (if present) are $\mathbf{w} \leq w_{\max} \mathbf{1}$, which can be combined into a single inequality system. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

The quadratic-program form is:

$$\mathbf{w}^* = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{H} \mathbf{w} + \mathbf{c}^\top \mathbf{w},$$

where $\mathbf{H} = 2 \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top \tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ is the Hessian (positive semidefinite if $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ has full column rank), and $\mathbf{c} = -2 \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top \mathbf{y}$ is the linear coefficient vector. The constraints are:

$$\mathbf{C}_{\text{eq}} \mathbf{w} = \mathbf{d}_{\text{eq}}, \quad \mathbf{G}_{\text{ineq}} \mathbf{w} \leq \mathbf{h}_{\text{ineq}},$$

where $\mathbf{G}_{\text{ineq}}$ encodes the non-negativity and optional upper-bound constraints. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

The Lagrangian is:

$$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, \lambda, \boldsymbol{\mu}) = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{H} \mathbf{w} + \mathbf{c}^\top \mathbf{w} - \lambda (\mathbf{C}_{\text{eq}} \mathbf{w} - \mathbf{d}_{\text{eq}}) - \boldsymbol{\mu}^\top (\mathbf{G}_{\text{ineq}} \mathbf{w} - \mathbf{h}_{\text{ineq}}),$$

where $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ is the multiplier for the equality constraint and $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^{2K}$ (or $\mathbb{R}^{K}$ if only non-negativity is enforced) are the multipliers for the inequality constraints. The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions for optimality are:

$$\nabla_{\mathbf{w}} \mathcal{L} = \mathbf{H} \mathbf{w}^* + \mathbf{c} - \lambda^* \mathbf{C}_{\text{eq}}^\top - \mathbf{G}_{\text{ineq}}^\top \boldsymbol{\mu}^* = \mathbf{0},$$
$$\mathbf{C}_{\text{eq}} \mathbf{w}^* = \mathbf{d}_{\text{eq}},$$
$$\mathbf{G}_{\text{ineq}} \mathbf{w}^* \leq \mathbf{h}_{\text{ineq}},$$
$$\boldsymbol{\mu}^* \geq \mathbf{0}, \quad \boldsymbol{\mu}^* \odot (\mathbf{G}_{\text{ineq}} \mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{h}_{\text{ineq}}) = \mathbf{0}.$$

The complementarity condition (the last line) states that at optimality, each inequality constraint is either active (the multiplier is positive and the constraint is binding) or inactive (the multiplier is zero). [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

Standard convex optimization algorithms such as active-set methods, interior-point methods, or second-order cone programming (SOCP) solvers can solve this problem. The R package `quadprog` (which implements the Goldfarb-Idnani active-set algorithm) can be extended to handle the equality constraint by incorporating it into the constraint matrix. Alternatively, the general convex-optimization framework presented in SLOT 7 of the KB can be specialized for the sum-to-one case by setting the constraint directly in disciplined convex programming (DCP) syntax. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

In R, the `quadprog::solve.QP` function solves problems of the form:

$$\min \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{D} \mathbf{w} - \mathbf{d}^\top \mathbf{w}$$

subject to $\mathbf{A} \mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{b}$. To enforce the sum-to-one constraint using `quadprog`, the equality $\sum w_k = 1$ must be replaced by two inequality constraints: $\sum w_k \geq 1$ and $-\sum w_k \geq -1$, or the `solve.QP` function can be supplemented with additional equality constraint handling through matrix augmentation. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

The extended formulation addresses the practical requirement that weights in an epistemic logic tree represent a probability distribution. Unlike the bounded-variable and NNLS formulations, which permit weights summing to any positive value, the sum-to-one constraint ensures that the fitted weights can be directly interpreted as branch probabilities. This is not merely a normalization artifact but a fundamental requirement for consistent PSHA computation, where the weighted average hazard curve is constructed as $\bar{\lambda}_I(i^*) = \sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k \lambda_I^{(k)}(i^*)$ and the weighting scheme must represent a valid discrete probability distribution. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

## SLOT 2: Validation of Normalization Approach

A critical practical question arises when implementing weight calibration: is it mathematically valid to compute weights from an unconstrained or partially constrained optimization problem and then post-hoc normalize them by dividing by their sum, or must the sum-to-one constraint be enforced directly during optimization?

The mathematical relationship between post-hoc normalization and direct constrained optimization depends on the specific objective function and constraint structure. Consider an unconstrained solution $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{unconstrained}}$ obtained by minimizing $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ over all $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K$. A post-hoc normalization scheme would define $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{unconstrained}} / \sum_k \hat{w}_k$. The question is whether this normalized vector $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ satisfies the optimality conditions for the constrained problem.

The constrained problem is to minimize the same objective $f(\mathbf{w}) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ subject to $\sum_k w_k = 1$ and $w_k \geq 0$. The answer to the normalization question is negative in general: post-hoc normalization does not preserve optimality. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

**Mathematical Proof of Inequivalence**

Define the constrained optimum as $\mathbf{w}^* = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}: \sum w_k = 1, w_k \geq 0} f(\mathbf{w})$. Consider the case in which the unconstrained optimum $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{unconstrained}}$ has a sum $s = \sum_k \hat{w}_k \neq 1$. Post-hoc normalization produces $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{unconstrained}} / s$.

The objective function evaluated at $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ is:

$$f(\tilde{\mathbf{w}}) = \left\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \frac{\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{unconstrained}}}{s} - \mathbf{y}\right\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{s^2} \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{unconstrained}} - s\mathbf{y}\|_2^2.$$

Expanding:

$$f(\tilde{\mathbf{w}}) = \frac{1}{s^2} \left( \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{unconstrained}}\|_2^2 - 2s \langle \tilde{\mathbf{A}} \hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{unconstrained}}, \mathbf{y} \rangle + s^2 \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2 \right).$$

The constrained optimum $\mathbf{w}^*$ must satisfy:

$$f(\mathbf{w}^*) = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 = \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \mathbf{w}^*\|_2^2 - 2 \langle \tilde{\mathbf{A}} \mathbf{w}^*, \mathbf{y} \rangle + \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2.$$

Comparing these expressions, $f(\tilde{\mathbf{w}}) \neq f(\mathbf{w}^*)$ in general because the scaling by $1/s^2$ and the change in the cross-product term $\langle \tilde{\mathbf{A}} \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{y} \rangle$ are not equivalent to the optimization performed under the constraint. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

A concrete counterexample illustrates the inequivalence. Suppose $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^\top$ and $\mathbf{y} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \end{pmatrix}$. The unconstrained minimizer is $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{unconstrained}} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^\top$, with objective value $f(\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{unconstrained}}) = 0$. Post-hoc normalization gives $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^\top$, with objective value $f(\tilde{\mathbf{w}}) = 1$. The constrained optimum subject to $w_1 + w_2 = 1$ and $w_k \geq 0$ is also $\mathbf{w}^* = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^\top$ (since the constraint forces $w_1 = 1$ when $w_2 = 0$), with objective value $f(\mathbf{w}^*) = 1$. In this case, post-hoc normalization happens to yield the constrained optimum, but this is accidental; the unconstrained optimum is infeasible. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

In the general multidimensional case, post-hoc normalization is typically suboptimal because the scaling operation distorts the residual vector. The constrained optimization problem is fundamentally different: it seeks the point on the simplex (or constrained polytope) that minimizes the residual norm, rather than first finding the global unconstrained minimum and then projecting it onto the feasible set. Projection onto the simplex is not equivalent to constrained minimization on the simplex. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

**Conditions Under Which Normalization May Be Approximately Valid**

Post-hoc normalization can be approximately valid under specific structural conditions. If the unconstrained optimum $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{unconstrained}}$ lies already within a small neighborhood of the simplex (i.e., if $|\sum_k \hat{w}_k - 1| \ll 1$ and all $\hat{w}_k \approx 0$ or $\hat{w}_k \gg 0$ without extremal scaling), then the scaling by $1/s$ is a small perturbation and post-hoc normalization approximates the constrained solution. This occurs when the design matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ is well-conditioned and the target $\mathbf{y}$ is achievable or near-achievable by a linear combination of spectral ordinates. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

However, this approximate validity requires careful verification in each application and does not eliminate the optimality gap. A more robust approach is to use the formulation presented in SLOT 1, which directly incorporates the sum-to-one constraint into the optimization problem from the outset. Modern convex-optimization solvers (such as `quadprog` or CVXR in R) handle such constrained problems efficiently and are computationally comparable to unconstrained methods. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

**Comparison with Constrained Optimization**

The direct constrained formulation avoids the optimality gap by solving the true minimization problem over the feasible set. The KKT conditions for the constrained problem (presented in SLOT 1) ensure that the solution is a true global minimum on the simplex (or polytope), not a projection of an unconstrained minimizer. The equality constraint introduces a single Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$, which can be interpreted as the marginal cost of the normalization requirement: it quantifies how much the objective function increases due to the requirement that weights sum to unity. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

From a practical standpoint, when the unconstrained weights naturally sum close to unity (within a few percent), post-hoc normalization introduces a relative error in the objective function that is second-order in the deviation from normality. In such cases, the normalized weights are a reasonable approximation. However, when unconstrained weights deviate significantly from unit sum (for example, when the candidate spectrum set spans a large range of scales or when the target spectrum is far from the convex hull of candidate spectra), the approximation breaks down and direct constrained optimization is essential. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]

**Recommendation**

For epistemic logic tree weight calibration, the mathematically sound approach is direct constrained optimization with the formulation in SLOT 1. Post-hoc normalization is not equivalent to constrained optimization and can introduce non-negligible errors in the fitted weights. While post-hoc normalization may be acceptable as a heuristic approximation when the unconstrained weights already lie near the simplex, it should not be relied upon as a general method. The computational overhead of adding the sum-to-one constraint is negligible; modern QP solvers integrate the constraint handling seamlessly into the optimization procedure. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]
