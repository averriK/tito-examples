# Epistemic Logic-Tree Weight Calibration with Unit-Sum Constraint

## SLOT 1: Extension of error minimization formulation to the unit-sum weight constraint

The log-domain non-negative least-squares (NNLS) formulation in the knowledge base establishes the weight calibration problem as follows. Let $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times K}$ denote the log-transformed design matrix with entries $\tilde{A}_{ik} = \ln S_{a,k}(T_i)$, where $S_{a,k}(T_i)$ is the spectral acceleration of the $k$-th candidate record at period $T_i$, and let $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ be the log-domain target vector with components $y_i = \ln S_a^{\mathrm{obj}}(T_i)$. The weight vector $\mathbf{w} = (w_1, \dots, w_K)^\top$ is determined by minimizing the squared residual norm subject only to the non-negativity requirement on each component [KB:spec.compile.research.md]:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The NNLS formulation, the log-transformed design matrix with entries ln S_{a,k}(T_i), and the log-target vector are all explicitly defined in SLOT 5 of the KB. The notation is reproduced faithfully here.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K} \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad w_k \geq 0, \quad k = 1, \dots, K.$$

Setting $\mathbf{Q} = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$ and $\mathbf{c} = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^K$, the objective expands as $\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w} - 2\mathbf{c}^\top\mathbf{w} + \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2$. The feasible region is the non-negative orthant $\mathbb{R}^K_+$, a closed convex cone. When $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ has full column rank -- a condition satisfied when $n \geq K$ and no two candidate log-spectral vectors are collinear over the period mesh -- the Gram matrix $\mathbf{Q}$ is positive definite and the objective is strictly convex, guaranteeing a unique global minimizer. The canonical active-set method of Lawson and Hanson (1974) solves this problem by maintaining a partition of the index set $\{1, \dots, K\}$ into a passive set $\mathcal{P}$ (indices where $w_k > 0$) and an active set $\mathcal{Z}$ (indices where $w_k = 0$), iteratively updating the partition until the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions are satisfied [KB:spec.compile.research.md] [WEB:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative_least_squares].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The positive definiteness of Q under full column rank of the design matrix is a standard result in linear algebra. The description of the Lawson-Hanson active-set algorithm and the passive/active partition is consistent with SLOT 5 of the KB and corroborated by the Wikipedia article on NNLS.]

The epistemic logic-tree application imposes an additional requirement beyond non-negativity: the branch weights must constitute a discrete probability measure over the set of $K$ candidate ground-motion prediction equations (GMPEs), so their sum must equal unity. This normalization condition introduces one linear equality constraint, $\sum_{k=1}^K w_k = 1$, alongside the $K$ non-negativity constraints already present. The joint feasible set is the standard probability simplex:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The requirement that branch weights form a discrete probability distribution summing to unity is stated in the CONTEXT section of the TASK_FILE and is standard in probabilistic seismic hazard analysis. The definition of the probability simplex as the intersection of the non-negative orthant with the unit-sum hyperplane is standard in convex analysis.]

$$\Delta^K = \bigl\{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K : \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1,\; \mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}\bigr\}.$$

The set $\Delta^K$ is the convex hull of the $K$ standard basis vectors $\{\mathbf{e}_1, \dots, \mathbf{e}_K\}$, a compact convex polytope of dimension $K-1$ embedded in $\mathbb{R}^K$. Adding the equality constraint to the KB NNLS formulation yields the unit-sum-constrained extension:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The probability simplex as the convex hull of the standard basis vectors and as a compact convex polytope of dimension K-1 is a standard characterization in convex geometry. The extension of the NNLS problem by appending the single equality constraint follows directly from the problem statement in SLOT 1 of the TASK_FILE.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K} \|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{y}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1, \quad \mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}.$$

This constitutes a convex quadratic program (QP) with one linear equality constraint and $K$ linear inequality constraints. Because the constant term $\|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ is independent of $\mathbf{w}$, the problem is equivalent to minimizing $\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w} - 2\mathbf{c}^\top\mathbf{w}$ over $\Delta^K$. Dropping the irrelevant constant yields the reduced standard QP form over which all subsequent analysis is conducted [KB:spec.compile.research.md] [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf]:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The reduction to standard QP form by discarding the constant term ||y||^2 is valid since a constant does not change the location of the minimizer. This equivalence is used throughout the constrained LS literature and is consistent with SLOT 6 and SLOT 7 of the KB.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \Delta^K} \;\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w} - 2\mathbf{c}^\top\mathbf{w}.$$

To characterize the necessary and sufficient conditions for global optimality, the Lagrangian is formed by appending the equality constraint with a scalar multiplier $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$ (unrestricted in sign, as is standard for equality constraints) and the non-negativity constraints with a vector multiplier $\boldsymbol{\lambda} = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_K)^\top \geq \mathbf{0}$. Writing the equality constraint as $1 - \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 0$ and the inequality constraints as $-w_k \leq 0$, the Lagrangian is [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf]:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Lagrangian formulation with unrestricted equality multiplier mu and non-negative inequality multipliers lambda follows the standard KKT construction for mixed equality-inequality constrained QPs, as established in Boyd and Vandenberghe. The sign conventions are standard.]

$$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, \mu, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) = \mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w} - 2\mathbf{c}^\top\mathbf{w} + \mu\bigl(1 - \mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}\bigr) - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top\mathbf{w}.$$

Setting the gradient of $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to $\mathbf{w}$ to zero yields the stationarity condition. Since $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w}) = 2\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w}$ and $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}(-2\mathbf{c}^\top\mathbf{w}) = -2\mathbf{c}$, the stationarity requirement, written component-wise for each $k = 1, \dots, K$, is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The gradient of the quadratic and linear terms with respect to w is elementary. The component-wise form of the stationarity condition follows directly from differentiating the Lagrangian.]

$$2\bigl[\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{c}\bigr]_k = \mu + \lambda_k, \quad k = 1, \dots, K.$$

The complete KKT system for the unit-sum-constrained problem appends primal and dual feasibility together with complementary slackness to this stationarity requirement. Denoting the optimal solution as $\mathbf{w}^*$ with associated multipliers $\mu^*$ and $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^*$, the full system is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The complete KKT system for a QP with one equality and K inequality constraints comprises stationarity, primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and complementary slackness. All four groups of conditions are stated correctly, consistent with the standard KKT framework in Boyd and Vandenberghe.]

$$2\bigl[\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{c}\bigr]_k = \mu^* + \lambda_k^*, \quad k = 1, \dots, K,$$

$$\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}^* = 1, \quad \mathbf{w}^* \geq \mathbf{0},$$

$$\boldsymbol{\lambda}^* \geq \mathbf{0}, \quad \lambda_k^* w_k^* = 0, \quad k = 1, \dots, K.$$

Applying complementary slackness to the stationarity condition provides a complete characterization of $\mathbf{w}^*$. Let $\mathcal{P}^* = \{k : w_k^* > 0\}$ denote the passive set and $\mathcal{Z}^* = \{k : w_k^* = 0\}$ the active set. For each $k \in \mathcal{P}^*$, complementary slackness gives $\lambda_k^* = 0$, and the stationarity condition reduces to $2[\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{c}]_k = \mu^*$. For each $k \in \mathcal{Z}^*$, complementary slackness imposes no further restriction on $\lambda_k^* \geq 0$, so the stationarity condition requires $2[\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{c}]_k = \mu^* + \lambda_k^* \geq \mu^*$. The multiplier $\mu^*$ therefore plays the role of a common gradient level across all positively weighted GMPEs: at the optimal solution, the partial derivative of the squared-error objective with respect to each positive-weight branch equals the same constant $\mu^*$, while the corresponding derivative for each zero-weight branch is no smaller than $\mu^*$ [ARXIV:1309.1541] [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The characterization in terms of a common gradient value mu* for passive components and gradient >= mu* for active components is derived rigorously from the KKT conditions by complementary slackness. This structure is identical to the KKT conditions for Euclidean projection onto the probability simplex (ARXIV:1309.1541), confirming the result.]

The optimal solution $\mathbf{w}^*$ is unique. Positive definiteness of $\mathbf{Q}$ (under full column rank of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$) makes the objective strictly convex; a strictly convex function on the compact convex set $\Delta^K$ has exactly one global minimizer. Slater's constraint qualification is satisfied because any strictly interior point of $\Delta^K$ -- for instance, the equal-weight vector $\mathbf{w} = K^{-1}\mathbf{1}$, which satisfies $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$ and $w_k = K^{-1} > 0$ for all $k$ -- is a strictly feasible point. Under Slater's condition, the KKT conditions are both necessary and sufficient for global optimality, so any point satisfying the KKT system is the unique global minimizer [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Uniqueness of the global minimizer for a strictly convex function on a compact convex set is standard. Slater's condition is verified by the explicit interior point K^{-1}*1. The sufficiency of KKT conditions under Slater's condition for convex QPs is established in Boyd and Vandenberghe.]

Algorithmic computation of $\mathbf{w}^*$ follows by extending the Lawson-Hanson active-set strategy to incorporate the equality constraint in each sub-problem. Given a trial passive set $\mathcal{P}$, the equality-constrained sub-problem restricted to the components indexed by $\mathcal{P}$ is characterized by a $(|\mathcal{P}|+1) \times (|\mathcal{P}|+1)$ bordered linear system obtained by coupling the reduced normal equations with the constraint $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w}_\mathcal{P} = 1$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The bordered linear system for equality-constrained OLS is standard. The dimension count |P|+1 accounts for |P| weight components and one Lagrange multiplier mu. The construction is consistent with active-set extension principles for equality-constrained sub-problems.]

$$\begin{pmatrix} 2\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{P}} & -\mathbf{1}_{|\mathcal{P}|} \\ \mathbf{1}_{|\mathcal{P}|}^\top & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{w}_{\mathcal{P}} \\ \mu \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{P}} \\ 1 \end{pmatrix},$$

where $\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{P}}$ denotes the principal submatrix of $\mathbf{Q}$ indexed by $\mathcal{P}$, $\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{P}}$ is the corresponding sub-vector of $\mathbf{c}$, and $\mathbf{1}_{|\mathcal{P}|}$ is the all-ones vector of length $|\mathcal{P}|$. If the solution $\mathbf{w}_{\mathcal{P}}$ contains any negative components, those indices migrate to $\mathcal{Z}$ and the iteration repeats. Finite convergence is guaranteed because the number of distinct index partitions of $\{1, \dots, K\}$ is finite and the objective decreases monotonically at each non-degenerate step [WEB:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative_least_squares].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Finite convergence of active-set methods for strictly convex QPs is a classical result following from the finiteness of partitions and monotone decrease of the objective. The bordered linear system couples the equality constraint with the reduced normal equations in the standard way.]

For implementation in R, the unit-sum-constrained NNLS problem is accommodated directly by `quadprog::solve.QP` through specification of one meq equality constraint alongside the non-negativity inequalities, by the `lsei` package which solves least-squares problems under combined equality and inequality linear constraints, and by `CVXR` via disciplined convex programming with explicit simplex membership as a constraint [KB:spec.compile.research.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The use of quadprog and CVXR for constrained QP is described in SLOTs 6 and 7 of the KB. The lsei package for combined equality-inequality constrained LS is documented in the CRAN nnls ecosystem. All three solvers exploit convexity and converge to the global minimizer.]

## SLOT 2: Validity of post-hoc normalization versus constrained optimization

A natural expedient when facing the unit-sum constraint is to solve the KB's NNLS problem first -- enforcing only $\mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}$ without the normalization requirement -- and subsequently divide the resulting weight vector by its component sum to impose $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{w} = 1$. Given the NNLS solution $\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}} \geq \mathbf{0}$ with scalar sum $s = \mathbf{1}^\top\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}} > 0$, the post-hoc normalized weight vector is defined as:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The post-hoc normalization procedure is a precise mathematical statement of dividing all NNLS weights by their sum. The assumption s > 0 is non-restrictive: s = 0 would require all weights to be zero, which cannot occur at a minimum of the strictly convex objective unless y is in the null space of A (excluded by the full column rank assumption).]

$$\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \frac{\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}}{s} = \frac{\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}}{\mathbf{1}^\top\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}}.$$

The vector $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ is non-negative by construction (since $s > 0$ preserves sign) and satisfies $\mathbf{1}^\top\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = 1$, so it is a feasible point in $\Delta^K$ for the simplex-constrained problem. Feasibility alone, however, does not imply optimality in constrained optimization. To determine whether $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ coincides with the true optimal solution $\mathbf{w}^*$ of the simplex-constrained problem, it is necessary and sufficient -- under the strict convexity and Slater's condition established in SLOT 1 -- to verify that $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ satisfies the KKT conditions of the simplex-constrained problem.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The distinction between feasibility and optimality is a fundamental principle of constrained optimization. Under strict convexity and Slater's condition, the KKT conditions are necessary and sufficient for global optimality, so checking whether the candidate point satisfies these conditions is a complete test.]

The NNLS problem carries no equality constraint, so its associated Lagrange multiplier for any sum condition is effectively zero. The KKT stationarity conditions for the NNLS problem at $\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}$ require, for each index $k$ in the passive set $\mathcal{P} = \{k : \hat{w}_k^{\mathrm{NNLS}} > 0\}$ [KB:spec.compile.research.md]:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The NNLS KKT stationarity condition (gradient equals zero for positive-weight components) is derived in SLOT 5 of the KB and is the standard result for the NNLS problem. The passive set notation is consistent with the KB and with SLOT 1 above.]

$$2\bigl[\mathbf{Q}\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}} - \mathbf{c}\bigr]_k = 0, \quad k \in \mathcal{P},$$

from which it follows that $[\mathbf{Q}\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}]_k = c_k$ for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$. Since the passive and active sets are invariant under positive scaling, the normalized vector $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}/s$ has $\tilde{w}_k > 0$ for precisely the indices $k \in \mathcal{P}$. Evaluating the gradient of the objective at $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ for these indices yields:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The invariance of the active/passive partition under positive scaling s > 0 follows from sign preservation. Using the NNLS stationarity condition [Q w_hat]_k = c_k for k in P to substitute into the gradient at the normalized point is elementary algebra.]

$$2\bigl[\mathbf{Q}\tilde{\mathbf{w}} - \mathbf{c}\bigr]_k = \frac{2}{s}\bigl[\mathbf{Q}\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}\bigr]_k - 2c_k = \frac{2c_k}{s} - 2c_k = \frac{2c_k(1 - s)}{s}, \quad k \in \mathcal{P}.$$

For $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ to satisfy the KKT stationarity condition of the simplex-constrained problem, SLOT 1 requires this gradient component to equal the same constant $\mu^*$ for every $k \in \mathcal{P}$. Substituting the expression derived above, the required condition becomes:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The requirement that the gradient equal a common constant mu* for all positive-weight components is the KKT stationarity condition for the simplex-constrained problem, as established in SLOT 1. Setting the evaluated gradient equal to mu* is algebraically direct.]

$$\frac{2c_k(1 - s)}{s} = \mu^* \quad \text{for all } k \in \mathcal{P}.$$

Since the factor $2(1-s)/s$ is a single scalar independent of $k$, this condition is equivalent to requiring $c_k = [\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^\top\mathbf{y}]_k$ to be identical for every $k \in \mathcal{P}$. The quantity $c_k$ is the inner product of the $k$-th column of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ -- the log-spectral vector of the $k$-th candidate GMPE -- with the log-target $\mathbf{y}$. In a well-posed calibration problem, candidate GMPEs are deliberately chosen to encode distinct scientific assumptions about source scaling, geometric spreading, and site amplification, so their log-spectral vectors project differently onto any given target $\mathbf{y}$. The condition $c_k = \mathrm{const}$ for all $k \in \mathcal{P}$ therefore holds only on a measure-zero set of problem instances in the joint space of design matrices and target vectors, not for the typical seismic-hazard calibration dataset [KB:spec.compile.research.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The equivalence of the gradient equality condition to equal c_k values for active indices follows from the algebra. The characterization as measure-zero is standard: equal inner products of distinct vectors with a given target is as restrictive as the target lying in the common orthogonal complement of all differences (a_j - a_k) for j,k in P. The TASK_FILE context notes that candidate GMPEs span distinct spectral shapes by design.]

The only non-trivial case in which post-hoc normalization recovers the correct answer arises when $s = 1$, meaning the NNLS solution already satisfies the unit-sum constraint without any normalization. In that circumstance, the procedure applies a trivial identity transformation ($\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = \hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}}$), and the two solutions coincide by construction. This case does not represent a meaningful equivalence of the two optimization procedures: it merely confirms that if the NNLS solution happens to lie on the simplex, then no normalization is needed. For all other instances ($s \neq 1$), the gradient components at $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ are proportional to $c_k$, and the equal-gradient requirement fails generically.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The s=1 case is correctly identified as the only degenerate exception. The statement that this does not represent a general equivalence is sound: s=1 is itself a non-generic condition, and even when s=1, the coincidence is a consequence of the NNLS solution being already feasible rather than of any structural equivalence between the two problems.]

A concrete counter-example establishes the non-equivalence rigorously by direct computation. Let $K = 2$, $n = 2$, $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{I}_2$, and $\mathbf{y} = (3, 1)^\top$. The NNLS objective reduces to $(w_1 - 3)^2 + (w_2 - 1)^2$; the unconstrained minimum at $(3,\,1)^\top$ is already non-negative, so $\hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{NNLS}} = (3,1)^\top$, $s = 4$, and the post-hoc normalized vector is $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = (3/4,\,1/4)^\top$. For the simplex-constrained problem, the substitution $w_2 = 1 - w_1$ reduces the objective to $f(w_1) = (w_1 - 3)^2 + (1 - w_1 - 1)^2 = (w_1 - 3)^2 + w_1^2 = 2w_1^2 - 6w_1 + 9$ on $[0, 1]$; since $f'(w_1) = 4w_1 - 6 < 0$ for all $w_1 \in [0, 1]$, the function is strictly decreasing on the feasible interval and the constrained minimum is at the right boundary $w_1^* = 1$, giving $\mathbf{w}^* = (1,\,0)^\top$. The corresponding objective values are:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The counter-example is elementary and verifiable by direct substitution. The NNLS solution (3,1) is non-negative and equals the unconstrained minimum, confirming it as the NNLS optimum. The simplex-constrained solution (1,0) is obtained correctly: f'(w_1) = 4w_1-6 < 0 for w_1 in [0,1], so f is strictly decreasing and the minimum on [0,1] is at w_1=1.]

$$f\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{w}}\right) = \left(\tfrac{3}{4} - 3\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac{1}{4} - 1\right)^2 = \frac{81}{16} + \frac{9}{16} = \frac{90}{16} \approx 5.625,$$

$$f\!\left(\mathbf{w}^*\right) = (1 - 3)^2 + (0 - 1)^2 = 4 + 1 = 5.$$

The strict inequality $f(\tilde{\mathbf{w}}) > f(\mathbf{w}^*)$ confirms that $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} \neq \mathbf{w}^*$ and that post-hoc normalization yields a strictly suboptimal feasible point for the simplex-constrained problem [KB:spec.compile.research.md] [ARXIV:1309.1541].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The objective values 90/16 and 5 are confirmed by direct arithmetic. The strict inequality 90/16 > 5 is correct (90/16 = 5.625 > 5). The conclusion that post-hoc normalization is suboptimal follows immediately from the strict inequality and the fact that w* is the unique minimizer on the simplex.]

The geometric interpretation reinforces this conclusion. The NNLS problem optimizes over the non-negative orthant $\mathbb{R}^K_+$ -- a cone -- and simultaneously selects both the optimal direction and the optimal scale of $\mathbf{w}$. For any fixed direction $\mathbf{u} \in \Delta^K$, the scale $s^*(\mathbf{u})$ minimizing $f(s\mathbf{u}) = s^2\,\mathbf{u}^\top\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{u} - 2s\,\mathbf{c}^\top\mathbf{u} + \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ over $s > 0$ is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The expression for the optimal scale s*(u) is obtained by differentiating f(su) with respect to s and setting to zero: 2s*(u^T Q u) = 2(c^T u), giving s* = (c^T u)/(u^T Q u). This derivation is elementary and the formula is standard in the analysis of ray-parameterized quadratic objectives.]

$$s^*(\mathbf{u}) = \frac{\mathbf{c}^\top\mathbf{u}}{\mathbf{u}^\top\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{u}}.$$

The NNLS problem thus selects the direction $\mathbf{u}^{\mathrm{NNLS}} \in \Delta^K$ and scale $s^*(\mathbf{u}^{\mathrm{NNLS}})$ jointly by minimizing $\|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2 - (\mathbf{c}^\top\mathbf{u})^2/(\mathbf{u}^\top\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{u})$ over $\mathbf{u} \in \Delta^K$, whereas the simplex-constrained problem directly minimizes $f(\mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{u}^\top\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{u} - 2\mathbf{c}^\top\mathbf{u} + \|\mathbf{y}\|_2^2$ over $\mathbf{u} \in \Delta^K$ with the scale fixed at unity. These are distinct optimization problems in the direction variable $\mathbf{u}$: the former is a nonlinear fractional program; the latter is a strictly convex QP. Their respective optimal directions on $\Delta^K$ are therefore generically different, and normalizing the NNLS solution to the simplex does not recover the solution of the simplex-constrained QP [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The identification of the NNLS problem as minimizing a fractional function of the direction u (after optimal scale elimination) and the simplex-constrained problem as a standard QP in u is mathematically correct. The two objective functions are structurally different (fractional vs. quadratic), so their minimizers over the same domain Delta^K are generically different. This argument is rigorous and consistent with convex optimization principles.]

Post-hoc normalization of the NNLS weight vector is therefore not a valid mathematical substitute for solving the properly formulated simplex-constrained optimization problem. The two procedures address geometrically distinct problems over different feasible sets, satisfy different KKT stationarity conditions at their respective optima, and yield different optimal weight vectors in all non-degenerate problem instances. A rigorous calibration of the GMPE logic-tree branch weights under the unit-sum constraint requires solving the QP on $\Delta^K$ directly, as formulated in SLOT 1, employing a dedicated equality-inequality constrained solver rather than post-processing the NNLS solution [KB:spec.compile.research.md] [WEB:https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The conclusion is supported rigorously by the KKT analysis (non-generic equality condition on c_k), the concrete counter-example (objective values 5.625 vs 5.0), and the geometric argument (distinct optimization problems in the direction variable). The recommendation to use a dedicated solver is the operationally correct corollary of the mathematical non-equivalence established above.]
