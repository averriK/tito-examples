## SLOT 5: Summarize spectral matching methodologies

State-of-practice ground motion selection methodologies generally fall into three categories. Amplitude scaling applies uniform scalar factors to ground motion records to match a target intensity measure while preserving the original frequency content and duration characteristics of the recorded motions. [WEB:https://ngawest2.berkeley.edu/] Spectral matching modifies ground motion records in the frequency domain using wavelet addition or time-domain adjustment techniques to achieve close agreement with a target response spectrum across multiple periods, though this approach alters the frequency content and phase characteristics of the original records. Unscaled selection chooses ground motion records based on scenario characteristics such as magnitude, distance, and site conditions without amplitude modification, relying on a sufficient number of records to capture variability. The choice among these approaches involves trade-offs between preserving the natural characteristics of recorded ground motions, reducing record-to-record variability in structural response, and meeting code or project requirements for spectrum compatibility across specified period ranges.

## SLOT 6: Investigate PEER NGA spectral matching methodology

The PEER NGA-West2 online ground motion selection tool provides amplitude scaling functionality but does not perform spectral matching that modifies frequency content. [WEB:https://ngawest2.berkeley.edu/] All downloaded records from the PEER database are provided as unscaled and as-recorded (unrotated) time histories. [WEB:https://ngawest2.berkeley.edu/] The online tool includes a scaling calculator that assists users in determining appropriate scalar factors to apply to selected records, but applying these scale factors is performed by the user outside the database system to avoid confusion with file versioning. [WEB:https://ngawest2.berkeley.edu/] This approach preserves the original frequency content, phase characteristics, and duration properties of the recorded ground motions. The NGA-East database follows a similar philosophy, providing recorded ground motions for stable continental regions without built-in spectral matching capabilities that would alter waveform characteristics. Users seeking to perform frequency-domain spectral matching must employ separate software tools outside the PEER database environment.

## SLOT 8: Explain spectral matching drift at short periods

The poor spectral matching at short periods despite good matching at medium and long periods is attributed to a mismatch between the site ground condition at Louvicourt ($V_{S30}$ = 1,853 m/s) and the softer ground conditions where the selected ground motion records were originally recorded. [KB:output.md] The selected records shown in the suite for the short period range have site conditions ranging from $V_{S30}$ = 312 m/s to 826 m/s, substantially softer than the Louvicourt bedrock condition. [KB:output.md] Moderate- to high-frequency ground motions are attenuated faster by weaker seismic ground conditions compared to stiffer ground conditions, and high-frequency ground motions attenuate faster than low-frequency ground motions. [KB:output.md] To capture the high-frequency (short-period) ground motions indicated by the target uniform hazard spectrum at the hard rock Louvicourt site, records with shorter source-to-site distances than suggested by disaggregation were required, as evidenced by several selected records having distances substantially shorter than the modal 170 km from disaggregation. [KB:output.md] Some records required large scale factors (notably RSN 1833 with a factor of 8.28 and RSN 2927 with a factor of 6.85) to achieve even approximate matching in the short period range. [KB:output.md] The absence of suitable candidate motions from intraplate stable continental conditions at the required hard rock site condition necessitated the use of plate boundary records with inherently different high-frequency characteristics.
