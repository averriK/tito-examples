## SLOT 2: Highest-density metal and structural relevance

Among the three metals in the KB, copper has the greatest density at 8.96 g/cm$^{3}$, surpassing iron at 7.874 g/cm$^{3}$ and aluminum at 2.70 g/cm$^{3}$. High density is relevant to structural applications because mass per unit volume governs the inertial and gravitational behavior of a component. A material with the density of copper, approximately 3.3 times that of aluminum, provides substantially greater resistance to displacement and deformation for an equivalent geometric cross-section. This property makes dense metals advantageous in applications that require counterweights, vibration damping, radiation shielding, or resistance to dynamic impact, where the inherent mass of the material contributes directly to functional performance.
