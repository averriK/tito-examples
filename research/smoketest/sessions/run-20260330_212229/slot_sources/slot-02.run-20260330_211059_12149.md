## SLOT 2: Highest-density metal and structural relevance

Among the three metals catalogued in the knowledge base, copper possesses the greatest density at $8.96\ \text{g/cm}^3$, surpassing iron at $7.874\ \text{g/cm}^3$ and aluminum at $2.70\ \text{g/cm}^3$. [KB:metals.md]
^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The density values for all three metals are stated explicitly in metals.md. The identification of copper as the densest follows by direct comparison of the reported figures without additional inference.]

High density is relevant to structural applications because it determines the mass contained within a given volume of material. A greater mass per unit volume increases the self-weight of structural components, which affects the loads transferred to foundations and supporting elements, and can constrain geometric and material selection decisions. In applications where load-bearing capacity per unit cross-section is the primary criterion, higher density may accompany greater mechanical stiffness or strength for certain materials; however, the additional weight imposes design constraints in contexts where minimizing total mass is a priority. The net suitability of a high-density material in any structural role depends on the balance between mechanical performance requirements and permissible weight. [KB:metals.md]
