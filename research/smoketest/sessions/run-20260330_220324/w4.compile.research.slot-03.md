## SLOT 3: Iron versus aluminum comparison

The principal property differences between iron and aluminum, as documented in the knowledge base, are summarized below. [@MetalsKB]

- **Melting point**: Iron melts at $1538\,^\circ\text{C}$; aluminum melts at $660\,^\circ\text{C}$, a difference of $878\,^\circ\text{C}$. Iron therefore exhibits substantially greater thermal resistance, maintaining its solid state at temperatures well above aluminum's melting threshold. [@MetalsKB][@RSCIron][@RSCAluminium]
- **Density**: Iron has a density of $7.874\,\text{g/cm}^3$; aluminum has a density of $2.70\,\text{g/cm}^3$. Iron is approximately 2.9 times denser, resulting in a substantially greater mass per unit volume; aluminum offers a corresponding mass advantage in weight-critical applications. [@MetalsKB]
- **Atomic number**: Iron has atomic number 26; aluminum has atomic number 13, reflecting their distinct positions in the periodic table. [@MetalsKB]

Taken together, iron is denser and thermally more stable, while aluminum is substantially lighter and melts at a significantly lower temperature.
