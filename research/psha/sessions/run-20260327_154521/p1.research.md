# Particularization of PSHA Equations for a Single Circular Areal Source

## SLOT 1: Distance Probability Density Function for the Circular Areal Source

For a site located at the center of a circular areal source of radius $R$ with uniformly distributed seismicity, the probability density function $f_R(r)$ is derived from first principles of geometric probability. The uniform seismicity assumption implies that earthquake epicenters are distributed with constant areal density $\rho = 1/(\pi R^2)$ over the disk of area $\pi R^2$. The probability that any individual earthquake originates within the annular ring bounded by radii $r$ and $r + dr$ is proportional to the area of that ring, which is the fundamental geometric element of the derivation. [KB:hazard.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The derivation relies on standard geometric probability. The uniform seismicity and single circular source assumptions are stated in the task and are consistent with the Cornell-McGuire PSHA framework described in KB:hazard.qmd. No external source is required to verify this premise.]

The differential area element of an annular ring at radius $r$ with thickness $dr$ is $dA(r) = 2\pi r\, dr$. Since seismicity is uniform, the probability that an earthquake originates within this ring equals the ratio of the ring area to the total disk area $\pi R^2$, giving $dP = 2\pi r\, dr / (\pi R^2) = (2r/R^2)\,dr$. This probability element identifies the distance probability density function directly:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The derivation follows from standard geometric probability applied to uniform areal seismicity. The annular area element $2\pi r\,dr$ is elementary, and the normalization over the disk area $\pi R^2$ is a direct consequence of uniform density. The step is self-contained and requires no external reference beyond the geometric setup stated in the task.]

$$f_R(r) = \frac{2r}{R^2}, \qquad 0 \leq r \leq R$$

The cumulative distribution function $F_R(r)$ follows by integration over $[0, r]$:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The PDF $f_R(r) = 2r/R^2$ is the standard result for a site at the center of a uniformly seismic circular source, well established in the PSHA literature [WEB:https://www.jackwbaker.com/Publications/Baker_(2013)_Intro_to_PSHA_v2.pdf] and consistent with the general distance distribution framework in KB:hazard.qmd. The lead-in to the CDF is an analytic deduction requiring no further citation.]

$$F_R(r) = \int_0^r \frac{2u}{R^2}\, du = \frac{r^2}{R^2}, \qquad 0 \leq r \leq R$$

Normalization is confirmed by $F_R(R) = R^2/R^2 = 1$. The density $f_R(r)$ is monotonically increasing in $r$, reflecting the fact that a uniformly distributed source contains more area at larger distances from the central site; the probability of occurrence at very small epicentral distance is correspondingly small, approaching zero as $r \to 0$. [KB:hazard.qmd] [WEB:https://www.jackwbaker.com/Publications/Baker_(2013)_Intro_to_PSHA_v2.pdf]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The CDF expression and its normalization are exact results from direct integration of the PDF. The monotonicity observation is a simple consequence of the linear form of $f_R(r)$ and is consistent with standard treatments in KB:hazard.qmd and in Baker (2013) [WEB:https://www.jackwbaker.com/Publications/Baker_(2013)_Intro_to_PSHA_v2.pdf].]

Under the uniform seismicity assumption, earthquake locations are spatially independent of earthquake magnitude, so the conditional distance distribution given magnitude reduces to the unconditional form, $f_{R|M}(r|m) = f_R(r) = 2r/R^2$. This independence property carries through to all subsequent integrals and is used explicitly in the particularized hazard and disaggregation formulations of Slots 2 and 4. [KB:hazard.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The independence of source location and magnitude is a standard assumption of uniform areal source models in PSHA. It is implicit in the general formulation of KB:hazard.qmd, which separates $f_{M,s}(m)$ from $f_{\mathbf{R}|M,s}(\mathbf{r}|m)$ as independent factors in the hazard integral. For a spatially uniform source, the conditional reduces to the marginal.]

## SLOT 2: Particularized Annual Exceedance Rate for the Single Circular Source

Particularization of the general hazard integral to the single circular areal source requires explicit specification of the occurrence rate, the magnitude PDF, the conditional exceedance probability, and the distance PDF derived in Slot 1. Starting from @eq-hazard-integral in `kb/hazard.qmd`, the single-source annual exceedance rate is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The general hazard integral @eq-hazard-integral is taken directly from KB:hazard.qmd, which is the authoritative KB source for this derivation. Reducing to a single source drops the summation over sources present in @eq-hazard-sum. The claim is fully supported.]

$$\lambda_I(i^*) = \nu_0 \int_{M_{\min}}^{M_{\max}} \int_D P\!\left[I > i^* \mid m, \mathbf{r}\right] f_M(m)\, f_{\mathbf{R}|M}(\mathbf{r}|m)\, d\mathbf{r}\, dm$$

The four components of this integral are developed in turn below. [KB:hazard.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The statement that four components are required is an organizational observation about the derivation structure, fully consistent with the form of @eq-hazard-integral in KB:hazard.qmd.]

**Component (i): Annual occurrence rate.** The Gutenberg-Richter recurrence law specifies $\log_{10} N(M) = a - bM$, where $N(M)$ is the mean annual number of earthquakes with magnitude at or above $M$. Evaluating at the engineering minimum magnitude $M_{\min}$ yields the annual occurrence rate above $M_{\min}$, consistent with @eq-branch-rate in `kb/uncertainty_model.qmd`:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The expression for $\nu_0$ follows from direct evaluation of the Gutenberg-Richter law at $M_{\min}$, as formalized in @eq-branch-rate in KB:uncertainty_model.qmd. For a single epistemic branch, the branch superscript $(k)$ is dropped. The derivation is standard and unambiguous.]

$$\nu_0 = 10^{a - b\,M_{\min}}$$

**Component (ii): Truncated Gutenberg-Richter magnitude PDF.** Consistent with @eq-branch-pdf in `kb/uncertainty_model.qmd`, the normalized magnitude density over the support $[M_{\min}, M_{\max}]$ is obtained by dividing the Gutenberg-Richter density by its integral over the magnitude range. Evaluating the denominator gives $\int_{M_{\min}}^{M_{\max}} 10^{a - bu}\,du = (10^a / b\ln 10)(10^{-bM_{\min}} - 10^{-bM_{\max}})$, and canceling the common factor $10^a$ yields the simplified form:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The PDF is derived directly from @eq-branch-pdf in KB:uncertainty_model.qmd by evaluating the normalization integral in closed form. Factoring out $10^{-bM_{\min}}$ from numerator and denominator is an elementary algebraic step. The resulting expression matches the standard doubly-truncated Gutenberg-Richter PDF [ZOTERO:XSFGD78R].]

$$f_M(m) = \frac{b\ln 10\; 10^{-b(m - M_{\min})}}{1 - 10^{-b(M_{\max}-M_{\min})}}, \qquad M_{\min} \leq m \leq M_{\max}$$

**Component (iii): Conditional exceedance probability.** No specific GMPE is assumed; the ground-motion parameter $I$ is treated as lognormally distributed with median $\hat{\eta}_I(m, r)$ and logarithmic standard deviation $\sigma_{\ln I}$, consistent with @eq-branch-exc in `kb/uncertainty_model.qmd`. The conditional probability of exceeding intensity $i^*$ given an event of magnitude $m$ at distance $r$ under reference rock conditions is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The conditional exceedance probability expression is taken from @eq-branch-exc in KB:uncertainty_model.qmd. The lognormal ground-motion model is the standard PSHA assumption. Reference rock conditions are satisfied by the absence of site amplification terms in $\hat{\eta}_I(m,r)$, consistent with the task constraint. The claim is fully supported [ZOTERO:XSFGD78R].]

$$P\!\left[I > i^* \mid m, r\right] = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln i^* - \ln\hat{\eta}_I(m, r)}{\sigma_{\ln I}}\right)$$

where $\Phi(\cdot)$ is the standard normal CDF. The argument of $\Phi$ is the signed number of logarithmic standard deviations by which $i^*$ lies above the median prediction $\hat{\eta}_I(m,r)$; when $i^* = \hat{\eta}_I$ the exceedance probability equals exactly 0.5. [KB:uncertainty_model.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The interpretation of the $\Phi$ argument follows from the lognormal model. The claim that exceedance probability equals 0.5 at the median is an exact analytical property of the symmetric normal distribution. Both statements are consistent with KB:uncertainty_model.qmd.]

**Component (iv): Particularized double integral.** Substituting $\nu_0 = 10^{a-bM_{\min}}$, the magnitude PDF from Component (ii), and the circular source distance PDF $f_R(r) = 2r/R^2$ (with the independence $f_{R|M}(r|m) = f_R(r)$) into the general hazard integral, the particularized annual exceedance rate for the single circular areal source is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The expression assembles the four components derived individually above, all traceable to @eq-hazard-integral in KB:hazard.qmd, @eq-branch-rate and @eq-branch-pdf in KB:uncertainty_model.qmd, and the distance PDF from Slot 1. The substitution is algebraically direct and fully supported by the KB sources.]

$$\lambda_I(i^*) = \nu_0 \int_{M_{\min}}^{M_{\max}} \int_0^R \left[1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln i^* - \ln\hat{\eta}_I(m, r)}{\sigma_{\ln I}}\right)\right] \frac{b\ln 10\; 10^{-b(m-M_{\min})}}{1 - 10^{-b(M_{\max}-M_{\min})}} \cdot \frac{2r}{R^2}\, dr\, dm$$

with $\nu_0 = 10^{a - b\,M_{\min}}$. For a general GMPE $\hat{\eta}_I(m,r)$, this double integral does not admit a closed-form solution, and numerical quadrature over the rectangle $[M_{\min}, M_{\max}] \times [0, R]$ is required. The integrand is non-negative throughout the domain, approaches zero as $r \to 0$ (because $f_R(r) \to 0$), and decays to zero as $i^*$ grows large relative to $\hat{\eta}_I(m, r)$. [KB:hazard.qmd] [KB:uncertainty_model.qmd] [WEB:https://www.jackwbaker.com/Publications/Baker_(2013)_Intro_to_PSHA_v2.pdf]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The requirement for numerical evaluation of the double integral for general GMPE forms is well established in the PSHA literature. The limiting behaviors are direct consequences of the mathematical properties of $f_R(r)$ and $\Phi(\cdot)$. Both claims are consistent with KB:hazard.qmd and standard PSHA practice [ZOTERO:XSFGD78R].]

## SLOT 3: Maximum Credible Earthquake Ground-Motion Intensity

The maximum credible earthquake (MCE) at the site is defined by the largest earthquake magnitude the source can produce, $M_{\max}$, occurring at source-to-site distance $r$. The objective is to determine the ground-motion intensity $i^*_{\text{MCE}}$ such that its annual exceedance probability equals a prescribed target AEP -- for example, $\text{AEP} = 1/T_R$ for a given return period $T_R$. The AEP relation from @eq-aep in `kb/hazard.qmd` links the annual exceedance rate to the annual exceedance probability under the Poisson occurrence assumption:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The MCE definition and the reference to @eq-aep in KB:hazard.qmd are taken directly from the task and from the KB source. The Poisson assumption underlying @eq-aep is stated explicitly in KB:hazard.qmd. The claim is fully supported.]

$$\text{AEP} = P_{1\text{yr}}[I > i^*] = 1 - \exp\!\left[-\lambda_I(i^*)\right]$$

Solving for the rate corresponding to the target AEP yields:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The display of @eq-aep is taken verbatim from KB:hazard.qmd. The lead-in correctly notes the Poisson assumption. The result is fully supported.]

$$\lambda_I(i^*_{\text{MCE}}) = -\ln\!\left(1 - \text{AEP}\right) = -\ln\!\left(1 - \frac{1}{T_R}\right) \approx \frac{1}{T_R}, \qquad T_R \gg 1$$

The approximation $-\ln(1 - 1/T_R) \approx 1/T_R$ holds for large return periods and is noted in the footnote to @eq-aep in `kb/hazard.qmd`. This relation defines $i^*_{\text{MCE}}$ implicitly as the intensity level on the hazard curve at which the annual exceedance rate equals the target value. Because the full hazard integral (Slot 2) requires numerical evaluation, the inversion of the hazard curve to recover $i^*_{\text{MCE}}$ likewise requires numerical methods. [KB:hazard.qmd]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The algebraic inversion of @eq-aep is exact. The large-$T_R$ approximation is explicitly noted in KB:hazard.qmd. The statement that numerical inversion is required follows from the non-closed-form nature of the particularized hazard integral established in Slot 2. All claims are supported by KB:hazard.qmd.]

For the MCE scenario specifically, the ground motion is conditioned on an event of magnitude $M_{\max}$ at distance $r$. The intensity $I$ is lognormal with median $\hat{\eta}_I(M_{\max}, r)$ and logarithmic standard deviation $\sigma_{\ln I}$, and the conditional exceedance probability is:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The conditional exceedance probability follows directly from @eq-branch-exc in KB:uncertainty_model.qmd restricted to the scenario $m = M_{\max}$. The lognormal model applies here without modification. The claim is fully supported.]

$$P\!\left[I > i^* \mid M_{\max}, r\right] = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln i^* - \ln\hat{\eta}_I(M_{\max}, r)}{\sigma_{\ln I}}\right)$$

Setting this probability equal to the target AEP and inverting:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The step preceding the displayed equations is a standard algebraic setup for inverting the lognormal conditional exceedance probability. It is fully consistent with the GMPE model in KB:uncertainty_model.qmd.]

$$\Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln i^*_{\text{MCE}} - \ln\hat{\eta}_I(M_{\max}, r)}{\sigma_{\ln I}}\right) = 1 - \text{AEP}$$

$$i^*_{\text{MCE}} = \hat{\eta}_I(M_{\max}, r)\cdot\exp\!\left[\sigma_{\ln I}\cdot\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1 - \text{AEP}\right)\right]$$

For $\text{AEP} = 1/T_R$, the expression becomes:^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The algebraic inversion of the normal CDF relationship to isolate $i^*_{\text{MCE}}$ is exact. The result follows by exponentiation after applying $\Phi^{-1}$ to both sides. The derivation is consistent with KB:uncertainty_model.qmd.]

$$i^*_{\text{MCE}} = \hat{\eta}_I(M_{\max}, r)\cdot\exp\!\left[\sigma_{\ln I}\cdot\Phi^{-1}\!\!\left(1 - \frac{1}{T_R}\right)\right]$$

The factor $\varepsilon^*_{\text{MCE}} = \Phi^{-1}(1 - 1/T_R)$ is the number of logarithmic standard deviations above the median at which $i^*_{\text{MCE}}$ lies; for large $T_R$ this factor is positive and increases with $T_R$, reflecting the expectation that rarer ground-motion levels require above-median GMPE residuals. The representative distance $r$ for the MCE scenario may be taken as the mean distance of the circular source, $\bar{r} = \int_0^R r\, f_R(r)\, dr = 2R/3$, or as the distance $r \to 0^+$ for a conservative bound; the choice must be stated explicitly for any specific application. [KB:hazard.qmd] [KB:uncertainty_model.qmd]^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The interpretation of $\varepsilon^*_{\text{MCE}}$ and its monotone growth with $T_R$ are analytically correct consequences of the properties of $\Phi^{-1}$. The computation of the mean distance $\bar{r} = 2R/3$ follows from integrating $r \cdot f_R(r) = 2r^2/R^2$ over $[0,R]$, which is exact. The recommendation to use $\bar{r}$ or $r \to 0^+$ is physically motivated but represents a modeling choice not uniquely specified by KB:hazard.qmd or KB:uncertainty_model.qmd; it is presented as a suggestion, not a KB-derived result, which justifies the MEDIUM confidence.]

The two perspectives on $i^*_{\text{MCE}}$ are complementary. The full probabilistic approach (inversion of the complete hazard curve from Slot 2) is consistent with the Poisson-PSHA framework of @eq-aep and integrates over the continuous distributions of all magnitude-distance pairs; the scenario-conditioned expression provides an analytical closed form for rapid estimation at a representative distance. Both expressions are anchored to the AEP definition of @eq-aep in `kb/hazard.qmd`. [KB:hazard.qmd] [ZOTERO:B9QX9ZYI]^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The methodological comparison between the full probabilistic and scenario-conditioned MCE approaches is consistent with the PSHA framework in KB:hazard.qmd.
