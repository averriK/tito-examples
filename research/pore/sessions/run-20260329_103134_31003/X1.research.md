# Pore Pressure Proxy Model: Site Response and Shear Strains

## SLOT 1: Closed-form analytical solutions for the seismic site acceleration response

### Governing Wave Equation and Boundary Conditions

The one-dimensional seismic response of a saturated fine-grained soil stratum is governed by the equilibrium of a horizontally polarized shear (SH) wave propagating vertically through a viscoelastic medium. For a stratum of thickness $H_s$ with depth coordinate $z$ measured downward from the free surface ($z = 0$ at the surface, $z = H_s$ at the rock interface), the equation of motion in terms of horizontal displacement $u(z,t)$ is

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\!\left[G(z)\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}\right] = \rho\,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}$$

where $G(z)$ is the (possibly depth-dependent) shear modulus and $\rho$ is the mass density, assumed uniform. [WEB:https://nheri-simcenter.github.io/TFT-Documentation/theory.html]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This is the standard 1D SH wave equation appearing in every major reference on seismic site response (Kramer 1996; Roesset 1977); the form is confirmed by the cited documentation and is internally consistent with Newton's second law and the constitutive relation for a linear viscoelastic solid.]

For a linear viscoelastic medium with frequency-independent hysteretic damping ratio $D$, the shear modulus is replaced by the complex modulus $G^* = G_0\,(1 + 2iD)$ and the analysis proceeds in the frequency domain. Transforming to the frequency domain with $U(z,\omega)$ as the Fourier amplitude of $u(z,t)$, the equation becomes

$$\frac{d}{dz}\!\left[G(z)\,\frac{dU}{dz}\right] + \rho\,\omega^2\,U = 0$$

[WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0267726122003773]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The frequency-domain form of the 1D wave equation for a viscoelastic stratum is a standard result; the cited source (Analytical 1D transfer functions for layered soils, SDEE 2022) derives this formulation explicitly for layered inhomogeneous deposits.]

Two boundary conditions complete the problem. At the free surface ($z = 0$), the shear stress vanishes:

$$G(0)\,\frac{dU}{dz}\bigg|_{z=0} = 0$$

At the rock interface ($z = H_s$), the horizontal acceleration is prescribed as the input ground motion $a_g(t)$, corresponding to a prescribed displacement amplitude $U_g(\omega) = A_g(\omega)/(-\omega^2)$ in the frequency domain:

$$U(H_s,\,\omega) = U_g(\omega)$$

When the underlying rock is modeled as a compliant half-space rather than a rigid boundary, the base condition is replaced by a radiation condition incorporating the impedance ratio $\kappa = \rho_s V_s / (\rho_r V_r)$ between the soil and the rock. [WEB:https://nheri-simcenter.github.io/TFT-Documentation/theory.html]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Boundary conditions for 1D site response are well established. The free-surface zero-stress condition and the prescribed base displacement (rigid rock) or impedance radiation condition (compliant rock) are documented consistently across the cited sources and in the broader literature (Kramer 1996, Chapter 7).]

### Uniform-Stiffness Stratum

When the stratum is homogeneous, $G(z) = G_0 = \text{const}$, the frequency-domain wave equation reduces to

$$\frac{d^2 U}{dz^2} + (k^*)^2\,U = 0, \qquad k^* = \frac{\omega}{V_s^*}$$

where $V_s^* = \sqrt{G^*/\rho}$ is the complex shear wave velocity. The general solution is

$$U(z,\omega) = A\,e^{ik^*z} + B\,e^{-ik^*z}$$

Applying the boundary conditions: the zero-stress condition at $z = 0$ gives $A = B$, and the prescribed base displacement at $z = H_s$ determines the amplitude. The displacement field is

$$U(z,\omega) = U_g(\omega)\,\frac{\cos(k^*z)}{\cos(k^*H_s)}$$

The corresponding acceleration transfer function, relating the acceleration at depth $z$ to the base input acceleration $A_g(\omega) = -\omega^2 U_g(\omega)$, takes the same form:

$$H_a(z,\omega) = \frac{A(z,\omega)}{A_g(\omega)} = \frac{\cos(k^*z)}{\cos(k^*H_s)}$$

[WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0267726122003773][WEB:https://nheri-simcenter.github.io/TFT-Documentation/theory.html]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The cosine-ratio transfer function for a uniform viscoelastic layer between a free surface and a rigid base is a classical result derived in Roesset (1977), Kramer (1996), and confirmed in both cited sources. The derivation follows directly from the general solution to the constant-coefficient ODE under the stated boundary conditions.]

At the free surface ($z = 0$), this simplifies to the well-known surface amplification function:

$$H_a(0,\omega) = \frac{1}{\cos(k^*H_s)}$$

The denominator vanishes when $\cos(k^*H_s) = 0$, yielding resonant (natural) frequencies. For the undamped case ($D = 0$, $k^* = \omega/V_s$), these are

$$\omega_n = \frac{(2n-1)\,\pi\,V_s}{2H_s}, \qquad f_n = \frac{(2n-1)\,V_s}{4H_s}, \qquad n = 1,2,3,\ldots$$

The fundamental frequency $f_1 = V_s/(4H_s)$ corresponds to the quarter-wavelength resonance condition $H_s = \lambda_1/4$. When damping is nonzero, the complex wavenumber $k^* = \omega/\sqrt{G_0(1+2iD)/\rho}$ shifts the poles off the real frequency axis and bounds the amplification at resonance to a finite value proportional to $1/(2D)$ for small $D$. [WEB:https://nheri-simcenter.github.io/TFT-Documentation/theory.html]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Natural frequency formulas for a uniform viscoelastic layer are standard results (Kramer 1996; Roesset 1977) fully consistent with the transfer function poles derived above; the resonance condition and the finite amplification at damped resonance are well documented in both cited and general references.]

#### Modal Decomposition

The time-domain acceleration field can be reconstructed by modal superposition. The mode shapes for the uniform undamped stratum (orthogonal with respect to mass) are

$$\phi_n(z) = \cos\!\left(\frac{(2n-1)\pi z}{2H_s}\right), \qquad n = 1,2,3,\ldots$$

where $z = 0$ is the free surface. The response is expanded as

$$u(z,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \phi_n(z)\,q_n(t)$$

Each generalized coordinate $q_n(t)$ satisfies the equation of a single-degree-of-freedom (SDOF) oscillator driven by the base acceleration:

$$\ddot{q}_n + 2D\,\omega_n\,\dot{q}_n + \omega_n^2\,q_n = -\Gamma_n\,a_g(t)$$

The modal participation factor for a uniform deposit with constant density $\rho$ is

$$\Gamma_n = \frac{\int_0^{H_s} \rho\,\phi_n(z)\,dz}{\int_0^{H_s} \rho\,\phi_n^2(z)\,dz} = \frac{4\,(-1)^{n+1}}{(2n-1)\,\pi}$$

The total acceleration at depth $z$ is then

$$a(z,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \phi_n(z)\,\ddot{q}_n(t)$$

[WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0267726122003773]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The modal expansion for a uniform layer, mode shapes, natural frequencies, and participation factors are standard results in structural and geotechnical dynamics; participation factor values are confirmed by direct integration and are consistent with the frequency-domain transfer function via residue analysis.]

### Inhomogeneous Stratum: Power-Law Stiffness Profile

For a stratum where the shear modulus increases with depth according to a power-law profile,

$$G(z) = G_b\!\left(\frac{z}{H_s}\right)^m, \qquad 0 < m < 2$$

where $G_b = G(H_s)$ is the base modulus and $z$ is measured downward from the free surface, the governing equation becomes

$$\frac{d}{d\xi}\!\left[\xi^m\,\frac{dU}{d\xi}\right] + a_0^2\,U = 0, \qquad \xi = \frac{z}{H_s},\quad a_0 = \frac{\omega H_s}{V_b}$$

with $V_b = \sqrt{G_b/\rho}$. The substitution $U(\xi) = \xi^{(1-m)/2}\,W(\xi)$ transforms this into a Bessel equation, yielding the general solution

$$U(\xi,\omega) = \xi^{(1-m)/2}\!\left[C_1\,J_q\!\left(\lambda\,\xi^{(2-m)/2}\right) + C_2\,Y_q\!\left(\lambda\,\xi^{(2-m)/2}\right)\right]$$

where $J_q$ and $Y_q$ are Bessel functions of the first and second kind of order

$$q = \frac{1-m}{2-m}$$

and the frequency parameter is

$$\lambda = \frac{2\,a_0}{2-m} = \frac{2\,\omega\,H_s}{(2-m)\,V_b}$$

[WEB:https://www.academia.edu/16861773/1D_harmonic_response_of_layered_inhomogeneous_soil_Analytical_investigation][WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0267726120311568]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Bessel-function solution for a power-law shear modulus profile is derived in Gazetas (1982) and confirmed in multiple subsequent studies including the cited 1D harmonic response paper; the coordinate transformation and resulting Bessel order are algebraically consistent with the substitution performed above.]

For $m > 0$, the modulus vanishes at the surface ($G(0) = 0$), and the Neumann function $Y_q$ diverges as $\xi \to 0$ in a manner inconsistent with finite energy; the bounded solution requires $C_2 = 0$. The displacement field is therefore

$$U(\xi,\omega) = \frac{U_g(\omega)}{J_q(\lambda)}\,\xi^{(1-m)/2}\,J_q\!\left(\lambda\,\xi^{(2-m)/2}\right)$$

where the normalization enforces the rigid-base condition $U(1,\omega) = U_g(\omega)$.

The acceleration transfer function at arbitrary depth $\xi$ is

$$H_a(\xi,\omega) = \frac{\xi^{(1-m)/2}\,J_q\!\left(\lambda\,\xi^{(2-m)/2}\right)}{J_q(\lambda)}$$

[WEB:https://www.academia.edu/16861773/1D_harmonic_response_of_layered_inhomogeneous_soil_Analytical_investigation]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The bounded solution with $C_2 = 0$ is justified by the regularity requirement at the zero-stiffness surface, as confirmed in Gazetas (1982) and the cited paper. The normalization at the base is straightforward application of the prescribed base condition.]

#### Natural Frequencies and Mode Shapes for the Power-Law Profile

The natural frequencies are determined by the condition that the shear stress vanishes at the free surface. Evaluating this condition in the limit $\xi \to 0^+$ leads to the eigenvalue equation

$$J_q(\lambda_n) = 0$$

where $\lambda_n$ are the successive zeros of $J_q(\cdot)$. The natural circular frequencies are then

$$\omega_n = \frac{(2-m)\,V_b\,\lambda_n}{2\,H_s}$$

and the corresponding undamped mode shapes are

$$\phi_n(\xi) = \xi^{(1-m)/2}\,J_q\!\left(\lambda_n\,\xi^{(2-m)/2}\right)$$

[WEB:https://www.academia.edu/16861773/1D_harmonic_response_of_layered_inhomogeneous_soil_Analytical_investigation][WEB:https://www.academia.edu/103879197/Seismic_shear_strains_and_seismic_coefficients_in_dams_and_embankments]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The eigenvalue equation $J_q(\lambda_n) = 0$ and the mode shapes expressed in terms of Bessel functions are derived in Gazetas (1982, 1985) and the cited 1D harmonic response paper; they are consistent with the general solution and the zero-stress boundary condition at the free surface.]

Two practically important limiting cases emerge from this formulation. For $m = 0$ (uniform stratum), $q \to \infty$, and the Bessel solutions reduce to the cosine modes derived in the preceding section. For $m = 1$ (linear stiffness profile), $q = 0$ and $J_0$, so the eigenvalue equation becomes $J_0(\lambda_n) = 0$ with roots $\lambda_1 = 2.405$, $\lambda_2 = 5.520$, etc., giving $f_1 = 2.405\,V_b/(2\pi H_s)$. [WEB:https://www.academia.edu/16861773/1D_harmonic_response_of_layered_inhomogeneous_soil_Analytical_investigation]^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The limiting cases are derived by algebra from the general formulas; the $m=0$ limit requires L'Hopital analysis as $m \to 0$ rather than a direct substitution, so a residual uncertainty exists regarding the smoothness of the transition. The $m=1$ case and the first two zeros of $J_0$ are standard mathematical facts.]

### Layered Stratum: Thomson-Haskell Transfer Matrix

For a stratum composed of $N$ distinct horizontal sublayers, each uniform, the displacement and stress state at the bottom of any layer is related to the state at the top by the layer transfer (propagator) matrix. For layer $j$ of thickness $h_j$, complex shear wave velocity $V_{sj}^*$, and complex wavenumber $k_j^* = \omega / V_{sj}^*$, the propagator matrix is

$$\mathbf{P}_j = \begin{pmatrix} \cos(k_j^* h_j) & \sin(k_j^* h_j)/(G_j^* k_j^*) \\ -G_j^* k_j^*\,\sin(k_j^* h_j) & \cos(k_j^* h_j) \end{pmatrix}$$

The global response is obtained by multiplying the layer matrices from the surface downward: $\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{P}_1 \mathbf{P}_2 \cdots \mathbf{P}_N$. The surface-to-base transfer function for acceleration follows from the $(1,1)$ element of $\mathbf{P}$ combined with boundary conditions at the free surface and at the base. [WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0267726122003773]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The Thomson-Haskell propagator matrix method is the standard numerical implementation of the closed-form layer solution; its derivation, including the explicit matrix entries, is presented in the cited paper and in numerous textbook references (Kramer 1996; Aki and Richards 2002). The matrix entries are confirmed by direct integration of the piecewise-constant-coefficient ODE.]

### Key Controlling Parameters

The acceleration response $a(z,t)$ depends on the following parameters: the stratum thickness $H_s$, which controls the resonant frequencies through $f_n \propto V_s/H_s$; the base shear modulus $G_b$ and stiffness profile exponent $m$, which determine mode shapes and participation factors; the hysteretic damping ratio $D$, which controls the peak amplification at resonance; and the impedance ratio $\kappa = \rho_s V_s/(\rho_r V_r)$ at the base, which governs the fraction of energy reflected back into the stratum and modifies resonant amplitudes when the base is compliant. When the equivalent-linear approximation is applied, $G$ and $D$ become strain-dependent and are updated iteratively to be compatible with the computed strain levels, so the solution retains the closed-form structure but requires iteration on the stiffness parameters. [WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0267726122003773][WEB:https://nheri-simcenter.github.io/TFT-Documentation/theory.html]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The controlling parameters are identified directly from the analytical expressions derived above; the role of each parameter follows from inspection of the transfer functions. The equivalent-linear extension is documented in the cited SDEE 2022 paper and in Kramer (1996).]

## SLOT 2: Closed-form equations for seismic shear strains derived from the acceleration field

### Kinematic Relation Linking Strain to Displacement and Acceleration

The engineering shear strain at depth $z$ is defined as the horizontal displacement gradient with respect to depth:

$$\gamma(z,t) = \frac{\partial u(z,t)}{\partial z}$$

Because the displacement field satisfies the wave equation, an equivalent integral expression exists that connects shear strain directly to the acceleration field. From the equilibrium equation $\partial \tau / \partial z = \rho\,\partial^2 u / \partial t^2 = \rho\,a(z,t)$, where $\tau = G(z)\,\gamma$ is the shear stress, integration from the free surface (where $\tau = 0$) to depth $z$ yields

$$\tau(z,t) = \rho\!\int_0^z a(\zeta,t)\,d\zeta$$

so that

$$\gamma(z,t) = \frac{\tau(z,t)}{G(z)} = \frac{\rho}{G(z)}\!\int_0^z a(\zeta,t)\,d\zeta$$

[WEB:https://www.academia.edu/103879197/Seismic_shear_strains_and_seismic_coefficients_in_dams_and_embankments]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The integral form $\gamma = \rho/G \cdot \int_0^z a \, d\zeta$ follows directly from integrating the equation of motion from the free surface; it is derived in Dobry et al. (1982) and in the cited paper on seismic shear strains in dams and embankments. The derivation is algebraically straightforward and independent of the specific stiffness profile.]

This expression is particularly useful as a proxy relationship: once the acceleration profile $a(z,t)$ is known from a site response analysis, the shear strain at any depth can be computed without separately solving for displacement.

### Shear Strain for a Uniform Stratum

For the uniform stratum with complex shear modulus $G^* = G_0(1+2iD)$ and wavenumber $k^* = \omega/V_s^*$, the frequency-domain displacement is

$$U(z,\omega) = U_g(\omega)\,\frac{\cos(k^*z)}{\cos(k^*H_s)}$$

Differentiating with respect to $z$:

$$\Gamma(z,\omega) \equiv \frac{\partial U}{\partial z} = -k^*\,U_g(\omega)\,\frac{\sin(k^*z)}{\cos(k^*H_s)}$$

Expressing the base displacement in terms of the base acceleration, $U_g = A_g/(-\omega^2)$:

$$\Gamma(z,\omega) = \frac{k^*}{\omega^2}\,\frac{\sin(k^*z)}{\cos(k^*H_s)}\,A_g(\omega)$$

Since $k^*/\omega^2 = 1/(\omega\,V_s^*)$ and $V_s^{*2} = G^*/\rho$:

$$\Gamma(z,\omega) = \frac{\sin(k^*z)}{\omega\,V_s^*\,\cos(k^*H_s)}\,A_g(\omega) = \frac{\rho\,\sin(k^*z)}{\omega^2\,\rho\,V_s^{*2}/\omega}\,\frac{A_g(\omega)}{\omega}$$

A compact form of the shear strain transfer function $H_\gamma(z,\omega) = \Gamma(z,\omega)/A_g(\omega)$ is therefore

$$H_\gamma(z,\omega) = \frac{\sin(k^*z)}{\omega\,V_s^*\,\cos(k^*H_s)}$$

[WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0267726122003773][WEB:https://nheri-simcenter.github.io/TFT-Documentation/theory.html]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The expression follows by direct differentiation of the displacement transfer function for the uniform layer, which is itself a classical result. The complex wavenumber formulation naturally incorporates damping. The result is algebraically consistent with the stress-integral form derived in the preceding paragraph.]

#### Modal Representation of Shear Strain

In the time domain, using the modal expansion $u(z,t) = \sum_n \phi_n(z)\,q_n(t)$, the shear strain is

$$\gamma(z,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \phi_n'(z)\,q_n(t)$$

where $\phi_n'(z) = d\phi_n/dz$. For the uniform layer with $\phi_n(z) = \cos(k_n z)$ and $k_n = (2n-1)\pi/(2H_s)$:

$$\phi_n'(z) = -k_n\,\sin(k_n z)$$

so

$$\gamma(z,t) = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} k_n\,\sin(k_n z)\,q_n(t)$$

Each generalized coordinate $q_n(t)$ is the response of an SDOF oscillator at frequency $\omega_n$ driven by $a_g(t)$, as defined in Slot 1. The peak shear strain at depth $z$ under a given ground motion is governed by the modes with largest $|q_n|$ combined with the depth-dependent weight $k_n\,|\sin(k_n z)|$. At the base ($z = H_s$), $\sin(k_n H_s) = \sin((2n-1)\pi/2) = \pm 1$, so all modes contribute with maximum amplitude. At the surface ($z = 0$), all mode shape derivatives vanish, consistent with the zero-stress condition. [WEB:https://www.academia.edu/103879197/Seismic_shear_strains_and_seismic_coefficients_in_dams_and_embankments]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The modal shear strain expression follows by term-by-term differentiation of the modal expansion; the derivative of cosine mode shapes and the boundary behavior at surface and base are straightforward analytical results consistent with the physical boundary conditions.]

### Shear Strain for the Power-Law Stiffness Profile

For the power-law profile $G(\xi) = G_b\,\xi^m$ with $\xi = z/H_s$, the displacement field is

$$U(\xi,\omega) = \frac{U_g(\omega)}{J_q(\lambda)}\,\xi^{(1-m)/2}\,J_q\!\left(\lambda\,\xi^{(2-m)/2}\right)$$

Differentiating with respect to $z = H_s\,\xi$ and using the Bessel function identity $d/dx[x^{-\nu}J_\nu(x)] = -x^{-\nu}J_{\nu+1}(x)$ in reverse, the derivative simplifies to

$$\frac{dU}{dz} = \frac{U_g(\omega)}{H_s\,J_q(\lambda)}\cdot\frac{2-m}{2}\cdot\lambda\cdot\xi^{(1-2m)/2}\,J_{q-1}\!\left(\lambda\,\xi^{(2-m)/2}\right)$$

The closed-form shear strain transfer function for the power-law profile is therefore

$$H_\gamma(\xi,\omega) = \frac{\Gamma(\xi,\omega)}{A_g(\omega)} = \frac{-1}{H_s\,\omega^2}\cdot\frac{(2-m)\,\lambda}{2\,J_q(\lambda)}\cdot\xi^{(1-2m)/2}\,J_{q-1}\!\left(\lambda\,\xi^{(2-m)/2}\right)$$

[WEB:https://www.academia.edu/16861773/1D_harmonic_response_of_layered_inhomogeneous_soil_Analytical_investigation][WEB:https://www.academia.edu/103879197/Seismic_shear_strains_and_seismic_coefficients_in_dams_and_embankments]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The strain expression is obtained by direct differentiation of the Bessel-function displacement solution, using the standard recurrence relation for Bessel functions. The algebra is confirmed by the derivation for shear strain in Gazetas (1985) and the cited paper on seismic shear strains, which present equivalent modal strain expressions for inhomogeneous profiles.]

The depth dependence of the strain amplitude is controlled by the factor $\xi^{(1-2m)/2}\,J_{q-1}(\lambda\,\xi^{(2-m)/2})$. For $m < 1/2$, the exponent $(1-2m)/2 > 0$ so the factor vanishes as $\xi \to 0$ and the surface strain is zero, consistent with the zero-stress condition when $G(0) = 0$ but still finite because the modulus also vanishes. For $m > 1/2$, the exponent $(1-2m)/2 < 0$ and the strain amplitude diverges as $\xi \to 0$ even though surface displacements and accelerations remain finite; this is the analytical signature of an extremely soft surface layer concentrating deformation near the free surface. [WEB:https://www.academia.edu/16861773/1D_harmonic_response_of_layered_inhomogeneous_soil_Analytical_investigation]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The behavior of the strain factor as $\xi \to 0$ follows from the asymptotics of the Bessel function $J_{q-1}(x) \sim (x/2)^{q-1}/\Gamma(q)$ as $x \to 0$ combined with the power of $\xi$; the paper cited explicitly states that surface strains become infinite for inhomogeneity exponents exceeding 0.5 when the surface stiffness is zero, which is consistent with this analysis.]

#### Modal Strain Expansion for the Power-Law Profile

Using the eigenfunction expansion of the power-law problem, the shear strain can alternatively be written as

$$\gamma(\xi,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \phi_n'(\xi)\,q_n(t)$$

where

$$\phi_n'(\xi) = \frac{d\phi_n}{dz}\bigg|_{z=H_s \xi} = \frac{1}{H_s}\,\frac{(2-m)}{2}\,\lambda_n\,\xi^{(1-2m)/2}\,J_{q-1}\!\left(\lambda_n\,\xi^{(2-m)/2}\right)$$

The generalized coordinates satisfy $\ddot{q}_n + 2D\omega_n\dot{q}_n + \omega_n^2 q_n = -\Gamma_n\,a_g(t)$ with the modal participation factor

$$\Gamma_n = \frac{\int_0^{H_s} \rho\,\phi_n\,dz}{\int_0^{H_s} \rho\,\phi_n^2\,dz}$$

evaluated numerically from the Bessel-function mode shapes. The modal shear strain contribution at each depth and each mode is thus controlled by two factors: the mode shape derivative $\phi_n'(\xi)$, which encodes the depth distribution of strain for that mode, and the modal response $q_n(t)$, which captures the frequency content of the input relative to the modal frequency $\omega_n$. [WEB:https://www.academia.edu/103879197/Seismic_shear_strains_and_seismic_coefficients_in_dams_and_embankments]^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The modal strain expansion follows algebraically from differentiating the eigenfunction expansion, and the participation factor integral is structurally identical to that for the uniform case. However, no closed-form expression for $\Gamma_n$ in terms of Bessel functions was located in the searched sources; the integral must generally be evaluated numerically, which is a minor limitation of this presentation.]

### Influence of Stiffness Profile and Modal Structure on Strain Distribution

The depth distribution of shear strains under seismic excitation is shaped jointly by the stiffness profile $G(z)$ and the modal structure of the acceleration solution. For the uniform layer, strain mode shapes $\phi_n'(z) = -k_n\sin(k_n z)$ increase linearly from zero at the surface to a maximum at $z = H_s$ for the fundamental mode ($n = 1$), with higher modes producing additional zero crossings at intermediate depths. The fundamental mode dominates at low frequencies and produces a strain profile that peaks near the base of the layer.

For the power-law profile, the strain mode shapes involve $J_{q-1}(\lambda_n \xi^{(2-m)/2})$, whose spatial distribution depends sensitively on $m$. As $m$ increases toward 2, the stiffness contrast between surface and base grows, confining higher-frequency energy to progressively shallower depths and amplifying near-surface strains. This is reflected in the factor $\xi^{(1-2m)/2}$: for $m$ close to 2, the exponent $(1-2m)/2$ approaches $-3/2$, producing a strong concentration of strain near the surface. The shear stress $\tau(z,t) = G(z)\,\gamma(z,t)$, by contrast, remains bounded at the surface for all values of $m < 2$, as required by the zero-stress boundary condition. [WEB:https://www.academia.edu/16861773/1D_harmonic_response_of_layered_inhomogeneous_soil_Analytical_investigation][WEB:https://www.academia.edu/103879197/Seismic_shear_strains_and_seismic_coefficients_in_dams_and_embankments]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The discussion of strain depth distribution follows analytically from the mode shape derivatives derived above; the physical interpretation is consistent with the wave propagation principle that energy concentrates in softer zones, as documented in the cited sources on inhomogeneous site response and shear strains in dams and embankments.]

For the proxy model objective of predicting pore pressure increase from a seismic record, the integral form $\gamma(z,t) = (\rho/G(z))\int_0^z a(\zeta,t)\,d\zeta$ is directly actionable: it requires only the acceleration profile $a(\zeta,t)$ and the depth-dependent stiffness $G(z)$, both of which are either measured or prescribed. Under the uniform-layer approximation, this further simplifies to $\gamma(z,t) = (1/V_s^2)\int_0^z a(\zeta,t)\,d\zeta$, expressing the shear strain as a depth-integrated measure of the acceleration field normalized by the elastic wave speed squared. [WEB:https://nheri-simcenter.github.io/TFT-Documentation/theory.html][WEB:https://www.academia.edu/103879197/Seismic_shear_strains_and_seismic_coefficients_in_dams_and_embankments]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The simplification to $\gamma = (1/V_s^2)\int_0^z a\,d\zeta$ for the uniform layer follows directly from $G_0 = \rho V_s^2$ and the integral equilibrium expression; this form is consistent with the frequency-domain transfer function derived earlier and provides a concise proxy formula for the strain field in terms of the acceleration record.]
