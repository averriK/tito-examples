# Ground Motion Models

## SLOT 1: Definitions, Notation, and Equations

A ground-motion prediction equation (GMPE), also referred to as a ground-motion model (GMM), characterizes the conditional probability distribution of a ground-motion intensity measure (IM) at a site given a set of seismic source, propagation path, and local site parameters. The fundamental representation expresses the natural logarithm of the IM as the sum of a deterministic median prediction term and a stochastic residual, reflecting the observed log-normality of ground-motion amplitudes.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The log-normal parameterization of ground-motion intensity measures is a foundational assumption of the GMPE literature, consistent with all models documented in the KB files and with the NGA-West2 and NGA-Subduction model suites. No unsupported claims are introduced.]

The formal prediction equation is:

$$\ln IM = \mu_{\ln IM}(\mathbf{X}) + \varepsilon$$

where $\mu_{\ln IM}(\mathbf{X})$ is the median prediction expressed on the natural-log scale as a function of the predictor vector $\mathbf{X}$, and $\varepsilon$ is the total residual capturing aleatory variability. The intensity measure IM is therefore log-normally distributed with median $\exp[\mu_{\ln IM}]$ and total dispersion characterized by the total standard deviation $\sigma_{total}$.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The notation and interpretation are standard across the GMPE literature. No quantitative claims are made beyond those derivable from the log-normal assumption itself.]

The predictor vector $\mathbf{X}$ comprises parameters from three classes. Source parameters include moment magnitude $M_w$, depth to the top of rupture $Z_{TOR}$, fault dip $\delta$, fault width $W$, hypocentral depth $Z_{hyp}$, and rake angle $\lambda$. Path parameters consist of one or more source-to-site distance metrics (defined below). Site parameters include the time-averaged shear-wave velocity in the top 30 m, $V_{S30}$, the depth to the 1.0 km/s shear-wave velocity horizon $Z_{1.0}$, and the depth to the 2.5 km/s horizon $Z_{2.5}$. The specific subset of parameters required by each model varies with tectonic setting and is documented per model in subsequent sections [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The parameter definitions are standard and consistent with the OpenQuake hazardlib requires_sites_parameters and requires_rupture_parameters fields listed in all four KB documents.]

The total aleatory variability is partitioned into between-event and within-event components according to:

$$\sigma_{total}^2 = \tau^2 + \phi^2$$

where $\tau$ is the standard deviation of the between-event (inter-event) residual $\eta$, and $\phi$ is the standard deviation of the within-event (intra-event) residual $\delta W$. A single ground-motion realization is expressed as:

$$\ln IM = \mu_{\ln IM}(M_w, R, \boldsymbol{\theta}) + \eta + \delta W, \qquad \eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\,\tau^2), \qquad \delta W \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\,\phi^2)$$

The between-event term $\eta$ captures earthquake-to-earthquake variability in source properties not explained by the magnitude scaling function; the within-event term $\delta W$ captures residual site-to-site and record-to-record variability not explained by the site characterization parameters.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The partitioning of total sigma into tau (between-event) and phi (within-event) is a standard result applied uniformly across the NGA-West2, NGA-East, and NGA-Subduction model families. The notation is internally consistent and does not require external citation beyond standard domain knowledge.]

Several distance metrics appear across the models documented in this chapter. The Joyner-Boore distance $R_{JB}$ is the shortest horizontal distance from the site to the surface projection of the fault rupture plane. The closest distance to the fault rupture surface is $R_{rup}$. The horizontal distance from the site to the surface projection of the top edge of the rupture, measured perpendicular to fault strike, is $R_x$. Hypocentral distance is denoted $R_{hypo}$ and epicentral distance $R_{epi}$. The choice of distance metric is model-dependent and reflects both the tectonic regime and the data available for calibration; the metric required by each model in the OpenQuake hazardlib is listed under the per-tectonic-region descriptions [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Distance metric definitions (RJB, Rrup, Rx, Rhypo) are standard and consistent with OpenQuake hazardlib parameter names used in the KB files. The cross-reference to subsequent sections is accurate.]

## SLOT 2: Definition of GMM/GMPE in the Context of PSHA

In probabilistic seismic hazard analysis (PSHA), the annual rate of exceedance of an intensity measure IM above a threshold level $im$ at a given site is computed by integrating the conditional exceedance probability over all earthquake sources, magnitudes, and distances using the total probability theorem. For a set of discrete sources indexed by $i$, the hazard integral takes the form:

$$\lambda(IM > im) = \sum_i \nu_i \iint P(IM > im \mid m, r)\, f_{M_i}(m)\, f_{R_i}(r \mid m)\, dm\, dr$$

where $\nu_i$ is the mean annual rate of earthquakes on source $i$, $f_{M_i}(m)$ is the probability density function of magnitude, $f_{R_i}(r \mid m)$ is the conditional density of source-to-site distance, and $P(IM > im \mid m, r)$ is the conditional exceedance probability furnished by the GMM.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The PSHA hazard integral is a foundational result of probabilistic risk analysis applied to seismology and is not in dispute. The formulation is consistent with standard references and with the implementation in OpenQuake.]

Because GMMs specify a log-normal distribution for IM conditioned on the source and path parameters, the conditional exceedance probability entering the integral is:

$$P(IM > im \mid m, r) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln im - \mu_{\ln IM}(m,\, r,\, \boldsymbol{\theta})}{\sigma_{total}}\right)$$

where $\Phi$ denotes the standard normal cumulative distribution function. Both the median prediction $\mu_{\ln IM}$ and the total standard deviation $\sigma_{total}$ of the GMM directly control the computed hazard curve. The sensitivity of the hazard estimate to the choice of GMM is particularly pronounced at long return periods, where the integral weights the upper tail of the log-normal distribution at multiple standard deviations above the median; small differences among competing GMMs in their median predictions or sigma values can translate into large differences in the computed exceedance rates at these probability levels.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The log-normal exceedance probability formula follows directly from probability theory and is universally applied in PSHA software including OpenQuake. The qualitative observation about sensitivity at long return periods is a well-established result in hazard sensitivity studies and does not require specific citation.]

GMMs constitute one of the principal sources of epistemic uncertainty in PSHA. Epistemic uncertainty arises because the true attenuation relationships governing ground motion in a given tectonic region are not known with certainty and can only be estimated from finite, imperfect datasets. This is distinct from aleatory variability, which is the irreducible scatter about the conditional median that persists even if the median relationship were known perfectly. In data-rich active tectonic regions, GMM epistemic uncertainty can be partially constrained through residual analysis and model ranking against observed accelerograms. In data-sparse regions, where strong-motion recordings are too few or too limited in magnitude-distance coverage to discriminate among competing models, the epistemic uncertainty associated with the GMM logic tree typically becomes the dominant contributor to total hazard uncertainty, particularly at the spectral periods and return periods of engineering interest.^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The distinction between aleatory variability and epistemic uncertainty, and the general characterization of GMMs as major PSHA uncertainty sources, are well established in the literature. The specific claim that GMM epistemic uncertainty dominates in data-sparse regions is a qualitative generalization from sensitivity studies; it is stated here without a directly cited source, warranting MEDIUM confidence.]

## SLOT 3: Epistemic Uncertainty and Ground-Motion Logic Trees

Epistemic uncertainty in ground-motion prediction is represented in PSHA through a ground-motion logic tree. Each branch of the tree corresponds to a candidate GMPE, and each branch is assigned a non-negative weight $w_k$. The weights are required to sum to unity across all $N$ branches in the tree:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k = 1, \qquad w_k \geq 0$$

The mean hazard curve is computed as the weighted sum of the $N$ branch-specific hazard curves:

$$\lambda_{mean}(IM > im) = \sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k\, \lambda_k(IM > im)$$

where $\lambda_k$ is the annual exceedance rate computed using the $k$-th GMM. In addition to the mean hazard, the set of $N$ branch results constitutes a discrete approximation to the epistemic uncertainty distribution, from which fractile (quantile) hazard curves can be derived.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The logic tree formalism, the normalization condition, and the mean hazard calculation are standard PSHA methods implemented in OpenQuake and documented in the hazard literature. The KB files confirm that each logic tree in this assessment follows this structure [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].]

The structure of a ground-motion logic tree is organized hierarchically. At the top level, a separate logic tree is defined for each tectonic region type (TRT), reflecting the physical differences in source mechanism, wave propagation path, and site response among crustal, stable continental, and subduction tectonic environments. Within each TRT-level logic tree, one or more branching levels may be defined; in the present assessment each TRT tree contains a single branching level. At that branching level, a branch set is defined, and the candidate GMPEs are assigned as branches within the set. The branch weights within a set must satisfy the normalization condition and reflect the relative plausibility of the candidate models for that tectonic environment [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The hierarchical organization (logicTreeID, branchingLevelID, branchSetID, branches) is directly documented in the KB files for all four tectonic region types. The description is fully consistent with the NRML format and OpenQuake logic tree implementation.]

The assignment of weights to GMM branches encodes the analyst's judgment of the relative credibility of each model given the available evidence for the target region. Where region-specific strong-motion records are available in sufficient quantity, this judgment can be informed by quantitative performance metrics including log-likelihood scores, residual bias analyses, and inter-model distance measures. Where records are scarce or absent, the assignment of unequal weights would require an extra-evidential judgment that cannot be grounded in empirical observation; in this case, equal weighting is the epistemically conservative choice. The basis for equal weighting in the present assessment is discussed in the final section of this chapter.^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The discussion of performance metrics (log-likelihood, residual bias) and the framing of equal weighting as epistemically conservative are consistent with the contemporary PSHA literature, including treatments of the ESHM20 craton model [DOI:10.1007/s10518-020-00940-x]. The specific characterization of equal weighting as the conservative choice in data-sparse contexts is a general principle rather than a directly cited result; MEDIUM confidence reflects this.]

## SLOT 8: Basis for Equally Weighted Logic Tree Branches

All four ground-motion logic trees in this assessment adopt equal weights across branches within each TRT. The ASC, SIF, and SIS trees each contain ten equally weighted branches at 0.10000; the SCC tree contains six branches at approximately 0.16667. The adoption of equal weights is a principled response to the epistemic situation in regions where the available strong-motion catalog is insufficient to derive defensible differential weights through model testing [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The equal weights are documented directly in the KB files. The rationale connecting equal weights to data insufficiency is a standard argument in the PSHA literature, consistent with the approaches documented for ESHM20 [DOI:10.1007/s10518-020-00940-x] and NGA-East [WEB:https://peer.berkeley.edu/new-peer-report-201703].]

In regions where strong-motion recordings are abundant and cover a wide range of magnitudes and distances, model performance can be evaluated through quantitative metrics including log-likelihood scores, residual bias analysis, and inter-model spread measures; differential weights can then be assigned on an empirical basis. In data-sparse regions, such metrics either cannot be computed at all or yield statistically insignificant differences among competing models. Any assignment of unequal weights in such a context would encode an extra-evidential judgment about model quality that is not grounded in observable evidence. Equal weighting is therefore not merely a computational default but an epistemically conservative choice consistent with treating all models in the suite as equally plausible representations of an unknown attenuation relationship [DOI:10.1007/s10518-020-00940-x].^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The argument for equal weighting as epistemically conservative in data-sparse regions is a well-established position in PSHA practice. The DOI cited (Weatherill & Cotton 2020) supports this in the European craton context. The specific quantitative metrics listed (log-likelihood, residual bias) are general domain knowledge; MEDIUM confidence reflects that the claim is well-supported in principle but stated without a single primary citation that makes it explicit across all four TRT contexts.]

The use of a large number of branches serves to sample the epistemic range of GMM predictions broadly. Competing GMPEs can differ substantially in their median predictions, distance attenuation rates, and standard deviations across the magnitude-distance ranges of interest at a given site; a large and diverse model suite reduces the risk that the mean hazard curve is driven by a narrow cluster of models sharing common assumptions or training data. This consideration is especially important for the SIF and SIS trees, which draw on models calibrated from distinct regional subduction databases spanning Chile, Japan, Taiwan, Mexico, New Zealand, and the global NGA-SUB compilation, ensuring that the full observable range of subduction-zone ground-motion behavior is represented in the epistemic uncertainty distribution [KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md]. For the SCC tree, the six-member suite spans Eastern North American, European craton, and Australian cratonic environments, sampling across recognized regional variability in stable continental attenuation [KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][DOI:10.1007/s10518-020-00940-x]. For the ASC tree, models calibrated from European, New Zealand, Taiwanese, and global NGA-West2 datasets are combined, providing coverage of the diversity of active crustal attenuation behavior worldwide [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The characterization of model diversity across regional calibration datasets (Chile, Japan, Taiwan, Mexico, New Zealand, NGA-SUB for SIF/SIS; Eastern North America, Europe, Australia for SCC; Europe, NZ, Taiwan, NGA-West2 for ASC) is directly verifiable from the model names and origins documented in this chapter and the KB files. The inference that diversity reduces single-model domination is standard practice in PSHA logic-tree design.]
