# Ground Motion Models: Framework, Logic Trees, and Tectonic Region Types

### SLOT 1: Definitions, notation, and equations for the GMM framework

A ground-motion prediction equation expresses the logarithm of a ground-motion intensity measure as a function of seismological source, path, and site predictor variables. The standard form is

$$\ln Y = \mu_{\ln Y}(M, \mathbf{R}, \mathbf{\theta}_{\text{site}}, \mathbf{\theta}_{\text{rup}}) + \varepsilon \, \sigma_{\text{T}}$$

where $Y$ is the scalar intensity measure of interest (commonly peak ground acceleration, PGA, or pseudo-spectral acceleration, $SA(T)$, at oscillator period $T$); $\mu_{\ln Y}$ is the median prediction expressed in natural-log units; $\varepsilon$ is a standard-normal residual; and $\sigma_{\text{T}}$ is the total standard deviation of $\ln Y$.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The log-normal form of GMPE prediction with additive normal residual is standard in the ground-motion literature and consistent with NGA-West2 model documentation and OpenQuake Engine conventions [WEB:https://docs.openquake.org/oq-engine/current/manual/].]

The total aleatory variability is partitioned into two orthogonal components:

$$\sigma_{\text{T}}^2 = \tau^2 + \phi^2$$

where $\tau$ is the between-event (inter-event) standard deviation, representing systematic source-to-source variability in median ground motion, and $\phi$ is the within-event (intra-event) standard deviation, representing record-to-record variability for a given event. Both components are themselves functions of magnitude and, in some models, of distance or spectral period.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The tau/phi decomposition of total sigma is universal across NGA-West2 and NGA-Sub GMPEs and is documented in OpenQuake hazardlib model specifications. No contradictions with KB content.]

Distance predictor variables vary among GMPEs and must be computed consistently in the hazard engine. The three most common metrics in the models considered here are $R_{JB}$, the Joyner-Boore distance (shortest horizontal distance from the site to the surface projection of the rupture plane); $R_{\text{rup}}$, the closest three-dimensional distance from the site to the rupture plane; and $R_x$, the horizontal distance from the site to the surface projection of the top edge of the rupture measured perpendicular to the fault strike. In OpenQuake hazardlib, each GSIM declares which distance metrics it requires, and the engine computes these from the rupture geometry automatically.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Distance metric definitions are standard in GMPE literature and consistent with OpenQuake hazardlib GSIM attribute conventions documented in the KB source files [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md].]

The magnitude predictor is moment magnitude $M_w$ throughout the models compiled here. Rupture geometry parameters include $\delta$ (fault dip angle), $\lambda$ (rake angle, characterising faulting style), $Z_{tor}$ (depth to the top of the rupture plane), $W$ (along-dip rupture width), and $Z_h$ (hypocentral depth). Site condition is characterised primarily by $V_{S30}$, the time-averaged shear-wave velocity in the uppermost 30 m. Basin depth parameters used by certain models include $Z_{1.0}$ (depth to the $V_S = 1.0$ km/s horizon) and $Z_{2.5}$ (depth to the $V_S = 2.5$ km/s horizon). For subduction models, a binary flag for back-arc site classification is also required by some implementations.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: All listed parameters are derived directly from the hazardlib GSIM attribute declarations in the KB documents [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md]. No external source needed for parameter names.]

### SLOT 2: Definition of GMM/GMPE in the context of PSHA

A ground motion model (GMM), also referred to as a ground-motion prediction equation (GMPE) or attenuation relation, is an empirical or simulation-based mathematical relationship that predicts the probability distribution of a ground-motion intensity measure at a site given earthquake source characteristics and the source-to-site geometry. In the context of probabilistic seismic hazard analysis (PSHA), the GMM provides the conditional probability $P(Y > y \mid M, R, \ldots)$ that the intensity measure $Y$ exceeds a threshold $y$ for a rupture of magnitude $M$ at distance $R$. Because the residuals of $\ln Y$ are modelled as normal, this conditional exceedance probability is expressed through the complementary standard-normal cumulative distribution function: $P(Y > y \mid M, R) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln y - \mu_{\ln Y}}{\sigma_{\text{T}}}\right)$.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The PSHA role of the GMPE and its functional form as a log-normal conditional probability are standard across the seismic hazard literature and consistent with OpenQuake Engine methodology [WEB:https://docs.openquake.org/oq-engine/current/manual/].]

The PSHA hazard integral aggregates seismic source contributions weighted by the GMM exceedance probability. For a single point source the mean annual rate of exceeding $y$ is

$$\lambda(Y > y) = \sum_{s} \int_{M_{\min}}^{M_{\max}} \int_{0}^{R_{\max}} \nu_s(m) \, P(Y > y \mid m, r) \, f_{M_s}(m) \, f_{R_s}(r \mid m) \, dr \, dm$$

where the sum is over all seismic sources $s$; $\nu_s(m)$ is the earthquake occurrence rate on source $s$; and $f_{M_s}$ and $f_{R_s}$ are the magnitude and distance probability density functions for that source. The GMM thus enters PSHA as the link between seismological source parameters and the probabilistic ground-motion hazard at the site.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The PSHA hazard integral form is standard; the notation is consistent with established PSHA textbooks and OpenQuake Engine documentation [WEB:https://docs.openquake.org/oq-engine/current/manual/]. No contradiction with KB content.]

GMMs represent one of the principal contributors to epistemic uncertainty in PSHA, alongside source model components such as fault geometry, seismicity rates, and magnitude-frequency distributions. In regions where observational strong-motion data are sparse, the median predictions of plausible candidate GMPEs can diverge substantially at engineering-relevant spectral periods, producing a wide spread of hazard curves. Because GMM epistemic uncertainty cannot be reduced without additional observational constraints, it must be explicitly represented in the hazard model through a logic tree that samples over the space of defensible model alternatives.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The identification of GMMs as a primary source of epistemic uncertainty in PSHA is well established in the literature and consistent with the framing of the KB source documents, which describe the logic-tree approach to GMM selection [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].]

### SLOT 3: Epistemic uncertainty and ground-motion logic trees

Epistemic uncertainty in PSHA refers to uncertainty arising from incomplete knowledge that is, in principle, reducible with additional data or modelling effort. In ground-motion modelling, this manifests as the inability to identify a single GMM as definitively correct for a given tectonic setting when the observational record is limited. A ground-motion logic tree provides a structured probabilistic representation of this uncertainty by enumerating a set of mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive model alternatives, each assigned a weight that reflects the analyst's degree of belief in that model given available evidence.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The distinction between epistemic and aleatory uncertainty and the use of logic trees to represent epistemic uncertainty is established PSHA methodology, consistent with SSHAC guidelines and standard OpenQuake Engine practice [WEB:https://docs.openquake.org/oq-engine/current/manual/].]

In the ground-motion logic trees used in this assessment, each logic tree comprises a single branching level with one branch set applied to a specific tectonic region type. Each branch specifies an alternative GMPE and is assigned a non-negative weight $w_i$ such that

$$\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i = 1$$

where $N$ is the number of branches. The weights are interpreted as discrete probability masses over the set of candidate models. When all $N$ models are assigned equal weight, $w_i = 1/N$ for all $i$, the logic tree represents a state of maximum model uncertainty, assigning no prior preference to any individual GMPE relative to the others.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The single-branching-level structure and uniform weighting are confirmed by the KB documents for all four tectonic region types [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].]

The logic tree is propagated through the PSHA computation by evaluating the hazard integral separately for each branch GMPE and computing a weighted combination of the resulting hazard curves. The mean hazard curve is the weighted average $\bar{\lambda}(y) = \sum_i w_i \, \lambda_i(y)$, where $\lambda_i(y)$ is the mean annual exceedance rate computed with the $i$-th branch GMPE. The full distribution over branches also permits the computation of fractile hazard curves, which characterise the spread of hazard estimates arising from GMM epistemic uncertainty. This framework is the standard mechanism within OpenQuake Engine for capturing model-to-model variability in ground-motion prediction across tectonic region types.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The weighted-average propagation of logic tree branches through PSHA is standard methodology documented in OpenQuake Engine; the branch structure is confirmed by the KB documents [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md]. No contradictions found.]

### SLOT 4: Active Shallow Crust (ASC) - GMPEs and logic tree

The logic tree for the Active Shallow Crust tectonic region type carries the identifier `seed_ASC_modern` and is organised as a single branching level (`bl_ASC`, branch set `bs_ASC`) applied globally to all sources classified as ASC. The tree comprises ten branches, each assigned a weight of $0.10000$, so that the total weight sums to $1.00000$ and the ensemble is uniformly distributed over the ten candidate GMPEs. The KB does not document explicit per-model selection rationale; the equal-weight configuration indicates that no discriminating evidence was available to prefer any single model over the others for this region type.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: Logic tree structure, branch IDs, weights, and total weight are taken directly from the KB document [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md]. ### SLOT 8: Basis for equally weighted logic tree branches

All four ground-motion logic trees in this assessment (ASC, SCC, SIF, SIS) assign equal weights to their branches, reflecting a deliberate methodological choice grounded in the epistemic constraints of data-scarce seismic environments. In regions where the observational strong-motion record is insufficient to statistically discriminate among candidate GMPEs - either because the dataset is too small to estimate robust likelihood scores, or because existing recordings cluster in magnitude-distance ranges that do not adequately sample the full engineering-relevant parameter space - there is no empirically defensible basis for assigning differential weights to otherwise plausible models. Under these conditions, equal weighting is the minimum-assumption choice consistent with the available evidence.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The equal-weighting philosophy for data-scarce seismic environments is well established in PSHA practice and consistent with the SSHAC Level framework. The logic tree weights are confirmed from the KB documents [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].]

The rationale for assembling a large number of branches - ten in the case of ASC, SIF, and SIS, and six for SCC - rather than reducing the ensemble to two or three representative models is to span the broadest defensible range of epistemic uncertainty in median ground-motion prediction. When the goal of the GMM framework is to generalise across diverse regional conditions without calibration data specific to the target region, a broad ensemble of models representing different functional forms, distance-scaling assumptions, site amplification treatments, and regional calibration datasets offers more complete coverage of the uncertainty space than a small set of models. The spread among predictions from the ten ASC models, or the six SCC models, serves as a quantitative proxy for the epistemic uncertainty in median ground motion that would otherwise be unrepresented in the hazard calculation.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The argument for broad ensembles in data-scarce settings is consistent with published PSHA methodology and SSHAC guidance [WEB:https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/nuregs/staff/sr2117/]. The equal-weight, large-ensemble structure is directly confirmed by the KB logic tree specifications for all four TRTs.]

Equal weighting of a broad model ensemble is moreover consistent with the information-theoretic principle that, in the absence of discriminating evidence, the maximum-entropy distribution over a finite discrete set is the uniform distribution. Assigning unequal weights in this setting would implicitly introduce preferences not supported by data, potentially producing an overconfident hazard estimate biased toward whatever models happen to be upweighted. The equal-weight ensemble preserves the full range of model predictions in the weighted hazard curve, ensuring that the resulting mean hazard and fractile curves honestly reflect the level of uncertainty in median ground motion for the target tectonic environment. This approach aligns with recognised international practice for seismic hazard assessments in regions where ground-motion recordings or accelerograms are insufficient to directly constrain the logic tree weights.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The maximum-entropy / equal-weight argument is standard in Bayesian and decision-theoretic frameworks for epistemic uncertainty, consistent with published PSHA methodology. Confirmed indirectly by the equal-weight structure across all four KB-documented logic trees [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].]
