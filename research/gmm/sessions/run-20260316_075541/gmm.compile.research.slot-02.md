## SLOT 2: Definition of GMM/GMPE in the Context of PSHA

A ground-motion model (GMM), also referred to as a ground-motion prediction equation (GMPE) or attenuation relationship, is an empirical or semi-empirical function that predicts the probability distribution of a ground-motion intensity measure conditional on an earthquake scenario specified by source magnitude, source-to-site distance, and ancillary parameters. In probabilistic seismic hazard analysis (PSHA), the GMM provides the conditional exceedance probability $P(Y > y \mid M, R, \ldots)$ that the intensity measure $Y$ exceeds a threshold $y$ for a rupture of magnitude $M$ at distance $R$. Under the log-normal assumption on $Y$, this probability is evaluated through the complementary standard-normal cumulative distribution function [@OpenQuakeEngine]:

$$P(Y > y \mid M, R) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln y - \mu_{\ln Y}(M,\, R)}{\sigma_{\ln Y}}\right)$$

where $\Phi$ denotes the standard normal CDF, $\mu_{\ln Y}$ is the median prediction, and $\sigma_{\ln Y}$ is the total aleatory standard deviation of the GMPE.

The PSHA hazard integral aggregates the contributions of all seismic sources by weighting the GMPE exceedance probability by the earthquake occurrence rate. For a set of seismic sources $s$, the mean annual rate of exceeding the ground-motion level $y$ at the site is [@NHESS2024a]:

$$\lambda(Y > y) = \sum_{s} \nu_s \int_{M} \int_{R} f_{M_s}(m)\, f_{R_s}(r)\, P(Y > y \mid m, r)\, dr\, dm$$

where $\nu_s$ is the annual rate of earthquakes above a minimum magnitude on source $s$; $f_{M_s}(m)$ and $f_{R_s}(r)$ are the probability density functions of magnitude and distance for source $s$; and $P(Y > y \mid m, r)$ is the GMPE conditional exceedance probability. The GMPE thus enters PSHA through both its median prediction $\mu_{\ln Y}$ and its total standard deviation $\sigma_{\ln Y}$: small differences among competing GMPEs in either quantity can produce substantial differences in computed exceedance rates, particularly at long return periods where the hazard integral weights the upper tail of the log-normal distribution at several standard deviations above the median [@OpenQuakeEngine].

GMMs constitute one of the principal contributors to epistemic uncertainty in PSHA, alongside source model components such as fault geometry, seismicity rates, and magnitude-frequency distributions [@GmmLTASC][@GmmLTSCC][@GmmLTSIF][@GmmLTSIS]. Epistemic uncertainty, as distinct from aleatory variability, represents incomplete scientific knowledge of the true ground-motion process; it is in principle reducible with additional data and is represented in PSHA by a discrete set of alternative models. Different GMPEs are constructed from distinct datasets, adopt distinct functional forms, and encode distinct assumptions about source scaling, geometric spreading, anelastic attenuation, and site amplification. In regions where observational strong-motion data are sparse, the median predictions of plausible candidate GMPEs can diverge substantially across engineering-relevant spectral periods and magnitude-distance ranges, producing a wide spread of hazard curves. The spread among GMM predictions at low annual exceedance rates is frequently the dominant contributor to the epistemic uncertainty band on the hazard curve [@BEE2022a][@PAG2022a]. Because this uncertainty cannot be reduced without additional observational constraints, it must be explicitly represented in the hazard model through a logic tree that samples the space of defensible model alternatives.
