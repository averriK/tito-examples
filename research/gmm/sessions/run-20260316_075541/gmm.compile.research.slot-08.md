## SLOT 8: Basis for Equally Weighted Logic Tree Branches

All four ground-motion logic trees in this assessment (ASC, SCC, SIF, SIS) assign equal or quasi-equal weights to their branches: each of the ten ASC, SIF, and SIS branches carries $w_k = 0.10000$, while five of the six SCC branches carry $w_k = 0.16667$ and the collapsed NGA-East branch carries $w_k = 0.16665$. In all cases, no individual branch is assigned greater weight than any other on the basis of expert judgment or model performance metrics [@GmmLTASC][@GmmLTSCC][@GmmLTSIF][@GmmLTSIS].

Equal weighting constitutes a principled epistemic response to the conditions that characterize the regions for which this GMM framework is intended. In regions where the catalog of recorded strong ground motions is insufficient to statistically discriminate among alternative GMPEs -- either because the dataset is too small to yield robust likelihood scores, or because existing recordings cluster in magnitude-distance ranges that do not adequately sample the full engineering-relevant parameter space -- no empirical basis exists for assigning systematically higher weights to one family of models over another [@BEE2022a][@PAG2022a]. Assigning differential weights in such a context would introduce apparent precision not warranted by the available evidence and would risk misrepresenting the true range of epistemic uncertainty by implicitly downweighting models that could be equally or more applicable to the target region.

From an information-theoretic perspective, the uniform distribution is the maximum-entropy assignment of probability over a finite discrete set under a state of equal prior knowledge [@Springer2023LT]. When no evidence distinguishes among the candidate models, assigning equal probability to each is the most defensible representation of the epistemic state. Equal weighting of a broad model ensemble thereby preserves the full range of scientifically credible predictions in the weighted hazard curve and ensures that the resulting mean hazard and fractile curves honestly reflect the level of uncertainty in median ground motion for the target tectonic environment.

The rationale for assembling a large number of branches -- ten in the case of ASC, SIF, and SIS, and six for SCC -- rather than reducing the ensemble to a smaller number of differentially weighted alternatives is to span the broadest defensible range of epistemic uncertainty in median ground-motion prediction [@NRCSSHAC2012]. Competing GMPEs can differ substantially in their median predictions, distance attenuation rates, and standard deviations across the magnitude-distance ranges relevant to a given site; a large and diverse model suite reduces the risk that the mean hazard curve is dominated by a narrow cluster of models sharing common assumptions or training data. Each GMPE in the logic tree encodes distinct assumptions about source scaling, geometric spreading, anelastic attenuation, and site amplification, collectively covering a wide range of epistemic uncertainty.

This diversity in model genealogy is reflected concretely in the composition of each tree. For the ASC tree, models calibrated from European (ESHM20 backbone), New Zealand, Taiwanese, and global NGA-West2 datasets are combined, providing coverage of the diversity of active crustal attenuation behavior worldwide [@GmmLTASC]. For the SCC tree, the six-member suite spans Eastern North American, European craton, and Australian cratonic environments, sampling across recognized regional variability in stable continental attenuation [@GmmLTSCC][@WeatherillCotton2020]. For the SIF and SIS trees, which draw on models calibrated from distinct regional subduction databases spanning Chile, Japan, Taiwan, Mexico, New Zealand, and the global NGA-SUB compilation, the ensemble ensures that the full observable range of subduction-zone ground-motion behavior is represented in the epistemic uncertainty distribution [@GmmLTSIF][@GmmLTSIS]. This broad, equal-weight approach aligns with recognized international practice for seismic hazard assessments in regions where ground-motion recordings or accelerograms are insufficient to directly calibrate, validate, or rank the candidate GMPEs [@BEE2022a][@PAG2022a].
