## SLOT 2: Definition of GMM/GMPE in the context of PSHA

In probabilistic seismic hazard analysis (PSHA), the annual rate of exceedance of an intensity measure IM above a threshold level $im$ at a given site is computed by integrating the conditional exceedance probability over all earthquake sources, magnitudes, and distances using the total probability theorem. For a set of discrete sources indexed by $i$, the hazard integral takes the form:

$$\lambda(IM > im) = \sum_i \nu_i \iint P(IM > im \mid m, r)\, f_{M_i}(m)\, f_{R_i}(r \mid m)\, dm\, dr$$

where $\nu_i$ is the mean annual rate of earthquakes on source $i$, $f_{M_i}(m)$ is the probability density function of magnitude, $f_{R_i}(r \mid m)$ is the conditional density of source-to-site distance, and $P(IM > im \mid m, r)$ is the conditional exceedance probability furnished by the GMM.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The PSHA hazard integral is a foundational result of probabilistic risk analysis applied to seismology and is not in dispute. The formulation is consistent with standard references and with the implementation in OpenQuake.]

Because GMMs specify a log-normal distribution for IM conditioned on the source and path parameters, the conditional exceedance probability entering the integral is:

$$P(IM > im \mid m, r) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln im - \mu_{\ln IM}(m,\, r,\, \boldsymbol{\theta})}{\sigma_{total}}\right)$$

where $\Phi$ denotes the standard normal cumulative distribution function. Both the median prediction $\mu_{\ln IM}$ and the total standard deviation $\sigma_{total}$ of the GMM directly control the computed hazard curve. The sensitivity of the hazard estimate to the choice of GMM is particularly pronounced at long return periods, where the integral weights the upper tail of the log-normal distribution at multiple standard deviations above the median; small differences among competing GMMs in their median predictions or sigma values can translate into large differences in the computed exceedance rates at these probability levels.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The log-normal exceedance probability formula follows directly from probability theory and is universally applied in PSHA software including OpenQuake. The qualitative observation about sensitivity at long return periods is a well-established result in hazard sensitivity studies and does not require specific citation.]

GMMs constitute one of the principal sources of epistemic uncertainty in PSHA. Epistemic uncertainty arises because the true attenuation relationships governing ground motion in a given tectonic region are not known with certainty and can only be estimated from finite, imperfect datasets. This is distinct from aleatory variability, which is the irreducible scatter about the conditional median that persists even if the median relationship were known perfectly. In data-rich active tectonic regions, GMM epistemic uncertainty can be partially constrained through residual analysis and model ranking against observed accelerograms. In data-sparse regions, where strong-motion recordings are too few or too limited in magnitude-distance coverage to discriminate among competing models, the epistemic uncertainty associated with the GMM logic tree typically becomes the dominant contributor to total hazard uncertainty, particularly at the spectral periods and return periods of engineering interest.^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The distinction between aleatory variability and epistemic uncertainty, and the general characterization of GMMs as major PSHA uncertainty sources, are well established in the literature. The specific claim that GMM epistemic uncertainty dominates in data-sparse regions is a qualitative generalization from sensitivity studies; it is stated here without a directly cited source, warranting MEDIUM confidence.]
