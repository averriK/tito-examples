## SLOT 8: Basis for equally weighted logic tree branches

All four ground-motion logic trees in this assessment (ASC, SCC, SIF, SIS) assign equal weights to their branches, reflecting a deliberate methodological choice grounded in the epistemic constraints of data-scarce seismic environments. In regions where the observational strong-motion record is insufficient to statistically discriminate among candidate GMPEs - either because the dataset is too small to estimate robust likelihood scores, or because existing recordings cluster in magnitude-distance ranges that do not adequately sample the full engineering-relevant parameter space - there is no empirically defensible basis for assigning differential weights to otherwise plausible models. Under these conditions, equal weighting is the minimum-assumption choice consistent with the available evidence.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The equal-weighting philosophy for data-scarce seismic environments is well established in PSHA practice and consistent with the SSHAC Level framework. The logic tree weights are confirmed from the KB documents [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].]

The rationale for assembling a large number of branches - ten in the case of ASC, SIF, and SIS, and six for SCC - rather than reducing the ensemble to two or three representative models is to span the broadest defensible range of epistemic uncertainty in median ground-motion prediction. When the goal of the GMM framework is to generalise across diverse regional conditions without calibration data specific to the target region, a broad ensemble of models representing different functional forms, distance-scaling assumptions, site amplification treatments, and regional calibration datasets offers more complete coverage of the uncertainty space than a small set of models. The spread among predictions from the ten ASC models, or the six SCC models, serves as a quantitative proxy for the epistemic uncertainty in median ground motion that would otherwise be unrepresented in the hazard calculation.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The argument for broad ensembles in data-scarce settings is consistent with published PSHA methodology and SSHAC guidance [WEB:https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/nuregs/staff/sr2117/]. The equal-weight, large-ensemble structure is directly confirmed by the KB logic tree specifications for all four TRTs.]

Equal weighting of a broad model ensemble is moreover consistent with the information-theoretic principle that, in the absence of discriminating evidence, the maximum-entropy distribution over a finite discrete set is the uniform distribution. Assigning unequal weights in this setting would implicitly introduce preferences not supported by data, potentially producing an overconfident hazard estimate biased toward whatever models happen to be upweighted. The equal-weight ensemble preserves the full range of model predictions in the weighted hazard curve, ensuring that the resulting mean hazard and fractile curves honestly reflect the level of uncertainty in median ground motion for the target tectonic environment. This approach aligns with recognised international practice for seismic hazard assessments in regions where ground-motion recordings or accelerograms are insufficient to directly constrain the logic tree weights.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The maximum-entropy / equal-weight argument is standard in Bayesian and decision-theoretic frameworks for epistemic uncertainty, consistent with published PSHA methodology. Confirmed indirectly by the equal-weight structure across all four KB-documented logic trees [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].]
