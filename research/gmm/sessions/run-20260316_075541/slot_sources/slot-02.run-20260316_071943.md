## SLOT 2: Definition of GMM/GMPE in the context of PSHA

A ground motion model (GMM), also referred to as a ground-motion prediction equation (GMPE) or attenuation relation, is an empirical or simulation-based mathematical relationship that predicts the probability distribution of a ground-motion intensity measure at a site given earthquake source characteristics and the source-to-site geometry. In the context of probabilistic seismic hazard analysis (PSHA), the GMM provides the conditional probability $P(Y > y \mid M, R, \ldots)$ that the intensity measure $Y$ exceeds a threshold $y$ for a rupture of magnitude $M$ at distance $R$. Because the residuals of $\ln Y$ are modelled as normal, this conditional exceedance probability is expressed through the complementary standard-normal cumulative distribution function: $P(Y > y \mid M, R) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln y - \mu_{\ln Y}}{\sigma_{\text{T}}}\right)$.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The PSHA role of the GMPE and its functional form as a log-normal conditional probability are standard across the seismic hazard literature and consistent with OpenQuake Engine methodology [WEB:https://docs.openquake.org/oq-engine/current/manual/].]

The PSHA hazard integral aggregates seismic source contributions weighted by the GMM exceedance probability. For a single point source the mean annual rate of exceeding $y$ is

$$\lambda(Y > y) = \sum_{s} \int_{M_{\min}}^{M_{\max}} \int_{0}^{R_{\max}} \nu_s(m) \, P(Y > y \mid m, r) \, f_{M_s}(m) \, f_{R_s}(r \mid m) \, dr \, dm$$

where the sum is over all seismic sources $s$; $\nu_s(m)$ is the earthquake occurrence rate on source $s$; and $f_{M_s}$ and $f_{R_s}$ are the magnitude and distance probability density functions for that source. The GMM thus enters PSHA as the link between seismological source parameters and the probabilistic ground-motion hazard at the site.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The PSHA hazard integral form is standard; the notation is consistent with established PSHA textbooks and OpenQuake Engine documentation [WEB:https://docs.openquake.org/oq-engine/current/manual/]. No contradiction with KB content.]

GMMs represent one of the principal contributors to epistemic uncertainty in PSHA, alongside source model components such as fault geometry, seismicity rates, and magnitude-frequency distributions. In regions where observational strong-motion data are sparse, the median predictions of plausible candidate GMPEs can diverge substantially at engineering-relevant spectral periods, producing a wide spread of hazard curves. Because GMM epistemic uncertainty cannot be reduced without additional observational constraints, it must be explicitly represented in the hazard model through a logic tree that samples over the space of defensible model alternatives.^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The identification of GMMs as a primary source of epistemic uncertainty in PSHA is well established in the literature and consistent with the framing of the KB source documents, which describe the logic-tree approach to GMM selection [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].]
