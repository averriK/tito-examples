## SLOT 8: Basis for equally weighted logic tree branches

All four ground-motion logic trees in this assessment adopt equal weights across branches within each TRT. The ASC, SIF, and SIS trees each contain ten equally weighted branches at 0.10000; the SCC tree contains six branches at approximately 0.16667. The adoption of equal weights is a principled response to the epistemic situation in regions where the available strong-motion catalog is insufficient to derive defensible differential weights through model testing [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md][KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The equal weights are documented directly in the KB files. The rationale connecting equal weights to data insufficiency is a standard argument in the PSHA literature, consistent with the approaches documented for ESHM20 [DOI:10.1007/s10518-020-00940-x] and NGA-East [WEB:https://peer.berkeley.edu/new-peer-report-201703].]

In regions where strong-motion recordings are abundant and cover a wide range of magnitudes and distances, model performance can be evaluated through quantitative metrics including log-likelihood scores, residual bias analysis, and inter-model spread measures; differential weights can then be assigned on an empirical basis. In data-sparse regions, such metrics either cannot be computed at all or yield statistically insignificant differences among competing models. Any assignment of unequal weights in such a context would encode an extra-evidential judgment about model quality that is not grounded in observable evidence. Equal weighting is therefore not merely a computational default but an epistemically conservative choice consistent with treating all models in the suite as equally plausible representations of an unknown attenuation relationship [DOI:10.1007/s10518-020-00940-x].^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The argument for equal weighting as epistemically conservative in data-sparse regions is a well-established position in PSHA practice. The DOI cited (Weatherill & Cotton 2020) supports this in the European craton context. The specific quantitative metrics listed (log-likelihood, residual bias) are general domain knowledge; MEDIUM confidence reflects that the claim is well-supported in principle but stated without a single primary citation that makes it explicit across all four TRT contexts.]

The use of a large number of branches serves to sample the epistemic range of GMM predictions broadly. Competing GMPEs can differ substantially in their median predictions, distance attenuation rates, and standard deviations across the magnitude-distance ranges of interest at a given site; a large and diverse model suite reduces the risk that the mean hazard curve is driven by a narrow cluster of models sharing common assumptions or training data. This consideration is especially important for the SIF and SIS trees, which draw on models calibrated from distinct regional subduction databases spanning Chile, Japan, Taiwan, Mexico, New Zealand, and the global NGA-SUB compilation, ensuring that the full observable range of subduction-zone ground-motion behavior is represented in the epistemic uncertainty distribution [KB:gmmLT_SIF.md][KB:gmmLT_SIS.md]. For the SCC tree, the six-member suite spans Eastern North American, European craton, and Australian cratonic environments, sampling across recognized regional variability in stable continental attenuation [KB:gmmLT_SCC.md][DOI:10.1007/s10518-020-00940-x]. For the ASC tree, models calibrated from European, New Zealand, Taiwanese, and global NGA-West2 datasets are combined, providing coverage of the diversity of active crustal attenuation behavior worldwide [KB:gmmLT_ASC.md].^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The characterization of model diversity across regional calibration datasets (Chile, Japan, Taiwan, Mexico, New Zealand, NGA-SUB for SIF/SIS; Eastern North America, Europe, Australia for SCC; Europe, NZ, Taiwan, NGA-West2 for ASC) is directly verifiable from the model names and origins documented in this chapter and the KB files. The inference that diversity reduces single-model domination is standard practice in PSHA logic-tree design.]
