# Ground Motion Models for Probabilistic Seismic Hazard Analysis

This chapter documents the ground-motion model (GMM) framework adopted in the probabilistic seismic hazard analysis (PSHA). The framework is organized around four tectonic region types -- Active Shallow Crust (ASC), Stable Continental Crust (SCC), Subduction Interface (SIF), and Subduction Intraslab (SIS) -- each assigned an independent ground-motion logic tree implemented in the OpenQuake Engine. The chapter proceeds from foundational definitions and notation through the PSHA role of GMMs and the epistemic uncertainty framework, then documents the logic tree contents for each tectonic region type in turn, and concludes with the rationale for the adopted equal-weighting scheme.

---

## 1. Definitions, Notation, and Equations for the GMM Framework

A ground-motion prediction equation (GMPE), also referred to as a ground-motion model (GMM) or attenuation relationship, provides a mathematical description of the conditional probability distribution of a ground-motion intensity measure (IM) at a site given a specified earthquake scenario. Intensity measures considered in this chapter include the spectral acceleration $\mathrm{SA}(T)$ at oscillator period $T$, peak ground acceleration ($\mathrm{PGA}$), and peak ground velocity ($\mathrm{PGV}$). The conventional ergodic formulation expresses the natural logarithm of a scalar IM as the sum of a deterministic median prediction and a zero-mean random residual [@OpenQuakeEngine]:

$$\ln Y = \mu_{\ln Y}(M_w,\, \mathbf{R},\, \boldsymbol{\theta}) + \varepsilon$$

In this expression, $Y$ is the ground-motion intensity measure; $M_w$ is moment magnitude; $\mathbf{R}$ denotes the vector of relevant source-to-site distance metrics; $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ collects additional predictor variables including rupture geometry descriptors and site parameters; $\mu_{\ln Y}$ is the median prediction expressed in natural-log units; and $\varepsilon$ is the total residual, assumed to follow a zero-mean normal distribution with standard deviation $\sigma$, so that $Y$ is log-normally distributed with median $\exp[\mu_{\ln Y}]$ [@BEE2022a].

The total aleatory standard deviation $\sigma$ satisfies the quadrature relation:

$$\sigma^2 = \tau^2 + \phi^2$$

where $\tau$ is the between-event (inter-event) standard deviation and $\phi$ is the within-event (intra-event) standard deviation. Under the mixed-effects decomposition adopted by most modern empirical GMPEs, the total residual for record $j$ belonging to event $i$ is partitioned as:

$$\varepsilon_{ij} = \eta_i + \delta_{ij}$$

where $\eta_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^2)$ is the between-event residual for event $i$, representing systematic source-to-source variation not captured by the median functional form, and $\delta_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \phi^2)$ is the within-event residual for record $j$, representing path and site variability not resolved by the median prediction. The two residual components are assumed mutually independent [@BEE2017]. All three quantities $\sigma$, $\tau$, and $\phi$ are period-dependent in most modern GMPEs and are tabulated or parameterized as part of the published model. Non-ergodic formulations further partition $\tau$ and $\phi$ into site-specific and path-specific components; the models documented in this chapter are predominantly ergodic.

The predictor variables entering a GMPE vary by model and tectonic region type, but the following notation applies consistently across all models documented in this chapter [@GmmLTASC][@GmmLTSCC][@GmmLTSIF][@GmmLTSIS]. Moment magnitude $M_w$ is the primary source-size parameter. For source-to-site geometry, the principal distance metrics are as follows:

- $R_{JB}$: the Joyner-Boore distance, defined as the shortest horizontal distance from the site to the surface projection of the fault rupture plane
- $R_{rup}$: the closest three-dimensional distance from the site to the rupture plane
- $R_x$: the horizontal distance from the site to the surface projection of the top edge of the rupture, measured perpendicular to the fault strike
- $R_{hypo}$: hypocentral distance
- $R_{epi}$: epicentral distance

The specific distance metric required by each model in the OpenQuake hazardlib is declared as part of the GSIM implementation and is listed in the per-tectonic-region sections that follow. Site amplification is parameterized primarily by $V_{S30}$, the time-averaged shear-wave velocity in the uppermost 30 m. Basin depth is captured by $z_{1.0}$, the depth to the horizon where shear-wave velocity first reaches 1.0 km/s, and by $z_{2.5}$, the depth to the 2.5 km/s horizon. Rupture geometry descriptors include $z_{tor}$, the depth to the top of the rupture plane; the fault dip angle; the rake angle characterizing faulting style; the along-dip rupture width $W$; and hypocentral depth $h$. For subduction models, a binary backarc flag distinguishing forearc and backarc site positions is additionally required by certain implementations [@GmmLTSIF][@GmmLTSIS].

## 2. Definition of GMM/GMPE in the Context of PSHA

A ground-motion model (GMM), also referred to as a ground-motion prediction equation (GMPE) or attenuation relationship, is an empirical or semi-empirical function that predicts the probability distribution of a ground-motion intensity measure conditional on an earthquake scenario specified by source magnitude, source-to-site distance, and ancillary parameters. In probabilistic seismic hazard analysis (PSHA), the GMM provides the conditional exceedance probability $P(Y > y \mid M, R, \ldots)$ that the intensity measure $Y$ exceeds a threshold $y$ for a rupture of magnitude $M$ at distance $R$. Under the log-normal assumption on $Y$, this probability is evaluated through the complementary standard-normal cumulative distribution function [@OpenQuakeEngine]:

$$P(Y > y \mid M, R) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln y - \mu_{\ln Y}(M,\, R)}{\sigma_{\ln Y}}\right)$$

where $\Phi$ denotes the standard normal CDF, $\mu_{\ln Y}$ is the median prediction, and $\sigma_{\ln Y}$ is the total aleatory standard deviation of the GMPE.

The PSHA hazard integral aggregates the contributions of all seismic sources by weighting the GMPE exceedance probability by the earthquake occurrence rate. For a set of seismic sources $s$, the mean annual rate of exceeding the ground-motion level $y$ at the site is [@NHESS2024a]:

$$\lambda(Y > y) = \sum_{s} \nu_s \int_{M} \int_{R} f_{M_s}(m)\, f_{R_s}(r)\, P(Y > y \mid m, r)\, dr\, dm$$

where $\nu_s$ is the annual rate of earthquakes above a minimum magnitude on source $s$; $f_{M_s}(m)$ and $f_{R_s}(r)$ are the probability density functions of magnitude and distance for source $s$; and $P(Y > y \mid m, r)$ is the GMPE conditional exceedance probability. The GMPE thus enters PSHA through both its median prediction $\mu_{\ln Y}$ and its total standard deviation $\sigma_{\ln Y}$: small differences among competing GMPEs in either quantity can produce substantial differences in computed exceedance rates, particularly at long return periods where the hazard integral weights the upper tail of the log-normal distribution at several standard deviations above the median [@OpenQuakeEngine].

GMMs constitute one of the principal contributors to epistemic uncertainty in PSHA, alongside source model components such as fault geometry, seismicity rates, and magnitude-frequency distributions [@GmmLTASC][@GmmLTSCC][@GmmLTSIF][@GmmLTSIS]. Epistemic uncertainty, as distinct from aleatory variability, represents incomplete scientific knowledge of the true ground-motion process; it is in principle reducible with additional data and is represented in PSHA by a discrete set of alternative models. Different GMPEs are constructed from distinct datasets, adopt distinct functional forms, and encode distinct assumptions about source scaling, geometric spreading, anelastic attenuation, and site amplification. In regions where observational strong-motion data are sparse, the median predictions of plausible candidate GMPEs can diverge substantially across engineering-relevant spectral periods and magnitude-distance ranges, producing a wide spread of hazard curves. The spread among GMM predictions at low annual exceedance rates is frequently the dominant contributor to the epistemic uncertainty band on the hazard curve [@BEE2022a][@PAG2022a]. Because this uncertainty cannot be reduced without additional observational constraints, it must be explicitly represented in the hazard model through a logic tree that samples the space of defensible model alternatives.

## 3. Epistemic Uncertainty and Ground-Motion Logic Trees

Epistemic uncertainty in PSHA refers to uncertainty arising from incomplete knowledge that is, in principle, reducible with additional data or modelling effort. In ground-motion modelling, this manifests as the inability to identify a single GMM as definitively correct for a given tectonic setting when the observational record is limited. A ground-motion logic tree provides a structured probabilistic representation of this uncertainty by enumerating a discrete set of mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive model alternatives, each assigned a weight that reflects the analyst's degree of belief in that model given the available evidence [@PAG2022a][@PAG2022b].

In the ground-motion logic trees used in this assessment, each tree is defined for a specific tectonic region type (TRT) and comprises a single branching level with one branch set. Each branch specifies an alternative GMPE and is assigned a non-negative weight $w_k$ such that the normalization condition holds [@GmmLTASC][@GmmLTSCC][@GmmLTSIF][@GmmLTSIS]:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k = 1, \qquad w_k \geq 0$$

where $N$ is the number of branches in the set. The weights define a discrete probability distribution over the space of candidate GMPEs. When all $N$ models are assigned equal weight, $w_k = 1/N$ for all $k$, the logic tree represents a state of maximum model uncertainty, assigning no prior preference to any individual GMPE relative to the others.

The logic tree is propagated through the PSHA computation by evaluating the hazard integral separately for each branch GMPE and computing a weighted combination of the resulting hazard curves. The mean hazard curve is the weighted average:

$$\bar{\lambda}(Y > y) = \sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k\, \lambda_k(Y > y)$$

where $\lambda_k(Y > y)$ is the mean annual exceedance rate computed with the $k$-th branch GMPE [@OpenQuakeEngine]. The full distribution over branches also permits the computation of fractile hazard curves at prescribed probability levels, which characterize the spread of hazard estimates arising from GMM epistemic uncertainty. This framework is the standard mechanism within the OpenQuake Engine for capturing model-to-model variability in ground-motion prediction.

The four tectonic region types covered in this assessment are Active Shallow Crust (ASC), Stable Continental Crust (SCC), Subduction Interface (SIF), and Subduction Intraslab (SIS). Each TRT is assigned its own independent logic tree, reflecting the physical differences in source mechanism, wave propagation path, and site response among crustal, stable continental, and subduction tectonic environments. Each logic tree is identified by a unique logic tree ID and a branch set ID as specified in the OpenQuake NRML input files. The per-TRT logic tree contents, GMPE identifiers, weights, and hazard library parameter requirements are detailed in the following sections [@GmmLTASC][@GmmLTSCC][@GmmLTSIF][@GmmLTSIS].

## 4. Active Shallow Crust (ASC) - GMPEs and Logic Tree

The ASC ground-motion logic tree carries the identifier `seed_ASC_modern` and is organized under a single branching level (`bl_ASC`, branch set `bs_ASC`) applied to all sources classified as the Active Shallow Crust tectonic region type. The tree comprises ten branches, each assigned equal weight $w_k = 0.10000$, with the total weight summing to exactly $1.00000$. The uniform weighting reflects the equal epistemic standing afforded to all ten GMPEs; the supporting documentation does not provide explicit per-model selection rationale beyond ASC regime applicability [@GmmLTASC].

The ten branches are documented below with their hazardlib identifier, assigned weight, calibration context, and OpenQuake hazard library parameter requirements as declared in the GSIM implementation [@GmmLTASC]:

**`AristeidouEtAl2024`** (weight: 0.10000). A GMPE for active shallow crustal earthquakes published in Soil Dynamics and Earthquake Engineering, developed using an artificial neural network regression on strong-motion data [@Aristeidou2024]. The model is calibrated for the ASC regime with records from events of $M_w \geq 4.5$. The hazardlib implementation requires the distance metrics $R_{JB}$, $R_{rup}$, and $R_x$; the site parameters $V_{S30}$ and $z_{2.5}$; and the rupture parameters hypocentral depth, magnitude, rake, and $z_{tor}$.

**`Atkinson2022Crust`** (weight: 0.10000). A backbone crustal GMPE developed by Atkinson (2022) for the 2022 Aotearoa New Zealand National Seismic Hazard Model (NZ22), applying an equivalent point-source framework calibrated against New Zealand strong-motion observations spanning approximately $M_w$ 4.0 to 8.0 at rupture distances up to approximately 400 km [@Atkinson2022NZ][@Bradley2022NZ]. The model is calibrated specifically for the New Zealand crustal (ASC) setting. The hazardlib implementation requires $R_{rup}$, $V_{S30}$, and moment magnitude.

**`BooreEtAl2020`** (weight: 0.10000). A GMPE for active shallow crustal earthquakes belonging to the NGA-West2 family, calibrated for the ASC regime [@GmmLTASC]. The hazardlib implementation requires the distance metric $R_{JB}$, the site parameter $V_{S30}$, and the rupture parameters magnitude and rake.

**`BozorgniaCampbell2016`** (weight: 0.10000). The NGA-West2 vertical-component ground-motion model by Bozorgnia and Campbell (2016), published in Earthquake Spectra [@BozorgniaCampbell2016]. The model is calibrated for active shallow crustal earthquakes from the global NGA-West2 database, spanning approximately $M_w$ 3.3 to 8.5 at distances up to 300 km. The hazardlib implementation requires the distance metrics $R_{JB}$, $R_{rup}$, and $R_x$; the site parameters $V_{S30}$ and $z_{2.5}$; and the rupture parameters dip, hypocentral depth, magnitude, rake, down-dip width, and $z_{tor}$.

**`CampbellBozorgnia2019`** (weight: 0.10000). The Campbell-Bozorgnia NGA-West2 model, implemented in OpenQuake via the `campbell_bozorgnia_2014.py` module and representing a 2019 update to the 2014 NGA-West2 CB model [@CampbellBozorgnia2014]. The model is calibrated for active shallow crustal earthquakes using the NGA-West2 database, covering approximately $M_w$ 3.3 to 8.5 at distances up to 300 km. The hazardlib implementation requires the distance metrics $R_{JB}$, $R_{rup}$, and $R_x$; the site parameters $V_{S30}$ and $z_{2.5}$; and the rupture parameters dip, hypocentral depth, magnitude, rake, down-dip width, and $z_{tor}$.

**`ChaoEtAl2020Asc`** (weight: 0.10000). The active shallow crustal variant of a horizontal ground-motion model for Taiwan developed by Chao et al. (2020) [@Chao2020], calibrated from a Taiwan strong-motion flat-file of more than 40,000 recordings spanning 1992 to 2016 using a two-step maximum-likelihood regression procedure. The model is calibrated for the Taiwan crustal regime. The hazardlib implementation requires the distance metric $R_{rup}$; the site parameters $V_{S30}$, a measured-$V_{S30}$ flag, and $z_{1.0}$; and the rupture parameters magnitude, rake, and $z_{tor}$.

**`KothaEtAl2020`** (weight: 0.10000). The backbone GMPE by Kotha et al. (2020) for active shallow crustal earthquakes in Europe, published in the Bulletin of Earthquake Engineering [@Kotha2020], serving as the ASC component of the 2020 European Seismic Hazard Model (ESHM20). The model was calibrated from the Engineering Strong Motion (ESM) dataset comprising 23,014 recordings from 2,179 earthquakes predominantly in Italy, Turkey, and Greece, spanning $M_w$ 3.0 to 7.4 and Joyner-Boore distances from 0 to approximately 300 km. A hinge magnitude $M_h = 6.2$ separates quadratic from linear magnitude scaling. Uniquely among the ASC logic tree members, the hazardlib implementation requires only $R_{JB}$ and the rupture parameters hypocentral depth and magnitude; no explicit site velocity parameter is required in the base implementation [@NHESS2024b].

**`PhungEtAl2020Asc`** (weight: 0.10000). The active shallow crustal variant of a GMPE by Phung et al. (2020) [@Phung2020], calibrated from the Taiwan strong-motion database using 13,415 ground-motion records from 187 crustal events. The model adopts a functional form based on Chiou and Youngs (2014) with Taiwan-specific recalibration and spans approximately $M_w$ 3.5 to 8.0. The hazardlib implementation requires the distance metrics $R_{JB}$, $R_{rup}$, and $R_x$; the site parameters $V_{S30}$ and $z_{1.0}$; and the rupture parameters dip, magnitude, rake, and $z_{tor}$.

**`Stafford2022`** (weight: 0.10000). A backbone crustal GMPE developed by Stafford (2022) for the 2022 New Zealand National Seismic Hazard Model as part of the NZ22 model set [@Bradley2022NZ], derived through adjustments to the Chiou and Youngs (2014) response spectral model. The model is calibrated for the New Zealand ASC setting, spanning $M_w$ 4.5 to 8.4 at rupture distances from 0 to approximately 300 km. The hazardlib implementation requires the distance metrics $R_{JB}$, $R_{rup}$, and $R_x$; the site parameters $V_{S30}$, a measured-$V_{S30}$ flag, and $z_{1.0}$; and the rupture parameters dip, magnitude, rake, and $z_{tor}$.

**`StewartEtAl2016`** (weight: 0.10000). A GMPE for active shallow crustal earthquakes by Stewart, Boore, Seyhan, and Atkinson (2016), published in Earthquake Spectra as part of the NGA-West2 program [@Stewart2016]. The model is calibrated from the NGA-West2 database over approximately $M_w$ 3.0 to 7.9 at distances up to approximately 300 km. The hazardlib implementation requires the distance metric $R_{JB}$, the site parameter $V_{S30}$, and the rupture parameters magnitude and rake.

## 5. Stable Continental Crust (SCC) - GMPEs and Logic Tree

> Confidence: MIXED -- This section includes HIGH-confidence content plus selected MEDIUM-confidence structured details preserved to avoid dropping essential structure during compilation.

The SCC ground-motion logic tree carries the identifier `seed_SCC_modern` and is organized under a single branching level (`bl_SCC`, branch set `bs_SCC`) applied to all sources classified as the Stable Continental Crust tectonic region type. The tree comprises six branches with a quasi-uniform weighting scheme: five branches each carry a weight of $0.16667$ and the sixth (`CollapsedNGAEastUSGS`) carries the complementary residual weight of $0.16665$, ensuring the total sums to exactly $1.00000$. The SCC tree is the only one among the four tectonic region types in this assessment that does not achieve exact uniform weighting, owing to the rounding required by the six-branch structure. The SCC tree contains fewer branches than the ASC, SIF, and SIS trees, reflecting the more limited number of GMPEs calibrated directly for stable continental regions. The supporting documentation does not provide explicit per-model selection rationale beyond SCC regime applicability [@GmmLTSCC].

The six branches are documented below with their hazardlib identifier, assigned weight, calibration context, and OpenQuake hazard library parameter requirements [@GmmLTSCC]:

**`Atkinson2008prime`** (weight: 0.16667). A modified form of the Atkinson (2008) referenced empirical GMPE for Eastern North America (ENA), representing a variant that applies modified stress-drop scaling to match newly available regional data. The model is calibrated for the stable continental (ENA) regime. The hazardlib implementation, accessed via the `boore_atkinson_2011.py` module, requires $R_{JB}$, $V_{S30}$, and the rupture parameters magnitude and rake.

**`AtkinsonBoore2006Modified2011`** (weight: 0.16667). The Atkinson and Boore (2006) stochastic-based GMPE for Eastern North America [@AtkinsonBoore2006], subsequently modified in 2011 to incorporate magnitude-dependent stress-drop scaling that improved agreement with newer observational data. The model is calibrated for the stable continental ENA regime. The hazardlib implementation requires $R_{rup}$, $V_{S30}$, and moment magnitude.

**`PezeshkEtAl2011`** (weight: 0.16667). A hybrid empirical GMPE for Central and Eastern North America (CENA) by Pezeshk, Zandieh, and Tavakoli (2011), published in the Bulletin of the Seismological Society of America [@Pezeshk2011]. The model combines NGA-West1 model predictions with stochastic ground-motion simulations adjusted to ENA source and attenuation parameters, spanning $M_w$ 4.0 to 8.0 at rupture distances up to approximately 1,000 km, reflecting the long-range attenuation characteristics of stable continental crust. The hazardlib implementation requires only $R_{rup}$ and moment magnitude; no site amplification term is included.

**`ESHM20Craton`** (weight: 0.16667). The craton-specific GMPE developed by Weatherill and Cotton (2020) for the 2020 European Seismic Hazard Model (ESHM20), published in the Bulletin of Earthquake Engineering [@WeatherillCotton2020]. The model is constructed through a scaled backbone approach adapting Eastern North American GMPEs to characterize epistemic uncertainty in European craton ground motion, designed for stable cratonic environments such as the East European Craton. The hazardlib implementation requires $R_{rup}$, $V_{S30}$, and moment magnitude [@NHESS2024b].

**`SomervilleEtAl2009YilgarnCraton_SS14`** (weight: 0.16667). A ground-motion model by Somerville et al. (2009) for rock sites in the Australian Yilgarn Craton, derived from stochastic simulations constrained by regional seismological parameters [@Somerville2009]. The model covers source magnitudes in the range $M_w$ 5.0 to 7.5 and captures enhanced long-period ground motion associated with surface waves in this stable cratonic environment. The `SS14` designation indicates that the standard deviation model has been updated using the Seyhan and Stewart (2014) formulation. The hazardlib implementation requires $R_{JB}$, $V_{S30}$, and moment magnitude.

**`CollapsedNGAEastUSGS`** (weight: 0.16665). A custom GSIM registered via the project's `gmm.gmpe` extension module, extending the OpenQuake `NGAEastUSGSGMPE` base class [@Goulet2017]. This implementation collapses the ensemble of NGA-East USGS ground-motion models into a single GSIM by pre-computing model predictions into a runtime HDF5 lookup table (`NGAEastUSGS_collapsed.hdf5`) and inflating the total standard deviation with a stored between-model epistemic term, thereby preserving the spread of the original multi-model suite within a single logic tree branch. The underlying NGA-East USGS models were developed for Central and Eastern North America (CENA) through a SSHAC Level 3 process, covering $M_w$ 4.0 to 8.2 at distances up to approximately 1,500 km [@GmmLTSCC]. The collapsed implementation requires only $V_{S30}$ and moment magnitude; distance enters the model internally through the precomputed lookup table.

The intraslab backbone GMPE developed by Atkinson (2022) for the NZ22 model set [@Atkinson2022NZ], applying an equivalent point-source framework to intraslab earthquake kinematics. Unlike the interface variant, this implementation requires a backarc classification flag in addition to $R_{rup}$, $V_{S30}$, and moment magnitude, distinguishing forearc and backarc site attenuation conditions. The backarc parameter accounts for the systematically reduced ground-motion amplitudes observed at backarc sites in subduction environments.

**`ChaoEtAl2020SSlab`** (weight: 0.10000). The intraslab variant of the Taiwan ground-motion model by Chao et al. (2020) [@Chao2020], calibrated from Taiwan intraslab records. The hazardlib implementation requires $R_{rup}$; the site parameters $V_{S30}$, a measured-$V_{S30}$ flag, and $z_{1.0}$; and the rupture parameters magnitude and $z_{tor}$.

**`JaimesEtAl2020SSlab`** (weight: 0.10000). An updated GMPE for Mexican intermediate-depth intraslab earthquakes by Jaimes and Garcia-Soto (2020), published in Earthquake Spectra [@Jaimes2020]. The model was calibrated from 366 accelerogram records of 23 intraslab events on the Cocos plate subduction system, extended to include recordings from the September 2017 intraslab events in Mexico (including the $M_w$ 8.1 Chiapas normal-faulting event), covering $M_w$ approximately 5.0 to 8.2, $R_{rup}$ up to approximately 400 km, and focal depths up to approximately 75 km. The hazardlib implementation requires both $R_{hypo}$ and $R_{rup}$, along with $V_{S30}$, hypocentral depth, and moment magnitude.

**`KuehnEtAl2020SSlab`** (weight: 0.10000). The intraslab component of the partially non-ergodic NGA-Subduction GMPE by Kuehn et al. (2020) [@Kuehn2020], using the same Bayesian regression framework as the interface variant but calibrated on intraslab records from the NGA-Sub database. The hazardlib implementation requires $R_{rup}$, $V_{S30}$, and the rupture parameters magnitude and $z_{tor}$.

**`MontalvaEtAl2017SSlab`** (weight: 0.10000). The intraslab variant of the Chilean subduction GMPE by Montalva et al. (2017) [@Montalva2017]. In contrast to the interface variant, which uses $R_{rup}$ as the primary distance metric, the intraslab implementation uses $R_{hypo}$, consistent with the deeper and more diffuse rupture geometry of intraslab events. The hazardlib implementation requires $R_{hypo}$; the site parameters $V_{S30}$ and a backarc classification flag; and the rupture parameters hypocentral depth and magnitude.

**`ParkerEtAl2020SSlab`** (weight: 0.10000). The intraslab component of the global NGA-Subduction GMPE by Parker et al. (2022) [@Parker2022], calibrated for intraslab events spanning $M_w$ 4.0 to 8.4 at $R_{rup}$ from approximately 35 to 2,000 km. The hazardlib implementation requires $R_{rup}$, $V_{S30}$, and the rupture parameters hypocentral depth and magnitude; hypocentral depth is required for the intraslab variant but not for the interface variant.

**`PhungEtAl2020SSlab`** (weight: 0.10000). The intraslab variant of the GMPE by Phung et al. (2020) [@Phung2020], calibrated from Taiwan intraslab records. The hazardlib implementation requires $R_{rup}$; the site parameters $V_{S30}$ and $z_{1.0}$; and the rupture parameters magnitude and $z_{tor}$.

**`SiEtAl2020SSlab`** (weight: 0.10000). The intraslab variant of the Japan-specific NGA-Sub regional GMPE by Si et al. (2020), published as PEER Report 2020/06 [@Si2020]. The hazardlib implementation requires $R_{rup}$; the site parameters $V_{S30}$ and $z_{2.5}$; and the rupture parameters hypocentral depth and magnitude. The explicit hypocentral depth requirement reflects the well-documented sensitivity of intraslab ground-motion amplitudes to slab depth in Japanese subduction data.

## 8. Basis for Equally Weighted Logic Tree Branches

All four ground-motion logic trees in this assessment (ASC, SCC, SIF, SIS) assign equal or quasi-equal weights to their branches: each of the ten ASC, SIF, and SIS branches carries $w_k = 0.10000$, while five of the six SCC branches carry $w_k = 0.16667$ and the collapsed NGA-East branch carries $w_k = 0.16665$. In all cases, no individual branch is assigned greater weight than any other on the basis of expert judgment or model performance metrics [@GmmLTASC][@GmmLTSCC][@GmmLTSIF][@GmmLTSIS].

Equal weighting constitutes a principled epistemic response to the conditions that characterize the regions for which this GMM framework is intended. In regions where the catalog of recorded strong ground motions is insufficient to statistically discriminate among alternative GMPEs -- either because the dataset is too small to yield robust likelihood scores, or because existing recordings cluster in magnitude-distance ranges that do not adequately sample the full engineering-relevant parameter space -- no empirical basis exists for assigning systematically higher weights to one family of models over another [@BEE2022a][@PAG2022a]. Assigning differential weights in such a context would introduce apparent precision not warranted by the available evidence and would risk misrepresenting the true range of epistemic uncertainty by implicitly downweighting models that could be equally or more applicable to the target region.

From an information-theoretic perspective, the uniform distribution is the maximum-entropy assignment of probability over a finite discrete set under a state of equal prior knowledge [@Springer2023LT]. When no evidence distinguishes among the candidate models, assigning equal probability to each is the most defensible representation of the epistemic state. Equal weighting of a broad model ensemble thereby preserves the full range of scientifically credible predictions in the weighted hazard curve and ensures that the resulting mean hazard and fractile curves honestly reflect the level of uncertainty in median ground motion for the target tectonic environment.

The rationale for assembling a large number of branches -- ten in the case of ASC, SIF, and SIS, and six for SCC -- rather than reducing the ensemble to a smaller number of differentially weighted alternatives is to span the broadest defensible range of epistemic uncertainty in median ground-motion prediction [@NRCSSHAC2012]. Competing GMPEs can differ substantially in their median predictions, distance attenuation rates, and standard deviations across the magnitude-distance ranges relevant to a given site; a large and diverse model suite reduces the risk that the mean hazard curve is dominated by a narrow cluster of models sharing common assumptions or training data. Each GMPE in the logic tree encodes distinct assumptions about source scaling, geometric spreading, anelastic attenuation, and site amplification, collectively covering a wide range of epistemic uncertainty.

This diversity in model genealogy is reflected concretely in the composition of each tree. For the ASC tree, models calibrated from European (ESHM20 backbone), New Zealand, Taiwanese, and global NGA-West2 datasets are combined, providing coverage of the diversity of active crustal attenuation behavior worldwide [@GmmLTASC]. For the SCC tree, the six-member suite spans Eastern North American, European craton, and Australian cratonic environments, sampling across recognized regional variability in stable continental attenuation [@GmmLTSCC][@WeatherillCotton2020]. For the SIF and SIS trees, which draw on models calibrated from distinct regional subduction databases spanning Chile, Japan, Taiwan, Mexico, New Zealand, and the global NGA-SUB compilation, the ensemble ensures that the full observable range of subduction-zone ground-motion behavior is represented in the epistemic uncertainty distribution [@GmmLTSIF][@GmmLTSIS]. This broad, equal-weight approach aligns with recognized international practice for seismic hazard assessments in regions where ground-motion recordings or accelerograms are insufficient to directly calibrate, validate, or rank the candidate GMPEs [@BEE2022a][@PAG2022a].
