## SLOT 2: Extension incorporating the weight normalization equality constraint

The constrained formulation from SLOT 1 is extended by incorporating the equality constraint that the weights must sum to unity, thereby ensuring that the calibrated weights define a proper discrete probability distribution over the candidate GMPEs. This extension transforms the problem into a fully constrained optimization with both inequality and equality constraints. [@eq-weight-norm]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The normalization requirement is explicitly stated in the task context as a fundamental property of logic-tree weights, consistent with probability distribution semantics in PSHA.]

The complete constrained optimization problem incorporating both the SLOT 1 inequality constraints and the normalization equality constraint is formulated as follows: ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The combined formulation unifies the prediction-error minimization and probability-distribution requirements, which are both essential for logic-tree weight calibration in PSHA.]

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N} \;\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{b}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k = 1, \quad w_k \geq 0, \quad k = 1, \dots, N.$$

The equality constraint $\sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k = 1$ is the normalization condition defined in the task context [@eq-weight-norm]; it ensures that the weights form a valid discrete probability distribution with total mass equal to unity. [KB:spec.compile.research.md][@eq-weight-norm]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The normalization condition is explicitly required in the task context and is consistent with the probability-distribution interpretation of logic-tree branch weights in PSHA.]

The feasible region is now the standard $N$-dimensional simplex $\Delta_N = \{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N : \sum_k w_k = 1, w_k \geq 0\}$, a compact convex polytope. The constraint set is the intersection of the hyperplane defined by the equality constraint and the non-negative orthant, yielding a closed convex set. The strictly convex quadratic objective function over this convex feasible set remains strictly convex, guaranteeing that a unique global minimiser exists. [@OpenQuakeEngine][@eq-weight-norm]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The simplex is a standard compact convex set in optimization; the uniqueness of the solution for strictly convex objectives over compact convex feasible regions is a foundational result in convex optimization.]

The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions characterize the optimality of the solution to the fully constrained problem. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KKT conditions are the standard necessary and sufficient conditions for optimality in constrained convex optimization problems, applicable here due to the strict convexity of the objective and the convexity of the constraint set.]

For the minimiser $\mathbf{w}^*$, there exist a Lagrange multiplier $\mu$ associated with the equality constraint and Lagrange multipliers $\nu_k \geq 0$ associated with the inequality constraints, such that the gradient of the Lagrangian vanishes at optimality: ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The existence of Lagrange multipliers and the stationarity condition are direct consequences of the KKT theorem for constrained optimization with both equality and inequality constraints.]

$$\nabla_w \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}^*, \mu, \boldsymbol{\nu}) = 2\mathbf{A}^\top(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w}^* - \mathbf{b}) - \mu \mathbf{1} + \boldsymbol{\nu} = \mathbf{0},$$

where $\mathbf{1} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ is the vector of ones, $\boldsymbol{\nu} = (\nu_1, \dots, \nu_N)^\top$, and complementarity holds: $\nu_k w_k^* = 0$ for all $k$. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The KKT conditions are standard in constrained optimization and provide the mathematical characterization of optimality for problems with both equality and inequality constraints.]

The incorporation of the normalization constraint transforms the optimization problem from an unbounded non-negative least-squares (NNLS) problem to a constrained problem on the simplex. This extension ensures that the calibrated weights not only minimize prediction error but also form a valid discrete probability distribution consistent with the logic-tree normalization condition [@eq-weight-norm]. The resulting weight vector $\mathbf{w}^*$ may exhibit sparsity, with some weights driven to exactly zero, thereby performing simultaneous calibration and implicit model selection. When one or more candidate GMPEs are eliminated ($w_k^* = 0$), the effective logic tree reduces to the subset of GMPEs with strictly positive weights. The fully constrained formulation provides a principled means of deriving branch weights that balance fidelity to observed or reference ground-motion data with the requirement of a proper probability distribution over model alternatives.
