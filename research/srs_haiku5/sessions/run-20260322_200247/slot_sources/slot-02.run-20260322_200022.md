## SLOT 2: Extension incorporating the weight normalization equality constraint

The NNLS formulation from SLOT 1 enforces non-negativity but does not restrict the sum of the weights. In the context of epistemic logic trees, the weights represent a discrete probability distribution over the candidate GMPEs and must be normalized so that they form a proper probability measure, defined by the constraint $\sum_{k=1}^N w_k = 1$. [@eq-weight-norm] This normalization condition ensures that the weighted hazard curve represents a valid probabilistic combination and is consistent with the standard PSHA framework, in which the mean hazard integral is computed as a weighted average of branch-specific hazard integrals. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The normalization requirement is explicitly stated in the task context in equation [@eq-weight-norm] and is fundamental to logic-tree construction in PSHA. The constraint reflects the requirement that branch weights define a proper discrete probability distribution.]

The constrained optimization formulation incorporating both the non-negativity bounds and the weight normalization equality constraint is:

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N} \|\mathbf{\Lambda} \mathbf{w} - \bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \sum_{k=1}^N w_k = 1, \quad w_k \geq 0, \quad k = 1, \ldots, N. \quad (\text{Constrained Minimization})$$

This formulation simultaneously enforces the equality constraint (weight normalization) and the inequality constraints (non-negativity), yielding a fully constrained quadratic programming problem. The feasible region is a closed convex polytope (the standard simplex in $\mathbb{R}^N$), defined by the intersection of the hyperplane $\sum w_k = 1$ and the non-negative orthant. Since the objective is a strictly convex quadratic and the feasible set is a closed convex polytope, a unique global minimizer exists. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The mathematical structure of the constrained problem (strictly convex objective + convex polytope constraint set) guarantees uniqueness and global optimality. This follows directly from convex optimization theory.]

The solution to this constrained problem can be obtained through several algorithmic approaches. For problems without Lasso-type sparsity penalties (i.e., when only the squared-error term and linear constraints are present), standard quadratic programming solvers or active-set methods adapted for the simplex constraint are computationally efficient. The objective function and constraint structure ensure that the calibrated weights define a proper discrete probability distribution over the $N$ candidate GMPEs, consistent with the logic-tree normalization condition specified in the PSHA framework and in equation [@eq-weight-norm]. The minimization of the residual sum of squares subject to these constraints yields weights that optimally reconcile the empirical or target hazard curve with the candidate GMPE predictions while maintaining the probabilistic interpretation required for valid hazard integration. [KB:spec.compile.research.md]^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The computational feasibility and probabilistic consistency of the constrained formulation are well-established in the KB for analogous constrained least-squares problems in the spectral-matching context. The extension to the simplex constraint is mathematically straightforward and preserves the desirable properties of the NNLS problem.]
