## SLOT 2: Extension incorporating the weight normalization equality constraint

The constrained optimization formulation from SLOT 1 is now extended by incorporating the weight normalization condition mandated by the logic-tree structure. The requirement that branch weights define a discrete probability distribution over the $N$ candidate GMPEs is expressed as an equality constraint on the sum of all weights:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k = 1, \qquad w_k \geq 0,$$

This normalization condition [@eq-weight-norm] ensures that the weights constitute a valid probability distribution with all components non-negative and summing to unity. Incorporating this equality constraint transforms the problem into a fully constrained optimization problem that simultaneously enforces both inequality bounds and the linear equality constraint. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The normalization condition is explicitly provided in the task context as a fundamental requirement of logic-tree weight definitions. Its incorporation as an equality constraint is mathematically straightforward and consistent with standard constrained optimization practice.]

The complete formulation of the weight calibration problem with both non-negativity bounds and weight normalization is:

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^N} \;\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{b}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad w_k \geq 0, \quad k = 1, 2, \ldots, N, \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k = 1.$$

[KB:spec.compile.research.md] The feasible set is now defined as the intersection of the non-negative orthant with the hyperplane $\sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k = 1$, forming a closed, convex, compact $N-1$ dimensional simplex within $\mathbb{R}^N$. This simplex represents all possible probability distributions over the $N$ candidate GMPEs. The objective function remains the same strictly convex quadratic, guaranteeing that a unique global minimizer exists within the simplex whenever $\mathbf{A}$ has full column rank. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The feasible set geometry (simplex) is a standard construction in constrained optimization; intersection of a non-negative orthant with a linear equality constraint generates this polytope. The convexity and compactness of the simplex ensure existence and uniqueness of the minimizer for a strictly convex objective function.]

Minimizing the prediction error over the simplex yields calibrated weights $\mathbf{w}^* = (w_1^*, w_2^*, \ldots, w_N^*)^\top$ that satisfy all three conditions: the weights are non-negative ($w_k^* \geq 0$), they sum to unity ($\sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k^* = 1$), and they minimize the squared prediction error subject to these constraints. The optimal weights define a proper discrete probability distribution over the candidate GMPEs, fully consistent with the logic-tree normalization condition specified in the epistemic uncertainty framework. [KB:spec.compile.research.md] ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The solution properties follow directly from convex optimization theory applied to the simplex-constrained problem. The consistency with logic-tree requirements is assured by the mathematical definition of the feasible set.]

The fully constrained formulation extends the inequality-constrained problem of SLOT 1 by restricting the feasible region from the entire non-negative orthant to the simplex. This restriction makes the weight calibration problem suitable for direct integration into probabilistic seismic hazard analysis, where the mean hazard curve is computed as the weighted average $\bar{\lambda}_I(i^*) = \sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k^* \lambda_I^{(k)}(i^*)$ [@OpenQuakeEngine], and the calibrated weights are guaranteed to define a valid discrete probability distribution. The combination of non-negativity bounds ($w_k \geq 0$) and weight normalization ($\sum w_k = 1$) ensures that the epistemic uncertainty introduced by model-to-model variability among candidate GMPEs is properly represented through defensible, physically motivated weights assigned to each branch of the logic tree. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The integration with PSHA is confirmed through the mean hazard equation provided in the task context. The physical motivation and mathematical consistency are assured by the derivation in SLOT 1 and the standard application of constrained optimization to probability simplex problems.]
