## 5. Manantiales

### 5.1 Manante 1

Constituye el manantial principal de la subcuenca Titijones, se ubica en la ruta Titijones hacia laguna Suches, proximo a vivienda de comunero, coordenadas de ubicacion: E-340860 y N-8124765; las aguas discurren hacia la cabecera de la quebrada Titijones con formacion de amplios bofedales y humedales sobre extensa llanura fluvioglaciar, las aguas discurren en direccion NO-SE. Los flujos hidricos alimentan los extensos humedales y bofedales de pampa Titijones.
^[Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH, Rationale: The description of springs feeding bofedales (high-Andean wetlands) over fluvioglacial plains is consistent with established hydrogeological patterns in Andean high-altitude aquifer systems. Research confirms that perennial groundwater discharge from springs is indispensable for maintaining saturation in bofedales, particularly during dry periods [@DOI:10.1038/s41598-021-87162-6]. Laguna Suches is documented north of Tutupaca volcano [@WEB:https://www.getamap.net]. The spatial relationship between spring emergence and bofedal formation aligns with documented mechanisms in the Peruvian Altiplano at ~4000 masl [@DOI:10.5194/hess-26-483-2022]. Sessions rated MEDIUM to HIGH confidence, with consensus recognizing sound hydrogeological context but noting specific coordinates and "Titijones" location cannot be externally verified.]

Origen de los manantiales posiblemente por control litologico-estructural del basamento volcanico, las aguas son provenientes de las altas cumbres del cerro Limani y Tutupaca; principal fuente se estima caudal de 20 l/s. (Figura 5-1).
^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The interpretation of lithological-structural control for spring emergence is geologically plausible in volcanic terrains. Structural controls on groundwater flow and spring discharge in volcanic terrains are well-documented [@DOI:10.1007/s10040-022-02545-x]. Tutupaca volcano is confirmed in Tacna region with known geothermal manifestations and hot springs [@WEB:http://ovi.ingemmet.gob.pe]. Surface geochemistry in Cuajone mine area includes "chemically enriched groundwaters via thermal springs," confirming structural control of spring emergence in this volcanic terrain [@DOI:10.3390/w16050769]. However, "Cerro Limani" could not be independently verified in scientific literature. The discharge estimate of 20 l/s is moderate for spring flows but specific measurement cannot be verified. All three sessions rated MEDIUM confidence.]

### 5.2 Manante 2

Constituye el manantial principal ubicada en la ruta Titijones hacia laguna Suches, proximo a vivienda de comunero, discurren sus aguas hacia la quebrada Titijones, coordenadas de ubicacion: E-340674, N-8124823; inmediatamente agua abajo y ambas margenes se desarrolla bofedales y humedales sobre extensa llanura fluvioglaciar, las aguas discurren en direccion NO-SE.
^[Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH, Rationale: The spatial clustering of multiple springs in the same geographic area is characteristic of aquifer discharge zones where preferential flow paths intersect topographic lows. The coordinate proximity (~186m horizontal distance from Manante 1) suggests shared hydrogeological control. Development of wetlands on both margins immediately downstream is consistent with shallow groundwater tables maintained by spring discharge, well-documented in Andean fluvioglacial plains [@WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com]. Sessions rated MEDIUM to HIGH confidence, noting sound hydrogeomorphological patterns but inability to verify specific location. One session noted internal inconsistency: designated as Manante 2 but called "manantial principal" despite lower discharge.]

Origen de los manantiales posiblemente por control litologico-estructural del basamento volcanico, las aguas son provenientes de las altas cumbres del cerro Limani y Tutupaca; se estima caudal de 0.5 l/s. (Apendice B, Figura 52_53)
^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The same hydrogeological origin mechanism (lithologic-structural control) is appropriate for springs in close proximity within the same system. The discharge of 0.5 l/s (40 times smaller than Manante 1) is reasonable given heterogeneous permeability in volcanic rocks controlled by fracture networks [@DOI:10.1007/s10040-022-02545-x]. Order-of-magnitude differences in discharge between nearby springs are common in structurally-controlled volcanic aquifer systems. All three sessions rated MEDIUM confidence, noting plausible hydrogeological reasoning but unverifiable specific discharge measurements and "Cerro Limani" reference.]
