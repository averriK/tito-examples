## 10. Cursos de agua

### 10.1 Curso de agua 1

En la carretera Titijones en dirección hacia Suches y próximo a vivienda de comunero, se presenta dos manantiales los cuales discurren sus aguas hacia la quebrada Titijones, coordenadas de ubicación: E-340860 y N-8124765; E-340674, N-8124823; inmediatamente agua abajo y ambas márgenes se desarrolla bofedales y humedales sobre extensa llanura fluvioglaciar, las aguas discurren en dirección NO-SE.

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: This description synthesizes information from Section 5 (Manantiales) describing the same two springs (Manante 1 and Manante 2) and their contribution to watercourse formation. The coordinates match exactly, providing internal consistency. The hydrogeological sequence—spring emergence → watercourse formation → downstream bofedal/wetland development—is well-documented for Andean high-altitude systems. Research confirms that bofedales "contribute to the recharge of aquifers and the local regulation of streamflow" [WEB:https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-87162-6], indicating bidirectional interaction between groundwater, springs, and surface water. The development of wetlands on both margins (ambas márgenes) immediately downstream reflects shallow groundwater tables maintained by spring discharge and surface water infiltration, a characteristic pattern in fluvioglacial plains with high hydraulic conductivity. The NW-SE flow direction aligns with the regional topographic gradient from the high volcanic peaks toward quebrada Titijones.]

Origen de los manantiales posiblemente por control litológico-estructural del basamento volcánico, las aguas son provenientes de las altas cumbres del cerro Limani y Tutupaca; principal fuente se estima caudal de 20 l/s, mientras el segundo manantial se estima en 0.5 l/s. (Apéndice B, Figura 46_52)

^[Confidence: MEDIUM. Rationale: This paragraph repeats information from Section 5.1 and 5.2 verbatim, maintaining the same confidence assessment. The flow rate estimates (20 l/s and 0.5 l/s) provide useful hydrological context for understanding the relative magnitude of surface water initiation but would benefit from field measurement data.]

### 10.2 Curso de agua 2

Corresponde al curso superior de la quebrada Titijones, cuyas aguas se originan mayormente en dos manantiales ubicadas próximo a vivienda de comunero; coordenadas de ubicación: E-340860 y N-8124765; E-340674, N-8124823; aguas abajo discurre de manera meandriforme a sinuosa, baja pendiente, con aportes de tributarios en algunos casos delimitados por bofedales y humedales; cauce principal corta a la amplia llanura fluvioglaciar, en general tiene una orientación de ángulo recto invertido, posiblemente causado por control tectónico-estructural del basamento volcánico. (Apéndice B, Figura 61)

^[Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH. Rationale: The description of the upper quebrada Titijones transitioning from spring-fed headwaters to a meandering/sinuous channel with low gradient and tributary inputs is characteristic of Andean high-altitude drainage systems. The low-gradient, meandering morphology reflects the gentle topography of the fluvioglacial plain (confirmed by pumping station descriptions indicating "superficie llana"). The association of tributaries with bofedales and wetlands indicates that multiple groundwater discharge zones contribute to streamflow, consistent with the diffuse spring systems documented in Sections 5-7. The observation of an "inverted right-angle orientation" (orientación de ángulo recto invertido) with attributed tectonic-structural control is geologically plausible. The report documents neotectonic faults (Section 4.2: Falla Neotectónica Ticsani-Suches, N-340º-350º) that could impose structural control on drainage patterns. Research confirms that "high-elevation sources of recharge and groundwater flow paths" in volcanic mountain terrain of southern Peru are influenced by structural features [WEB:https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/26/483/2022/]. However, the specific claim of right-angle control requires structural geological analysis (e.g., lineament analysis, fault mapping) to confirm that the drainage geometry reflects structural rather than purely geomorphic controls (e.g., glacial erosion patterns, differential bedrock resistance).]

### 10.3 Curso de agua 3

Corresponde al curso medio de la quebrada Titijones ubicada en el lado meridional de la cuenca Titijones, aguas abajo discurre de manera meandriforme a sinuosa, baja pendiente, con aportes de tributarios en algunos casos delimitados por bofedales y humedales; cauce principal sigue un valle ligeramente cerrado cuya orientación NE-SO posiblemente originado por control tectónico-estructural del basamento volcánico. (Apéndice B, Figura 62_63)

^[Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH. Rationale: The description of the middle reach of quebrada Titijones maintains morphological consistency with the upper reach—meandering pattern, low gradient, tributary inputs associated with wetlands. The transition from "wide fluvioglacial plain" (upper reach) to "slightly closed valley" (valle ligeramente cerrado) in the middle reach reflects typical downstream valley morphology evolution as the stream incises into underlying bedrock. The NE-SW orientation attributed to tectonic-structural control is consistent with documented fault orientations in the area: the Falla Neotectónica Ticsani-Suches is described as N-340º-350º (equivalent to NNW-SSE), and local faults in the Capillune Formation are described as N-330º to 340º-E. These fault trends could plausibly control valley orientation. The persistence of tributary inputs "in some cases delimited by bofedales and wetlands" indicates continued groundwater-surface water interaction in the middle reach, consistent with the aquifer system described throughout the report. Studies confirm that drainage patterns in the Moquegua region's volcanic terrain are influenced by structural geology [WEB:https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/5/769]. However, as with Curso de agua 2, definitive confirmation of structural control would require lineament analysis and comparison with mapped fault traces.]

## Summary of Findings

**Overall Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH**

The section on cursos de agua (watercourses) provides a reasonable characterization of surface water drainage in the Titijones basin, with particular strength in describing groundwater-surface water interactions. Key strengths include:

1. **Spring-stream connection**: Correctly links spring emergence to watercourse initiation
2. **Channel morphology**: Appropriately describes meandering/sinuous patterns consistent with low-gradient fluvioglacial settings
3. **Groundwater-surface water interaction**: Recognizes tributary inputs associated with bofedales and wetlands, indicating ongoing groundwater discharge
4. **Structural control hypothesis**: Proposes tectonic-structural influence on drainage geometry, which is geologically plausible given documented fault systems

**Limitations identified:**

1. **Repetition**: Section 10.1 duplicates content from Section 5 without adding new information
2. **Structural control verification**: Claims of tectonic control on drainage patterns (right-angle geometry, NE-SW orientation) are plausible but lack supporting analysis (lineament maps, structural correlation)
3. **Quantification**: No discharge measurements for watercourses, limiting hydrological utility
4. **Seasonal variability**: No discussion of flow regime changes between wet and dry seasons

**Recommendation**: The section would benefit from: (1) streamflow measurements at multiple points along quebrada Titijones to quantify longitudinal gains/losses, (2) lineament analysis overlaying drainage patterns on structural maps to confirm tectonic control hypotheses, and (3) discussion of seasonal flow variability given the strong precipitation seasonality documented for the Andean Altiplano.
