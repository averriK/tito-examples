## 8. Estaciones de Bombeo

### 8.1 Estación de Bombeo (TW-2A)

Corresponde a una estación de Bombeo "operativo", se ubica en la amplia llanura fluvioglaciar de pampa Titijones, sobre una superficie llana, delimitada por cerca, coordenada de ubicación: E-338579, N-8126363, cota 4503, profundidad referencial 300m. (Apéndice B, Figura 57).

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: The description of an operational pumping station on the fluvioglacial plain of pampa Titijones with a reference depth of 300m is consistent with documented groundwater extraction infrastructure in the Titijones aquifer system. Southern Peru Copper Corporation has "pumped ground water from the Titijones area for use at Cuajone for many years" [WEB:https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/5/769], with water rights for "up to 1,950 liters per second from well fields at the Huaitire, Vizcachas and Titijones aquifers" [WEB:https://southerncoppercorp.com/eng/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/03/10k2022.pdf]. The 300m depth is appropriate for accessing the Formación Capillune aquifer, which the report describes as extending to significant depths (the C-5 borehole reached 568m). The location on a flat fluvioglacial plain (superficie llana) is typical for production well siting, where thick permeable sediments and minimal topographic relief facilitate well installation and water accumulation. The elevation of 4503 masl is consistent with the high-altitude setting of the Titijones aquifer system, which is situated above 4000 masl in the Andean Altiplano. The designation "operativo" indicates this is an active extraction point, consistent with ongoing water supply to the Cuajone mine.]

### 8.2 Estación de Bombeo (TW-03)

La estación de bombeo (TW-03) actualmente "operativo", se ubica en la amplia llanura fluvioglaciar de pampa Titijones, sobre una superficie llana, delimitada por cerca, coordenada de ubicación: E-338976, N-8125656, cota 4528, profundidad referencial 300m. Apéndice B, Figura 58).

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: This second pumping station exhibits identical characteristics to TW-2A: operational status, location on the fluvioglacial plain, 300m reference depth, and similar elevation (4528 masl vs. 4503 masl). The spatial separation of ~780m from TW-2A is typical of wellfield design to minimize interference between pumping wells while maximizing aquifer capture. The slightly higher elevation (25m difference) suggests TW-03 is positioned further up-gradient in the aquifer system. Multiple pumping stations are consistent with the scale of water extraction documented for SPCC operations. Historical data indicate that "SPCC has pumped ground water from the Titijones area for use at Cuajone for many years, depleting most of the local surface water" [WEB:https://remwater.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/Moran-Robert-E.-2002-The-Quellaveco-Mine-Free-Water-for-Mining-in-Peru%E2%80%99s-Driest-Desert.pdf], implying extensive wellfield infrastructure. The fenced enclosures (delimitada por cerca) indicate security and operational control, standard for industrial water supply facilities.]

### 8.3 Estación de Bombeo (TW-04)

Comprende a estación de Bombeo actualmente "operativo", sobre amplia llanura fluvioglaciar de pampa Titijones, protegida con cerca de seguridad, coordenada de ubicación: E-339560, N-8124863, cota 4570, profundidad referencial 300m. (Apéndice B, Figura 59).

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: The third pumping station maintains the same operational and technical characteristics as TW-2A and TW-03. The elevation of 4570 masl (67m higher than TW-2A, 42m higher than TW-03) indicates this well is positioned further up-gradient, potentially capturing recharge closer to its source areas. The spatial distribution of the three wells (TW-2A, TW-03, TW-04) across the pampa Titijones suggests a strategic wellfield layout designed to maximize aquifer interception. The consistent 300m depth across all three stations indicates targeting of the same productive horizons within the Formación Capillune. The mention of "security fence" (cerca de seguridad) reflects the critical importance of this water supply infrastructure to mine operations. Water infrastructure for the Cuajone mine "begins with a water supply at Lake Suche at 14,500 feet in the Andes" [WEB:https://www.xylem.com/en-bg/making-waves/construction-and-mining/providing-water-to-perus-largest-copper-mine/], with the Titijones wellfield representing a key component of this system. The operational status of all three stations confirms active extraction from the aquifer system at the time of the report.]

## Summary of Findings

**Overall Confidence: HIGH**

The section on estaciones de bombeo (pumping stations) provides factual documentation of groundwater extraction infrastructure consistent with independently confirmed operations by Southern Peru Copper Corporation. Key strengths include:

1. **Operational confirmation**: All three stations designated as "operativo" aligns with documented ongoing extraction from the Titijones aquifer
2. **Technical specifications**: The 300m depth is appropriate for the Capillune Formation aquifer
3. **Spatial distribution**: The wellfield layout across pampa Titijones reflects sound hydrogeological planning
4. **Security measures**: Fencing indicates proper management of critical water supply infrastructure
5. **Elevational gradient**: The 67m elevation difference from TW-2A to TW-04 suggests strategic positioning to capture up-gradient recharge

**Limitations identified:**

1. **Extraction volumes**: No pumping rates or cumulative extraction volumes provided
2. **Water levels**: No data on static or dynamic water levels in the wells
3. **Well construction**: No details on casing depth, screen intervals, or completion methods
4. **Historical context**: No information on installation dates or operational history

**Recommendation**: Integration of pumping test data, water level monitoring records, and extraction volume histories would significantly enhance the hydrogeological value of this section. These data are likely available from SPCC operational records and would support water balance calculations and aquifer sustainability assessments.
