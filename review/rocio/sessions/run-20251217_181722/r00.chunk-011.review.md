## 7. Filtraciones

### 7.1 Filtraciones 1

Aguas abajo de quebrada Titijones, en ladera derecha y contacto roca suelo morrénico, coordenadas de ubicación: E-334541, N-8125209, se ubica de media ladera superior, parte baja húmeda con desarrollo suelos saturados, el escurrimiento de agua esta originada por varios ojos de agua y precisamente en contacto de rocas andesíticas y depósitos morrénicos, la orientación e inclinación del basamento rocoso ayudan al desarrollo de las filtraciones; la fuente de agua lo constituye las altas cumbres del centro volcánico Iruma; no se ha medido el caudal, actualmente presenta bajo caudal, sin embargo durante épocas lluviosas se incrementa el flujo. (Apéndice B, Figura 55).

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: The description of seeps at the rock-soil contact between andesitic bedrock and morainic deposits is hydrogeologically sound and typical of Andean volcanic terrains. This represents a classic "perched aquifer" or "contact spring" scenario where permeable morainic deposits overlie less permeable volcanic bedrock, and groundwater flows along the contact interface until it intersects topography and emerges as seepage. The report correctly identifies that "the orientation and inclination of the rocky basement help develop the seeps" (la orientación e inclinación del basamento rocoso ayudan al desarrollo de las filtraciones), which is a fundamental hydrogeological principle: dipping impermeable layers channel groundwater flow toward discharge points. Research on high-altitude Andean hydrogeology confirms that morainic deposits have "high porosity and permeability" [WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0048969722030650], and their contact with volcanic bedrock commonly produces seepage zones. The attribution to the "high peaks of the Iruma volcanic center" as the water source is geologically reasonable given the topographic gradient and the described bedrock dip direction. The observation of seasonal variability—low flow currently but increased flow during rainy periods—is entirely consistent with shallow groundwater systems recharged by precipitation at higher elevations. The acknowledgment that flow has not been measured is appropriately transparent.]

### 7.2 Filtración 2

Aguas abajo de quebrada Titijones, en ladera derecha y contacto roca suelo morrénico, coordenadas referencial: E-333809, N-8124777, se ubica varios ojos de agua desarrollados en media ladera superior, los flujos discurren hacia la margen derecha de la quebrada Titijones; la disposición del basamento rocoso en dirección hacia la quebrada Titijones, ayudan al flujo que nacen en las partes altas del centro volcánico Iruma; actualmente con caudal medio-bajo, se incrementa durante épocas lluviosas. (Apéndice B, Figura 56).

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: This second seep location exhibits the same hydrogeological pattern as Filtraciones 1, reinforcing the conceptual model of contact-controlled groundwater discharge. The spatial clustering of multiple seepage points (varios ojos de agua) along the same stratigraphic contact is characteristic of linear spring systems controlled by lithologic boundaries. The coordinates indicate this seep is located ~800m southwest of Filtraciones 1, both on the right margin (ladera derecha) of quebrada Titijones, suggesting a continuous hydrogeological feature along this contact zone. The description that "the arrangement of the rocky basement in the direction toward quebrada Titijones helps the flow" (la disposición del basamento rocoso en dirección hacia la quebrada Titijones, ayudan al flujo) correctly identifies structural control of groundwater flow paths. Attribution to the same recharge source (centro volcánico Iruma) is consistent given the similar position on the watershed. The reported seasonal variability (medium-low flow currently, increasing during rainy periods) matches the pattern described for Filtraciones 1, supporting a shared hydrogeological control mechanism. Research confirms that groundwater in volcanic mountain terrain of southern Peru follows "flow paths that convey groundwater to lower elevations where the springs emerge" [WEB:https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/26/483/2022/], consistent with the described seepage pattern.]

## Summary of Findings

**Overall Confidence: HIGH**

The section on filtraciones (seeps) demonstrates strong hydrogeological understanding and accurate characterization of groundwater discharge mechanisms in Andean volcanic terrain. Key strengths include:

1. **Hydrogeological mechanism**: Correctly identifies contact-controlled seepage at the interface between permeable morainic deposits and less permeable volcanic bedrock
2. **Structural control**: Recognizes the role of bedrock orientation and dip in controlling groundwater flow paths
3. **Recharge attribution**: Plausibly links seepage to recharge from high-elevation volcanic centers based on topographic position
4. **Seasonal variability**: Appropriately documents temporal variation in seepage discharge related to precipitation
5. **Transparency**: Acknowledges lack of flow measurements

**Limitations identified:**

1. **Quantification**: Flow rates are not measured; qualitative descriptors ("bajo caudal," "caudal medio-bajo") limit hydrological utility
2. **Recharge confirmation**: Attribution to centro volcánico Iruma as water source, while geologically plausible, lacks hydrochemical or isotopic confirmation

**Recommendation**: Installation of simple monitoring infrastructure (e.g., V-notch weirs, staff gauges) at these seepage points would enable quantitative assessment of seasonal discharge variability and improve water balance calculations for the Titijones aquifer system.
