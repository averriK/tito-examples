## 11. Lagunas - Lagunillas

### 11.1 Laguna seca

Se ubica próximo a garita de control de Suches, en el lado septentrional de la cuenca Titijones a una distancia promedio de 3.5 km, es una depresión morfológica alargada en dirección NE-SO, dimensiones: 420 m de largo x 185 m ancho, está delimitada por morrenas alargadas dique natural formado por intersección de morrenas terminales.

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: The description of laguna seca as a morphological depression bounded by elongated moraines and formed by intersection of terminal moraines is entirely consistent with well-documented moraine-dammed lake formation in the Peruvian Andes. Research confirms that "approximately 28% of all glacial lakes in Peru are moraine-dammed" and that "retreat of glaciers along narrow linear bedrock valleys has increased the number and size of moraine-dammed glacial lakes" [WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0921818121001594]. The NE-SW elongation aligns with typical glacial valley orientations. The dimensions (420m × 185m) and location at 3.5 km from Suches indicate a relatively small moraine-dammed depression, consistent with the "lagunillas" (small lakes) designation used elsewhere in the section. The mechanism described—"natural dam formed by intersection of terminal moraines" (dique natural formado por intersección de morrenas terminales)—accurately characterizes the dam structure common to moraine-dammed lakes. Studies in Cordillera Blanca document moraine-dammed lakes "located in the altitudinal zone ranging from 4155 to 4960 masl" [WEB:https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41748-022-00330-0], consistent with the Titijones elevation range above 4000 masl.]

Subyace depósitos morrénicos conformada por: bolones, bloques, gravas, matriz areno-limosa, medianamente densa, coloración marrón pardusca.

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: The description of substrate morainic deposits composed of boulders (bolones), blocks (bloques), gravels (gravas) with sandy-silty matrix accurately characterizes glacial till lithology. This unsorted, polymodal sediment composition is diagnostic of morainic materials. The moderate density and brownish coloration are typical of weathered glacial deposits in high-altitude Andean settings.]

Se desarrolla aguas abajo de laguna Parincota el cual constituye el aporte principal del volumen hidráulico de laguna seca, asimismo podrían ser alimentados por filtraciones de aguas que se originan en las partes altas del domo Eslabón y volcánico fisural Arichaua cuya pendiente del sustrato volcánico favorece el direccionamiento del flujo; durante temporadas de estiaje disminuye el nivel del agua, pero se incrementa durante épocas de nevadas y lluvias. (Apéndice B, Figura 64)

^[Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH. Rationale: The hydrological description identifies multiple water sources: (1) hydraulic input from upstream laguna Parincota, and (2) potential groundwater seepage from high-elevation recharge zones (domo Eslabón and volcánico fisural Arichaua). The cascade relationship between lakes (Parincota → Laguna seca) is common in glaciated valleys where multiple moraine-dammed lakes form in sequence. The supplementary groundwater contribution is plausible given the extensive seepage systems documented in Sections 7.1-7.2, where contact-controlled springs emerge from volcanic bedrock-moraine interfaces. The described seasonal water level variability—declining during dry periods (estiaje), increasing during snow/rain periods—is characteristic of high-altitude lake hydrology in regions with strong precipitation seasonality. Research confirms that "wet seasons deliver 100–130 mm monthly in some puna regions, while dry periods restrict inputs to 3–12 mm monthly" [WEB:https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-87162-6], resulting in pronounced seasonal lake level fluctuations. However, the attribution of Parincota as the "principal hydraulic input" (aporte principal del volumen hidráulico) lacks quantitative support; no flow measurements or water balance calculations are provided.]

### 11.2 Laguna Parincota

Se ubica al norte de laguna Seca, constituye una laguna de tamaño considerable cuyo eje principal en dirección N-S, dimensiones: 1000 m de largo x 450 m ancho, formada en una depresión morfológica, delimitada y encerrada por morrenas laterales y terminales, dique natural formado por intersección de morrenas terminales.

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: Laguna Parincota's larger size (1000m × 450m vs. 420m × 185m for Laguna seca) and N-S orientation indicate a more developed glacial valley lake. The description of lateral and terminal moraine boundaries with a natural moraine-dam is consistent with well-documented lake formation mechanisms. The N-S axis suggests control by the primary glacial valley orientation, perpendicular to ice flow direction. This represents a typical moraine-dammed lake configuration in Andean glaciated valleys.]

Subyace depósitos morrénicos conformada por: bolones, bloques, gravas, matriz areno-limosa, medianamente densa, coloración marrón pardusca.

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: Substrate description identical to Laguna seca, reflecting uniform morainic lithology across the glaciated landscape. This consistency strengthens confidence in field characterization.]

Laguna Parincota formado posiblemente por filtraciones de aguas que se originan en las partes altas del domo Eslabón y volcánico fisural Arichaua, cuyas pendientes del sustrato volcánico favorecen el direccionamiento de los flujos; durante temporadas de estiaje disminuyen ligeramente el nivel del agua, caso contrario en épocas de nevadas y alta precipitaciones se incrementa el nivel. (Apéndice B, Figura 65)

^[Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH. Rationale: The recharge attribution to domo Eslabón and volcánico fisural Arichaua via groundwater seepage is geologically plausible, particularly given the documented seepage zones at volcanic bedrock-moraine contacts (Section 7). The qualification that water levels "decrease slightly" (disminuyen ligeramente) during dry periods suggests that Parincota maintains more stable water levels than Laguna seca, which may reflect greater groundwater contribution buffering seasonal variability. However, as with Laguna seca, quantitative verification of recharge sources is absent.]

### 11.3 Lagunas Limani

Corresponde a dos lagunas ubicadas al Oeste del volcánico fisural Limani, sobre una depresión morfológica delimitada por depósitos morrénicos; presenta forma subredondeada a irregular, entre 200 m x 200 m y 326 m x 220 m, respectivamente; dique natural está formado por intersección de morrenas terminales; aguas excedentes de lagunas discurren en dirección a la subcuenca Titijones y constituyen la fuente principal de los acuíferos estudiados.

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: The description of two moraine-dammed lakes west of volcánico fisural Limani with subrounded to irregular shapes (200m × 200m and 326m × 220m) fits the pattern of small glacial lakes in volcanic highlands. The critical hydrogeological claim is that "excess waters from the lakes flow toward the Titijones subbasin and constitute the principal source of the studied aquifers" (aguas excedentes de lagunas discurren en dirección a la subcuenca Titijones y constituyen la fuente principal de los acuíferos estudiados). This establishes a direct hydrological connection between surface water bodies and groundwater recharge. The proximity to Limani, identified in Section 9.1 as the "principal water source" for the basin, reinforces the importance of this volcanic center in the water budget. Research confirms that high-altitude lakes can contribute to aquifer recharge: "bofedales contribute to the recharge of aquifers and the local regulation of streamflow" [WEB:https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-87162-6], and moraine-dammed lakes in volcanic terrains can similarly serve as recharge sources where permeable substrates allow infiltration. However, the claim that these lakes constitute the "principal source" (fuente principal) of aquifer recharge requires quantification through water balance analysis or tracer studies.]

Se desarrolla sobre depósitos morrénicos constituidos: bolones, bloques, gravas, matriz areno-limosa, medianamente densa, coloración marrón pardusca.

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: Consistent morainic substrate description, maintaining lithological uniformity across all lake descriptions.]

Lagunas Limani han sido formados por filtraciones de aguas que se originan en las partes altas del volcánico fisural Limani, las pendientes del sustrato volcánico favorecen el direccionamiento de los flujos; durante temporadas de estiaje disminuyen ligeramente el nivel del agua, se incrementa durante las épocas de nevadas y alta precipitaciones de lluvias. (Apéndice B, Figura 66)

^[Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH. Rationale: Recharge mechanism consistent with previous lake descriptions. The attribution to high-elevation seepage from Limani volcanic complex is supported by the topographic gradient and documented spring systems (Section 5 attributes Manante 1, the largest spring at 20 l/s, to Limani/Tutupaca sources). Seasonal water level behavior matches expected patterns.]

### 11.4 Lagunilla 1

Corresponde a una depresión morfológica ubicada dentro del contexto del volcánico fisural Arichahua; lagunilla presenta 265 m de largo x 120 m ancho, se desarrolla en el lado septentrional de la subcuenca Titijones, aproximadamente 8 km, sobre amplia geoforma negativa delimitada por suelos fluvioglaciares; se observa desde la carretera principal Moquegua-Puno

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: Small elongated lake (265m × 120m) within the Arichahua volcanic fissure context, situated on fluvioglacial soils rather than directly moraine-bounded. The visibility from the Moquegua-Puno highway provides independent verification of its existence. The geographic context is consistent with the northern portion of the study area.]

El entorno de lagunilla constituida por: bolones y gravas, matriz limo-arenosa, medianamente densa, coloraciones marrones pardusca.

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: Substrate description indicates coarser fluvioglacial deposits (boulders and gravels with silty-sandy matrix) rather than morainic till, consistent with the "suelos fluvioglaciares" context mentioned above.]

Posiblemente originada por filtraciones de aguas que se originan en las cumbres del volcánico fisural Arichaua, la pendiente del sustrato volcánico favorece el direccionamiento del flujo; en épocas de estiaje disminuye el volumen de agua y se incrementa durante temporadas de nevadas y lluvias. (Apéndice B, Figura 67)

^[Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH. Rationale: Recharge attribution to Arichaua volcanic fissure is consistent with the lake's position within that volcanic complex. The hydrogeological mechanism follows the established pattern of high-elevation seepage feeding lower lakes. Seasonal variability matches expected patterns.]

Subyace deposito morrénicos conformada por: bolones, bloques, gravas, matriz areno-limosa, medianamente densa.

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: Very small lake (100m × 50m) in elongated depression bounded by ridges oriented toward the basin. Morainic substrate description is consistent with previous descriptions. The association with a tributary originating from centro volcánico Arundaya establishes clear geographic context.]

Formación de lagunilla posiblemente originada por filtraciones de aguas que nacen de las partes altas centro volcánico Arundaya, la pendiente del sustrato volcánico favorece el direccionamiento del flujo; en época de estiaje disminuye el volumen de aguas, sin embargo, durante temporadas de nevadas y lluvias se incrementa el volumen. (Apéndice B, Figura 68)

^[Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH. Rationale: Recharge mechanism and seasonal behavior consistent with all previous lake descriptions. The pattern of high-elevation volcanic recharge feeding small lakes via subsurface flow is repeated consistently throughout this section, strengthening confidence in the conceptual hydrogeological model.]

## Summary of Findings

**Overall Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH to HIGH**

The section on lagunas and lagunillas provides detailed characterization of glacial and periglacial lakes in the Titijones basin, with strong consistency in hydrogeological interpretation and good alignment with published research on Andean moraine-dammed lakes. Key strengths include:

1. **Lake formation mechanisms**: Correctly identifies moraine-dammed lake formation via terminal and lateral moraine intersection, consistent with documented mechanisms in Peruvian Cordilleras [WEB:https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41748-022-00330-0]
2. **Substrate characterization**: Provides consistent, detailed descriptions of morainic and fluvioglacial substrates
3. **Spatial organization**: Clearly differentiates lake sizes, orientations, and positions relative to volcanic centers
4. **Recharge mechanisms**: Systematically attributes lake water sources to high-elevation volcanic centers via subsurface flow
5. **Seasonal variability**: Appropriately documents water level fluctuations consistent with strong precipitation seasonality in the Andean Altiplano [WEB:https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-87162-6]
6. **Hydrological connectivity**: Recognizes cascade relationships (e.g., Parincota → Laguna seca) and groundwater-lake interactions

**Limitations identified:**

1. **Quantitative verification**: No water level monitoring data, flow measurements, or water balance calculations provided
2. **Recharge attribution**: While geologically plausible, attributions to specific volcanic centers lack hydrochemical or isotopic confirmation
3. **"Principal source" claim**: Lagunas Limani described as "principal source of the studied aquifers" without quantitative support
4. **Temporal changes**: No discussion of lake evolution, expansion/contraction trends, or response to recent climate change (documented elsewhere in the Andes as significant [WEB:https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41748-022-00330-0])
5. **Hazard assessment**: No mention of potential glacial lake outburst flood (GLOF) risks, despite moraine-dammed lakes being "potentially most dangerous lakes that can trigger GLOFs"

**Recommendation**: The section would be significantly enhanced by: (1) installation of lake level gauges to quantify seasonal variability, (2) bathymetric surveys to estimate lake volumes and residence times, (3) water quality sampling to trace connectivity between lakes and aquifers, (4) assessment of moraine dam stability given GLOF risks documented elsewhere in Peru, and (5) integration of temporal satellite imagery to document lake area changes over recent decades. Despite these limitations, the descriptive characterization is hydrogeologically sound and provides valuable baseline documentation of the lake systems.
