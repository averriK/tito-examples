## 5. Manantiales

### 5.1 Manante 1

Constituye el manantial principal de la subcuenca Titijones, se ubica en la ruta Titijones hacia laguna Suches, próximo a vivienda de comunero, coordenadas de ubicación: E-340860 y N-8124765; las aguas discurren hacia la cabecera de la quebrada Titijones con formación de amplios bofedales y humedales sobre extensa llanura fluvioglaciar, las aguas discurren en dirección NO-SE. Los flujos hídricos alimentan los extensos humedales y bofedales de pampa Titijones.

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: The description of springs (manantiales) feeding bofedales and wetlands over fluvioglacial plains is consistent with established hydrogeological patterns in Andean high-altitude aquifer systems. Research confirms that perennial groundwater discharge from aquifers and springs is indispensable for maintaining saturation in bofedales, particularly during dry periods when precipitation drops to 3-12 mm monthly [WEB:https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-87162-6]. The spatial relationship between spring emergence and bofedal formation over fluvioglacial deposits aligns with documented hydrogeological mechanisms in the Peruvian Altiplano at ~4000 masl [WEB:https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/26/483/2022/]. The directional flow pattern (NO-SE) is geologically plausible given structural controls typical of Andean volcanic terrains.]

Origen de los manantiales posiblemente por control litológico-estructural del basamento volcánico, las aguas son provenientes de las altas cumbres del cerro Limani y Tutupaca; principal fuente se estima caudal de 20 l/s. (Figura 5-1).

^[Confidence: MEDIUM. Rationale: The attribution of spring origin to lithologic-structural controls in volcanic basement is well-supported by regional hydrogeology. Groundwater stable isotopes in southern Peru indicate that aquifer recharge originates from high-elevation precipitation (~4000 masl) in the Andean mountain ridge [WEB:https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/26/483/2022/]. The Cuajone mine area's surface geochemistry includes "chemically enriched groundwaters via thermal springs," confirming structural control of spring emergence in this volcanic terrain [WEB:https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/5/769]. However, the specific flow rate estimate of 20 l/s lacks external verification. Flow measurements for individual springs in the Titijones area are not publicly documented, and discharge estimates require direct field measurement. The cerro Limani and Tutupaca as source areas is geologically reasonable given their elevations and position relative to the aquifer system, but confirmation would require hydrochemical or isotopic tracing.]

### 5.2 Manante 2

Constituye el manantial principal ubicada en la ruta Titijones hacia laguna Suches, próximo a vivienda de comunero, discurren sus aguas hacia la quebrada Titijones, coordenadas de ubicación: E-340674, N-8124823; inmediatamente agua abajo y ambas márgenes se desarrolla bofedales y humedales sobre extensa llanura fluvioglaciar, las aguas discurren en dirección NO-SE.

^[Confidence: HIGH. Rationale: This description follows the same hydrogeological pattern as Manante 1, with spring discharge creating downstream bofedales and wetlands. The spatial clustering of multiple springs in the same geographic area is characteristic of aquifer discharge zones where preferential flow paths intersect topographic lows. The coordinate proximity (~200m horizontal distance from Manante 1) suggests a shared hydrogeological control. The development of wetlands on both margins (ambas márgenes) of the drainage immediately downstream is consistent with shallow groundwater tables maintained by spring discharge, a well-documented phenomenon in Andean fluvioglacial plains [WEB:https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0048969722030650].]

Origen de los manantiales posiblemente por control litológico-estructural del basamento volcánico, las aguas son provenientes de las altas cumbres del cerro Limani y Tutupaca; se estima caudal de 0.5 l/s. (Apéndice B, Figura 52_53)

^[Confidence: MEDIUM. Rationale: The hydrogeological interpretation of lithologic-structural control is consistent with Manante 1 and supported by regional evidence. The same high-elevation recharge source (cerro Limani and Tutupaca) is plausible given the geographic position. The estimated flow rate of 0.5 l/s (40 times smaller than Manante 1) reflects the typical variability in spring discharge within the same aquifer system, where individual fracture or stratigraphic pathways produce different flows. However, as with Manante 1, the specific discharge value lacks external verification and would require field measurement for confirmation.]

## Summary of Findings

**Overall Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH**

The section on manantiales (springs) demonstrates strong consistency with established hydrogeological principles for high-altitude Andean aquifer systems. Key strengths include:

1. **Hydrogeological mechanisms**: Spring emergence feeding bofedales is well-documented in the literature
2. **Structural controls**: Lithologic-structural control of springs in volcanic basement is confirmed for the region
3. **Spatial patterns**: The clustering of springs and their relationship to wetland development is typical

**Limitations identified:**

1. **Flow rate verification**: The specific discharge estimates (20 l/s and 0.5 l/s) cannot be independently verified from external sources
2. **Recharge source attribution**: While geologically plausible, the specific identification of cerro Limani and Tutupaca as source areas lacks hydrochemical or isotopic confirmation in the public literature

**Recommendation**: The section would benefit from citation of field measurements for spring discharge and, if available, hydrochemical or isotopic data confirming the recharge source attribution.
