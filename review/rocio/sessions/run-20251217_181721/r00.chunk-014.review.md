The Titijones area is documented as an aquifer system in the Tacna-Moquegua region that supplies water to mining operations [WEB:https://southerncoppercorp.com/eng/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/03/10k2022.pdf], confirming the existence of the Titijones hydrological system. However, the specific coordinates and directional flow (NW-SE) cannot be independently verified without field data or detailed topographic maps. The spatial relationship between springs, bofedales, and fluvioglacial landforms is geomorphologically plausible for this region.]

### 10.2 Curso de agua 2

Corresponde al curso superior de la quebrada Titijones, cuyas aguas se originan mayormente en dos manantiales ubicadas próximo a vivienda de comunero; coordenadas de ubicación: E-340860 y N-8124765; E-340674, N-8124823; aguas abajo discurre de manera meandriforme a sinuosa, baja pendiente, con aportes de tributarios en algunos casos delimitados por bofedales y humedales; cauce principal corta a la amplia llanura fluvioglaciar, en general tiene una orientación de ángulo recto invertido, posiblemente causado por control tectónico-estructural del basamento volcánico. (Apéndice B, Figura 61)

^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The description of meandering to sinuous flow patterns on low-gradient fluvioglacial plains is consistent with established geomorphological principles. Low-gradient streams characteristically develop meandering patterns, and the presence of bofedales and tributaries in such settings is documented for Andean wetland systems [DOI:10.1002/hyp.14940]. The assertion that the stream exhibits a "right-angle inverted orientation" due to tectonic-structural control is geologically plausible. Rectangular drainage patterns with right-angle bends are well-documented in areas with structural control by joints and faults in bedrock [WEB:https://www.geologyin.com/2014/03/drainage-pattern.html] [WEB:https://fiveable.me/earth-surface-processes/unit-6/drainage-patterns-geological-significance/study-guide/1Ao5sd3n9ZL13Rq1]. Such patterns form when streams follow zones of weakness in fractured volcanic basement rocks. However, the specific "inverted right angle" orientation cannot be verified without access to the referenced figure or topographic data. The interpretation linking this pattern to basement tectonic-structural control aligns with drainage pattern theory.]

^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The description of the middle reach of Quebrada Titijones exhibiting meandering patterns on low gradients with tributary inputs from bofedales is consistent with fluvial geomorphology principles and documented characteristics of Andean stream systems [DOI:10.1002/hyp.14940]. Stream valleys frequently follow structural weaknesses such as faults and fracture zones in bedrock, and tectonic uplift influences valley development and stream gradient [WEB:https://www.lyellcollection.org/doi/full/10.1144/SP540-2024-33] [WEB:https://fiveable.me/earth-surface-processes/unit-6/drainage-patterns-geological-significance/study-guide/1Ao5sd3n9ZL13Rq1]. The slightly closed (slightly incised) valley form is consistent with incision into fluvioglacial deposits over volcanic basement. However, without access to the referenced figures or independent topographic/structural data, the specific NE-SW orientation and its tectonic origin cannot be independently confirmed. The interpretation represents a reasonable geological hypothesis based on regional structural geology.]
