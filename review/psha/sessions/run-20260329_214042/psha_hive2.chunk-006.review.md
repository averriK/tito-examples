## Site Classification {#sec-vs30_appendix}

The **Vs30** parameter is widely measured in the field using methods such as borehole logging, seismic cone penetration tests, and surface wave techniques. Introduced into engineering practice in the early 1990s, it provides a simple, robust metric to characterize site conditions for earthquake ground motions. **Vs30** is defined as the time-averaged shear-wave velocity in the upper 30 meters of a site profile. In practical terms, it represents the overall stiffness of the near-surface soils and rock. Mathematically, Vs30 is computed as the harmonic average of shear-wave velocity ($V_{S}$) over the top 30 m: ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The definition of $V_{S30}$ as the time-averaged (harmonic mean) shear-wave velocity in the upper 30 m, its introduction in the early 1990s, and its widespread use in GMPE site characterisation are all confirmed by multiple authoritative sources including the USGS and NEHRP provisions [WEB:https://www.usgs.gov/publications/mapping-nehrp-vs30-site-classes]. The field measurement methods listed (borehole logging, seismic cone penetration test, surface wave techniques) are standard methods for determining shear-wave velocity profiles. The claim is well-supported.]

$$
V_{S30} = \frac{30\,\text{m}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{H_i}{V_{S,i}}}
$$

where $H_i$ and $V_{S,i}$ are the thickness and shear-wave velocity of the $i$-th layer in the upper 30 m. This formula gives the equivalent uniform velocity that yields the same travel time through 30 m of material as the actual layered profile. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The formula $V_{S30} = 30\,\text{m} / \sum_{i=1}^{N}(H_i / V_{S,i})$ is the standard harmonic-mean (travel-time-weighted) definition of $V_{S30}$ as specified in ASCE 7 and NEHRP provisions [WEB:https://www.usgs.gov/publications/mapping-nehrp-vs30-site-classes][WEB:https://www.daveboore.com/pubs_online/vs30_from_shallow_models.pdf]. The interpretation as the equivalent uniform velocity yielding the same travel time through 30 m is mathematically correct. The claim is fully supported.]

Vs30 has become the primary quantitative predictor of site effects in modern ground-motion models. Virtually all recent ground motion prediction equations (GMPEs) incorporate Vs30 as a key input parameter to adjust predicted shaking for site conditions. Empirical GMPEs (e.g., NGA-West and NGA-East) often use a functional form in which the median ground motion is scaled according to $\ln(V_{S30})$ or a similar proxy, with separate terms for linear scaling and nonlinear saturation at low velocities. Large datasets have validated the use of Vs30 in GMPEs, showing consistent correlations between Vs30 and site amplification across many locations [@Borcherdt2012]. ^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The use of $V_{S30}$ as the primary site parameter in NGA-West2 and NGA-East GMPEs, with $\ln(V_{S30})$-based functional forms and separate nonlinear terms, is well-established and confirmed by published GMPE documentation [WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/8755293019878193][WEB:https://opensha.org/resources/PSHA_Primer_v2_0.pdf]. The reference [@Borcherdt2012] likely refers to Borcherdt (2012) on site coefficients and site classification, a recognized authority on the topic [WEB:https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70022904]. However, the specific year (2012) and full citation details for this Borcherdt reference could not be independently confirmed from the available sources; published Borcherdt work on site classification is from several periods including 1994 and later. Confidence is MEDIUM due to the partially unverified citation year for the Borcherdt reference.]

In parallel, site-specific analyses (such as 1D site response simulations) rely on Vs30 as an initial classification of the site profile, although they incorporate the full shear-wave velocity profile with depth. While Vs30 does not capture all facets of site response—such as basin depth or resonance effects—it serves as a practical first-order proxy. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The statement that site-specific 1D site response simulations use the full shear-wave velocity profile rather than relying solely on $V_{S30}$, and that $V_{S30}$ does not capture basin depth or resonance effects, is accurate and consistent with the established literature on site response analysis [WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/8755293019878193][WEB:https://opensha.org/resources/PSHA_Primer_v2_0.pdf]. The description of $V_{S30}$ as a practical first-order proxy is standard and well-supported. The claim is accurate.]

### NEHRP Site Classes

The NEHRP provisions [-@BSSC2015] classify sites into categories (Site Class A through E, and special Class F) based on Vs30 ranges. This system provides a convenient way to assign site amplification factors in building codes and engineering practice. The standard classes and their Vs30 criteria are approximately: ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The NEHRP site classification system (Site Classes A through F) based on $V_{S30}$ thresholds is defined in the NEHRP provisions and adopted by ASCE 7 and IBC. The reference [@BSSC2015] corresponds to the 2015 NEHRP Recommended Seismic Provisions for New Buildings and Other Structures (FEMA P-1050), which documents this classification system [WEB:https://www.nehrp.gov/pdf/H-18-8.pdf]. The description is accurate and the claim is well-supported.]

* **Site Class A:** Hard rock, $V_{S30}>1500$ m/s (very high velocity material).
* **Site Class B:** Rock, $V_{S30}\approx$ 760–1500 m/s (typical competent rock; 760 m/s is the B/C boundary).
* **Site Class C:** Very dense soil or soft rock, $V_{S30}\approx$ 360–760 m/s.
* **Site Class D:** Stiff soil, $V_{S30}\approx$ 180–360 m/s.
* **Site Class E:** Soft soil, $V_{S30}<$ 180 m/s (includes clay-rich soils prone to high amplification).
* **Site Class F:** Soils requiring site-specific evaluation (e.g., very soft clays, peats, liquefiable soils, or thick high-plasticity clays irrespective of Vs30).

^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The $V_{S30}$ thresholds for NEHRP Site Classes A through F — A: $>1500$ m/s, B: 760-1500 m/s, C: 360-760 m/s, D: 180-360 m/s, E: $<180$ m/s, F: special conditions — are consistent with NEHRP provisions and are confirmed by multiple USGS and ASCE 7 sources [WEB:https://www.usgs.gov/publications/mapping-nehrp-vs30-site-classes][WEB:https://earthquake.usgs.gov/ws/designmaps/asce7-22.html]. The identification of 760 m/s as the B/C boundary is accurate. The description of Site Class F as defined by qualitative subsoil conditions rather than a $V_{S30}$ threshold is also correct. The claim is well-supported.]

Classes A–E are defined by the Vs30 thresholds above. Class F is defined by qualitative subsoil conditions (such as softness or thickness) rather than a Vs30 value; if those conditions are met, a detailed site response analysis is mandated in lieu of using generic factors. These site classes provide "unambiguous definitions of site categories and corresponding site coefficients" in building code applications. In structural design, each class is assigned factors for ground motion adjustment (e.g. $F_a$, $F_v$ in NEHRP/ASCE 7) that modify the standard response spectrum to account for local site amplification. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The description of Site Class F as requiring site-specific analysis and the use of $F_a$ and $F_v$ factors in NEHRP/ASCE 7 for Classes A-E to adjust the standard response spectrum are standard provisions confirmed by NEHRP and ASCE 7 documentation [WEB:https://www.nehrp.gov/pdf/H-18-8.pdf][WEB:https://earthquake.usgs.gov/ws/designmaps/asce7-22.html]. The quoted phrase "unambiguous definitions of site categories and corresponding site coefficients" is consistent with the purpose of the NEHRP site classification system. The claim is accurate and well-supported.]

### AS1726 Guidelines

The Australian Standard AS 1726-2017 [-@AS1726-2017] provides guidelines for conducting geotechnical investigations and includes a site classification system that categorises sites into classes based on their soil properties, such as shear wave velocity parameter $\bar v_S$, depth to bedrock, and soil type. The Australian Standard AS 1170.4 "Earthquake Actions on Structures" uses a similar site classification system based on the stiffness of the ground, but it does not explicitly use the shear wave velocity parameter $\bar v_S$ to classify the ground. The categories range from A (rock) to E (soft soil), with a special category (S) for sites requiring site-specific investigations. ^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: AS 1726-2017 is a current Australian standard for geotechnical site investigations and includes guidance on site classification. AS 1170.4 is the Australian standard for earthquake actions. The description of the A-to-E plus special category (S) classification hierarchy and the role of shear wave velocity in AS 1726-2017 but not explicitly in AS 1170.4 is plausible and consistent with the known structure of Australian standards. However, independent external confirmation of the specific AS 1170.4 classification details described here (particularly that it does not explicitly use $\bar v_S$) could not be established from the publicly available sources reviewed. Confidence is MEDIUM due to the limited publicly accessible external verification of the specific AS 1170.4 provisions.]

### ASCE/SEI 7-22 Guidelines

The ASCE/SEI 7-22 Standard [-@ASCE722] provides guidelines for conducting geotechnical investigations and includes a site classification system that categorises sites into classes based on their soil properties, such as shear wave velocity, depth to bedrock, and soil type. According to ASCE 7-22 [@ASCE722], the site soil shall be classified based on the average shear wave velocity parameter, $\bar v_S$, which is derived from the measured shear wave velocity profile from the ground surface to a depth of 30 m. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: ASCE 7-22 Chapter 20 specifies site classification based on $\bar v_S$ (average shear-wave velocity in the upper 30 m, equivalent to $V_{S30}$), along with average standard penetration test blow count and undrained shear strength. The description of the classification being based on soil properties including shear wave velocity, depth to bedrock, and soil type is accurate [WEB:https://amplify.asce.org/content/standard/9780784415788/part/provisions/standard-chapter/s11][WEB:https://earthquake.usgs.gov/ws/designmaps/asce7-22.html]. The claim is well-supported.]

### NBC-2020 Guidelines

The National Building Code of Canada [-@NBC2020] provides guidelines for site characterization and seismic site response. Similar to ASCE 7-22, it uses an average shear wave velocity in the upper 30 m (commonly denoted $v_{S30}$), standard penetration test (SPT) blow count, and/or undrained shear strength ($s_{u}$) to classify sites into categories A through F. Where site-specific investigations are warranted (e.g., liquefiable soils, highly sensitive clays, peat), the code requires more detailed analysis. ^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The National Building Code of Canada (NBC) 2020 includes seismic site classification provisions that use $v_{S30}$, SPT blow count, and undrained shear strength $s_u$ for site categories A through F, consistent with the description provided. The classification structure is aligned with the NEHRP/ASCE 7 framework [WEB:https://www.usgs.gov/publications/mapping-nehrp-vs30-site-classes]. The requirement for more detailed analysis for special site conditions (liquefiable soils, sensitive clays, peat) is standard across major building codes. Confidence is MEDIUM because independent external confirmation of the specific NBC 2020 site classification provisions from publicly accessible sources was limited; the description is consistent with the known NBC 2015/2020 framework but the specific 2020 edition details require access to the normative document.]
