## MCER and ELastic Design Spectra (ASCE) {#sec-mcer}

Seismic demand for all Risk Categories I–IV was derived in accordance with **ASCE/SEI 7-22**—as adopted by **IBC 2021**—using the site-specific uniform-hazard spectrum (UHS) established in the preceding chapter. Chapter 11 of ASCE 7 permits replacement of mapped spectral accelerations by values obtained from a probabilistic hazard analysis coupled with site-response modelling, provided the procedures of §11.4.7 are satisfied [@ASCE722 §11.4.7]. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: ASCE/SEI 7-22 is the current edition of the standard and IBC 2021 does adopt ASCE 7-22 for seismic design requirements. Section 11.4.7 of ASCE 7-22 explicitly permits site-specific procedures as an alternative to mapped spectral accelerations provided the requirements of Chapter 21 are satisfied [WEB:https://amplify.asce.org/content/standard/9780784415788/part/provisions/standard-chapter/s11]. The description of the UHS-based approach is accurate and the procedure is well-established. The claim is well-supported.]

When the procedures of either Section §21.1 or §21.2 are used, the design response spectrum shall be determined in accordance with Section §21.3, the design acceleration parameters shall be determined in accordance with Section §21.4. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: This paragraph accurately describes the procedural chain required by ASCE 7-22: site-specific ground motions are developed via §21.1 or §21.2, the design response spectrum is derived per §21.3, and design acceleration parameters $S_{DS}$ and $S_{D1}$ are determined per §21.4 [WEB:https://amplify.asce.org/content/standard/9780784415788/part/provisions/standard-chapter/s21]. This sequence is a direct and accurate reflection of the normative structure of ASCE 7-22 Chapter 21.]

The probabilistic spectral response accelerations shall be taken as the spectral response accelerations in the direction of maximum horizontal response represented by a 5% damped acceleration response spectrum that is expected to achieve a 1% probability of collapse within a 50-year period. If the spectral response accelerations predicted by the ground motion models do not represent the maximum response in the horizontal plane, then the response spectral accelerations computed from the hazard analysis shall be scaled by factors to increase the motions to the maximum response. If the ground motion models predict the geometric mean or similar metric of the two horizontal components, then the scale factors shall be 1.2 for periods less than, or equal to, 0.2 s, 1.25 for a period of 1.0 s, and 1.3 for periods greater than or equal to 10 s. ^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The definition of MCER as a 5% damped spectrum targeting a 1% probability of collapse in 50 years is consistent with the risk-targeted ground motion framework introduced in ASCE 7-10 and retained in ASCE 7-22 [WEB:https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/codes-and-standards/asce-sei-7-22]. The scale factors for converting geometric-mean (RotD50) predictions to the maximum horizontal component (RotD100) are specified in ASCE 7-22 §21.1 and are consistent with published research on the RotD100/RotD50 ratio [WEB:https://amplify.asce.org/content/standard/9780784415788/part/provisions/standard-chapter/s21]. The stated values of 1.2 at $T \leq 0.2$ s, 1.25 at $T = 1.0$ s, and 1.3 at $T \geq 10$ s are plausible period-anchored values consistent with the ASCE 7-22 framework; however, direct normative text confirmation of the precise endpoint value of 1.3 at 10 s could not be independently verified from publicly accessible sources without access to the full standard text. The paragraph does not specify that log-log interpolation is used between anchored period values, which is a relevant omission for practitioners applying the scaling. Confidence is MEDIUM on account of this gap.]

Consistent with §21.3, the **design-level spectral accelerations** are obtained by scaling the MCER ordinates by the factor $2/3$ [@ASCE722 Eq. 21.3-1]: ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The two-thirds scaling relationship $S_a = (2/3)\,S_{aM}$ from MCER to design-level spectral acceleration is the standard relationship codified since ASCE 7-10 and retained in ASCE 7-22 as Equation 21.3-1 [WEB:https://amplify.asce.org/content/standard/9780784415788/part/provisions/standard-chapter/s21][WEB:https://earthquake.usgs.gov/ws/designmaps/asce7-22.html]. The claim is unambiguous and fully supported.]

$$
S_{a}=\frac{2}{3}S_{aM}
$$
