Near-surface geology modifies seismic waves as they propagate from bedrock to the ground surface. Soft soils amplify ground motion at intermediate-to-long periods while potentially deamplifying short-period motion through nonlinear strain effects; stiff rock sites transmit near-bedrock intensity levels with minimal modification [@Baker2021]. In probabilistic seismic hazard analysis, site effects can be incorporated through two mechanisms: (1) the site term embedded within ground-motion prediction equations (GMPEs), in which the median prediction and aleatory variability are functions of $V_{S30}$, or (2) an external amplification model applied to a reference-rock hazard curve [@Stewart2020]. The first mechanism is ergodic by construction—the empirical site term averages over many recording sites globally and may not capture conditions at a specific site or region. The second mechanism allows regionally calibrated amplification that accounts for nonlinear soil behavior as a function of shaking intensity. This assessment employs both approaches and selects the controlling (maximum) spectral ordinate for each period and hazard level to ensure that the design spectrum is not governed by whichever model happens to be less conservative at a given period. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The description of the two mechanisms for incorporating site effects in PSHA — the $V_{S30}$-dependent GMPE site term and an external amplification model applied to a reference-rock hazard curve — is accurate and consistent with current practice as described in Stewart et al. (2020) and the NGA-West2/NGA-East frameworks [WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/8755293019878193]. The characterisation of the GMPE site term as ergodic (averaging over many recording sites globally) is standard and correct [WEB:https://opensha.org/resources/PSHA_Primer_v2_0.pdf]. The envelope approach (taking the maximum of both models) is a recognized conservative design strategy. The claim is well-supported.]

Site effects were introduced in the probabilistic seismic hazard analysis through two complementary models. The first used an ergodic ground-motion framework in which a logic tree of $V_{S30}$-dependent GMPEs was evaluated for shear-wave velocities ranging from 200 m/s to 1100 m/s. For every branch, OpenQuake computed spectral accelerations $S_a(T_n, V_{S30})$ at the required service levels. Epistemic uncertainty was represented by the branch weights prescribed in the logic tree, and aleatory variability followed the total standard deviation of each GMPE. Because the site term is embedded empirically within the GMPEs, the resulting uniform-hazard spectra remain ergodic. ^[Confidence: MEDIUM, Rationale: The use of a GMPE logic tree evaluated across a range of $V_{S30}$ values (200-1100 m/s) within OpenQuake Engine is consistent with standard PSHA practice for site-class-dependent hazard calculations. The $V_{S30}$ range stated (200-1100 m/s) covers Site Classes E through B/C boundary, which is a reasonable design range [WEB:https://earthquake.usgs.gov/ws/designmaps/asce7-22.html]. The characterisation of branch weights as representing epistemic uncertainty and GMPE total standard deviation as representing aleatory variability is correct [WEB:https://opensha.org/resources/PSHA_Primer_v2_0.pdf]. Confidence is MEDIUM because this paragraph describes site-specific implementation choices (the specific GMPE logic tree used, the exact $V_{S30}$ range, and the service levels targeted) that are not independently verifiable from external sources; these are project-specific methodological choices whose adequacy depends on the specific GMPEs selected and their applicability to the site region.]

The second model is an amplification model based on a reference-rock hazard curve at $V_{\mathrm{ref}} = 760\,\text{m/s}$ and transforms it to the target condition $V_{S30}$ by applying period-dependent, nonlinear amplification factors $F(T_n, V_{S30})$, according to [@eq-site-af]. The amplification model follows the formulation of Stewart and co-authors, which expresses $F$ as a log-normal random variable whose mean $\eta_{F\mid\mathrm{PGA}}\!\left(T_n, V_{S30}\right)$ and dispersion $\sigma_{\ln F}$ depend on oscillator period, shear-wave velocity, and input PGA [@Stewart2020; @Hashash2020]. For $V_{S30} < V_{\mathrm{ref}}$, the model captures nonlinear deamplification at short periods and amplification at intermediate-to-long periods; for stiffer sites, the amplification asymptotically approaches unity. Formally, the amplification factor is modeled as a lognormal random variable [@eq-site-lnf]: ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The description of the amplification model using a reference-rock hazard curve at $V_{\mathrm{ref}} = 760$ m/s transformed by period-dependent nonlinear amplification factors is consistent with the Stewart et al. and Hashash et al. frameworks for NGA-East site amplification. Hashash et al. (2020) explicitly describes two median nonlinear amplification models as functions of $V_{S30}$ and PGA for reference conditions of $V_{S} = 3000$ m/s and $V_{S30} = 760$ m/s, composed of three additive components ($F_{760}$, $F_V$, $F_{nl}$) [WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/8755293019878193]. The log-normal characterisation of the amplification factor $F$ and its dependence on $V_{S30}$, period, and input PGA is accurate [WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/8755293019878193]. The behavior at $V_{S30} < V_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (nonlinear deamplification at short periods, amplification at long periods) and the asymptotic approach to unity for stiff sites are consistent with published models. The claim is well-supported.]

$$
\ln F \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mu_{\ln F},\; \sigma_{\ln F}^2\right)
$${#eq-site-lnf}

where $\mu_{\ln F}$ is the conditional mean of the log-amplification factor and $\sigma_{\ln F}$ is the total aleatory standard deviation, both for a given ($V_{S30}$, $T_n$, $pga^*$). The mean $\mu_{\ln F}$ decomposes additively into three period-dependent terms [@Stewart2020]: a reference-condition adjustment $F_{760}$ that bridges the difference between the 760 m/s and 3000 m/s reference horizons, a linear site term $F_V$ that scales with $V_{S30}$ relative to the reference velocity, and a nonlinear factor $F_{nl}$ that captures PGA-dependent soil deamplification at high strain levels. Similarly, the total dispersion decomposes in quadrature [@eq-site-sigma-decomp]: ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The three-component additive decomposition of $\mu_{\ln F}$ into $F_{760}$, $F_V$, and $F_{nl}$ is consistent with the Hashash et al. (2020) model for central and eastern North America, which explicitly defines these three components [WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/8755293019878193]. The $F_{760}$ component bridges the 760 m/s and 3000 m/s reference horizons; the $F_V$ component provides linear $V_{S30}$ scaling; and $F_{nl}$ captures nonlinear PGA-dependent deamplification, all consistent with the published model [WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/8755293019878193]. The description of total dispersion decomposing in quadrature is consistent with standard uncertainty propagation for independent dispersion components. The claim is well-supported.]

$$
\sigma_{\ln F}^2 = \sigma_{L}^2 + \sigma_{I}^2 + \sigma_{NL}^2
$${#eq-site-sigma-decomp}

where $\sigma_L$ is the linear site-term dispersion (dependent on $V_{S30}$ and period), $\sigma_I$ is the inter-reference dispersion (nonzero only when $V_{\mathrm{ref}} = 3000\,\text{m/s}$), and $\sigma_{NL}$ is the nonlinear dispersion (dependent on $V_{S30}$ and $pga^*$). All components are period-dependent and tabulated in the published model [@Stewart2020]. The detailed functional forms and calibration of each component are documented in [@sec-site_model]. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The identification of $\sigma_L$ (linear $V_{S30}$-dependent dispersion), $\sigma_I$ (inter-reference dispersion, nonzero only when $V_{\mathrm{ref}} = 3000$ m/s), and $\sigma_{NL}$ (nonlinear, $V_{S30}$- and PGA-dependent dispersion) is consistent with the structure of the Hashash et al. (2020) model [WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/8755293019878193]. The statement that $\sigma_I$ is nonzero only when $V_{\mathrm{ref}} = 3000$ m/s (the NGA-East hard-rock reference condition) correctly reflects the model's construction, where the inter-reference component accounts for uncertainty in the adjustment from the 3000 m/s to the 760 m/s reference [WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/8755293019878193]. The claim is well-supported.]

$$
S_a\!\left(T_n, V_{S30}\right) = F\!\left(T_n, V_{S30}\right)\,S_a\!\left(T_n, V_{\mathrm{ref}}\right).
$${#eq-site-af}

For each return period $T_R$, oscillator period $T_n$, fractile $p$, and site class $V_{S30}$, the design spectral ordinate is defined as the maximum across the GMPE-based and amplification-based estimates [@eq-site-envelope]: ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The envelope approach — selecting the maximum spectral ordinate from the two model approaches at each period and hazard level — is a recognized and conservative method for design spectrum construction that protects against underestimation by either model individually. The notation and formulation are internally consistent with the preceding sections. The claim is well-supported.]

$$
S_a^{\mathrm{max}}(T_n, V_{S30}) = \max\!\left\{S_a^{\mathrm{GMPE}}(T_n, V_{S30}),\; S_a^{F}(T_n, V_{S30})\right\}
$${#eq-site-envelope}

where $S_a^{\mathrm{GMPE}}$ is the spectral ordinate obtained from the GMPE logic tree evaluated directly at the target $V_{S30}$, and $S_a^{F}$ is the spectral ordinate obtained by applying the amplification factor $F$ to the reference-rock hazard curve per [@eq-site-af]. This envelope ensures that the controlling (most hazardous) estimate governs the design spectrum at every period. GMPEs may underpredict at certain periods or $V_{S30}$ ranges where their empirical site terms are poorly constrained by the underlying strong-motion database, while the amplification model may underpredict where the reference-rock hazard curve does not fully represent near-field or source-specific effects. The envelope eliminates the risk of unconservative design by selecting the larger ordinate at each spectral period. ^[Confidence: HIGH, Rationale: The rationale for the envelope — that GMPEs may underpredict at poorly constrained $V_{S30}$ ranges while amplification models may underpredict for near-field or source-specific effects — is technically sound and reflects known limitations of both approaches [WEB:https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/8755293019878193][WEB:https://opensha.org/resources/PSHA_Primer_v2_0.pdf]. The selection of the maximum ordinate at each period as the governing design value is a standard conservative design choice. The claim is well-supported and technically accurate.]
